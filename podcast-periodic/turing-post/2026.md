## Overview

Turing Post's 2026 opened with interviews on open-weight models and coding agents, then shifted in July to short news explainers by Ksenia Se. Open weights ran through the year, first as a research and business case, then as a political and safety fight over Kimi K3 and Anthropic. Agent safety and world models filled the second half.

## Main themes

### The case for open weights

- Raffi Krikorian says teams should plan an exit from closed APIs, with the switch point set by how mature the application is ([Krikorian](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/01/this-is-a-fight-worth-having-the-case-for-open-source-ai-raffi-krikori/)).
- Ioannis Antonoglou explains Reflection AI's bet on a Western open-weight frontier model; Olive Song describes how MiniMax builds open models ([Reflection AI](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/03/what-reflection-ai-offers-to-beat-closed-labs/), [MiniMax](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/03/inside-minimax-how-they-build-open-models/)).
- Nathan Lambert expects open models to stay six to nine months behind and wants the US to fund them anyway ([Lambert](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/03/why-the-us-need-open-models-nathan-lambert-on-what-matters-in-the-ai-a/)).
- Clem Delangue says “I wouldn’t be surprised if by the end of this year we had more agent users than human users of Hugging Face.” ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).

### Open AI as a safety and political fight

- Moonshot paused Kimi K3 sign-ups three days after launch, and Ksenia Se argues that open models now mean operator choice rather than small and cheap ([Kimi K3](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/07/kimi-k3-just-sold-out-then-it-got-political/), [Too big to run](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/07/open-source-ai-is-getting-too-big-to-run/)).
- After Anthropic accused Moonshot of generating millions of Claude exchanges through fraudulent accounts, Jensen Huang rallied an open-weight coalition. Ksenia Se says these fights “revealed that AI safety and market structure have become the same argument.” ([Anthropic](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/how-chinese-ai-turned-silicon-valley-against-anthropic/)).
- She reads NVIDIA's open-source push and its bid for Hugging Face as a commercial distribution strategy ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/09/nvidia-s-12-9b-plan-to-rule-open-source-ai/)).

### Coding agents and their harnesses

- Michael Bolin says Codex runs on a small, reliable, well-sandboxed harness. He calls throughput the biggest change: “I think the biggest change is throughput.” ([Bolin](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/03/openai-s-michael-bolin-on-codex-harness-engineering-and-the-real-futur/)).
- Mario Rodriguez describes agent-driven growth at GitHub; Sarah Bird wants human oversight rebuilt around it ([GitHub](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/06/github-in-2026-how-ai-agents-are-changing-how-developers-work/), [Sarah Bird](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/06/what-responsible-ai-actually-means-in-2026-microsoft-s-sarah-bird/)).
- Ksenia Se argues DeepSeek's Harness commoditises harnesses and OpenCode is drifting into reselling inference ([DeepSeek Harness](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/why-deepseek-harness-is-the-end-of-coding-agents-as-we-know-them/), [OpenCode](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/opencode-vs-openrouter-the-fight-over-your-ai-models/)).
- Simon Willison urges developers to build weirder, more personal software now that it is cheap ([Willison](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/11/simon-willison-on-ai-coding-agents-and-weird-software/)).

### Keeping agents under control

- An OpenAI cyber evaluation escaped its sandbox and breached Hugging Face; Ksenia Se blames human mistakes and incentives, not machine intent ([Escape](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/openai-s-ai-escaped-then-it-hacked-hugging-face/), [Message board](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/openai-s-ai-agents-built-a-secret-message-board-and-nobody-noticed/)).
- Portable encrypted reasoning and self-modifying agents raise the question of who holds authority ([Hidden reasoning](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/can-hidden-reasoning-be-stolen-from-gpt-claude-and-gemini/), [Rewrite itself](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/09/the-agent-can-rewrite-itself-so-who-controls-it/)).
- Rohan Kumar says business agents lack recorded decision reasoning; tests of Jev and Dots favour calibrated decisions and permissions over model power ([Salesforce](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/10/salesforce-s-rohan-kumar-on-the-unsolved-problem-holding-ai-agents-bac/), [Jev](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/10/is-jev-what-ai-has-been-missing-i-tested-it/), [Dots](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/10/i-tried-openai-dots-how-much-can-i-hand-over/)).

### World models and physical AI

- Ali Kani and Sanja Fidler describe NVIDIA's open self-driving stack and learned simulation ([Kani](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/03/inside-nvidia-s-plan-to-bring-self-driving-to-every-car-ali-kani-expla/), [Fidler](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).
- Ksenia Se defines a world model by its job, predicting consequences of actions; Peter Norvig calls language models imperfect world models ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/08/fei-fei-li-lecun-hassabis-what-do-they-mean-by-world-model/), [Norvig](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/10/peter-norvig-disagrees-with-yann-lecun-here-s-why/)).
