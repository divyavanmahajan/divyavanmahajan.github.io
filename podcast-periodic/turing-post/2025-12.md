## Overview

December on Turing Post was a busy month of seven interviews by Ksenia Se. Physical AI was the one theme that ran through more than one central conversation, from NVIDIA's simulation stack to visual memory for robots and glasses. The other episodes covered keeping enterprise agents under control, OpenAI's Atlas browser, and specialised AI for mathematics and pathology.

## Main themes

### Physical AI: simulation, world models and memory

- Spencer Huang says physical AI needs three NVIDIA computers: DGX for training, OVX/Omniverse for simulation and on-robot compute for deployment ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/spencer-huang-nvidia-s-big-plan-for-physical-ai-simulation-world-model/)).
- Huang says world models can generate diverse scenes, but “the physics is never grounded, and so it's never consistent.” He wants them combined with conventional simulators in a data flywheel ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/spencer-huang-nvidia-s-big-plan-for-physical-ai-simulation-world-model/)).
- Huang sees robotics' missing data corpus as the biggest problem and urges the industry to stop hoarding data. NVIDIA open-sources Newton, Isaac Lab, Warp and PhysX ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/spencer-huang-nvidia-s-big-plan-for-physical-ai-simulation-world-model/)).
- Shawn Shen of Memories.ai argues AI has built intelligence but not memory, and devices that see also need to remember. He treats memory as an indexing and retrieval system running on device ([Memories](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/are-memories-the-missing-half-of-ai-brain/)).

### Keeping enterprise agents under control

- Anneka Gupta of Rubrik frames resilience as visibility, then governance, then reversibility, because agents pursue an outcome regardless of what they change, such as dropping a database table ([Rubrik](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/can-we-control-ai-that-controls-itself-anneka-gupta-from-rubrik-on/)).
- Her practical advice is to “log every single thing your AI agent is doing” and stitch model calls and tool actions into an agent action timeline ([Rubrik](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/can-we-control-ai-that-controls-itself-anneka-gupta-from-rubrik-on/)).
- Gupta cites estimates that 70% to 90% of enterprise AI projects never leave prototyping, often slowed by governance committees worried about data access ([Rubrik](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/can-we-control-ai-that-controls-itself-anneka-gupta-from-rubrik-on/)).
- Renen Hallak of VAST Data argues agents moving out of siloed sandboxes need an AI operating system that adds security, observability, reproducibility and compliance to data and compute ([VAST Data](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/why-do-we-need-a-special-operating-system-for-ai/)).

### The AI browser and the agentic web

- Ben Goodger says ChatGPT Atlas is a Netscape 1.0 moment and that, for OpenAI, the browser is where AGI can take action ([Atlas](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/ben-goodger-on-chatgpt-atlas-why-openai-built-an-ai-browser-for-agi/)).
- Agent mode reads a site's DOM or uses computer-use vision. It is opt-in, stops on sensitive sites if you switch tabs, and has a big red stop button ([Atlas](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/ben-goodger-on-chatgpt-atlas-why-openai-built-an-ai-browser-for-agi/)).
- Ksenia Se sees the web splitting into a human web and an agentic web. Goodger advises site owners to improve accessibility, since what helps screen readers also helps agents ([Atlas](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/ben-goodger-on-chatgpt-atlas-why-openai-built-an-ai-browser-for-agi/)).

### Specialist AI for maths and medicine

- Carina Hong describes Axiom's AI mathematician: a prover, a growing knowledge base and a conjecturer in a self-improving loop, with every step verifiable in Lean ([Axiom Math](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/what-ai-is-missing-for-real-reasoning-axiom-math-s-carina-hong-on-how/)).
- Hong notes Lean has only about 10 million tokens against more than 1 trillion for Python, a cold-start problem ([Axiom Math](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/what-ai-is-missing-for-real-reasoning-axiom-math-s-carina-hong-on-how/)).
- Akash Parvatikar of HistoWiz sees AI as a diagnostic assistant for pathologists. He names scarce, inconsistent annotated data as the main barrier, and says “the AI is not able to tell its failure modes” ([Pathology](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/the-future-of-cancer-diagnosis-digital-pathology-and-ai/)).
