## Overview

Turing Post's April episodes asked two founders when a hard problem will be solved. Mati Staniszewski of ElevenLabs gave a timeline for live speech translation, and Sharon Zhou of Lamini argued that hallucinations are an engineering problem rather than a philosophical one. Both talked about what enterprises actually need: control, accuracy on their own tasks and models they can steer.

## Main themes

### Speech translation without a language barrier

- Staniszewski expects translated media streams this year, earbud-style live translation in two to three years and use anywhere within five ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-speak-without-language-barrier-a-conversation-with-mati-s/)).
- He breaks the problem into speech-to-text, LLM translation and text-to-speech. Recognition is mostly solved for major languages. Context such as sarcasm is still hard, and emotion is the missing piece because little speech data records how something was said ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-speak-without-language-barrier-a-conversation-with-mati-s/)).
- Latency is already low: about one to 1.2 seconds end to end, with 70 milliseconds for text-to-speech ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-speak-without-language-barrier-a-conversation-with-mati-s/)).

### Hallucinations as a technical problem

- Zhou says that for many enterprises a hallucination just means certain tokens must be deterministic in a given context ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/)).
- Lamini builds factual recall into the weights by turning the LoRA adapter layer into a mixture of experts, which Zhou describes as a learned index rather than an external retriever as in RAG ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/)).
- She argues gains are largest when outputs are objective, which is why text-to-SQL suits the approach: everyone can agree when a query has failed ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/)).

### What enterprises want from models

- Zhou says customers ignore public benchmarks such as MMLU, Spider and Bird, so Lamini helps them build internal evals. She cites Colgate moving from 30% to about 90% accuracy ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/)).
- Staniszewski recommends the three-step pipeline over end-to-end multimodal models for enterprises because it is easier to control ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-speak-without-language-barrier-a-conversation-with-mati-s/)).
- The two differ on openness. Zhou builds on Llama, DeepSeek and Mistral and wants customers to own their models; ElevenLabs keeps its models closed to protect its research lead ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/), [ElevenLabs](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-speak-without-language-barrier-a-conversation-with-mati-s/)).
