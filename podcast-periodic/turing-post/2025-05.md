## Overview

In May, Turing Post asked three infrastructure founders when AI will stop needing code, gain real memory and keep learning after deployment. Amjad Masad of Replit, Edo Liberty of Pinecone and Dev Rishi of Predibase each argued that the next step depends less on bigger models than on the platforms around them. All three were cautious about agent hype and focused on production scale.

## Main themes

### Agents above code

- Masad sees agents as the next abstraction after assembly, C, Python and JavaScript. He now expects most app builders to stop needing to code in about two years ([Replit](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-stop-coding-a-conversation-with-amjad-masad-ceo-and-co-fo/)).
- Replit's agents ran unsupervised for two to three minutes in V1 and 15 in V2; the goal for V3 is an hour. Masad says this needs platform support for long-running agents, testing and parallel forking ([Replit](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-stop-coding-a-conversation-with-amjad-masad-ceo-and-co-fo/)).
- Rishi is more sceptical: errors compound across chained calls, so agent demos break once users leave the expected path ([Predibase](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-train-once-and-learn-forever-insights-from-dev-rishi-ceo/)).

### Memory and knowledge as infrastructure

- Liberty separates a model's cognitive skills from knowledge, and argues RAG-based memory is roughly where language models were before ChatGPT ([Pinecone](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-give-ai-true-memory-a-conversation-with-edo-liberty-ceo-a/)).
- He compares vector databases to GPUs for training: the unlock is infrastructure again, but for storage rather than compute ([Pinecone](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-give-ai-true-memory-a-conversation-with-edo-liberty-ceo-a/)).
- Workloads have shifted to millions of small siloed indexes, which forced a rewrite of Pinecone in the last six months ([Pinecone](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-give-ai-true-memory-a-conversation-with-edo-liberty-ceo-a/)).

### Models that keep learning

- Rishi says train once, learn forever means pipelines that keep improving a model in production, with reinforcement fine-tuning as the enabling technique ([Predibase](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-train-once-and-learn-forever-insights-from-dev-rishi-ceo/)).
- RFT swaps large labelled datasets for about a dozen examples plus reward functions. Healthcare customers already combine LLM-as-judge scores with clinician labels ([Predibase](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-train-once-and-learn-forever-insights-from-dev-rishi-ceo/)).
- Masad's view of AGI fits here: an agent that learns new skills on the fly in any environment, not a remote worker trained on domain data ([Replit](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-stop-coding-a-conversation-with-amjad-masad-ceo-and-co-fo/)).

### Small teams, narrow models and scale

- Masad points to Cursor reaching a $10 billion valuation with about 50 people and expects every startup to become an AI startup ([Replit](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-stop-coding-a-conversation-with-amjad-masad-ceo-and-co-fo/)).
- Rishi expects most enterprise value from specialised models and says base-model inference will become a commodity, while inference tied to post-training will not ([Predibase](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-train-once-and-learn-forever-insights-from-dev-rishi-ceo/)).
- Liberty grants that Postgres and small open-source tools work for around 10,000 documents; teams move to Pinecone when production scale hurts performance and cost ([Pinecone](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-give-ai-true-memory-a-conversation-with-edo-liberty-ceo-a/)).
