## Overview

April 2023 was built largely from Peter Diamandis's Abundance360 stage sessions. Emad Mostaque appeared twice to argue for open models and a short window for AI governance, and Palmer Luckey spent two episodes on Anduril, defense procurement and competition with China. The month closed with two health episodes on scaling up bioengineered organs and on food policy.

## Main themes

### The pause letter and AI governance

- Emad Mostaque explains why he signed the open letter calling for a six-month pause on training large models, saying he was “the only private Company CEO in AI apart from Elon” to do so. He does not expect a real pause ([EP #36](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-36-stability-ai-founder-halting-ai-training-is-hyper-critical-for-o/)).
- His argument rests on compute: chips like Nvidia's H100 scale almost linearly, so 30,000 to 100,000-chip supercomputers will soon spread GPT-4-class models widely. The next six months are the time for transparent governance and industry norms ([EP #36](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-36-stability-ai-founder-halting-ai-training-is-hyper-critical-for-o/)).
- Mostaque doubts a sufficiently intelligent AI can be perfectly aligned and lists job displacement, training-data rights and existential risk among the concerns. He calls himself part of the augmentation camp and wants people to have their own protective AIs ([EP #36](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-36-stability-ai-founder-halting-ai-training-is-hyper-critical-for-o/)).
- On the panel he asks OpenAI for more transparency about its governance, while Alexandr Wang praises its deployment as responsible ([EP #39](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-39-should-we-be-fearful-of-artificial-intelligence-the-ai-panel-w-e/)).

### Open models, data and AI in business

- Mostaque says value lies in private data, and that “every Nation wants to have their own models because this is a question of sovereignty”. Stability AI is setting up national subsidiaries to serve that demand ([EP #36](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-36-stability-ai-founder-halting-ai-training-is-hyper-critical-for-o/)).
- Wang tells two physicians that data is the moat: be first, collect the most data, label it and train a custom model ([EP #39](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-39-should-we-be-fearful-of-artificial-intelligence-the-ai-panel-w-e/)).
- Andrew Ng describes the chief AI officer role he says he coined, and admits “even as someone in AI I feel like man it's tiring there's so much going on” ([EP #39](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-39-should-we-be-fearful-of-artificial-intelligence-the-ai-panel-w-e/)).
- Both episodes touch on education: Mostaque predicts a personal AI tutor for every child within five years, and Ng says openly disclosed ChatGPT use is not cheating ([EP #36](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-36-stability-ai-founder-halting-ai-training-is-hyper-critical-for-o/), [EP #39](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-39-should-we-be-fearful-of-artificial-intelligence-the-ai-panel-w-e/)).

### Palmer Luckey, Anduril and defense

- Luckey says he founded Anduril because big tech refused Pentagon work, incumbent primes were weak in AI and startups could not raise money to sell to government. Anduril builds products with its own money and sells them once they work ([EP #37](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-37-how-the-founder-of-oculus-plans-to-save-america-w-palmer-luckey/)).
- He criticises cost-plus contracting at firms such as Lockheed and argues that precise weapons make the use of force more accountable ([EP #38](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-38-palmer-luckeys-ama-the-politics-of-winning-the-a-i-race/)).
- On China, he thinks the US is militarily strong for now but says rebuilding advanced manufacturing will take decades ([EP #38](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-38-palmer-luckeys-ama-the-politics-of-winning-the-a-i-race/)).
- Looking back on Oculus, he recounts the $2.3 billion sale plus $700 million in bonuses, calls the success luck and timing, and tells VCs to back mission-driven founders instead ([EP #37](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-37-how-the-founder-of-oculus-plans-to-save-america-w-palmer-luckey/), [EP #38](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-38-palmer-luckeys-ama-the-politics-of-winning-the-a-i-race/)).

### Health: manufacturing, funding and prevention

- Doris Taylor argues the bottleneck for personalised replacement hearts is now manufacturing rather than science, and that automation at Dean Kamen's Department of Defense-funded ARMI BioFab makes a five-year timeline possible ([EP #40](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-40-no-more-waiting-on-heart-transplants-w-dr-doris-taylor/)).
- She says her company has run mostly on non-dilutive grants and family money and needs about $300 million to reach approval, pitching lower lifetime transplant costs as the case for investors ([EP #40](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-40-no-more-waiting-on-heart-transplants-w-dr-doris-taylor/), [EP #41](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-41-why-youre-not-leading-a-healthy-life-w-dr-doris-taylor-dr-mark-h/)).
- Mark Hyman argues the food system drives most US healthcare costs and calls for policy changes to labelling, marketing to children and school lunches ([EP #41](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/04/ep-41-why-youre-not-leading-a-healthy-life-w-dr-doris-taylor-dr-mark-h/)).
