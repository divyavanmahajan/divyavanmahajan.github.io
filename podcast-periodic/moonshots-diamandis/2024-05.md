## Overview

May moved from April's big AI debates to more varied ground. Eric Schmidt and Guillaume Verdon took opposite angles on how AI should be governed, a news roundup covered robots and Bitcoin, and Dean Kamen, Marc Lore and Austin Russell talked about building hard ventures. Most episodes were again recorded at Abundance360 2024.

## Main themes

### Regulating frontier AI and protecting elections

- Eric Schmidt expects a small number of very powerful AGI systems that “will be heavily regulated because they're so powerful”, alongside many open-source mid-sized models ([Schmidt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/how-governments-should-handle-ai-policy-deepfakes-w-eric-schmidt-ep-99/)).
- Schmidt calls the US executive order's 10^26 flops notification threshold arbitrary, and wants scientists and industry to set safety lines first, such as unplugging systems that self-improve recursively ([Schmidt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/how-governments-should-handle-ai-policy-deepfakes-w-eric-schmidt-ep-99/)).
- On elections, Schmidt says platforms' content choices will decide how hard they are regulated. Citing the Taylor Swift deepfakes, he says “the lock makers need to win” ([Schmidt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/how-governments-should-handle-ai-policy-deepfakes-w-eric-schmidt-ep-99/)).
- The news roundup plays a Sam Altman clip saying personalised one-on-one persuasion worries him more than mass misinformation ([AI & Bitcoin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/ai-bitcoin-how-our-world-will-change-this-decade-w-salim-ismail-ep-101/)).

### Accelerationism and a new kind of chip

- Guillaume Verdon presents e/acc as a counter to AI doom and overregulation, arguing variance and decentralisation keep civilisation adaptive ([Verdon](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/forget-everything-you-believed-about-computing-w-gill-verdon-ep-102/)).
- Verdon says market competition among model providers aligns systems more gently than central compute caps, and he is more wary of centralisation than of broad access to AI ([Verdon](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/forget-everything-you-believed-about-computing-w-gill-verdon-ep-102/)).
- His company Extropic is building thermodynamic, probabilistic hardware for more intelligence per watt. After a superconducting prototype, the next chips will be room-temperature silicon for edge devices ([Verdon](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/forget-everything-you-believed-about-computing-w-gill-verdon-ep-102/)).

### Simulation, robots and Bitcoin

- Diamandis and Salim Ismail see AI-driven simulation as the main engine of progress, from DeepMind's AlphaFold 3 to Nvidia's DrEureka robot dog trained in simulation ([AI & Bitcoin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/ai-bitcoin-how-our-world-will-change-this-decade-w-salim-ismail-ep-101/)).
- Ismail questions the humanoid form and battery life of about an hour. Diamandis, a Figure investor, counters with leases of roughly $400-1,000 a month ([AI & Bitcoin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/ai-bitcoin-how-our-world-will-change-this-decade-w-salim-ismail-ep-101/)).
- Ismail predicts Bitcoin reaches $1 million within three to four years and says his holdings are now 85-90% Bitcoin ([AI & Bitcoin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/ai-bitcoin-how-our-world-will-change-this-decade-w-salim-ismail-ep-101/)).

### Building moonshots: manufacturing, regulators and risk

- Dean Kamen argues the bottleneck in regenerative medicine is manufacturing, not science. ARMI, funded by the Department of Defense, has a new $100 million grant and 200 member organisations, and its first hire was a former FDA policy director ([Kamen](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/the-world-changing-science-of-organ-manufacturing-w-dean-kamen-ep-100/)).
- Kamen calls the US shortage of scientists “not an education crisis, it's a culture crisis”, and says FIRST now runs events in 182 cities ([Kamen](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/the-world-changing-science-of-organ-manufacturing-w-dean-kamen-ep-100/)).
- Marc Lore tests skeptics by asking why his thesis fails, and pushed Wonder to abandon profitable trucks for better store economics. Austin Russell says Luminar bet on safety systems for production cars from Volvo and Mercedes rather than robotaxis ([Lore & Russell](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/05/managing-risk-to-build-a-moonshot-venture-w-marc-lore-austin-russell-e/)).
