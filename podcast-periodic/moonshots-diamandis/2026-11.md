## Overview

November on Moonshots was dominated by two stories: Anthropic's IPO and the Nvidia crisis around Jensen Huang, who resigned and was reinstated 11 days later. Around them the Mates argued that recursive self-improvement is already under way, followed chip export politics, and spent one episode with Figure AI's Brett Adcock on humanoid robots. The month closed with a Thanksgiving episode of personal setbacks.

## Main themes

### Anthropic goes public

- Peter Diamandis read from Anthropic's IPO filing: internal Claude usage grew more than 250-fold from January to September and its value now exceeds cash payroll. Salim Ismail cited the claim that Claude wrote over 90% of September's code ([EP #308](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/big-techs-3t-ai-bet-claude-writes-90-of-anthropics-code-ais-editing-ai/)).
- Diamandis cited about $64 billion raised at a $1.4 trillion valuation, with 5x demand for the shares. Ismail says “we're seeing intelligence become an asset class now”, and Alexander Wissner-Gross called for IPO reform ([$1.4T IPO](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/anthropics-1-4t-ipo-the-biggest-wealth-event-in-human-history/)).
- The stock popped 17% and fell 12% the next day. Dave Blundin compared it to Meta's IPO, and Ismail saw tension between a 50-year mission and quarterly scrutiny ([#314](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/ai-market-shocks-anthropics-stock-slide-gemini-releases-and-elons-435m/)).

### Nvidia without, then with, Jensen Huang

- Wissner-Gross cited reports that about $8 billion of GPUs may have reached China through a Singapore syndicate. Blundin saw no succession plan, and Emad Mostaque said “this is more an influence operation than an espionage operation” ([#310](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/can-jensen-huang-remain-ceo-inside-nvidias-next-steps-moonshots-310/)).
- After the resignation, shares fell 13% at the open, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. Mostaque expected customers to diversify towards AMD ([#311](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/jensens-departure-nvidias-succession-plan-ai-doomer-response-and-demis/)).
- Huang's return added about $1.09 trillion in market value. Wissner-Gross called the reported presidential intervention a quasi nationalization, and Blundin expects congressional scrutiny ([EP #315](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/jensen-huang-is-back-inside-nvidias-1-trillion-comeback-after-11-days/)).

### Export controls and data-centre politics

- Wissner-Gross argued the scandal shows Pax Silica is more porous than hoped, and tied the leak to the Remote Access Security Act ([#310](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/can-jensen-huang-remain-ceo-inside-nvidias-next-steps-moonshots-310/), [#311](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/jensens-departure-nvidias-succession-plan-ai-doomer-response-and-demis/)).
- On licence rules for chips and disclosure of Chinese model use, Diamandis preferred a label to a ban, and Blundin argued only hardware can be controlled ([$1.4T IPO](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/anthropics-1-4t-ipo-the-biggest-wealth-event-in-human-history/)).
- Data centres became a midterm election issue. Blundin expects building to shift to welcoming states and Canada, and Wissner-Gross sees the backlash pushing compute to orbit ([#309](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/the-great-collision-between-politics-and-ai-309/)).

### Recursive self-improvement

- Wissner-Gross said the filing means RSI has arrived by spending, but holds that “It's only real RSI when we have the robots or the nanobots building better copies of themselves” ([EP #308](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/big-techs-3t-ai-bet-claude-writes-90-of-anthropics-code-ais-editing-ai/)).
- Ismail described a METR study finding Anthropic's AI development at about 1.9 times its historical rate ([#309](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/the-great-collision-between-politics-and-ai-309/)).
- On Demis Hassabis's closed-loop warning, Wissner-Gross said the labs are already deep in the loop. All four rejected Paul Christiano's AI-takeover warning ([#311](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/jensens-departure-nvidias-succession-plan-ai-doomer-response-and-demis/)).

### Robots and SpaceX compute

- Brett Adcock says humanoids are now a data and compute problem. Figure has over a thousand robots on campus and expects home leases of $300 to $500 a month ([Figure AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/how-figure-ai-is-outcompeting-china-and-tesla-in-robotics-robotic-deep/)).
- With Steve Jurvetson, the hosts covered SpaceX adding about 500 MW of AI compute a month, and Blundin warned that training without backpropagation could strand GPU clusters ([#314](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/11/ai-market-shocks-anthropics-stock-slide-gemini-releases-and-elons-435m/)).
