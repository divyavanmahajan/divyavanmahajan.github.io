## Overview

June on Moonshots leaned towards company building. Bill Gross and Francis Pedraza gave two very different playbooks, one from a startup studio and one from a team-owned services firm. Alvin Graylin made the case for an AI-powered metaverse built through global cooperation, and Dan Buettner closed the month on Blue Zones and longevity.

## Main themes

### Why startups win or fail

- Bill Gross says timing is the biggest factor in startup outcomes. Companies fail when “they run out of money because the world isn't quite ready for what you have yet” ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/)).
- He points to Uber, which arrived just as GPS phones and a recession made drivers easy to recruit, and to Idealab's own wearable health-tracking idea in 2001, a decade before Fitbit ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/)).
- Gross describes Idealab as a one-year experiment funded with about $3.5 million, modelled on how Steven Spielberg ran a studio. Companies must be majority owned by the people running them, so Idealab soon becomes a minority shareholder ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/)).
- Diamandis recalls BlastOff, the secret Idealab Moon lander venture of 1999-2001. It was killed by the dot-com crash and later became the Google Lunar XPRIZE ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/)).

### Team-owned services and the sovereignty game

- Francis Pedraza describes Invisible Technologies as operations as a service and names Accenture as the incumbent to displace. His three levers are results-based pricing, a digital assembly line of 300 integrated AI and automation tools, and an ownership culture ([Pedraza](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/how-to-build-implement-systems-to-grow-your-business-w-francis-pedraza/)).
- Invisible is 70% owned by its team. Pedraza argues that wherever the alpha comes from labour rather than capital, the partner model “can and should work” ([Pedraza](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/how-to-build-implement-systems-to-grow-your-business-w-francis-pedraza/)).
- His sovereignty game means raising as little capital as possible to reach profitability. Invisible raised about $6 million over six years, grew from a $1M to a $100M run rate, and returns money through buybacks rather than an IPO ([Pedraza](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/how-to-build-implement-systems-to-grow-your-business-w-francis-pedraza/)).
- Both Gross and Pedraza touched on the one-person unicorn, the idea that very small teams can build very large companies ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/), [Pedraza](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/how-to-build-implement-systems-to-grow-your-business-w-francis-pedraza/)).

### The AI-powered metaverse

- Alvin Graylin, co-author of Our Next Reality, defines the metaverse as the 3D version of the internet. He expects XR devices of 100 grams or less within one to two years ([Graylin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-ai-agi-and-the-metaverse-mean-for-the-future-of-humanity-w-alvin/)).
- He argues a metaverse economy could give workers displaced by AI a parallel place to earn income, where birthplace matters less ([Graylin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-ai-agi-and-the-metaverse-mean-for-the-future-of-humanity-w-alvin/)).
- Graylin opposes sovereign AI. He calls for a CERN-style global programme that pools compute and data instead of 200 rival national efforts ([Graylin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-ai-agi-and-the-metaverse-mean-for-the-future-of-humanity-w-alvin/)).

### Climate bets and longevity by design

- Gross now works almost entirely on climate. He says cheap storage is the missing piece for solar, aiming for about 3 cents per kWh all-in ([Bill Gross](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/what-separates-billion-dollar-companies-from-failed-startups-w-bill-gr/)).
- Dan Buettner argues that Blue Zones residents live long because their environment makes healthy behaviour the default, so redesigning cities beats asking individuals to change ([Buettner](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/why-americans-live-10-15-years-less-than-other-countries-w-dan-buettne/)).
- His Blue Zones Projects are paid by insurers and hospital systems out of the healthcare savings they produce ([Buettner](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/06/why-americans-live-10-15-years-less-than-other-countries-w-dan-buettne/)).
