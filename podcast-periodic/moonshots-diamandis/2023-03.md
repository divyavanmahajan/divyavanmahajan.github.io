## Overview

March on Moonshots ranged widely, with each of the five episodes on its own subject. Guests made the case for crypto after the 2022 collapses, for superabundance against Malthusian fears, and for GPT-4 as an affordable tutor. Dan Sullivan and Steven Kotler covered the mindset behind entrepreneurship and healthy ageing.

## Main themes

### Crypto after the crash

- Abra CEO Bill Barhydt argues “it was centralized actors in This crypto space that failed defi or decentralized Finance kept working”. He compares the moment to the dot-com crash, when the internet kept scaling while companies failed ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-32-why-cryptocurrency-is-still-relevant-in-2023-w-bill-barhydt/)).
- Barhydt says FTX sent client deposits to Alameda and borrowed against its own token. He announces Abra Bank, a state-chartered depository institution, and says the rules FTX broke already existed ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-32-why-cryptocurrency-is-still-relevant-in-2023-w-bill-barhydt/)).
- He separates Bitcoin as decentralised money from Ethereum as a platform for DeFi, stablecoins and NFTs. He predicts the largest banks in 20 years will run on DeFi rails, though DeFi needs 5 to 10 more years of hardening ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-32-why-cryptocurrency-is-still-relevant-in-2023-w-bill-barhydt/)).
- Barhydt predicts Bitcoin reaches $1 million within a couple of years of a billion funded wallets. Diamandis, an Abra adviser and customer, says he targets 20% of his portfolio in crypto; both stress this is not financial advice ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-32-why-cryptocurrency-is-still-relevant-in-2023-w-bill-barhydt/)).

### Superabundance and population

- Marian Tupy and Gale Pooley measure prices in hours of work. Tupy says abundance of resources grew about 250% between 1980 and 2018, and that the gap over population growth is “the new knowledge created in the human mind” ([Overpopulation](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-31-the-truth-about-overpopulation-w-dr-marian-l-tupy-dr-gale-l-pool/)).
- Tupy blames rising costs in health care, education and child care on subsidies and over-regulation. He cites projections of a population peak around 10 billion and warns about degrowth thinking ([Overpopulation](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-31-the-truth-about-overpopulation-w-dr-marian-l-tupy-dr-gale-l-pool/)).

### The entrepreneur mindset

- Dan Sullivan cites Jean-Baptiste Say's 1804 definition of an entrepreneur as someone who moves resources to higher productivity. He calls entrepreneurship a lifetime choice, not a career choice ([Dan Sullivan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-33-the-entrepreneur-mindset-w-dan-sullivan/)).
- Sullivan says “if there's a God in the entrepreneurial Heaven the name of that God is cash flow”. After two bankruptcies he insists on payment up front, and says only someone who would write a check can tell you an idea is good ([Dan Sullivan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-33-the-entrepreneur-mindset-w-dan-sullivan/)).
- Diamandis contrasts bootstrapped companies built on early sales with moonshots that need outside capital, and prefers the founder or executive chairman role with a hired CEO ([Dan Sullivan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-33-the-entrepreneur-mindset-w-dan-sullivan/)).

### Mindset and healthspan

- Steven Kotler, who taught himself park skiing at 53 for his book Gnar Country, argues that skills once thought to decline with age are use it or lose it. He says fear and risk aversion block learning in midlife ([Steven Kotler](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-34-the-mindset-that-extends-your-life-w-steven-kotler/)).
- Both recommend a massively transformative purpose. Diamandis is optimistic about regenerative medicine within about five years, while Kotler is more cautious; both put exercise, sleep and social connection first ([Steven Kotler](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-34-the-mindset-that-extends-your-life-w-steven-kotler/)).

### GPT-4 and AI tutoring

- Sal Khan says mass education borrowed the industrial batch model. He argues GPT-4 makes affordable one-to-one tutoring possible through Khanmigo, a tutor for students and an assistant for teachers ([Sal Khan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-35-ai-gpt-4-revolutionize-education-w-sal-khan/)).
- Khan says letting the model reason privately before judging student work raised math accuracy three- to four-fold. He calls voice and persistent memory engineering work, expected within months ([Sal Khan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/03/ep-35-ai-gpt-4-revolutionize-education-w-sal-khan/)).
