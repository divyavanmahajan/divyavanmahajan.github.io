## Overview

January on No Priors was split between AI infrastructure and the Los Angeles fires. Two episodes, with Bill Clerico of Convective Capital and developer Rick Caruso, dealt with the Palisades fire, what made it so destructive and how to rebuild. The other two covered Decagon's customer support agents and Modal's on-demand GPU cloud.

## Main themes

### What made the Palisades fire so destructive

- Bill Clerico argues climate change worsens fire seasons, but most of the recent damage comes from human causes: forests left about three times denser by a century of suppression, an ageing grid, housing codes and regulated insurance pricing ([Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/)).
- Clerico contrasts Florida's 2 to 3 million acres of prescribed burning a year with California's 100,000-acre goal. He says “the forests are going to burn” on our terms or theirs; Elad Gil cites 4.7 to 7.5 years of environmental review for burn projects ([Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/)).
- On water, Clerico blames pressure lost through burning houses' plumbing and abandoned cars blocking engines. Rick Caruso instead points to a drained reservoir and empty hydrants, calling it bad planning ([Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/), [Rick Caruso](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/rick-caruso-on-la-s-wildfires-policy-failures-and-the-path-forward/)).
- Caruso's Palisades Village survived thanks to non-combustible materials, fire retardant and a private crew, and saved eight adjacent homes ([Rick Caruso](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/rick-caruso-on-la-s-wildfires-policy-failures-and-the-path-forward/), [Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/)).

### Rebuilding and resilience

- Caruso wants the burned area split into quadrants with several incentivised contractors working in parallel, power lines put underground and water systems upgraded during cleanup, with rebuilding starting within a year ([Rick Caruso](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/rick-caruso-on-la-s-wildfires-policy-failures-and-the-path-forward/)).
- Clerico says utilities cause 11% of ignitions but half the damage, that rate regulators resist the spending, and that insurers left the market when they could not price reinsurance ([Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/)).
- He expects pre-positioned drone swarms to handle initial attack within 5-10 years, and points to Convective's investment in Overstory, which helps utilities decide where to trim trees ([Wildfires](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/what-can-we-do-about-wildfires-with-convective-capital-s-bill-clerico/)).

### Customer support as the first agent market

- Jesse Zhang calls customer service “the golden use case” for AI agents, because rollout can be gradual and ROI is easy to measure against support teams ([Decagon](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/how-ai-agents-are-transforming-customer-support-with-decagon-s-jesse-z/)).
- Zhang says Decagon's edge is orchestration and transparency built on commodity models, and that support agents need instruction following more than reasoning ([Decagon](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/how-ai-agents-are-transforming-customer-support-with-decagon-s-jesse-z/)).
- He is bearish on many other agent uses in the near term: security needs near-perfection from day one, and text-to-SQL agents became copilots with hard-to-price value ([Decagon](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/how-ai-agents-are-transforming-customer-support-with-decagon-s-jesse-z/)).

### GPUs on demand

- Erik Bernhardsson argues long-term GPU contracts are the wrong model for startups with volatile inference. Modal bills only while containers run and can usually provide 100 GPUs within seconds ([Modal](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/erik-bernhardsson-on-creating-tools-that-make-ai-feel-effortless/)).
- He says Modal rebuilt its file system, scheduler and container runtime, and that “a big part of the future of AI is like running a big pool of compute and slicing it very dynamically” ([Modal](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/01/erik-bernhardsson-on-creating-tools-that-make-ai-feel-effortless/)).
