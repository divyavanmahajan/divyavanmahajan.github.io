## Overview

May on No Priors leaned heavily on compute: two episodes covered inference, one from the cloud side (Baseten) and one from the chip side (Cerebras). The other three looked at what surrounds the models: buying services companies to run them on AI, securing the physical supply chain, and overseeing agents inside the enterprise.

## Main themes

### The inference crunch

- Tuhin Srivastava says Baseten grew 30x in a year, with about 95% of tokens on dedicated, mostly custom-model inference. He argues GPUs-as-a-service is a commodity while inference with a software layer is sticky ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/baseten-ceo-tuhin-srivastava-on-the-ai-inference-crunch-custom-models/)).
- On supply, he says “I don't think people realize how bad it really is.” Renting 1,024 B200s now means a 3-5 year contract with 20-30% of contract value prepaid ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/baseten-ceo-tuhin-srivastava-on-the-ai-inference-crunch-custom-models/)).
- Baseten runs 90 clusters across 18 clouds at mid-90s utilisation, and Srivastava rates only three or four of about a dozen good clouds as top-tier ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/baseten-ceo-tuhin-srivastava-on-the-ai-inference-crunch-custom-models/)).
- Both inference guests discussed open-weight models: Srivastava's customers start from capability, using models from GPT-OSS to DeepSeek, and he says the US needs its own ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/baseten-ceo-tuhin-srivastava-on-the-ai-inference-crunch-custom-models/), [Cerebras](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/the-story-behind-cerebras-63-billion-ipo-with-founder-and-ceo-andrew-f/)).

### Fast inference and the Cerebras IPO

- Andrew Feldman claims Cerebras's wafer-scale chip is 15-20x faster than GPUs at inference. He argues demand took off in 2025 once models became useful enough for daily work ([Cerebras](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/the-story-behind-cerebras-63-billion-ipo-with-founder-and-ceo-andrew-f/)).
- He compares slow inference to dial-up: “How big is the market for dial-up internet? It's zero.” ([Cerebras](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/the-story-behind-cerebras-63-billion-ipo-with-founder-and-ceo-andrew-f/)).
- Feldman recounts two years when the wafer could not be built while the company spent about $8 million a month, then an OpenAI deal he puts at over $20 billion and an IPO valuing Cerebras at about $63 billion ([Cerebras](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/the-story-behind-cerebras-63-billion-ipo-with-founder-and-ceo-andrew-f/)).

### AI roll-ups of services businesses

- Alexander Taubman explains why Long Lake buys services companies instead of selling them software: ownership aligns incentives and makes change management possible. Its latest deal is a $6.3 billion agreement to acquire Amex GBT ([Amex GBT](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/amex-global-business-travel-the-world-s-first-ai-take-private-with-lon/)).
- Taubman says labour-intensive firms keep about 20 cents of each incremental dollar. With AI, acquired HOA businesses went from 0-5% to 20%-plus growth, and he says the companies now look like software businesses ([Amex GBT](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/amex-global-business-travel-the-world-s-first-ai-take-private-with-lon/)).
- Elad Gil argues roll-ups need three rare skills together: PE-style acquisition, AI engineering and change management ([Amex GBT](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/amex-global-business-travel-the-world-s-first-ai-take-private-with-lon/)).

### Securing the AI supply chain

- Jacob Helberg says the AI supply chain covers thousands of inputs beyond chips, such as precision reducers, rare earth magnets and actuators, with high US concentration risk ([Pax Silica](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/pax-silica-inside-the-trump-administration-s-tech-strategy-with-us-und/)).
- Pax Silica, a 14-country coalition, puts private companies in the lead, unlike Belt and Road. An early target is robotics, a supply chain he says China completely dominates ([Pax Silica](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/pax-silica-inside-the-trump-administration-s-tech-strategy-with-us-und/)).

### Oversight for enterprise agents

- Maxim Bar Kogan argues human-in-the-loop review will not scale as agent actions multiply, and that identity tools break because users want agents to act with their own permissions ([Onyx Security](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/building-an-ai-guardian-for-enterprise-with-onyx-security-ceo-maxim-ba/)).
- Onyx trains small models that only decide when a smarter agent should review an action, which keeps cost and latency down. He says oversight should come from a party independent of the model vendors ([Onyx Security](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/building-an-ai-guardian-for-enterprise-with-onyx-security-ceo-maxim-ba/)).
- Both Taubman and Bar Kogan touched on enterprise adoption; Taubman estimates enterprise AI is around 1% penetrated ([Amex GBT](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/amex-global-business-travel-the-world-s-first-ai-take-private-with-lon/), [Onyx Security](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/05/building-an-ai-guardian-for-enterprise-with-onyx-security-ceo-maxim-ba/)).
