## Overview

April on No Priors moved from coding agents to what agents mean for money, enterprises and science. Circle's Jeremy Allaire argued that agents need new financial rails, and ServiceNow's Bill McDermott defended workflow platforms against the SaaS apocalypse story. Liam Fedus of Periodic Labs explained why AI for science needs closed-loop experiments.

## Main themes

### Money for an agentic economy

- Allaire says “the kind of agentic economy is being born as we speak”, and that banking cannot handle agents that need global, instant, programmable payments in the billions ([Circle](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/the-agentic-economy-how-ai-agents-will-transform-the-financial-system/)).
- He presents USDC as full-reserve money backed by short-duration Treasuries, repo and cash, with an average duration of about 13 days. Uses run from 25-cent game payments and agents paying agents to large trading settlements, with Visa and Ramp among users ([Circle](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/the-agentic-economy-how-ai-agents-will-transform-the-financial-system/)).
- Circle's Arc blockchain uses a known set of regulated validators, settles in hundreds of milliseconds and uses USDC as its native token. Allaire says it can process transactions for a millionth of a penny ([Circle](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/the-agentic-economy-how-ai-agents-will-transform-the-financial-system/)).
- Allaire expects contracts and corporations to become software machines, and a global renegotiation of the social contract. He warns that AI-driven growth could mean capital capturing more capital at the expense of humans ([Circle](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/the-agentic-economy-how-ai-agents-will-transform-the-financial-system/)).

### The SaaS apocalypse, from the incumbent's side

- McDermott argues that models think but workflow platforms act: closing a case means routing data through HR, finance, legal and compliance ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).
- He claims a simple ServiceNow app would cost ten times more to replicate with a language model, and says businesses “never will forgive software for making a mistake” ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).
- Sarah Guo adds that most businesses do not want to build their own workflow software. McDermott sees single-department vendors as most at risk, and pitches ServiceNow as a control tower across hyperscalers, models and about 800 systems of record ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).

### Agents and the workforce

- McDermott says 90% of ServiceNow's customer service cases are now handled by agents, and predicts net new headcount will drop sharply ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).
- He expects 2.2 billion agents to enter the workforce in the next couple of years, and links some of today's cuts to over-hiring in 2020-2024 ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).
- He says only 11% of the Brazilian companies he met had moved past experiments; the US leads and financial services moves fastest ([ServiceNow](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/scaling-global-organizations-in-the-age-of-ai-with-servicenow-chairman/)).

### Closed-loop AI for materials

- Fedus argues that science needs experiments, not just hard thinking, and that the power lies in a closed loop where each result drives the next experiment ([Periodic Labs](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/ai-for-atoms-how-periodic-labs-is-revolutionizing-materials-engineerin/)).
- Literature alone is noisy: reported values for one material property spanned many orders of magnitude, so Periodic grounds its models in lab data ([Periodic Labs](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/ai-for-atoms-how-periodic-labs-is-revolutionizing-materials-engineerin/)).
- Language models act as an orchestration layer that calls specialised atomic neural nets as tools. Periodic relies on Codex and Claude Code and sees itself as an intelligence layer for advanced manufacturing ([Periodic Labs](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/04/ai-for-atoms-how-periodic-labs-is-revolutionizing-materials-engineerin/)).
