## Overview

March on No Priors asked who is capturing value from AI so far. The hosts argued that incumbents such as Meta, the hyperscalers and Nvidia are, while enterprise adoption is only starting. Guests from Baseten, LangChain and Figma covered inference speed, how agents actually reach production, and how AI changes design work.

## Main themes

### Incumbents, capex and Nvidia's moat

- Sarah Guo calls Meta's earnings beat a one-day answer to the $200 billion AI ROI question, with $197 billion of market cap added in one session ([Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/)).
- Elad Gil says Gemini 1.5 made him more bullish on Google, which had the compute, data and talent and lacked only the will ([Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/)).
- Gil lists Nvidia's moat as chip performance, CUDA and Mellanox interconnect, with AMD, Groq, Cerebras and Etched as challengers. The hosts conclude “the moat is actually really really deep” ([Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/)).
- Tuhin Srivastava says premium chips still take three to six weeks of negotiation and doubts that running CUDA-style workloads on AMD is as easy as claimed ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/speed-will-win-the-ai-computing-battle-with-tuhin-srivastava-from-base/)).

### Agents that actually work

- The hosts see agent teams succeeding by targeting narrow domains such as code, RPA and customer support, rather than general agents ([Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/)).
- Harrison Chase describes production agents as controlled state machines that encode domain knowledge. He names weak LLM planning as the biggest gap, and customer support and coding as the leading use cases ([LangChain](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/open-sourcing-ai-app-development-with-harrison-chase-from-langchain/)).
- Chase separates procedural memory from personalisation memory and says he is “underwhelmed at the amount of like really interesting stuff that's going on there” ([LangChain](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/open-sourcing-ai-app-development-with-harrison-chase-from-langchain/)).
- Both episodes discussed long context. Guo and Chase argue it opens trade-offs with retrieval rather than killing RAG ([Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/), [LangChain](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/open-sourcing-ai-app-development-with-harrison-chase-from-langchain/)).

### Inference speed and enterprise spend

- Srivastava argues speed, of the product and of the team, decides who wins in AI. Inference is a repeatable workflow problem, so he says companies should buy it ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/speed-will-win-the-ai-computing-battle-with-tuhin-srivastava-from-base/)).
- He says state-of-the-art serving went from about 90 to over 200 tokens a second, and that Baseten led Artificial Analysis throughput and latency benchmarks ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/speed-will-win-the-ai-computing-battle-with-tuhin-srivastava-from-base/)).
- One customer's CTO called inference the second-biggest expense after payroll. Gil puts US software spend at about half a trillion dollars against $3.5-5 trillion of payroll AI could affect, citing Klarna's support assistant ([Baseten](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/speed-will-win-the-ai-computing-battle-with-tuhin-srivastava-from-base/), [Big tech earnings](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/big-tech-earnings-and-the-current-ai-debates-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-g/)).

### Figma, independence and AI in design

- Dylan Field says Figma called off the Adobe deal after 16 months of regulatory scrutiny and came out independent with a billion dollars ([Figma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/designing-the-future-dylan-field-on-ai-collaboration-and-independence/)).
- He expects AI to augment designers and lower the floor so more people can design, leading to more UI rather than less ([Figma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/designing-the-future-dylan-field-on-ai-collaboration-and-independence/)).
- Field says Figma was never meant to be just a design tool and wants to cover the path from idea to shipped code, with Dev Mode for developers ([Figma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/03/designing-the-future-dylan-field-on-ai-collaboration-and-independence/)).
