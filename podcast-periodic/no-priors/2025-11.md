## Overview

November on No Priors had three founder and CEO interviews, each about putting AI to work in a specific domain. Snowflake and Benchling described agents built on top of the data they already hold, while Sunday Robotics argued that home robots are close to a scalable recipe.

## Main themes

### Agents on top of enterprise data

- Sridhar Ramaswamy calls Snowflake Intelligence an opinionated agentic platform for every employee, grown out of an internal sales data assistant. He expects the line between agents and traditional software to blur ([Snowflake](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/meet-snowflake-intelligence-a-personalized-enterprise-intelligence-age/)).
- Snowflake built a credible foundation model but stopped. Ramaswamy says “We simply did not have the capital to be able to invest meaningfully” in competing with OpenAI or Anthropic ([Snowflake](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/meet-snowflake-intelligence-a-personalized-enterprise-intelligence-age/)).
- He says about half of qualifying Fortune 2000 companies are customers, and that the company had been slow to react to machine learning and AI before he took over ([Snowflake](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/meet-snowflake-intelligence-a-personalized-enterprise-intelligence-age/)).
- Sajith Wickramasekara describes Benchling's aim of agents that handle tasks across the scientific process, and argues that in a vertical most of the work is making AI trusted and placed in the right workflow ([Benchling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/how-ai-will-accelerate-breakthroughs-in-biotechnology-with-benchling-c/)).

### Where AI pays back

- Ramaswamy ranks coding agents as the easiest ROI, followed by customer support and wider data access. He advises spending on AI in small amounts rather than demanding ROI too early ([Snowflake](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/meet-snowflake-intelligence-a-personalized-enterprise-intelligence-age/)).
- He reorganised Snowflake to cut layers between engineers and customers, with the rule that speed wins, and requires an eval for every launch or model change ([Snowflake](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/meet-snowflake-intelligence-a-personalized-enterprise-intelligence-age/)).
- Wickramasekara says “most people aren't really using that much AI and R&D yet”, held back by accuracy, IP and security concerns. One customer cut a report for a regulator meeting from a day to about five minutes ([Benchling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/how-ai-will-accelerate-breakthroughs-in-biotechnology-with-benchling-c/)).

### Business pressure in biotech

- Wickramasekara frames drug development as slow and costly, and argues the near-term gain comes from augmenting scientists step by step rather than a fully autonomous AI scientist ([Benchling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/how-ai-will-accelerate-breakthroughs-in-biotechnology-with-benchling-c/)).
- He describes a post-COVID biotech bust and large pharma buying from fast, cheap Chinese biotechs, which adds urgency. He expects pure model companies to drift into therapeutics as model building commoditises ([Benchling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/how-ai-will-accelerate-breakthroughs-in-biotechnology-with-benchling-c/)).

### Home robots and data

- Tony Zhao and Cheng Chi say robotics sits between its GPT and ChatGPT moments: a scalable recipe for manipulation exists but has not yet become a consumer product ([Sunday Robotics](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/sunday-robotics-scaling-the-home-robot-revolution-with-co-founders-ton/)).
- They see high-quality real-world data as the bottleneck. More than 500 people use Sunday's data glove, and they found its data matches teleoperation in quality ([Sunday Robotics](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/sunday-robotics-scaling-the-home-robot-revolution-with-co-founders-ton/)).
- AI perception lets the Memo robot use cheap, compliant, imprecise actuators. Prototypes cost $6,000 to $20,000, with a home beta in 2026 and wider shipping in 2027 or 2028 ([Sunday Robotics](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/11/sunday-robotics-scaling-the-home-robot-revolution-with-co-founders-ton/)).
