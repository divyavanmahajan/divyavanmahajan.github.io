## Overview

December on No Priors paired two application companies with the hosts' year-end forecast. Harvey and ElevenLabs explained how vertical AI firms scale against the big labs, and Sarah Guo and Elad Gil closed the year with predictions on bubble fears, adoption, robotics and IPOs.

## Main themes

### Legal AI goes organisational

- Gabe Pereyra says Harvey is almost at 1,000 customers and 500 employees after about three and a half years. Its first phase was an IDE for lawyers; the next is making teams and whole firms more productive ([Harvey](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/scaling-legal-ai-and-building-next-generation-law-firms-with-harvey-co/)).
- Pereyra argues “you can kind of think of associates as agents” taking tasks from partners, and that the legal equivalent of a coding RL environment is a client matter ([Harvey](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/scaling-legal-ai-and-building-next-generation-law-firms-with-harvey-co/)).
- Building a reward function for long legal drafting remains open, he says; at firms the real signal is partner judgement, which public data such as SEC filings does not capture ([Harvey](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/scaling-legal-ai-and-building-next-generation-law-firms-with-harvey-co/)).
- Harvey will not run its own law firm. Pereyra sizes legal at about a trillion dollars and professional services at 3 to 5 trillion, and expects partners' roles to change little while junior work changes more ([Harvey](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/scaling-legal-ai-and-building-next-generation-law-firms-with-harvey-co/)).

### Voice as an interface

- Mati Staniszewski says ElevenLabs has about 350 people and $300 million in ARR, split roughly evenly between self-serve creators and enterprise ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-future-of-voice-ai-agents-dubbing-and-real-time-translation-with-e/)).
- He argues audio needs architectural breakthroughs more than scale, with perhaps 50 to 100 capable audio researchers worldwide and about 10 at ElevenLabs. The company organises small labs around one problem at a time ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-future-of-voice-ai-agents-dubbing-and-real-time-translation-with-e/)).
- Customer support is the fastest-growing voice agent use and is shifting from reactive to proactive. For reliable enterprise use he says “the cascaded approach is the approach for the next year too” ([ElevenLabs](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-future-of-voice-ai-agents-dubbing-and-real-time-translation-with-e/)).

### Bubble talk and real adoption

- Guo lists investor fears: demand not supporting the capex cycle, risk in data center credit agreements and concentration in Nvidia. She describes the market as running a little hot and volatile ([2026 Forecast](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-2026-ai-forecast-foundation-models-ipos-and-robotics-with-sarah-gu/)).
- Gil argues physicians, lawyers and accountants, usually slow adopters, are taking up AI fast. He expects new verticals to consolidate as coding, medical scribing and legal did ([2026 Forecast](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-2026-ai-forecast-foundation-models-ipos-and-robotics-with-sarah-gu/)).
- Aaron Levie predicts 2026 will be the year of enterprise agents in vertical domains, agent harnesses and useful evals ([2026 Forecast](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-2026-ai-forecast-foundation-models-ipos-and-robotics-with-sarah-gu/)).

### Robotics, IPOs and research labs

- Guo predicts small-scale humanoid deployments in 2026, with sentiment collapsing around some robotics companies when timelines slip ([2026 Forecast](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-2026-ai-forecast-foundation-models-ipos-and-robotics-with-sarah-gu/)).
- Gil expects the robotics winners to be some subset of Chinese firms, Tesla and perhaps a startup, as Waymo and Tesla won self-driving. He also expects many more AI IPOs, driven by retail appetite beyond Nvidia ([2026 Forecast](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/12/the-2026-ai-forecast-foundation-models-ipos-and-robotics-with-sarah-gu/)).
