## Overview

May on No Priors covered the inputs and the outputs of AI. Scale's Alexandr Wang and the hosts looked at data, capital and energy as the constraints on progress, while Cognition, Suno and Y Combinator showed what is being built on top. Evaluation came up twice, from very different angles.

## Main themes

### AI software engineers and the jobs question

- Scott Wu presents Devin as an AI software engineer that writes code, uses the shell and browser, and deploys, while a human watches and steers ([Cognition](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/cognition-s-scott-wu-on-how-devin-the-ai-software-engineer-will-work-f/)).
- Wu says Devin works well for devops and data analysis but needs a precise specification. He argues “there's going to be more Engineers not less” because demand for software keeps growing ([Cognition](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/cognition-s-scott-wu-on-how-devin-the-ai-software-engineer-will-work-f/)).
- Garry Tan says about 70% of YC companies are AI-related and rejects the wrapper label, comparing it to calling all SaaS a MySQL wrapper ([YC](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/how-yc-fosters-ai-innovation-with-garry-tan/)).
- Tan expects younger founders as students drop out for AI, and warns that raising too much money hurts ([YC](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/how-yc-fosters-ai-innovation-with-garry-tan/)).

### Music as a consumer AI product

- Mikey Shulman says Suno is a consumer product for everyone, not AI added to professional audio tools. Its models are “just transformers”, with most innovation in audio tokenization ([Suno](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/music-consumers-are-becoming-the-creators-with-suno-ceo-mikey-shulman/)).
- Shulman predicts creation and listening will blur and that time and money spent on music will rise ([Suno](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/music-consumers-are-becoming-the-creators-with-suno-ceo-mikey-shulman/)).
- Elad Gil places Suno and Udio in a sequence of content waves after text, images and video. Sarah Guo asks how many people actually want to create music ([Context windows](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/context-windows-computer-constraints-and-energy-consumption-with-sarah/)).

### Data and evaluation

- Wang presents Scale as the data foundry next to compute and algorithms. He frames running out of tokens as a choice between “data abundance or data scarcity” ([Scale](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/the-data-foundry-for-ai-with-alexandr-wang-from-scale/)).
- He says models now need expert reasoning chains, agent workflow and multimodal data, and that humans stay in the loop because models lack long-horizon reasoning ([Scale](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/the-data-foundry-for-ai-with-alexandr-wang-from-scale/)).
- Wang says many models are overfit on public benchmarks and plans private held-out leaderboards. Shulman, lacking audio benchmarks, evaluates by listening and A/B tests ([Scale](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/the-data-foundry-for-ai-with-alexandr-wang-from-scale/), [Suno](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/music-consumers-are-becoming-the-creators-with-suno-ceo-mikey-shulman/)).

### Capital, small models and energy

- Guo estimates hyperscaler AI compute spending at almost $200 billion this year, against $80 billion for oil majors and about $100 billion for broadband ([Context windows](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/context-windows-computer-constraints-and-energy-consumption-with-sarah/)).
- Guo reads Apple's small open models as a sign of local inference, which allows passive and proactive experiences without paying for compute ([Context windows](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/context-windows-computer-constraints-and-energy-consumption-with-sarah/)).
- Gil expects context windows of 10 million tokens plus to become common. Both hosts see energy and permitting as the point where scaling becomes a physical-world problem ([Context windows](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/05/context-windows-computer-constraints-and-energy-consumption-with-sarah/)).
