## Overview

September on No Priors moved between the shape of the model market and specific applications. Andrej Karpathy and the hosts both argued that smaller models have more room to improve, while the hosts debated whether frontier labs are consolidating. Bret Taylor and Rippling's Matt MacInnis showed two applications: branded agents for customer service and AI-assisted performance reviews.

## Main themes

### Company agents and customer service

- Bret Taylor predicts that “in 2025 existing digitally will probably mean having a branded AI agent that your customers can interact with”, much as websites were in 1995 ([Sierra](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/transforming-customer-service-through-company-agents-with-sierra-s-bre/)).
- He splits agents into personal, persona and company agents. Personal agents need more technology than exists and probably belong to Apple, Google and OpenAI, while company agents are ready to build now ([Sierra](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/transforming-customer-service-through-company-agents-with-sierra-s-bre/)).
- Taylor says a call-centre contact costs about $13 and AI brings it well below a dollar. He argues real service is about taking action across systems, not answering questions, so RAG alone falls short ([Sierra](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/transforming-customer-service-through-company-agents-with-sierra-s-bre/)).
- Sierra charges for outcomes, which Taylor compares to the shift from impression ads to cost-per-click ([Sierra](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/transforming-customer-service-through-company-agents-with-sierra-s-bre/)).

### Consolidation in the LLM market

- Elad Gil argues frontier labs now raise billions from hyperscalers or sovereigns because venture capital is too small to fund them. Teams from Inflection, Character and Adept joined larger firms ([LLM markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/future-of-llm-markets-consolidation-and-small-models-with-sarah-and-el/)).
- Sarah Guo counters that competition has grown, with API prices down roughly 200x in 18 to 24 months. Gil recalls that Friendster and MySpace were expected to win before Facebook emerged ([LLM markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/future-of-llm-markets-consolidation-and-small-models-with-sarah-and-el/)).
- The hosts read AMD's Silo deal, about $600 million, and its ZT acquisition as attempts to sell full systems like Nvidia ([LLM markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/future-of-llm-markets-consolidation-and-small-models-with-sarah-and-el/)).
- On risk, Gil contrasts Google's copyright fights over snippets with Napster, which the music industry sued “basically into the grave” ([LLM markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/future-of-llm-markets-consolidation-and-small-models-with-sarah-and-el/)).

### Smaller models

- Gil says performance per model size keeps rising and cites the 20 or 30 watt brain as a hint of what is possible ([LLM markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/future-of-llm-markets-consolidation-and-small-models-with-sarah-and-el/)).
- Karpathy says current models waste capacity memorising. He thinks a cognitive core that thinks and uses tools might need only a billion parameters ([Karpathy](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/the-road-to-autonomous-intelligence-with-andrej-karpathy/)).
- He argues the Transformer is no longer the bottleneck. Data is, and synthetic data must stay diverse to avoid silent model collapse ([Karpathy](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/the-road-to-autonomous-intelligence-with-andrej-karpathy/)).

### Self-driving, robots and education

- Karpathy sees self-driving as a preview of AGI, with a long gap from demo to deployment. He expects humanoids to start in Tesla's own factories, then B2B, with consumers last ([Karpathy](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/the-road-to-autonomous-intelligence-with-andrej-karpathy/)).
- At Eureka Labs the teacher designs the course and AI is the front end. He says the demo is near but the product is far ([Karpathy](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/the-road-to-autonomous-intelligence-with-andrej-karpathy/)).

### AI in performance reviews

- Matt MacInnis says Rippling's Talent Signal reads work product, the strongest predictor of career outcomes in early studies, rather than relying on a manager's vibe ([Rippling](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/using-ai-to-evaluate-employee-performance-with-rippling-s-coo-matt-mac/)).
- It uses no demographic data and gives one signal per employee after 90 days. Rippling's policy forbids significant decisions based on the model alone ([Rippling](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/09/using-ai-to-evaluate-employee-performance-with-rippling-s-coo-matt-mac/)).
