## Overview

In August, No Priors kept returning to the gap between AI excitement and production use. Weights & Biases and the hosts' listener Q&A both said that large enterprises were still planning rather than shipping, while scarce GPUs set the pace for everyone. Sal Khan and Guillermo Rauch showed where AI-native products were already working, in education and frontend development.

## Main themes

### Enterprise adoption lags the tooling

- Lukas Biewald says “there's probably more companies that have raised money as like llm tools than companies that have LMS in production” ([Weights & Biases](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/eradicating-machine-learning-pain-points-with-weights-biases-ceo-lukas/)).
- Biewald calls prompting an LLM for tasks like sentiment analysis his company's first real existential threat. His board saw nothing in the data, but he moved the whole company onto LLM workflow tooling ([Weights & Biases](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/eradicating-machine-learning-pain-points-with-weights-biases-ceo-lukas/)).
- Elad Gil says only AI-native companies and early adopters such as Notion and Zapier have shipped. Large enterprises are still in planning cycles, so big deployments are one to two years away ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/listener-q-a-2024-tech-market-predictions-long-term-implications-of-to/), [Weights & Biases](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/eradicating-machine-learning-pain-points-with-weights-biases-ceo-lukas/)).

### The GPU crunch and agents

- Sarah Guo says chip manufacturing cannot keep up with demand. She points to Nvidia's lead, dependence on TSMC and a specialised bonder tool used in GPU assembly ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/listener-q-a-2024-tech-market-predictions-long-term-implications-of-to/)).
- Gil expects GPU clouds, rented former crypto-mining hardware and alternative chips such as Cerebras to fill gaps. Guo says efficiency research is undervalued ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/listener-q-a-2024-tech-market-predictions-long-term-implications-of-to/)).
- Guo highlights agents that write executable code and so can use many more tools. Gil's advice is to start with something really targeted rather than a general assistant ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/listener-q-a-2024-tech-market-predictions-long-term-implications-of-to/)).
- Gil also predicts a reckoning for 2021-era unicorns: a third fail, a third have peaked and a third grow past their valuations ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/listener-q-a-2024-tech-market-predictions-long-term-implications-of-to/)).

### An AI tutor for every student

- Sal Khan describes Khanmigo as “a tutor for every student it's a teaching assistant for every teacher”. It is built on GPT-4 and its prompts tell it to ask leading questions rather than give answers ([Sal Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/the-ai-tutor-for-every-child-and-the-next-frontier-of-education-from-k/)).
- Khan expects AI tutors to be mainstream in three to five years. He predicts nearly all teachers will use generative AI for lesson planning and grading, which takes about half their time ([Sal Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/the-ai-tutor-for-every-child-and-the-next-frontier-of-education-from-k/)).
- Safeguards include chat logs visible to parents and teachers, a second AI that moderates conversations, and no training on student data ([Sal Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/the-ai-tutor-for-every-child-and-the-next-frontier-of-education-from-k/)).

### Building AI-native products

- Guillermo Rauch calls foundation models a Cloud 2.0 backend. He says founders do not need to train or fine-tune a model to build a useful product, and warns against random acts of AI ([Vercel](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/ai-superpowers-for-frontend-developers-with-vercel-founder-ceo-guiller/)).
- Rauch describes Vercel's AI SDK and streaming functions, and token theft by people proxying free GPT-4 ([Vercel](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/ai-superpowers-for-frontend-developers-with-vercel-founder-ceo-guiller/)).
- Transformer co-author Jakob Uszkoreit argues that architectures succeed when they fit the hardware, and that models still spend compute by length rather than difficulty. He now applies deep learning to designing RNA medicines at Inceptive ([Inceptive](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/08/ai-powered-biological-software-with-jakob-uszkoreit-ceo-of-inceptive/)).
