## Overview

October on No Priors was dominated by security. Three guests from OpenAI, CrowdStrike and Cloudflare described how AI has made finding vulnerabilities cheap and why defenders must move fast. Around that ran a second thread on open-weight models, plus episodes on inference chips and coding agents for enterprise IT.

## Main themes

### Security after cheap vulnerability discovery

- Greg Brockman says the internet was built for resilience, not security, and AI will surface tech debt that now has to be paid down. OpenAI put about 25% of its production engineers on securing its systems with its best model and has a $1 billion commitment for frontline defenders ([Brockman](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-changing-physics-of-cybersecurity-and-drug-development-with-openai/)).
- Brockman expects formal verification and secure-by-default AI code to follow. He has a feeling that “security spend, should actually vastly outstrip software spend”, while admitting he may be wrong ([Brockman](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-changing-physics-of-cybersecurity-and-drug-development-with-openai/)).
- George Kurtz says agents have collapsed the attacker hierarchy. Average breakout time is now 29 minutes, and as fast as 27 seconds, and attacks are prompts rather than malware ([CrowdStrike](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-new-apex-predator-crowdstrike-ceo-george-kurtz-on-adversaries-arme/)).
- Dane Knecht says reported CVEs have risen sharply in recent months, often found by high-school students running agents for bug bounties. He expects most engineering time over the next 18 months to go to security ([Cloudflare](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/democratizing-security-for-the-agentic-web-with-cloudflare-cto-dane-kn/)).

### Open-weight models for business and for attackers

- Misha Laskin presents Beam, ReflectionAI's 500B-parameter open-weight reasoning model, which used more compute for RL than for pre-training. He says, “I expect majority of tokens to be going to open source” ([Beam](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/beam-the-great-american-open-model-with-reflectionai-co-founder-and-ce/)).
- Laskin argues openness improves safety, citing Linus's law, and that removing offensive cyber capabilities also removes defensive ones. Sarah Guo notes AI-native companies negotiate closed-model deals harder because open models exist ([Beam](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/beam-the-great-american-open-model-with-reflectionai-co-founder-and-ce/)).
- Kurtz says most attacks use open-weight models such as GLM and Qwen, because frontier models refuse even a cybersecurity slide deck. CIOs tell him open models three months behind are good enough. Knecht expects security-capable models to end up open anyway ([CrowdStrike](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-new-apex-predator-crowdstrike-ceo-george-kurtz-on-adversaries-arme/), [Cloudflare](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/democratizing-security-for-the-agentic-web-with-cloudflare-cto-dane-kn/)).

### Specialised defence models and the agentic web

- CrowdStrike built red and blue security models with Nvidia on Nemotron, post-trained on a decade of its data. Kurtz quotes remediation at about 3 cents against about $10 on a top frontier model ([CrowdStrike](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-new-apex-predator-crowdstrike-ceo-george-kurtz-on-adversaries-arme/)).
- Knecht describes a messy middle where humans and agents share the same rails. Cloudflare has released agent wallets and is building Signals with OpenAI so agents can find new content without re-crawling ([Cloudflare](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/democratizing-security-for-the-agentic-web-with-cloudflare-cto-dane-kn/)).

### AI for science

- Brockman says the resolution of almost 400 open maths problems settles whether AI can produce new knowledge. He sees data as the limit for applying the same methods in biology, and argues regulation should adapt once AI proves itself in drug development ([Brockman](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/the-changing-physics-of-cybersecurity-and-drug-development-with-openai/)).
- Laskin argues AI could compress PhD-level work into a week and wants AI connected to real experiments ([Beam](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/beam-the-great-american-open-model-with-reflectionai-co-founder-and-ce/)).

### Chips and enterprise agents

- Walter Goodwin says flops grew about a millionfold in 20 years against roughly 40x for memory bandwidth. Fractile targets about 25 times the bandwidth per chip of HBM parts using cheaper DRAM ([Fractile](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/frontier-chips-for-frontier-ai-labs-with-walter-goodwin-founder-ceo-of/)).
- Jake Stauch argues code is the right way to express enterprise automation. Serval gives IT teams coding agents wrapped in approvals and audit trails to displace ServiceNow ([Serval](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/10/taking-on-servicenow-coding-agents-the-rest-of-us-with-serval-co-found/)).
