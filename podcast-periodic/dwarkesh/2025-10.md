## Overview

October's AI episode was Andrej Karpathy's long conversation on why agents still need about a decade of work. Sarah Paine returned with a lecture on how Russia held back China's rise and why the balance has now flipped. Nick Lane covered the origins of life and how common it may be elsewhere.

## Main themes

### The decade of agents, not the year

- Karpathy says an agent should be like an employee or intern you would hire, and today's agents fall short because “They don’t have continual learning. You can’t just tell them something and they’ll remember it.” ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- Building nanochat, he found coding agents not net useful: they are weak on code that has never been written before. Autocomplete is his sweet spot ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- He calls outcome-based RL noisy, notes that LLM judges get gamed, and draws on Tesla's self-driving work to describe a slow march of nines in reliability ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- He wants a cognitive core of perhaps a billion parameters, stripped of memorised knowledge, because frontier models waste capacity compressing a mostly garbage internet ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).

### AI, growth and education

- Karpathy says we are already in an intelligence explosion and have been for decades, and expects AI to blend into the usual 2% GDP growth as computers and iPhones did ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- Dwarkesh Patel argues true AGI, as added labour, could lift growth far above trend. Karpathy says that assumes a discrete jump with no historical precedent ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- He chose education over a lab because he fears humanity being disempowered by AI. His company Eureka aims to build “the Starfleet Academy”, though he says current AI cannot yet make a good tutor ([Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).

### Russia and China under continental rules

- Sarah Paine argues both countries follow the rules of continental empire: avoid two-front wars and allow no great power on your borders. For a century a strong Russia used that logic against a weak China ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/sarah-paine-how-russia-sabotaged-china-s-rise/)).
- She traces territorial losses from the 1858 and 1860 treaties, Soviet interference in Chinese politics, the stripping of Manchuria after 1945 and the Sino-Soviet split ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/sarah-paine-how-russia-sabotaged-china-s-rise/)).
- Today, she says, China has nine times Russia's population and GNP after about 20 years of double-digit growth, so the partnership is likely to end on China's terms ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/sarah-paine-how-russia-sabotaged-china-s-rise/)).

### Is life common?

- Nick Lane argues that simple life follows almost inevitably from the chemistry of wet, rocky planets, of which he estimates the Milky Way has 20 to 40 billion ([Nick Lane](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/nick-lane-life-as-we-know-it-is-chemically-inevitable/)).
- He sees complex life as the real bottleneck, a one-off event on Earth about 2 billion years ago. Patel pushes back on whether evolution has no other route ([Nick Lane](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/nick-lane-life-as-we-know-it-is-chemically-inevitable/)).
