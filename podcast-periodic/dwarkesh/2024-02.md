## Overview

February had two guests. Demis Hassabis set out Google DeepMind's view of scaling, planning and safety on the road to AGI. Patrick Collison argued that culture and organisation, more than money, decide how much progress science and companies make, with Stripe and Arc as his examples.

## Main themes

### Scaling is a craft, and models need planning on top

- Hassabis says “I look at the large models today and I think they’re almost unreasonably effective for what they are” ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- He calls scaling an art form: training loss can be predicted, but capabilities such as MMLU do not always follow, so about one order of magnitude per generation is the most he would attempt ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- Hassabis sees large models as necessary but probably insufficient for AGI. DeepMind is working on AlphaZero-like planning on top of them, since a better world model makes search more efficient ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- Collison, from a different angle, finds the constancy of US GDP growth one of the weirdest things and suspects diminishing returns in science are organisational rather than a matter of exhausted ideas ([Collison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/patrick-collison-why-silicon-valley-s-most-talented-should-leave/)).

### Compute, timelines and Gemini

- Hassabis says Gemini 1 used roughly the compute rumoured for GPT-4, and that Google will have the most compute of any research lab this year ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- He declines to give specific AGI dates but would not be surprised by AGI-like systems within a decade. He calls Gemini the first fruit of the Brain and DeepMind merger ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).

### Safety tooling and securing weights

- Hassabis says: “I think we don’t have good enough evaluations and benchmarks for things like if the system can deceive you.” He treats deception as a root trait to rule out ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- He proposes hardened sandboxes that keep the AI in and hackers out, and promises published frameworks similar to responsible scaling policies ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- On weights, he describes Google's firewall plus DeepMind-specific protections and expects labs may want air gaps within three to five years. He questions open-sourcing general-purpose foundation models ([Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).

### Culture and organisation as the engine of progress

- Collison notes the US had about 1% as many professional scientists before World War II, yet output did not scale with spending. Arc funds scientists rather than projects and offers careers outside the principal investigator track ([Collison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/patrick-collison-why-silicon-valley-s-most-talented-should-leave/)).
- He says Stripe handles about 1% of global GDP, roughly a trillion dollars a year, deploys core services about 1,000 times a day at 99.9995% reliability, and makes millions of LLM calls a day ([Collison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/patrick-collison-why-silicon-valley-s-most-talented-should-leave/)).
- Collison calls moats overrated, expects billing to slide towards purchases made by AI agents, and describes Frontier, an advance market commitment that raised a billion dollars for carbon removal ([Collison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/patrick-collison-why-silicon-valley-s-most-talented-should-leave/)).
