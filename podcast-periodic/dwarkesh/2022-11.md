## Overview

November was a month about cities. Historian Kenneth T. Jackson defended Robert Moses against Robert Caro's The Power Broker, and economist Edward Glaeser explained why cities outlast terrorism, pandemics and remote work. Both guests came back to the same problem: it has become too easy to block building.

## Main themes

### Robert Moses and The Power Broker

- Jackson calls Moses “the greatest builder in American history”, and argues that without him New York might have declined like Detroit or St. Louis ([Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- He says Moses held many posts at once and worked for a dollar a year, and points out that the man who shaped American car culture never drove himself ([Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- Jackson calls The Power Broker the best book he ever read and correct in broad strokes, but full of small errors. He disputes Caro's account of the Cross Bronx Expressway using census tracts ([Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- He also says Moses misread neighbourhoods and that he himself tends to side with Jane Jacobs. Glaeser wants builders with somewhat more power than today, but not Moses' methods ([Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/), [Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).

### Too much power to say no

- Jackson argues the US has gone too far in the ability to obstruct change, and doubts Moses could repeat his record in the 21st century ([Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- Glaeser says “we have too much local ability to say no to new housing projects”, since would-be residents get no vote. He frames the fix as stronger rights for landowners ([Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).
- He links expensive housing in high-opportunity areas to falling mobility among poorer Americans, and says urban decline persists for decades because housing stays put, as in Detroit ([Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).

### Cities, remote work and jobs

- Glaeser argues electronic and face-to-face contact substitute in the short run but complement each other in the long run, so he expects hybrid work for skilled staff ([Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).
- He says remote work makes it easier for firms and wealthy people to leave high-tax states, and calls Austin the superstar of the COVID era ([Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).
- He opposes universal basic income in rich countries because a job provides purpose and social connection, citing 1970s negative income tax experiments, but supports modest cash transfers in the poorest countries ([Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).
