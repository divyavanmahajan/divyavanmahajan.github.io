## Overview

February had two big-company interviews. Gemini co-leads Jeff Dean and Noam Shazeer looked back over 25 years at Google and ahead to inference compute, automated research and modular models. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella argued that AI should be judged by economic growth, not by an AGI milestone, and presented Microsoft's quantum chip.

## Main themes

### How Google builds models

- Shazeer argues algorithms follow hardware: arithmetic is very cheap and moving data is expensive, which shaped deep learning and makes chip-model co-design pay off. Dean adds that CPU process gains now take three years instead of two ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).
- Shazeer says talking to a model is “100 times cheaper than reading a paperback”, so spending more compute to think harder at inference has a lot of room. Dean names search as a lever ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).
- Dean wants sparse mixture-of-experts models with independently trained modules, so that 100 teams around Google could improve one model over time. He notes Gemini already trains across multiple metro areas ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).

### Automating code and research

- Dean says Gemini has been further trained on Google's internal code base, and cites Sundar Pichai saying 25% of checked-in characters come from AI coding models with human oversight ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).
- Dean credits algorithmic and architecture changes at least as much as scale. Shazeer imagines running thousands of experiments in parallel, but says the largest-scale runs stay single experiments that do not speed up ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).

### Growth, compute and adoption at Microsoft

- Nadella notes the developed world grows around 2%, near zero after inflation, and wants something like 10% growth as the real benchmark. He expects the broader industries using AI, not tech firms, to be the big winners ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).
- He sees hyperscalers as one sure winner: “if intelligence is log of compute, whoever can do lots of compute is a big winner.” Agents, he says, will multiply compute use, but no market will be winner-take-all ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).
- Nadella calls adoption a change-management problem, like Lean for knowledge work, and separates automatable knowledge work from knowledge workers, who move to higher-level tasks ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).
- He describes Majorana 1 as the transistor moment of quantum computing, aiming at a million physical qubits, with a fault-tolerant machine possibly in 2027 to 2029 ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).

### Safety and limits

- Dean favours human-in-the-loop safeguards, while Shazeer argues analysing text is easier than generating it, so models can help check models ([Dean & Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).
- Nadella argues deployment needs a human who indemnifies the AI, and that courts will act before any takeoff ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).
