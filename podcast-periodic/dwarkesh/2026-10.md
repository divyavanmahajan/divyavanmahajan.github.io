## Overview

October stepped away from AI labs. Two history episodes looked at how small groups take or hold power: the Spanish conquest of the Aztec and Inca empires, and Robert Caro's decades studying Robert Moses and Lyndon Johnson. In between, Intel engineers explained how chips will improve and why output cannot grow as fast as AI demand.

## Main themes

### Conquest through allies

- Dwarkesh sums up the puzzle: “A couple hundred men, an empire of 6 million people, two and a half years.” Pizarro later took the Inca Empire with 168 men amid a civil war ([Si Sheppard](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/si-sheppard-how-did-a-few-hundred-spanish-soldiers-topple-two-empires/)).
- Si Sheppard says diplomacy mattered as much as horses and steel. Subject peoples resented Aztec rule, and the conquistadors were maybe 0.5% of the force that marched on Tenochtitlan ([Si Sheppard](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/si-sheppard-how-did-a-few-hundred-spanish-soldiers-topple-two-empires/)).
- Natives adapted, with street fighting, captured crossbows and bolas against horses, but Sheppard says the learning curve was too steep and short. Aztec warfare also aimed to capture rather than kill ([Si Sheppard](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/si-sheppard-how-did-a-few-hundred-spanish-soldiers-topple-two-empires/)).
- Dwarkesh frames the episode as a precedent for a technologically advanced outside force taking over a much larger established order ([Si Sheppard](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/si-sheppard-how-did-a-few-hundred-spanish-soldiers-topple-two-empires/)).

### Chips: better designs, limited tools

- Dwarkesh notes the AI industry has tripled compute each year for a decade. Intel CTO Pushkar Ranade says existing tools could support about 2x more leading-edge wafers, not 10x ([Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/)).
- Ranade says output cannot rise unless the whole supply chain produces more, and that 18-month tool lead times plus fab construction push new capacity towards the end of the decade ([Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/)).
- Chris Auth says Intel's lesson from 10nm is to limit innovation per node, and that backside power lets Intel print front-side wiring in a single High-NA EUV pass ([Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/)).
- Jack Kavalieros and Tyler Osborn cover ruthenium wiring, stacked CFET transistors and hybrid bonding pitches shrinking to 25 micrometres in production ([Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/)).

### Power and getting things built

- Caro says Johnson's persuasion worked because senators knew he could give or withhold committee seats and bills. He argues Kennedy lacked Johnson's drive, which traced back to teaching poor children in Cotulla ([Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).
- Dwarkesh cites the Second Avenue subway's slow progress. Caro does not want Moses's ruthlessness, but says “you need someone who’s going to find a way to get things built” ([Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).
- Caro describes how power hardened Moses, from civil-service reformer to the builder who evicted tens of thousands for the Cross Bronx Expressway ([Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).

### How Caro works

- Caro reduces each book to one sentence or a paragraph before writing. He did about 700 interviews for The Power Broker and cut 350,000 words from the manuscript ([Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).
- He says digital archives help find documents faster, but finding is small next to reading them ([Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).
