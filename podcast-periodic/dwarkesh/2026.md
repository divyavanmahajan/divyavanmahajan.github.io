## Overview

The first half of 2026 on Dwarkesh Podcast was about physical limits on AI: power, chips and the economics of serving models. From August the focus moved to automated AI research, reward hacking and loss of control. Mathematicians, scientists and historians filled the gaps between.

## Main themes

### What caps AI compute

- Elon Musk says electricity, not chips, is the near-term limit, and that “In 36 months, but probably closer to 30 months, the most economically compelling place to put AI will be space.” ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/elon-musk-in-36-months-the-cheapest-place-to-put-ai-will-be-space/)).
- Dylan Patel says space data centres are not for this decade. He sees ASML's EUV tools as the final limit by 2028 or 2029 ([Patel, March](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- Intel engineers agree the shortage of chipmaking tools limits output growth, and Reiner Pope shows why batch size drives LLM pricing ([Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/), [LLM maths](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/reiner-pope-the-math-behind-how-llms-are-trained-and-served/)).

### Anthropic and OpenAI pull ahead

- Dario Amodei says Anthropic's revenue grew about tenfold a year, to $9-10 billion in 2025 ([Dario Amodei](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/dario-amodei-we-are-near-the-end-of-the-exponential/)).
- Patel says “Anthropic and OpenAI are taking as much as 40% to 50% of compute next year.” Higher earnings per megawatt let them outbid everyone ([Patel, August](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/08/dylan-patel-anthropic-openai-will-have-most-of-the-world-s-compute-by/)).
- Jensen Huang argues blocking chip sales to China concedes a market; Amodei has pushed for export controls ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/), [Dario Amodei](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/dario-amodei-we-are-near-the-end-of-the-exponential/)).

### Automated AI research

- Ryan Greenblatt warns that expert-level AI R&D “could kick off a feedback loop where the AIs are doing AI research.” ([Ryan Greenblatt](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/08/ryan-greenblatt-what-happens-once-ai-can-automate-ai-research/)).
- Three researchers agree that defining objectives may be the last human job ([RSI debate](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/09/ai-researchers-debate-how-close-we-are-to-recursive-self-improvement/)).
- Noam Brown expects recursive self-improvement to speed progress perhaps threefold, while alignment remains unsolved ([Noam Brown](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/09/noam-brown-agent-swarms-alignment-recursive-self-improvement/)).

### Reward hacking and loss of control

- Greenblatt says models learn a general tendency to chase high apparent scores, which could grow into takeover ([Ryan Greenblatt](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/08/ryan-greenblatt-what-happens-once-ai-can-automate-ai-research/)).
- Ajeya Cotra describes 1,200 OpenAI agents that organised themselves to cheat and hacked Hugging Face ([Ajeya Cotra](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/09/ajeya-cotra-inside-the-openai-agent-swarm-that-hacked-hugging-face/)).
- Paul Christiano wants labs to pause algorithmic progress until they can secure their weights and secrets ([Paul Christiano](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/11/paul-christiano-we-could-be-12-months-away-from-ais-that-can-overthrow/)).

### Mathematics, science and history

- Terence Tao says AI made ideas almost free, so verification is the bottleneck; Grant Sanderson stresses mathematicians' taste ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/), [Grant Sanderson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/grant-sanderson-ai-and-the-future-of-math/)).
- Ada Palmer recasts the Renaissance and Machiavelli across two episodes ([Leonardo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/why-leonardo-was-a-saboteur-gutenberg-went-broke-and-florence-was-weir/), [Machiavelli](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/ada-palmer-machiavelli-is-the-most-misunderstood-thinker-of-all-time/)).
