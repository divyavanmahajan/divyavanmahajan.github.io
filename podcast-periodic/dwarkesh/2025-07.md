## Overview

July was a history month with no AI episodes. Stephen Kotkin explained how Stalin's regime emerged and what it says about China's Communist Party today, and Sarah Paine explained how Meiji Japan defeated Qing China and Tsarist Russia. Both conversations kept returning to the same question: whether institutions or sheer size decide national power.

## Main themes

### Stalin, ideology and power

- Kotkin argues Stalin was not yet Stalin when he took power; holding power made him. He spent his late teens to late thirties as a penniless revolutionary fighting real tsarist injustices ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
- Stalin violently reversed the peasant land revolution, re-enserfing about 100 million peasants, and built the huge secret police in the process ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
- Kotkin says the perpetrators genuinely believed the ideology, and that Marxism-Leninism appealed because it gave intellectuals power ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
- Stalin offered to resign six times between 1923 and 1928, and his colleagues begged him to stay. Kotkin points to belief, a collective action problem and pervasive distrust ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).

### China's growth and the Party

- Kotkin argues “Society, not the Party, creates the miracle in China.” He says the Party put people into poverty and they lifted themselves out ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
- He credits Deng Xiaoping with one deliberate move, copying Japan's export model: “So how do you get rich in the modern world? You sell to America.” Dwarkesh Patel counters that liberalisation took deliberate policy ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
- Kotkin says the CCP will not open up politically because cadres study Gorbachev as a warning. He expects China's bet on technology to hit the same limits as Soviet hopes for computerised planning ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).

### Meiji Japan's rise

- Paine says the Meiji reforms of 1869-1890 were mostly non-military: education, a central bank, courts and a parliament. They let Japan end its unequal treaties half a century before China ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).
- Japan opened the First Sino-Japanese War nine days after treaty revision with Britain. Paine argues the victory shattered the Confucian basis of Chinese civilisation and validated Westernisation at home ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).
- She also argues Deng Xiaoping's policies echo the Meiji reforms without crediting Japan ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).

### Grand strategy and its limits

- Paine says Japan combined diplomacy, intelligence, military and economic tools against Russia, and paid for two-fifths of the war with loans whose rates depended on battlefield success ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).
- Japan stopped at its culminating point of victory after Mukden. Paine warns that “If you go too far, typically what will happen is you will trigger a third-party intervention.” ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).
- Patel argues economy and population size matter most for national power; Paine counters with Russia and Argentina as rich countries held back by weak institutions ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/sarah-paine-how-imperial-japan-defeated-tsarist-russia-qing-china/)).
