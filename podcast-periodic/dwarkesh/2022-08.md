## Overview

August had two long conversations. Will MacAskill set out the case of his new book for longtermism: technology will arrive anyway, but moral values are contingent and could be locked in, possibly through AI. Steve Hsu described Genomic Prediction and argued that predicting traits from DNA is now a machine learning problem limited mainly by data.

## Main themes

### What history could have gone differently

- MacAskill argues that in the long run “economic or technological progress is very non-contingent”, because strong incentives push societies towards growth ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).
- He says general relativity was plausibly decades early, but religious leaders, Marx and Engels, and moral activists had far more contingent long-run influence ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).
- He treats Effective Altruism itself as contingent: similar ideas, from the Mohists to Peter Singer, did not take off earlier. With the movement perhaps growing around 30% a year, he says value change need not take centuries ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).

### Longtermism and AI lock-in

- MacAskill's main long-term worry is a single ideology becoming permanently entrenched. On the enabling technology he says “The key technology here is Artificial Intelligence”, since digital rulers could persist indefinitely ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).
- He puts the chance of such lock-in this century at roughly 10%, and argues that new institutions have moments of plasticity when norms get fixed, like the US Constitution ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).
- His priorities are targeted: safe and well-governed AI, preventing worst-case pandemics and a Third World War, and not assuming we are at the end of moral progress. Against Cowen and Collison he cites Moore's Law as a predictable long-run trend ([MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).

### Genomic prediction as a business

- Hsu frames trait prediction as a data-scaling problem whose resulting predictors are simple and additive, so the algorithms are not the moat ([Hsu](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/steve-hsu-intelligence-embryo-selection-the-future-of-humanity/)).
- He says Genomic Prediction works with 200 to 300 IVF clinics on six continents and sees a strong first-mover advantage in clinic trust and channel relationships. He concedes that a data-rich player such as 23andMe could build better predictors ([Hsu](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/steve-hsu-intelligence-embryo-selection-the-future-of-humanity/)).
- He expects clinics to sequence on site and upload to the cloud, which in his view makes the service hard to regulate by geography ([Hsu](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/steve-hsu-intelligence-embryo-selection-the-future-of-humanity/)).
- Hsu says most training data is European, expects non-European biobanks to close the gap in about five years, and argues that controversy keeps cognitive measures out of most genomic datasets ([Hsu](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/steve-hsu-intelligence-embryo-selection-the-future-of-humanity/)).
