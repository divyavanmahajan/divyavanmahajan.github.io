## Overview

December closed Sarah Paine's lecture series with an account of why the Soviet Union lost the Cold War, framed as lessons for a second one. Adam Marblestone then asked what AI is missing about how brains learn, and what it would cost to find out.

## Main themes

### Many causes of the Soviet collapse

- Paine opens: “Since we seem to be in a second Cold War, maybe it’s a good time to revisit the last one”. She tests the claim that Ronald Reagan won it single-handedly ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/sarah-paine-why-russia-lost-the-cold-war/)).
- External pressures included Western GNP seven times the Soviet figure, human-rights pressure from Helsinki and Carter, the China split and US submarines. Later data put Soviet defence spending at 40-50% of GNP or more, against CIA estimates of 20% ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/sarah-paine-why-russia-lost-the-cold-war/)).
- Internally, oil was up to 55% of the budget when prices collapsed and 20 to 40% of crops rotted. Paine says planners worked from garbage data, citing Soviet pride in making the heaviest TVs ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/sarah-paine-why-russia-lost-the-cold-war/)).
- She argues Gorbachev gave away political power before fixing the economy, the opposite of China's path, and that Kohl and Bush handled the ending well. Her conclusion is that the West barely won ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/sarah-paine-why-russia-lost-the-cold-war/)).

### What AI may be missing about the brain

- Marblestone argues machine learning has neglected loss functions. He suggests evolution built many reward signals, like “A lot of Python code, basically, generating a specific curriculum” for different parts of the brain ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).
- Building on Steve Byrnes's split between a learning system and a steering system, he says this answers Ilya Sutskever's question of how the genome encodes abstract desires. Dwarkesh Patel notes the whole genome is only about 3 gigabytes ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).
- Following Sutskever, he calls reinforcement learning in LLMs conceptually crude compared with how brains learn from reward ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).

### Funding science and automating math

- Marblestone says neuroscience needs large-scale data collection, and puts a concerted brain-mapping push at hundreds of millions to low billions of dollars, citing the fall in genome sequencing costs ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).
- He admits this is not practical in an AI 2027 scenario; his own AGI timeline is about 10 years ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).
- As a Lean board member, he expects reinforcement learning on verifiable proofs to automate the mechanical parts of math and enable formally verified software. Conjecturing new ideas remains hard ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).
