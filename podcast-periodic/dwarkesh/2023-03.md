## Overview

March turned towards AI, with OpenAI and alignment the only threads shared across episodes. Ilya Sutskever explained why next-token prediction could go beyond human ability, and Nat Friedman described building Copilot and funding an effort to read buried ancient scrolls. Brett Harrison gave an insider's account of FTX US before its collapse.

## Main themes

### Next-token prediction and its limits

- Sutskever argues a good enough next-token predictor must understand what produces the data, so the paradigm is not limited to imitating people ([Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/ilya-sutskever-openai-chief-scientist-why-next-token-prediction-could/)).
- He names reliability as the main risk to economic impact by 2030 and grounds his view in self-driving cars, which look capable but are not yet reliable ([Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/ilya-sutskever-openai-chief-scientist-why-next-token-prediction-could/)).
- He says data is still in good shape but will run out eventually, and hardware is not a limit. OpenAI dropped robotics because there was no path to enough data ([Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/ilya-sutskever-openai-chief-scientist-why-next-token-prediction-could/)).

### Alignment and the OpenAI business

- Sutskever expects “multiple definitions that look at alignment from different aspects” rather than one mathematical definition, and wants alignment to grow faster than capability ([Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/ilya-sutskever-openai-chief-scientist-why-next-token-prediction-could/)).
- Friedman notes there is no open source technical alignment community and cites Microsoft's Sydney as a warning ([Friedman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/nat-friedman-github-ceo-reading-ancient-scrolls-open-source-ai/)).
- On OpenAI's projected $1 billion of 2024 revenue, Sutskever points to growth since the GPT-3 API, DALL-E and ChatGPT, and praises Microsoft and Azure as a partner ([Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/ilya-sutskever-openai-chief-scientist-why-next-token-prediction-could/)).

### Copilot, GitHub and an inefficient world

- Friedman argues that “our default estimate of how efficient the world is is far too charitable”, citing hedge fund traders who expected an 80% profit cut from sharing old strategies ([Friedman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/nat-friedman-github-ceo-reading-ancient-scrolls-open-source-ai/)).
- He pitched buying GitHub to Satya Nadella because developers now drive purchasing. Copilot started as a disappointing chatbot prototype, then hit over 60% retention after 30 days ([Friedman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/nat-friedman-github-ceo-reading-ancient-scrolls-open-source-ai/)).
- He says Copilot now writes over half of code, up from the low 20s at launch, and expects AI models to proliferate rather than concentrate in a few labs ([Friedman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/nat-friedman-github-ceo-reading-ancient-scrolls-open-source-ai/)).
- With Daniel Gross he funds the Vesuvius Challenge to read carbonized Herculaneum scrolls. Some 600-plus intact scrolls remain, and the villa's main library may not yet be excavated ([Friedman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/nat-friedman-github-ceo-reading-ancient-scrolls-open-source-ai/)).

### Inside FTX and trading infrastructure

- Harrison says FTX looked badly managed rather than fraudulent from his seat. Gary Wang and Nishad Singh wrote over 90% of the code, and Sam Bankman-Fried spent his time on investors and media ([Harrison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/brett-harrison-ftx-us-former-president-speaks-out/)).
- He calls late mid-2022 bonuses the clearest red flag in hindsight, but says the inner circle deliberately falsified audited financials. He wants independent boards mandated at certain stages of growth ([Harrison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/brett-harrison-ftx-us-former-president-speaks-out/)).
- He defends latency competition in high-frequency trading and describes his new startup Architect, built so users never hand over private credentials ([Harrison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/03/brett-harrison-ftx-us-former-president-speaks-out/)).
