## Overview

April was a debate about the intelligence explosion, held across three episodes. Daniel Kokotajlo and Scott Alexander defended their fast AI 2027 scenario, Ege Erdil and Tamay Besiroglu argued for much longer timelines, and Mark Zuckerberg took a middle view grounded in compute and adoption. Historian Kyle Harper closed the gap with a look at why Rome fell.

## Main themes

### The intelligence explosion, for and against

- Alexander says “coding is what starts the intelligence explosion”. Kokotajlo estimates a superhuman coder would speed algorithmic progress about 5x and a superhuman AI researcher about 25x; Patel counters that compute, not researcher headcount, is the bottleneck ([AI 2027](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/ai-2027-month-by-month-model-of-intelligence-explosion-scott-alexander/)).
- Erdil puts a remote worker that can do everything around 2045. Besiroglu says capability unlocks came about every three orders of magnitude of compute, and both reject a software-only singularity ([AGI is still 30 years away](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/agi-is-still-30-years-away-ege-erdil-tamay-besiroglu/)).
- Zuckerberg finds automated AI research compelling, but says chips, permits, energy and testing capacity will keep progress from a sudden takeoff ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/mark-zuckerberg-ai-will-write-most-meta-code-in-18-months/)).

### Alignment and who should steer

- In AI 2027 the lab finds signs of misalignment in mid-2027; in one ending it rolls back, in the other it applies a shallow patch and races on. Kokotajlo puts his P(doom) at about 70%, Alexander his at about 20% ([AI 2027](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/ai-2027-month-by-month-model-of-intelligence-explosion-scott-alexander/)).
- Alexander expects training failures to get worse as models become agents, though he is not convinced alignment won't happen by default. Kokotajlo has lost faith in the labs and wants transparency and whistleblower protections ([AI 2027](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/ai-2027-month-by-month-model-of-intelligence-explosion-scott-alexander/)).
- Erdil sees little evidence that AIs become misaligned and argues pausing at a fixed compute level would teach little ([AGI is still 30 years away](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/agi-is-still-30-years-away-ege-erdil-tamay-besiroglu/)).

### Racing China, growing the economy

- Kokotajlo says “we can’t just unilaterally slow down and have China go take the lead”, while Patel argues China is more likely to race if the US does ([AI 2027](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/ai-2027-month-by-month-model-of-intelligence-explosion-scott-alexander/)).
- Zuckerberg credits DeepSeek's optimisations but says export controls left it text-only, and warns that coding models from other countries could plant vulnerabilities ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/mark-zuckerberg-ai-will-write-most-meta-code-in-18-months/)).
- Erdil and Besiroglu, now running Mechanize, forecast 30% annual growth once AI substitutes for labour, with regulation as the main obstacle ([AGI is still 30 years away](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/agi-is-still-30-years-away-ege-erdil-tamay-besiroglu/)).

### Meta's open models and AI-written code

- Zuckerberg presents Llama 4: Scout and Maverick first, with Behemoth to follow at more than 2 trillion parameters. He says Meta did not tune Maverick to top LM Arena because it measures user value instead ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/mark-zuckerberg-ai-will-write-most-meta-code-in-18-months/)).
- He predicts AI agents will write most code for Meta's Llama efforts within 12 to 18 months, and expects AI to lead to more support hires, not fewer ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/mark-zuckerberg-ai-will-write-most-meta-code-in-18-months/)).

### Rome's fall

- Harper argues that plague and a sixth-century volcanic cooling of one to two degrees, bringing famine, broke the Roman Empire just after Justinian's reconquests ([Kyle Harper](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/why-rome-actually-fell-plagues-slavery-ice-age-kyle-harper/)).
- He says Rome had advanced markets and finance but never industrialised because it lacked self-sustaining science, and rejects the idea that slavery blocked mechanisation ([Kyle Harper](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/why-rome-actually-fell-plagues-slavery-ice-age-kyle-harper/)).
