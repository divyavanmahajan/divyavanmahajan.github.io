## Overview

So far in 2026 Epoch After Hours has run two episodes, both about how to measure what AI can really do. In January mathematician Daniel Litt discussed where models help research mathematics and where they still fall short. In May Epoch's benchmarking team asked whether fast saturation and the gap with real-world use doom AI benchmarks, and concluded they do not. Epoch's new FrontierMath: Open Problems benchmark links the two conversations.

## Main themes

### Jagged ability in research mathematics

- Daniel Litt says models solve contest-level problems but remain uneven across fields. In algebraic geometry and number theory, he says, “the models are just not that good” compared with areas like optimisation ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).
- Litt sees nothing special about this jaggedness in maths. He thinks models do better at optimisation partly because experts at OpenAI generated a lot of data there ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).
- He rates open problems recently solved with or by AI at about the level of a mid- or low-tier IMO problem, and expects many little-studied, written-down problems to fall to AI within a year ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).

### How much AI speeds up maths

- Litt expects the main near-term effect to be that the cost of trying the first obvious idea gets very low ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).
- He argues that many people talk about accelerating science “without trying to rigorously run tests”. He doubts literature-search gains exceed a percent or two, and says an OpenAI paper on open problems gave no good evidence of acceleration ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).
- Litt also warns that LLM-written nonsense papers on arXiv are adding to a refereeing crisis in mathematics ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).

### Epoch's benchmarks, from FrontierMath to Open Problems

- Greg Burnham describes FrontierMath: Open Problems, a set of unsolved research problems whose answers can still be checked automatically ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/), [Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).
- Litt likes that it could steer lab resources towards real problems. He argues maths benchmarks mostly measure knowledge rather than reasoning, and that tasks a person can do in a few hours will saturate soon ([Daniel Litt](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/01/ai-math-capabilities-could-be-jagged-for-a-long-time-daniel-litt/)).
- Tom Adamczewski presents MirrorCode, an Epoch/METR benchmark in which AI reimplements command-line programs, designed to scale from small programs towards codebases the size of the Linux kernel ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).

### Are benchmarks doomed?

- Burnham links current pessimism to coding agents starting to just work, and notes that saturation spikes happened at least twice before, around GPT-4 and with reasoning models ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).
- Adamczewski argues costs and benefits of building benchmarks may keep pace, since unsaturated benchmarks become more valuable. He suspects something “inherently very difficult about squeezing all of the complexity of real-world, long-horizon tasks into something benchmarkable” ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).
- Burnham says claims from benchmarks should stay narrow, and that Deep Blue beating Kasparov did not herald AGI ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).

### AI-assisted building and human grading

- Adamczewski calls LLMs an essential tool that speeds up his benchmark work. Burnham sees a remaining bottleneck in taste for choosing tasks ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).
- Both point to human judging as a scoring route; Burnham suggests entering AI stories into short-story contests as a creative-writing benchmark ([Benchmarks](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2026/05/are-ai-benchmarks-doomed/)).
