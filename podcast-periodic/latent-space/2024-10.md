## Overview

October on Latent Space started at OpenAI Dev Day, where OpenAI presented itself as a platform and Sam Altman put agents next on the roadmap. The rest of the month asked how AI gets into real products: evals at Braintrust, Dash at Dropbox, audio overviews at NotebookLM, and national policy in Singapore. Evaluation came up in three of the five episodes.

## Main themes

### OpenAI's platform, o1 and agents

- Olivier Godement says OpenAI will focus on “the tools which are, like, the closest to the model itself”: fine-tuning, distillation and evals. Romain Huet cites more than 3 million developers on OpenAI ([Dev Day 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-agi-in-real-time-openai-dev-day-2024/)).
- Altman says OpenAI clearly reached level two (reasoners) with o1 and expects agents soon. He calls agent safety and trust the long pole, and frames AGI as a smooth exponential, not a single moment ([Dev Day 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-agi-in-real-time-openai-dev-day-2024/)).
- Alistair Pullen says Cosine's fine-tuned GPT-4o still outscores o1 on SWE-bench Verified. Ankur Goyal expects o1-style reasoning to pull agent logic into the model, which swyx partly disputes ([Dev Day 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-agi-in-real-time-openai-dev-day-2024/), [Braintrust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/production-ai-engineering-starts-with-evals-with-ankur-goyal-of-braint/)).

### Evals as the core workflow

- Goyal argues every change should be evaluated, and that evals turn debates over prompts and models into something almost scientific. Braintrust logs production traces in the eval format, which he says removes the ETL step ([Braintrust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/production-ai-engineering-starts-with-evals-with-ankur-goyal-of-braint/)).
- He says few customers fine-tune and open-weight models are under 5% of production workloads. He credits Haiku's price and tool calling for Anthropic's foothold ([Braintrust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/production-ai-engineering-starts-with-evals-with-ankur-goyal-of-braint/)).
- At Dev Day, Godement describes free evals for developers who opt in to share data ([Dev Day 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-agi-in-real-time-openai-dev-day-2024/)). The NotebookLM team listened critically before formal rater evals, defining quality largely as whether output sounds like a Deep Dive ([NotebookLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/how-notebooklm-was-made/)).

### AI-first products

- Drew Houston says a January 2023 memo made Dropbox AI-first. Dash is meant to replace the ten search boxes that “each search 10% of your stuff”, and integrates with Google Drive and OneDrive ([Dropbox](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-the-silicon-brain-with-drew-houston-of-dropbox/)).
- Houston has coded over 400 hours this year. He calls LLMs a self-commoditising business, expects value at Nvidia and the application layer, and says RAG and long context are both needed ([Dropbox](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-the-silicon-brain-with-drew-houston-of-dropbox/)).
- Raiza Martin and Usama Shafqat say NotebookLM's audio overviews work because two AI personas question each other, an editorial transform rather than text-to-speech. They shipped one format with no user controls ([NotebookLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/how-notebooklm-was-made/)).

### Singapore's approach to AI policy

- Josephine Teo compares AI to electricity: rules should govern specific uses. Singapore's guidelines are largely voluntary, and its only AI-specific law so far is a proposed one on synthetic content in elections ([Singapore](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-the-ai-engineer-nation-with-josephine-teo-minister-of-digital/)).
- The national strategy covers compute, data and talent. swyx points to the goal of tripling AI practitioners from 5,000 to 15,000, and Teo says employers send the strongest reskilling signal ([Singapore](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/10/building-the-ai-engineer-nation-with-josephine-teo-minister-of-digital/)).
