## Overview

January on Latent Space was shaped by DeepSeek V3, which came up in three of five episodes, from Simon Willison's review of 2024 to Baseten's work serving it. The other episodes were founder interviews about building on top of models: neural search at Exa, a consumer chat platform at Chai and the ComfyUI image workflow tool.

## Main themes

### DeepSeek V3 and cheaper frontier models

- Simon Willison calls DeepSeek V3 by far the best open-weights model on benchmarks, noting it “was trained for $5.5 million, which is a 10th of the price” people expected ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/ride-home-simon-willison-things-we-learned-about-llms-in-2024/)).
- Willison argues this breaks the assumption that ever fewer organisations can afford frontier training. He counts 18 organisations with models beating the original GPT-4 and cites a 100x drop in OpenAI prices since GPT-3 ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/ride-home-simon-willison-things-we-learned-about-llms-in-2024/)).
- William Beauchamp compares Chai's focus on performance per dollar at inference time to DeepSeek's approach ([Chai](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/outlasting-noam-shazeer-crowdsourcing-chai-ai-with-1-4m-dau-and-becomi/)).

### Running inference in production

- Amir Haghighat defines mission-critical inference as inference whose slowness or downtime takes the customer's main product down. Baseten was the first inference neo-cloud to get DeepSeek V3 online ([Baseten](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/everything-you-need-to-run-mission-critical-inference-ft-deepseek-v3-s/)).
- Yining Zhang explains that the 671-billion-parameter model does not fit on an eight-card H100 node even in FP8, so Baseten used H200s or multi-node setups with SGLang ([Baseten](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/everything-you-need-to-run-mission-critical-inference-ft-deepseek-v3-s/)).
- Haghighat says Baseten offers no shared per-token endpoints and prices on resource use. Teams moving to V3 often leave Claude over rate limits, price or latency ([Baseten](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/everything-you-need-to-run-mission-critical-inference-ft-deepseek-v3-s/)).

### Agents and search

- Willison's main frustration with agents is that everyone defines them differently. He trusts coding agents and research assistants, but sees agents that spend money for you as an AGI-level problem ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/ride-home-simon-willison-things-we-learned-about-llms-in-2024/)).
- Will Bryk argues Google is built on keyword-era technology. Exa's neural search spends more compute per query to return complete result lists, and he calls Exa the OpenAI of search ([Exa](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/beating-google-at-search-with-neural-pagerank-and-5m-of-h200s-with-wil/)).
- Bryk expects LLMs, not humans, to write most search queries. Exa runs its own crawler and index and bought a $5 million H200 cluster ([Exa](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/beating-google-at-search-with-neural-pagerank-and-5m-of-h200s-with-wil/)).

### Consumer AI built on human feedback

- Beauchamp says Chai reached 1.4 million DAU, with revenue rising from $10 million to over $22 million. First traction came from a therapist bot his sister built ([Chai](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/outlasting-noam-shazeer-crowdsourcing-chai-ai-with-1-4m-dau-and-becomi/)).
- Chaiverse ranks submitted models by Elo from about 5,000 completions each. Beauchamp says it cut a thirty-day retention test to about three hours, and warns “I can make it 20% funnier just by training it to throw in swear words” ([Chai](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/outlasting-noam-shazeer-crowdsourcing-chai-ai-with-1-4m-dau-and-becomi/)).
- He says a voice feature that took three months to build made no difference in A/B tests ([Chai](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/outlasting-noam-shazeer-crowdsourcing-chai-ai-with-1-4m-dau-and-becomi/)).

### Image workflows for power users

- comfyanonymous says he deliberately built a hard-to-use, node-based interface while others built easy ones. ComfyUI spread because it was the only way many people could run SDXL locally ([ComfyUI](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/ai-engineering-for-art-with-comfyanonymous-of-comfyui/)).
- The intro frames the move from Automatic1111 to ComfyUI as a shift from prompt tweaking to chained workflows. Easy custom nodes grew an ecosystem that now needs an official registry ([ComfyUI](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/01/ai-engineering-for-art-with-comfyanonymous-of-comfyui/)).
