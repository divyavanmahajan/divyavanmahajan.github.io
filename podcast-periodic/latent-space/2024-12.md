## Overview

December was a look-back month for Latent Space. Most episodes were Latent Space Live talks recorded at NeurIPS, each reviewing 2024 in one area, and the month closed with the hosts' 100th-episode year in review. Two interviews, with Bolt.new and Codeium, covered how coding agents were being sold to consumers and enterprises.

## Main themes

### Coding agents, from Bolt to Windsurf

- Eric Simons says Bolt.new worked because StackBlitz's in-browser OS met models good enough to write working apps. It reached about $4M ARR in four weeks, and non-developers stay in the product ([Bolt.new](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/bolt-new-flow-engineering-for-code-agents-and-8m-arr-in-2-months-as-a/)).
- Varun Mohan and Anshul explain why Codeium forked VS Code into Windsurf: the agent needs the developer's whole trajectory. Varun says limited human interaction is the goal, but approving every command and running binaries locally still block it ([Windsurf](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/windsurf-the-enterprise-ai-ide-with-varun-and-anshul-of-codeium-ai/)).
- Graham Neubig argues a strong coding agent needs about five or six tools, light planning and a good model. He predicts “every large language model will be a better agent model by mid-twenty twenty-five” ([Agents](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-agents-ls-live-neurips-2024/)).

### Selling AI coding to enterprises

- Anshul's thesis is to build security, compliance and scale in from the start. Varun says large companies care far less about price than individual developers, who churn easily ([Windsurf](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/windsurf-the-enterprise-ai-ide-with-varun-and-anshul-of-codeium-ai/)).
- Itamar Friedman contrasts Bolt's consumer market with enterprise code, where testing, review, task decomposition and on-prem deployment dominate ([Bolt.new](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/bolt-new-flow-engineering-for-code-agents-and-8m-arr-in-2-months-as-a/)).

### Video generation and vision

- Bill Peebles says Sora can generate 1080p video up to a minute long and that quality rises steadily with compute. Tali Dekel says implausible physics shows video generation is not solved ([ICML Part 1](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/generative-video-worldsim-diffusion-vision-reinforcement-learning-and/)).
- Dan Kondratyuk describes language models and diffusion as still competing for video. The recap notes how much of this work comes from Google DeepMind, including Genie and VideoPoet ([ICML Part 1](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/generative-video-worldsim-diffusion-vision-reinforcement-learning-and/)).
- Isaac Robinson calls Sora the biggest vision paper of the year. Peter Robicheaux shows vision language models still failing at fine detail such as reading a watch ([Vision](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-vision-ls-live-neurips/)).

### Open models, synthetic data and small models

- Luca Soldaini says Qwen, DeepSeek and Llama 3 came close to closed frontier models in 2024, but warns about compute barriers and shrinking access to web data ([Open Models](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-open-models-ls-live-neurips/)).
- Loubna Ben Allal says synthetic data now runs through the whole pipeline, so “you can train an LLM with like an entirely synthetic pipeline”. She finds model collapse fears overstated ([Synthetic Data](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-synthetic-data-and-smol-models-ls-live-neurips/)).
- Dan Fu and Eugene Cheah argue state space models and RWKV can match transformers at far lower compute ([Post-Transformers](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-post-transformers-architectures-state-space-models-rwkv-ls-liv/)).

### Cheaper intelligence and a scaling wall

- swyx and Alessio Fanelli say several NeurIPS speakers saw pre-training scaling hitting a wall, with inference-time compute taking over. Citing Ramp, they put OpenAI's share of production traffic at 50-75%, down from about 95% ([Year in Review](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/latent-space-2024-year-in-review/)).
- swyx says GPT-4-level quality fell from about $40 per million tokens to a few cents in a year. Sarah Guo argues demand is elastic, so cheaper tokens mean more calls ([Year in Review](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/latent-space-2024-year-in-review/), [AI Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-ai-startups-ls-live-neurips/)).
- Guo and Pranav Reddy argue cheaper, more competitive models made 2024 a market that favours startups over incumbents ([AI Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/12/2024-in-ai-startups-ls-live-neurips/)).
