## Overview

May on Latent Space was mostly about what agents need around the model: their own clouds, their own computers and a place inside the company. Two infrastructure founders and two coding-agent builders covered that ground. The rest of the month looked at AI applied to theoretical physics, clinical work and autonomous drones.

## Main themes

### Infrastructure built for agents

- Jake Cooper argues agents want the same primitives as humans (versioning, forking, observability) at a thousand times the scale. He says they need “something massively better than Cube” for orchestration, and that CLIs with many flags suit agents well ([Railway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/railway-the-agent-native-cloud-jake-cooper/)).
- Cooper explains why Railway runs its own bare metal: a payback period of about three months against renting cloud capacity, metal margins around 70%, and servers that gained value as RAM prices rose ([Railway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/railway-the-agent-native-cloud-jake-cooper/)).
- Ivan Burazin describes Daytona as composable computers for agents. Agents need speed and state together, which he says is “like combining a Lambda and an EC2” ([Daytona](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/giving-agents-computers-ivan-burazin-daytona/)).
- Burazin cites 60 ms to start one sandbox and about 75 seconds for 50,000. Spiky RL and eval workloads keep mean utilisation at 15%, and both founders touched on compute scarcity ([Daytona](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/giving-agents-computers-ivan-burazin-daytona/), [Railway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/railway-the-agent-native-cloud-jake-cooper/)).

### Coding agents move to the background

- Cole Murray says that around December 2025, Opus 4.5 and GPT 5.2 could take a good spec to a pull request with little hand-holding ([Async Agents](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/the-age-of-async-agents-cognition-s-walden-yan-openinspect-s-cole-murr/)).
- Walden Yan says Cognition's merged PRs grew about 7x in a few months while engineering headcount rose about 10%. Both guests prefer running the agent's "brain" in a control plane and using the sandbox as its hands ([Async Agents](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/the-age-of-async-agents-cognition-s-walden-yan-openinspect-s-cole-murr/)).
- Murray sees SRE first response, PMs fixing bugs from Slack and customer support as the main uses. He and Yan both warn about slop in unreviewed code ([Async Agents](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/the-age-of-async-agents-cognition-s-walden-yan-openinspect-s-cole-murr/)).
- Cooper told Railway staff that writing code by hand is now wrong. He also suggests measuring the share of tokens that reach production ([Railway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/railway-the-agent-native-cloud-jake-cooper/)).

### AI in physics and healthcare

- Alex Lupsasca says that in some directions AI has become “superhuman, at least on certain tasks”. GPT models produced the key formulas and proofs for gluon and graviton amplitudes ([Vibe Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/doing-vibe-physics-alex-lupsasca-openai/)).
- Lupsasca now sends parallel chats down different routes as scouts. He worries about AI-generated papers flooding arXiv ([Vibe Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/doing-vibe-physics-alex-lupsasca-openai/)).
- Janie Lee and Chai Asawa describe Abridge as a clinical intelligence layer that grew out of documentation, which costs clinicians 10 to 20 hours a week ([Abridge](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/ai-native-healthcare-100m-doctor-visits-10-20-hours-saved-prior-auth-i/)).
- Asawa points to close to 100 million medical conversations as proprietary data. Health systems want their own guidelines built into clinical decision support ([Abridge](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/ai-native-healthcare-100m-doctor-visits-10-20-hours-saved-prior-auth-i/)).

### Drones and manufacturing scale

- Yaroslav Azhnyuk calls FPV drones the first software-defined weapon platform and says autonomy multiplies their value ([Drones](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/the-autonomous-drone-tech-stack-economics-of-drones-yaroslav-azhnyuk-t/)).
- He says the West lags China in technology, mass manufacturing, components and rare earths. He warns that China could build billions of drones against Ukraine's four million ([Drones](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/the-autonomous-drone-tech-stack-economics-of-drones-yaroslav-azhnyuk-t/)).
