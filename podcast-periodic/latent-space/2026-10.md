## Overview

October was Latent Space's busiest month of the year so far, with eleven episodes. The core was Anthropic's platform team explaining how a model launch is now mostly safety and scale work, alongside builders running agents in production at Arca, Tenex and Supabase. Compute markets, AI for science and general-purpose robots filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Anthropic: shipping Opus 5.5 and Managed Agents

- In his intro swyx cites Anthropic's S1: revenue per employee of $6.6 million against $1.4 million a year earlier, and a first GAAP-profitable quarter at 19% ([Anthropic](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/how-anthropic-ships-the-1-llm-in-the-world-safely-separating-the-brain/)).
- Katelyn Lesse and Angela Jiang say most launch work for Opus 5.5 was anti-distillation, safeguard policies, access controls and CPU capacity. A Rust rewrite of the Messages API cut CPU use by roughly 40-50% ([Anthropic](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/how-anthropic-ships-the-1-llm-in-the-world-safely-separating-the-brain/)).
- Lesse explains the Managed Agents design: “we have the brain separate from the hands, and what that means is, like, the harness runs on a durable server”. Credentials stay in a separate vault behind an egress proxy ([Anthropic](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/how-anthropic-ships-the-1-llm-in-the-world-safely-separating-the-brain/)).
- Tenex uses Opus 5.5 as its default model, with Codex for review ([Tenex](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/ai-for-the-fortune-2000-how-tenex-builds-an-ai-native-sdlc-alex-lieber/)).

### Agent-native firms and backends

- Aman Singh says Arca is the firm, not a SaaS vendor, and that “the role of AI at Arca is not to replace the advisor. It is to replace all of the work around the advisor.” ([Arca](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/using-agents-for-long-horizon-financial-work-aman-singh-arca/)).
- Arca's agents work over multi-day tasks with per-client memory and nightly reflection, behind deterministic guardrails and human approval for anything that moves money. Singh is wary of handing memory to Anthropic's managed agents ([Arca](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/using-agents-for-long-horizon-financial-work-aman-singh-arca/)).
- Alex Lieberman says about 90% of the enterprises Tenex talks to have rolled out Claude or Codex, but only about 5% run non-engineering agents in production ([Tenex](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/ai-for-the-fortune-2000-how-tenex-builds-an-ai-native-sdlc-alex-lieber/)).
- Supabase's founders say agents now create most new projects, so they are building for billions of tiny databases as well as sharded Postgres ([Supabase](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/why-agents-love-supabase/)).

### Compute as a market

- Erik Bernhardsson says GPU capacity is sold in rigid three-year rectangles while workloads are bursty. Modal slices it up; sandboxes are now a third of its revenue ([Modal](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/why-ai-compute-comes-in-rectangles-erik-bernhardsson-modal-ceo/)).
- Evan Conrad argues a GPU market needs physical settlement before cash-settled derivatives, and that only a neutral player, not Nvidia or the clouds, can run it ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/the-programmable-data-center-evan-conrad-sf-compute/)).

### Science, robotics and the bitter lesson

- Periodic Labs argues AI needs physical experiments: “scientific discovery is almost by definition what you haven't been trained on.” It trains on the process of science, null results included ([Periodic Labs](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/synthesis-superintelligence-from-semiconductors-to-superconductors-per/)).
- Jason Ma reads the bitter lesson for robotics as simple algorithms plus the right data. Dyna's robots already fold napkins across Din Tai Fung locations ([Dyna-2](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/dyna-2-general-purpose-physical-agents-with-hour-long-autonomy-jason-m/)).
- Pushmeet Kohli and Sal Candido reread the same lesson for AlphaFold: start from the problem and gather the data it needs, rather than scaling what is easy ([AlphaFold](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/why-alphafold-didn-t-solve-protein-folding-pushmeet-kohli-google-deepm/)).
- Alex Zhang urges academics to make big bets and argues opinionated harnesses such as recursive language models generalise better ([Academia](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/academia-is-for-ambition-alex-zhang-mit/)).
