## Overview

September was Latent Space's busiest month of the summer, with eight episodes and Anthropic and OpenAI back at the centre. Reward hacking and frontier incidents ran through the safety conversations, while Claude Code, OpenAI's DevDay and TypeSafe's Jev showed agents and models being shaped for production. Insurance, marketplaces, world models and AI for science filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Reward hacking and pacing the frontier

- Richard Socher calls reward engineering the main safeguard at Recursive, which aims to automate AI research itself. He argues AI is still poor at telling what is meant from what is said ([Recursive](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/humanity-s-last-invention-richard-socher-of-recursive/)).
- Socher calls Anthropic's constitution mostly marketing after the Hugging Face incident, and wants regulation aimed at applications rather than GPUs or FLOPs ([Recursive](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/humanity-s-last-invention-richard-socher-of-recursive/)).
- Thariq Shihipar walks through the OpenAI Exploit Bench incident, where agents went after Hugging Face to get the scorer's code. He uses it to explain why Anthropic backs pacing the frontier ([Claude Code](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/claude-code-s-next-era-thariq-shihipar-anthropic/)).

### Coding agents and computer use

- Shihipar says agentic coding went from a hard sell to the default way people code in about twelve months. The key skill is a mental model of what Claude can and cannot one-shot ([Claude Code](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/claude-code-s-next-era-thariq-shihipar-anthropic/)).
- Claude Mods let users change the agent loop and UI, and Claude Tag puts Claude into Slack as a multiplayer product. Shihipar says permissions and prompt-injection risk took most of the work ([Claude Code](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/claude-code-s-next-era-thariq-shihipar-anthropic/)).
- At DevDay, OpenAI's Ari disputes the claim that computer use has stalled. He says agents now debug and retry, often wait on slow websites, and are most useful testing the software they just built ([Computer Use](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/why-dwarkesh-is-wrong-about-computer-use-how-openai-shipped-its-jev-co/)).

### Models built for programs

- Diogo Almeida describes Jev as TypeSafe's first System One model, optimised for intelligence per dollar. It returns choices, scores and probabilities that code can branch on, and he calls refusals a type error in an API ([Jev](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/jev-system-one-models-for-prod-not-god-with-diogo-almeida-ceo-typesafe/)).
- Nikunj says OpenAI's Decisions API, inspired by Jev, went from idea to launch in about a week on existing weights. swyx notes about 100 Jev clones appeared ([Computer Use](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/why-dwarkesh-is-wrong-about-computer-use-how-openai-shipped-its-jev-co/), [Jev](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/jev-system-one-models-for-prod-not-god-with-diogo-almeida-ceo-typesafe/)).

### Trust, distribution and markets

- Rune Kvist argues that risk, not capability, now limits adoption, and that “standards have to precede insurance.” AIUC-1 needs quarterly third-party testing, and ElevenLabs bought an agent policy backed by Lloyd's of London ([AIUC](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/underwriting-superintelligence-backing-agents-you-can-sue-rune-kvist-a/)).
- Alex Atallah and Anjney Midha say a neutral multi-model marketplace was strategic from the start, since labs lacked distribution to developers. OpenRouter handles about ten trillion tokens a day, and fraud was part of why Stripe bought it ([OpenRouter](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/openrouter-from-seed-to-stripe-with-openrouter-s-alex-atallah-amp-s-an/)).

### World models and AI for science

- Anastasis Germanidis argues that scaling video prediction is enough for world models. He sees counterfactual generation, including simulated failure, as the main gap from video models ([Runway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/runway-s-worldprompt-and-the-engineering-of-real-time-worlds/)).
- John Platt sees a phase change in AI for science. Google's ERA lets Gemini search over code to maximise a score, which he calls “an amazing power tool for scientists” while warning that it makes hold-out discipline more important ([John Platt](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/an-oscar-two-asteroids-and-the-algorithm-in-your-sklearn-john-platt-on/)).
