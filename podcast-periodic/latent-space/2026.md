## Overview

Latent Space's 2026 so far is about coding agents breaking out of the editor. Agents got their own computers, harnesses became the main engineering surface, and the tools spread into finance, law and sales. Evals came under strain and training data moved to experts. AI for science and world models ran alongside.

## Main themes

### Agents get their own computers

- Cursor engineers describe cloud agents that test their work and return demo videos ([Cursor's Third Era](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/cursor-s-third-era-cloud-agents/)); Felix Rieseberg argues for a local VM next to the user's files instead ([Claude Cowork](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/why-anthropic-thinks-ai-should-have-its-own-computer-felix-rieseberg-o/)).
- Railway, Daytona and Modal rebuilt their infrastructure for agents as users ([Railway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/railway-the-agent-native-cloud-jake-cooper/), [Daytona](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/giving-agents-computers-ivan-burazin-daytona/), [Modal](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/07/why-ai-infrastructure-must-evolve-for-agent-experience-akshat-bubna-mo/)).
- Anthropic's platform leads say keeping the harness apart from its sandbox makes agents secure by default ([Anthropic platform](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/how-anthropic-ships-the-1-llm-in-the-world-safely-separating-the-brain/)).

### Harness engineering and agents beyond code

- Ryan Lopopolo says that when agents write all the code, the engineer's job is encoding taste as text, tools and checks ([Extreme Harness Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/04/extreme-harness-engineering-for-token-billionaires-1m-loc-1b-toks-day/)).
- Aaron Levie says of coding, “The agent didn't really adapt to how we work. We basically adapted to how the agent works.” ([Box](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/every-agent-needs-a-box-aaron-levie-box/)).
- Satya Nadella pitches open harnesses and private evals ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/satya-nadella-no-priors-x-latent-space-crossover-special-at-microsoft/)), and OpenAI folded the Codex harness into ChatGPT Work ([Codex to 10M](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/07/codex-from-0-to-10m-users-building-chatgpt-work-akshay-nathan-openai/)).
- Notion weighs MCP against CLIs ([Notion](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/04/notion-s-token-town-5-rebuilds-100-tools-mcp-vs-clis-and-the-software/)); Linear and Clay rebuild their products around agents ([Linear](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/why-linear-says-issue-tracking-is-dead-cristina-cordova-coo/), [Clay](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/how-clay-builds-gtm-agents-jeff-barg-head-of-ai/)).

### Evals under strain

- LMArena and Artificial Analysis make the case for fresh, independent leaderboards ([LMArena](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/state-of-evals-lmarena-s-1-7b-vision-anastasios-angelopoulos-lmarena/), [Artificial Analysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/artificial-analysis-independent-llm-evals-as-a-service-with-george-cam/)).
- OpenAI declared SWE-Bench Verified saturated and contaminated ([End of SWE-Bench Verified](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/the-end-of-swe-bench-verified-mia-glaese-olivia-watkins-openai-frontie/)); Andon Labs tests models by letting them run businesses ([Andon Labs](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/reality-the-final-eval-lukas-petersson-and-axel-backlund-of-andon-labs/)).
- Handshake AI and G2i describe labs buying expert-built tasks and rubrics instead of cheap labels ([Handshake AI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/what-frontier-labs-buy-a-data-deep-dive-with-handshake-ai-garrett-lord/), [G2i](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/inside-the-ai-training-data-market-gabe-greenberg-g2i/)).

### AI for science and world models

- Andrew White argues agent loops with a shared world model can automate much of the scientific method ([Automating Science](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/automating-science-world-models-scientific-taste-agent-loops-andrew-wh/)); Alex Lupsasca says AI has passed a threshold in theoretical physics ([Vibe Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/doing-vibe-physics-alex-lupsasca-openai/)).
- Radical AI and Periodic Labs bet on self-driving labs, since experiments are the bottleneck for materials ([Radical AI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/the-self-driving-lab-joseph-krause-radical-ai/), [Periodic Labs](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/synthesis-superintelligence-from-semiconductors-to-superconductors-per/)).
- Moonlake, Runway and Dyna Robotics pursue world models for games, video and robots ([Moonlake](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/04/moonlake-causal-world-models-should-be-multimodal-interactive-and-effi/), [Runway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/runway-s-worldprompt-and-the-engineering-of-real-time-worlds/), [Dyna-2](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/dyna-2-general-purpose-physical-agents-with-hour-long-autonomy-jason-m/)).

### Lab economics, distillation and compute

- Martin Casado and Sarah Wang argue labs could outraise the app ecosystem built on them ([a16z](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/bitter-lessons-in-venture-vs-growth-anthropic-vs-openai-noam-shazeer-w/)).
- Jeff Dean says “you have to have the frontier model in order to then distill it into your, your smaller model.” ([Jeff Dean](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/owning-the-ai-pareto-frontier-jeff-dean/)).
- SF Compute and Modal explain how GPU capacity is priced and resold ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/the-programmable-data-center-evan-conrad-sf-compute/), [Modal](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/why-ai-compute-comes-in-rectangles-erik-bernhardsson-modal-ceo/)).
- Richard Socher argues “whenever we replace some human part of the process of creating AI with a learned system, improvements follow.” ([Recursive](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/humanity-s-last-invention-richard-socher-of-recursive/)).
