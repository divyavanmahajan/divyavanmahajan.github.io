## Overview

July on Latent Space was about how frontier-class models get trained and judged. Meta's Thomas Scialom explained the recipe behind Llama 3 405B, Hugging Face's Clémentine Fourrier made the case for reproducible leaderboards, and Yi Tay described training competitive models at Reka with a very small team. LMSYS Chatbot Arena came up in all three episodes.

## Main themes

### Llama 3: scale the data, lean on RLHF

- Scialom says Llama 3 kept the Llama 2 architecture recipe but trained on 15 trillion tokens instead of two trillion. Llama 2 was used as a classifier to pick good web tokens ([Llama 2, 3 & 4](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/llama-2-3-4-synthetic-data-rlhf-agents-on-the-path-to-open-source-agi/)).
- He describes the "Chinchilla trap": compute-optimal training suits a paper, but models served to billions should be smaller and trained longer. Still, on the 405B flagship he says “The decision is super simple. We want the best model.” ([Llama 2, 3 & 4](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/llama-2-3-4-synthetic-data-rlhf-agents-on-the-path-to-open-source-agi/)).
- Scialom credits post-training gains entirely to RLHF, because humans judge outputs better than they write them. Llama 3 post-training used almost no human-written answers and relied on synthetic data from Llama 2 ([Llama 2, 3 & 4](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/llama-2-3-4-synthetic-data-rlhf-agents-on-the-path-to-open-source-agi/)).
- He sees agents as the Llama 4 focus. On the GAIA benchmark, orchestrated agents reach about 60%, and he wants architectures that spend variable compute per token ([Llama 2, 3 & 4](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/llama-2-3-4-synthetic-data-rlhf-agents-on-the-path-to-open-source-agi/)).

### How to evaluate models

- Fourrier says the Open LLM Leaderboard V1 evaluated about 7,400 mostly community-submitted models. Some startups told the team a high ranking helped them raise funding ([Benchmarks 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/benchmarks-201-why-leaderboards-arenas-llm-as-judge/)).
- She argues “people should stop using LLM as judges” because judges prefer their own model family, first answers and long answers. If one is needed, she suggests a small open model such as Prometheus or JudgeLM for rankings ([Benchmarks 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/benchmarks-201-why-leaderboards-arenas-llm-as-judge/)).
- Fourrier calls arenas a sociological experiment that says more about users than models. People prefer confident wrong answers, and most Chatbot Arena voters appear to be men from the US. Yi Tay, by contrast, sees LMSYS as the most credible public eval ([Benchmarks 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/benchmarks-201-why-leaderboards-arenas-llm-as-judge/), [Yi Tay](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/the-10-000x-yolo-researcher-metagame-with-yi-tay-of-reka/)).
- Scialom says benchmarks stop being useful once teams optimise for them, so Meta mixes reward models, model judges, human evaluation and win rates ([Llama 2, 3 & 4](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/llama-2-3-4-synthetic-data-rlhf-agents-on-the-path-to-open-source-agi/)).

### A small lab against unreliable compute

- Reka has about 20 employees, with five on pre-training, yet Reka Core launched as roughly the fifth-best model. Tay credits the team's ability to de-risk with small runs ([Yi Tay](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/the-10-000x-yolo-researcher-metagame-with-yi-tay-of-reka/)).
- Reka waited months on 500 A100s while H100 deliveries slipped, and nodes often arrived broken. Tay says one bad node kills a whole job, and providers that share the cost of node failures would stand out ([Yi Tay](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/the-10-000x-yolo-researcher-metagame-with-yi-tay-of-reka/)).
- Tay calls the standard transformer recipe (SwiGLU, GQA, RoPE, RMSNorm) very hard to beat. He favours long context over RAG where accuracy matters and is bullish on MoEs ([Yi Tay](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/07/the-10-000x-yolo-researcher-metagame-with-yi-tay-of-reka/)).
