## Overview

January on Latent Space opened with two episodes on how models are measured, from LMArena and Artificial Analysis. The rest of the month moved between research and practice: world models in RL and science, on-policy RL behind Gemini Deep Think, and how Brex runs AI across a regulated business.

## Main themes

### Who measures the models

- Anastasios Angelopoulos says Arena's goal is “to create a benchmark that is constantly fresh, that does not suffer over fitting”, built on organic votes that nobody can pay to join or leave ([LMArena](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/state-of-evals-lmarena-s-1-7b-vision-anastasios-angelopoulos-lmarena/)).
- He rejects the Leaderboard Illusion paper's claims as containing factual errors, and plans occupational and expert categories, Code Arena and video ([LMArena](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/state-of-evals-lmarena-s-1-7b-vision-anastasios-angelopoulos-lmarena/)).
- George Cameron and Micah Hill-Smith run every model themselves because labs prompt their own evals differently. Many repeats give about plus or minus one point at 95% confidence on their Intelligence Index, and a mystery shopper policy checks public endpoints ([Artificial Analysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/artificial-analysis-independent-llm-evals-as-a-service-with-george-cam/)).
- New measures cover hallucination (Omniscience), white-collar agentic work (GDPval-AA, run in their Stirrup harness) and openness, where AI2's Olmo leads ([Artificial Analysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/artificial-analysis-independent-llm-evals-as-a-service-with-george-cam/)).

### Cheaper intelligence, bigger bills

- The Artificial Analysis founders say GPT-4-level intelligence is now over 100 times cheaper than at launch, yet reasoning tokens and long agentic runs push total spend up, the smiling curve ([Artificial Analysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/artificial-analysis-independent-llm-evals-as-a-service-with-george-cam/)).
- A host mentions a startup spending $5,000 per employee on coding agents. The guests say token efficiency and number of turns now matter more than the reasoning label ([Artificial Analysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/artificial-analysis-independent-llm-evals-as-a-service-with-george-cam/)).

### World models and automated science

- Andrew White of Future House and Edison Scientific argues current LLMs can already automate much of the scientific loop. In Cosmos, a world model fed by a data-analysis agent is the glue between agents, and he says there is “a nice analogy, uh, between a Git repo and, and what a world model is” ([Automating Science](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/automating-science-world-models-scientific-taste-agent-loops-andrew-wh/)).
- White sees lab logistics, verification and scientific taste as the bottlenecks, not model intelligence, and expects scientists to become agent wranglers ([Automating Science](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/automating-science-world-models-scientific-taste-agent-loops-andrew-wh/)).
- The Princeton NeurIPS team describes its self-supervised objective as an implicit world model ([1000 Layer Networks](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/neurips-best-paper-1000-layer-networks-for-self-supervised-rl-kevin-wa/)).

### Scaling RL

- Kevin Wang and colleagues trained RL networks up to 1,000 layers deep by swapping reward maximisation for a contrastive objective plus residual connections and layer norm. Benjamin Eysenbach stresses the objective, not just size ([1000 Layer Networks](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/neurips-best-paper-1000-layer-networks-for-self-supervised-rl-kevin-wa/)).
- Yi Tay says on-policy RL is now the main modelling tool. Gemini Deep Think won IMO gold end to end, without formal tools, and he expects one general model to absorb most specialist systems ([Yi Tay](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/captaining-imo-gold-deep-think-on-policy-rl-feeling-the-agi-in-singapo/)).

### AI inside a regulated company

- James Reggio splits Brex's AI work into corporate, operational and product pillars on a shared platform, with prompts and evals managed in Retool ([Brex](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/brex-s-ai-hail-mary-with-cto-james-reggio/)).
- He says simple SOP-driven agents beat cleverer methods: a web research agent outperformed an RL approach to credit decisions. An audit agent flags violations and a review agent decides which become cases ([Brex](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/brex-s-ai-hail-mary-with-cto-james-reggio/)).
- Brex lets employees choose among many AI tools. Reggio says the next problem is slop and fading code understanding, not adoption ([Brex](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/brex-s-ai-hail-mary-with-cto-james-reggio/)).
