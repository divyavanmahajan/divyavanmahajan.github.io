## Overview

February on Latent Space looked at agents from several sides: how their interfaces are designed, how to engineer and evaluate them, and what infrastructure they need to act on the web. Guests came from OpenAI, Google, Sierra, Pydantic, Browserbase and the wearable startup Bee.

## Main themes

### Designing interfaces for agents

- Karina Nguyen defines agents as a progression from one-off actions to collaboration to trusted long-horizon delegation. She sees collaboration as a key milestone because it reveals what users implicitly want ([Karina Nguyen](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-agent-reasoning-interface-o1-o3-claude-3-chatgpt-canvas-tasks-and/)).
- Nguyen describes how Canvas and Tasks were built by post-training models and building the product together. She calls behavioural design “extending like product design into like the model design” ([Karina Nguyen](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-agent-reasoning-interface-o1-o3-claude-3-chatgpt-canvas-tasks-and/)).
- Arush Selvan and Mukund Sridhar explain that Gemini Deep Research shows an editable research plan, browses iteratively and spends minutes on a report. They found users accept the wait when the output is worth it ([Deep Research](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-inventors-of-deep-research/)).

### Evaluating and engineering agents

- The Deep Research team tracks shifts in behaviour metrics such as plan length and step counts as early signals, and built an ontology of research behaviours instead of verticals ([Deep Research](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-inventors-of-deep-research/)).
- Nguyen says the hardest part of a model card is that none of the numbers compare like for like ([Karina Nguyen](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-agent-reasoning-interface-o1-o3-claude-3-chatgpt-canvas-tasks-and/)).
- Samuel Colvin argues most agent frameworks fall short of normal Python standards. He says frameworks and graphs make up “for the fact that right now the models are not that clever” ([Pydantic](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/agent-engineering-with-pydantic-graphs-with-samuel-colvin/)).
- Colvin admits he resisted graphs, but Pydantic AI now uses type-safe graphs that can be checked statically and resumed from any node for long-running workflows ([Pydantic](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/agent-engineering-with-pydantic-graphs-with-samuel-colvin/)).

### Domain agents and AI-native software

- Bret Taylor argues startups should build domain agents priced by outcomes. Sierra charges a pre-negotiated rate each time its agent resolves an issue, and customer experience teams at some customers now call themselves AI architects ([Bret Taylor](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-ai-architect-bret-taylor/)).
- Taylor says that with code becoming nearly free to write, “the fact that we're still writing Python with AI cracks me up”. He wants formal verification and automated review so people need not read every generated line ([Bret Taylor](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-ai-architect-bret-taylor/)).
- As chairman of the OpenAI board, Taylor describes OpenAI as a mission-driven nonprofit he sees primarily as a research lab ([Bret Taylor](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-ai-architect-bret-taylor/)).

### Infrastructure for computer-using agents

- Paul Klein argues cloud browsers are a hard, stateful infrastructure problem. Most Browserbase usage is browser automation rather than scraping ([Browserbase](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/open-operator-serverless-browsers-and-the-future-of-computer-using-age/)).
- Klein sees authentication, not CAPTCHAs, as the main barrier and expects an OAuth-like flow with scoped approval. swyx suggests pairing every human token with an agent token ([Browserbase](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/open-operator-serverless-browsers-and-the-future-of-computer-using-age/)).
- swyx is bearish on computer use, which he finds slow, expensive and imprecise ([Karina Nguyen](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/the-agent-reasoning-interface-o1-o3-claude-3-chatgpt-canvas-tasks-and/)).

### Ambient context from a wearable

- Bee's founders argue personal AI needs continuous real-life context. A phone app would monopolise the microphone, so Bee uses a cheap always-on audio device ([Bee](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/bee-ai-the-wearable-ambient-agent/)).
- Users rejected the pendant, so Bee moved to a bracelet or clip, and vision was dropped for power reasons. Daily shipments exceed 100 ([Bee](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/02/bee-ai-the-wearable-ambient-agent/)).
