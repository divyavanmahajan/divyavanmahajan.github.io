## Overview

August on Latent Space had three episodes with no shared thread, each about a kind of model the frontier labs do not focus on. Baseten explained how open models are served fast without degrading them, Simile argued for models trained on how people actually behave, and Anima Anandkumar made the case for foundation models of the physical world.

## Main themes

### Inference engineering for open models

- Philip Kiely and Ali Taha say supporting each new open model means redoing NVFP4 quantization, training a speculator and fixing bugs found in the wild. Baseten cuts a generation once the model repeats the same token four or more times ([Inference Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/the-inference-engineering-masterclass-philip-kiely-ali-taha-baseten/)).
- Big speedups come from stacking roughly 2x gains from quantization, speculators and disaggregation. Kiely notes that “Most inference optimizations are lossless”, with quantization the main lossy one, which Baseten checks with KL divergence against full precision ([Inference Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/the-inference-engineering-masterclass-philip-kiely-ali-taha-baseten/)).
- Kiely argues inference is far from optimised because published gains are still 20% to 200%. Taha describes GLM 5.2, running in Baseten's coding harness, writing some of the GPU kernels that now serve it ([Inference Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/the-inference-engineering-masterclass-philip-kiely-ali-taha-baseten/)).
- Customers often start on pay-per-token APIs to try open models, then move to dedicated deployments once a use case sticks ([Inference Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/the-inference-engineering-masterclass-philip-kiely-ali-taha-baseten/)).

### Simulating people

- Joon Sung Park says web-trained models learned what people say about themselves, not how they behave. Simile wants “models that are as dumb as I am”, post-trained on interviews, observed behaviour and randomised trials ([Simile](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/simulation-the-new-scaling-law-joon-sung-park-simile-ai/)).
- In the Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People study, digital twins replicated participants' answers 85% as accurately as people replicate their own. Park says frontier models can fall to 20-30% on niche populations ([Simile](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/simulation-the-new-scaling-law-joon-sung-park-simile-ai/)).
- Park reports early signs of a scaling law in simulation and says decision-makers want to learn how to shape the future rather than predict it. His long-term vision is a simulation of eight billion people ([Simile](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/simulation-the-new-scaling-law-joon-sung-park-simile-ai/)).

### Foundation models for physics

- Anandkumar argues that “we have foundation models for language, maybe vision, but not for physics.” Physical data is scarce, high-resolution and multi-scale, so it needs architectures with built-in structure ([Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/we-have-foundation-models-for-language-not-for-physics-anima-anandkuma/)).
- Her neural operators treat inputs and outputs as continuous functions, so they work at any resolution and can combine data with physics constraints. She says Fourier versions keep global connections at quasi-linear cost, while transformers cannot scale to trillion-length 3D-plus-time contexts ([Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/we-have-foundation-models-for-language-not-for-physics-anima-anandkuma/)).
- She also cites plasma modelling with the UK Atomic Energy Agency, carbon sequestration and car aerodynamics, and warns that regulation equating AI with language models would slow AI for science ([Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/we-have-foundation-models-for-language-not-for-physics-anima-anandkuma/)).

### AI weather forecasting

- FourCastNet, trained in 2021, was the first open-source AI weather model. Anandkumar says it was tens of thousands of times faster than traditional models on a consumer-grade GPU, and that ECMWF now publishes its output ([Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/we-have-foundation-models-for-language-not-for-physics-anima-anandkuma/)).
- She says it predicted Hurricane Lee's landfall days earlier than standard models. Fast models allow very large ensembles for risk assessment and give small agencies in the Global South fidelity once limited to big ones ([Physics](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/08/we-have-foundation-models-for-language-not-for-physics-anima-anandkuma/)).
