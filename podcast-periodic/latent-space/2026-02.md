## Overview

February on Latent Space came back to the big labs. Two episodes declared SWE-bench Verified finished, METR explained what its time horizon chart does and does not show, and distillation linked Jeff Dean's view of Gemini with Anthropic's accusations against Chinese labs. Investors and analysts filled in the money side: lab economics, custom chips and a memory shortage.

## Main themes

### Benchmarks break down

- Mia Glaese and Olivia Watkins of OpenAI say SWE-bench Verified is saturated around 80% and contaminated. An auditor agent found “many instances of contamination across, uh, across OpenAI models, across, like, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini Flash” ([End of SWE-Bench Verified](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/the-end-of-swe-bench-verified-mia-glaese-olivia-watkins-openai-frontie/)).
- They recommend SWE-bench Pro, and Glaese wants evals for longer tasks, design taste and maintainable code ([End of SWE-Bench Verified](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/the-end-of-swe-bench-verified-mia-glaese-olivia-watkins-openai-frontie/)).
- In a livestream, swyx says some models could recite tasks from just a task ID, and Nathan Lambert predicts frontier evals will cost tens to hundreds of millions of dollars ([Anthropic Distillation](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/live-anthropic-distillation-how-models-cheat-swe-bench-dead-nathan-lam/)).
- Joel Becker says METR's time horizon measures task difficulty in human hours at 50% reliability, not how long agents run. Opus 4.5 fits the faster four-month doubling line ([METR](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/metr-s-joel-becker-on-exponential-time-horizon-evals-threat-models-and/)).

### Distillation, inside labs and across them

- Jeff Dean says “you have to have the frontier model in order to then distill it into your, your smaller model”, which is how each Gemini Flash matches the previous Pro ([Jeff Dean](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/owning-the-ai-pareto-frontier-jeff-dean/)).
- Lambert argues Chinese labs short of GPUs should obviously distil from US APIs, and reads Anthropic's post on distillation attacks as partly political. Sebastian Raschka notes evaluation runs look almost identical to distillation ([Anthropic Distillation](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/live-anthropic-distillation-how-models-cheat-swe-bench-dead-nathan-lam/)).

### Lab economics and hardware

- Martin Casado warns that a frontier lab raising ever more capital could outspend the whole app ecosystem built on it. He says labs look margin-positive only against the last model's training cost ([a16z](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/bitter-lessons-in-venture-vs-growth-anthropic-vs-openai-noam-shazeer-w/)).
- Casado says a $1 billion training run justifies a custom chip, and the hosts point to OpenAI's Broadcom deal. Dean says TPU features are co-designed about two generations ahead ([a16z](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/bitter-lessons-in-venture-vs-growth-anthropic-vs-openai-noam-shazeer-w/), [Jeff Dean](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/owning-the-ai-pareto-frontier-jeff-dean/)).
- Doug O'Laughlin says Ironwood has its widest TCO lead over Nvidia now, and expects a memory shortage of about two years, with DRAM prices possibly doubling again ([SemiAnalysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/claude-code-for-finance-the-global-memory-shortage-doug-o-laughlin-sem/)).

### Coding agents reach information work

- O'Laughlin says Claude Code on Opus 4.5 now does multi-step analyst work, which he treats as a junior analyst's output. He built a history of memory cycles and calls it “a lifetime of work” done in a day or two ([SemiAnalysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/claude-code-for-finance-the-global-memory-shortage-doug-o-laughlin-sem/)).
- His team found about 4% of public GitHub commits signed by Claude Code, now nearer 5%. He argues Excel, Bloomberg and PowerPoint are human IDEs, leaving Microsoft the most to lose ([SemiAnalysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/claude-code-for-finance-the-global-memory-shortage-doug-o-laughlin-sem/)).

### Interpretability and materials science

- Myra Deng and Mark Bissell present Goodfire as an interpretability lab in production, catching PII in Rakuten's traffic and steering the trillion-parameter Kimi K2 live. They also report scientific discovery work with healthcare partners ([Goodfire](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/the-first-mechanistic-interpretability-frontier-lab-myra-deng-mark-bis/)).
- Max Welling describes CuspAI as a search engine for materials, with chemists in the loop and investment only alongside an industrial partner. He suggests the funding rush means AI for science is in a new bubble ([CuspAI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/searching-the-space-of-all-possible-materials-prof-max-welling-cuspai/)).
