## Overview

November on Latent Space looked at what frontier labs buy and how applied AI companies test what they ship. Founders also described products redesigned for agents as users, while educators and builders asked where humans still belong in the loop.

## Main themes

### Training data becomes expert-built environments

- Garrett Lord and his Handshake AI team say data labelling is the wrong word: experts now build simulated worlds, long-horizon tasks and rubrics, and one unit of data can take days. Pure synthetic generation produces slop, one guest says: “It doesn't work. So you need experts, like, really steering the agents and, like, iterating on it.” ([Handshake AI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/what-frontier-labs-buy-a-data-deep-dive-with-handshake-ai-garrett-lord/)).
- One Handshake speaker puts the market at $1 billion three years ago and $10 billion now, and says labs may use under ten percent of the data they procure ([Handshake AI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/what-frontier-labs-buy-a-data-deep-dive-with-handshake-ai-garrett-lord/)).
- Gabe Greenberg says G2i moved from mid-level gig engineers to staff-level engineers building RL environments. Labs buy fast, without RFPs, and choose on quality rather than price ([G2i](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/inside-the-ai-training-data-market-gabe-greenberg-g2i/)).

### Evals sit at the centre of applied AI

- Jacob Lauritzen says Legora builds legal agents without seeing client data. Three engineers and three lawyers build RL-style worlds and track models in a heat map by practice area; releases often regress some tasks, though one lifted citation accuracy from 70% to 99% ([Legora](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/building-legal-agents-when-you-can-t-see-the-data-jacob-lauritzen-cto/)).
- Handshake's Atlas finance benchmark has 100 tasks in 13 environments, and models score under 20% ([Handshake AI](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/what-frontier-labs-buy-a-data-deep-dive-with-handshake-ai-garrett-lord/)).
- Jeff Barg says Clay's qualification evals have been saturated for about a year, so frontier models matter mostly for orchestration ([Clay](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/how-clay-builds-gtm-agents-jeff-barg-head-of-ai/)).

### Products redesigned for agents

- Cristina Cordova says Linear launched its MCP server in 2025 and that “over 50% of issues created on Linear are created by agents”. She argues being a database behind an MCP server is not a good business ([Linear](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/why-linear-says-issue-tracking-is-dead-cristina-cordova-coo/)).
- Zeno Rocha treats agents as first-class Resend users, with an MCP server passing a million monthly calls and markdown versions of marketing pages ([Resend](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/designing-for-agents-zeno-rocha-resend/)).
- Barg rebuilt Clay's agent as a coding agent driving the Clay CLI, and says use through external harnesses such as Claude Code is close to 50/50 with Clay's own agent ([Clay](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/how-clay-builds-gtm-agents-jeff-barg-head-of-ai/)).

### AI-native engineering and the human loop

- Mihail Eric's Stanford course has students build a coding agent harness and contribute to open-source repos. He says none of his surveyed students type code by hand ([Stanford](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/one-million-ai-engineer-special-how-stanford-teaches-ai-native-enginee/)).
- Thariq Shihipar, moving from Anthropic to OpenAI, says the bottleneck is now how humans work with models, not tokens. He thinks “the harnesses are under-utilizing decision models right now”; the hosts reply that cheap, fast models are usually dumber ([Shihipar](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/the-revolution-vs-the-renaissance-thariq-shihipar-openai/)).

### Visual intelligence and AI for science

- Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell say Black Forest Labs trains one diffusion model on images, video and audio and derives editing, video and robot-action products from it. They cite $450 million raised and revenue past $100 million ([Black Forest Labs](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/building-visual-intelligence-andreas-blattmann-dustin-podell-black-for/)).
- Fabian Theis of Helmholtz Munich discusses AI models for biology at a high level, arguing the commercial moat lies in pairing models with unique data and that Germany loses companies at the scaling stage ([Helmholtz Munich](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/11/controlling-biology-fabian-theis-helmholtz-munich/)).
