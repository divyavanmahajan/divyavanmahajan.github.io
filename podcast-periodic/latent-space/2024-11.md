## Overview

November on Latent Space was mainly about agents at work: how to make them reliable, what tools they need and whether the model is still the bottleneck. Two Agents @ Work episodes and an Anthropic interview on the new Claude 3.5 Sonnet covered that ground. The other two episodes looked at how to evaluate models (Chatbot Arena) and how to serve them (Fireworks AI).

## Main themes

### Simple, constrained agents beat autonomous ones

- Stanislas Polu says Dust starts with simple agents scripted in plain English with a few tools, because “the agentic capability of models are not quite there yet” for Auto-GPT-style autonomy ([Dust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-dust-tt/)).
- Florent Crivello says the first Lindy, a large prompt plus tools, overestimated LLMs. Lindy 2.0 puts agents on rails with fixed triggers and steps, and he says 60 to 70 percent of prompts users type mean nothing, so a GUI works better ([Lindy](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-lindy-ai/)).
- Polu sees connectors and product design as the real constraints, not models. Dust's best customer reaches 88% daily active users, and he puts model choice at about a 5% gain ([Dust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-dust-tt/)).
- Both founders prefer APIs to clicking through interfaces. Polu calls headless browsing broken, and Crivello compares betting on computer use to years of Android lag ([Dust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-dust-tt/), [Lindy](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-lindy-ai/)).

### Claude 3.5 Sonnet and tool design

- Erik Schluntz says the new Sonnet needs little scaffolding: give it tools and let it loop until done. The hosts note 49% on SWE-bench Verified against a previous best of 45% ([Claude 3.5 Sonnet](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/the-new-claude-3-5-sonnet-computer-use-and-building-sota-agents-with-e/)).
- Schluntz says the team “did more engineering of the tools than the overall prompt”. String-replace edits and required absolute paths made the tools hard to misuse ([Claude 3.5 Sonnet](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/the-new-claude-3-5-sonnet-computer-use-and-building-sota-agents-with-e/)).
- Crivello says that since Claude 3.5 Sonnet the model is no longer the bottleneck, and Polu credits Claude models with a chain-of-thought step during function calling ([Lindy](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-lindy-ai/), [Dust](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/agents-work-dust-tt/)).

### Computer use as a way round integrations

- Schluntz sees computer use as cutting integration friction to near zero. At Cobalt Robotics an elevator API took about six months, while a robot arm pressing buttons shipped in a day ([Claude 3.5 Sonnet](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/the-new-claude-3-5-sonnet-computer-use-and-building-sota-agents-with-e/)).
- Anthropic ships computer use in a sandboxed Docker environment by default for security. swyx argues Anthropic shipped Adept as a feature ([Claude 3.5 Sonnet](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/the-new-claude-3-5-sonnet-computer-use-and-building-sota-agents-with-e/)).

### Evaluating models with live human votes

- Wei-Lin Chiang and Anastasios Angelopoulos argue static benchmarks cannot fully measure generative models. Chatbot Arena uses anonymous pairwise votes because absolute ratings are hard for humans ([Arena](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/in-the-arena-how-lmsys-changed-llm-benchmarking-forever/)).
- They mine the votes afterwards with regression to control for style and length. They admit 20-30% of users ask coding questions, and that live benchmarks are slower and costlier than static ones ([Arena](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/in-the-arena-how-lmsys-changed-llm-benchmarking-forever/)).

### Compound AI and open models

- Lin Qiao argues specialised open models, combined with APIs and data stores in a compound AI system, will beat one general model. swyx counters that this bets against the bitter lesson ([Fireworks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/why-compound-ai-open-source-will-beat-closed-ai/)).
- Qiao says Fireworks focused on inference because it scales with users while training scales with researchers. Fire Optimizer tunes deployments across quality, latency and cost ([Fireworks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/11/why-compound-ai-open-source-will-beat-closed-ai/)).
