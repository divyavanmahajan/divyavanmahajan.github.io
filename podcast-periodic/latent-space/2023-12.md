## Overview

December on Latent Space ended the year with two NeurIPS 2023 recaps, one on the best papers and one on startups, where synthetic data and simpler, cheaper methods kept coming up. Earlier in the month, Axolotl's Wing Lian explained open-model fine-tuning, Sourcegraph argued that context beats agents for coding, and Playground's Suhail Doshi made the case for an AI-first graphics editor.

## Main themes

### Simpler, cheaper training for open models

- Wing Lian built Axolotl so fine-tuning is a matter of switches in a YAML file. He says QLoRA was integrated seven days after it was announced, and estimates a Mistral 7B fine-tune at roughly $500 ([Axolotl](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-busy-person-s-intro-to-finetuning-open-source-ai-wing-lian-axolotl/)).
- At NeurIPS, the DPO authors called their method “simple, stable, and computationally cheaper than PPO”, and Tim Dettmers said QLoRA cuts a 70B fine-tune from 840 GB of GPU memory to 46 GB ([Best Papers](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-best-papers/)).
- DataComp found smaller, more aggressively filtered datasets can beat larger ones from the same pool. Chris Ré traced state space models from S4 to Mamba, which Lian also had fine-tuning working ([Best Papers](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-best-papers/), [Axolotl](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-busy-person-s-intro-to-finetuning-open-source-ai-wing-lian-axolotl/)).

### Synthetic data and evaluation

- swyx said synthetic data was the theme in his conversations with literally everybody at NeurIPS. Jonathan Frankle framed it as using human time well, by curating model output rather than writing data from scratch ([Top Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-top-startups/)).
- Lian described using GPT-4 to filter OpenOrca into SlimOrca, with about 30% less data and similar performance. He mostly agrees benchmarks are compromised by contamination ([Axolotl](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-busy-person-s-intro-to-finetuning-open-source-ai-wing-lian-axolotl/)).
- Suhail Doshi calls FID a weak metric and argues for comparing against the best model, Midjourney, even when you lose ([Playground](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-ai-first-graphics-editor-with-suhail-doshi-of-playground-ai/)).
- swyx questioned the best-paper award for the Mirage paper on emergent abilities, which he says only raises methodological disagreements ([Best Papers](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-best-papers/)).

### AI coding: context first, agents later

- Beyang Liu says Cody's “core differentiator is really the quality of the context”. Sourcegraph pairs parsers and code graphs with LLMs, an approach he calls Normsky ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-normsky-architecture-for-ai-coding-agents-with-beyang-liu-steve-ye/)).
- Liu is sceptical of agents built purely on transformers and expects search-based systems with the LLM as a proposal function. He names reliable first-try code generation as the open problem ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-normsky-architecture-for-ai-coding-agents-with-beyang-liu-steve-ye/)).
- Ida Momennejad's CogEval found tested LLMs poor at planning, with only GPT-4 reaching about 40% on harder graphs ([Best Papers](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-best-papers/)).
- Aman Sanger confirmed Cursor's $8 million raise from OpenAI with a team of seven, and argues implementation is the bottleneck for research ([Top Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-top-startups/)).

### Startups competing on inference and product

- Lin Qiao says Fireworks served Mixtral within a day by retrofitting its Llama code to the new weights. Its customers include Sourcegraph's Cody, which runs StarCoder on Fireworks ([Top Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-top-startups/), [Sourcegraph](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-normsky-architecture-for-ai-coding-agents-with-beyang-liu-steve-ye/)).
- Aravind Srinivas argues inference-as-a-service is commoditised and Perplexity is an app company first ([Top Startups](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/neurips-2023-recap-top-startups/)).
- Doshi says image models sit at a GPT-2 stage of utility. Playground trains its own open models and compares prompt boxes to MS-DOS, aiming to reimagine the graphics editor ([Playground](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/12/the-ai-first-graphics-editor-with-suhail-doshi-of-playground-ai/)).
