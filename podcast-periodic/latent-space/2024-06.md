## Overview

June 2024 on Latent Space kept returning to measurement: how to trust benchmarks, how to check model output and how to tell whether a training run is on track. Alongside that, the month built up to the AI Engineer World's Fair with two episodes on what an AI engineer is and how to hire one.

## Main themes

### Benchmarks under scrutiny

- Moritz Hardt's ICLR keynote finds that “diversity comes at the cost of sensitivity. There's no free lunch in multitask benchmarks.” Irrelevant metric changes can reorder most of BigBench-Hard ([ICLR Benchmarks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-benchmarks-reasoning-agents-ft-graham-neub/)).
- OpenAI's process reward model reached 78.2% on MATH against 72.4% for outcome supervision, suggesting verifiers now matter as much as bigger models ([ICLR Benchmarks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-benchmarks-reasoning-agents-ft-graham-neub/)).
- Imbue cleaned 11 benchmarks and gathered about 450,000 human judgments on ambiguity. Josh Albrecht says many benchmarks are saturated once ambiguous questions go ([Training 70B LLMs](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/state-of-the-art-training-70b-llms-on-10-000-h100-clusters/)).
- Jonathan Frankle says his Databricks customers want things like SQL-backed data chat, not abstract reasoning tests ([Training 70B LLMs](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/state-of-the-art-training-70b-llms-on-10-000-h100-clusters/)).

### Agents and grounded systems

- Graham Neubig says models scored under 15% on WebArena against 78% for humans; better agent design, not newer models, lifted results to 25-30% ([ICLR Benchmarks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-benchmarks-reasoning-agents-ft-graham-neub/)).
- Aman Sanger of Cursor is more bearish on autonomous agents and wants the developer to stay in control. He suspects SWE-bench scores are inflated by contamination ([ICLR Benchmarks](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-benchmarks-reasoning-agents-ft-graham-neub/)).
- Mike Conover says the worst failure is a system that is “wrong in a convincing way”. Brightwave splits filings into small reasoning units, uses time-aware retrieval and trains its own entailment checkers ([Brightwave](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/how-ai-is-eating-finance-with-mike-conover-of-brightwave/)).
- Conover prefers state-machine-like systems to open-ended agents and refuses to generate Excel models, since one subtle bug can waste hours ([Brightwave](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/how-ai-is-eating-finance-with-mike-conover-of-brightwave/)).

### What an AI engineer is

- swyx frames the AI engineer as working on zero-to-one problems while ML engineers optimise existing systems, and suggests about four AI engineers per ML engineer in mature teams ([World's Fair Preview](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/high-agency-ai-engineer-world-s-fair-preview/)).
- He says vertical startups such as Harvey, Perplexity and Brightwave grow fastest, and advises buying evals and observability before building ([World's Fair Preview](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/high-agency-ai-engineer-world-s-fair-preview/)).
- James Brady of Elicit looks for conventional engineering skill, curiosity about language models and a fault-first mindset, citing tenfold swings in P90 latency within an hour ([Hire AI Engineers](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/how-to-hire-ai-engineers-with-james-brady-adam-wiggins-of-elicit/)).
- Elicit has no ML-specific interview; its exercises force candidates to handle failures and simulate working together ([Hire AI Engineers](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/how-to-hire-ai-engineers-with-james-brady-adam-wiggins-of-elicit/)).

### Training at cluster scale

- Albrecht describes Imbue's 4,092-GPU H100 cluster with about 12,000 cables, built by an infrastructure team of three to six people ([Training 70B LLMs](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/state-of-the-art-training-70b-llms-on-10-000-h100-clusters/)).
- Imbue root-causes failures and automates health checks; a slow decline in utilisation turned out to be unsynchronised garbage collection ([Training 70B LLMs](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/state-of-the-art-training-70b-llms-on-10-000-h100-clusters/)).
- Its CARBS tuner sets hyperparameters at small scale so the 70B run works first time. Frankle, on six or seven clouds, standardises on Kubernetes and FSDP instead ([Training 70B LLMs](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/06/state-of-the-art-training-70b-llms-on-10-000-h100-clusters/)).
