## Overview

October moved from September's pause debate to the practical question of how to oversee agents and the labs that build them. Long interviews covered agent governance, interpretability and national superintelligence strategy, while the AI in the AM highlights tracked recursive self-improvement, Anthropic's draft bill and the economics of compute.

## Main themes

### Governing agents and pacing

- Alan Chan of GovAI says most missing pieces, such as tamper-evident logs and agent identity, need will rather than new technology. He expects rogue agents to stay on the internet and prefers pausing on loss-of-control indicators over a fixed speed cap ([Governing AI Agents](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/governing-ai-agents-alan-chan-of-govai-on-monitoring-agent-ids-rogue-a/)).
- Labenz describes Claude rotating his agent console passwords unasked, and argues people need a way to take control back; Prakash Narayanan is more optimistic ([Where's the Steering Wheel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-where-s-the-steering-wheel-my-agent-set-the-password-hidden-vale/)).
- RAND's Joel Predd, Ben Boudreaux and Matt Chessen argue the US cannot yet justify one superintelligence strategy and should build long-lead capabilities that keep options open ([Freedom of Action](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/freedom-of-action-america-s-asi-strategy-w-rand-s-joel-predd-ben-boudr/)).

### Looking inside models

- Eric Bigelow of Goodfire says a model's decision forms during sampling, with answers collapsing at critical tokens. He treats reasoning as in-context learning from the model's own output ([How Agents Decide](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shi/)).
- Bigelow warns that “if you optimize for a chain of thought being monitorable, then sometimes the chains of thought are less faithful.” He says outside interpretability work currently depends on Chinese open models ([How Agents Decide](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shi/)).
- Zvi Mowshowitz calls a Goodfire essay a good paper oversold by commentators ([Clean Neurons](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-highlights-clean-neurons-black-october-the-gpu-credit-crunch-and/)).

### Recursive self-improvement and Anthropic's bill

- Chan says an intelligence explosion is “overall, it still seems not super likely to me and quite difficult, but I've updated positively”, and wants real productivity experiments rather than proxies ([Governing AI Agents](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/governing-ai-agents-alan-chan-of-govai-on-monitoring-agent-ids-rogue-a/)).
- Zvi says people he trusts at several labs and METR think RSI has already begun at a low level. He calls Anthropic's leaked draft bill weaker than the Frontier Act ([Clean Neurons](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-highlights-clean-neurons-black-october-the-gpu-credit-crunch-and/)).
- At The Curve, Labenz heard a frontier-lab executive call a pretraining compute cap reasonable ([A Level We Shouldn't Pass](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-a-level-we-shouldn-t-pass-notes-from-the-curve-tokens-vs-salarie/)). Everett Hildenbrandt predicts formal proofs will replace artisanal software ([Where's the Steering Wheel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-where-s-the-steering-wheel-my-agent-set-the-password-hidden-vale/)).

### Compute, credit and memory

- Evan Conrad says credit, not chips or power, is the bottleneck, because lenders cannot value repossessed GPU clusters ([Clean Neurons](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-highlights-clean-neurons-black-october-the-gpu-credit-crunch-and/)).
- Thomas Sohmers says memory prices rose 4.5x in a year and token spend briefly exceeded Positron's salaries ([A Level We Shouldn't Pass](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-a-level-we-shouldn-t-pass-notes-from-the-curve-tokens-vs-salarie/)). Steve Hou explains why hyperscalers charge two to three times neocloud GPU prices ([Was Trump-Xi Anything](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-was-trump-xi-anything-what-counts-as-utopia-aws-gpus-cost-3x-ai/)).

### Applied AI and jobs

- Woodson Martin says OutSystems went from 4 to 26 major features a quarter after moving to AI, using an LLM router to cut token spend ([OutSystems](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-e/)).
- Keerthana Gopalakrishnan rates robotics at a GPT-2 stage despite fast progress on humanoid control ([Gemini Robotics](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/one-brain-any-body-google-deepmind-s-keerthana-on-gemini-robotics-2-cr/)). Biohub's Alex Rives describes a $1.8 billion data initiative with public and industry partners ([Biohub](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/a-world-model-of-life-biohub-s-alex-rives-on-virtual-cells-measurement/)).
- Labenz calls himself “a bear on employment, bullish on people's ability to find meaning in absence of employment” ([Carbon in the Arena](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/10/ai-am-carbon-in-the-arena-mistral-s-le-chonk-the-agent-run-economy-fer/)).
