## Overview

June on The Cognitive Revolution was mostly a debate about pace: how fast AGI might arrive, whether the US is racing China, and what regulation should cover. Nathan Labenz set out his "adoption accelerationist, hyperscaling pauser" position several times. Around that debate sat practical episodes on cheap Google models, private search, circuit board layout and AI for chemistry simulation.

## Main themes

### What to regulate, and how

- At the Hill & Valley Forum, Andrew Ng argues for rules on applications such as self-driving cars and medical devices rather than on foundation models. Jack Clark says outside national security “we can mostly rely on existing regulations and law and existing testing procedures that exist like the FDA or the FAA” ([Global Balance of Power](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/implications-of-ai-on-the-global-balance-of-power-w-alex-wang-andrew-n/)).
- Alex Wang's biggest fear is that defence and intelligence agencies use AI too little, and he warns against underestimating China ([Global Balance of Power](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/implications-of-ai-on-the-global-balance-of-power-w-alex-wang-andrew-n/)).
- Martin Casado says AI cannot be told apart from ordinary software and rejects the 10^26 compute thresholds behind SB 1047. He and Labenz agree that calling-agent providers should refuse clearly criminal requests ([Martin Casado](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/ai-safety-regulations-prudent-or-paranoid-with-a16z-s-martin-casado/)).

### AGI timelines, race dynamics and a pause

- Reacting to Leopold Aschenbrenner's essay, Flo Crivello expects AGI in about five years and a fast takeoff. He says “I think we don't need China, though, to be in an AI arms race.” ([Flo Crivello](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/feeling-the-agi-with-flo-crivello/)).
- Crivello worries most about misuse, especially cyber attacks, and wants an end to open-weight frontier releases. Labenz would rather try cooperating with China than race ([Flo Crivello](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/feeling-the-agi-with-flo-crivello/)).
- In a cross-post, Labenz puts p(doom) at 10 to 90 percent, gives eight reasons for hope, and says one more scaling step is probably fine before a pause ([Cautious AI Optimism](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/the-case-for-cautious-ai-optimism-from-the-consistently-candid-podcast/)).
- Labenz calls scaling laws trends that could break ([Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/ai-scouting-report-not-investment-advice-edition/)).

### Cheap capable models change what is practical

- Logan Kilpatrick compares the original GPT-4 at $30 per million input tokens with Gemini 1.5 Flash at 35 cents, and says Flash's bounding boxes make vision use cases practical ([Google AI Studio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/inside-google-s-ai-studio-with-logan-kilpatrick/)).
- Labenz fed about 250 emails into Flash for under 20 cents. Kilpatrick expects many competitors rather than one winner ([Google AI Studio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/inside-google-s-ai-studio-with-logan-kilpatrick/)).
- Google's Vivek Natarajan and Khaled Saab argue medical AI gains come from curated data and evaluation more than bigger models, with clinical evidence and public trust setting the pace of deployment ([AI Doctor](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/the-ai-doctor-can-see-you-now-with-vivek-natarajan-and-khaled-saab-fro/)).

### Building products in layers or end to end

- Eric Vishria backed Quilter because circuit board layout should be “AI and done. AI should just do it. Do it entirely.” Sergiy Nesterenko trains with reinforcement learning against physics simulation ([Hard Tech](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/investing-in-ai-for-hard-tech-with-eric-vishria-of-benchmark-and-sergi/)).
- Brave's Josep M. Pujol says search is built by adding techniques one layer at a time. Its summariser moved from Flan-T5 to Mixtral to Llama 3 in a week ([Brave](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/building-brave-private-search-one-ai-layer-at-a-time-with-josep-m-pujo/)).
- Tim Duignan uses small neural networks trained on quantum simulation data to model electrolytes about 1000 times faster, and wants public projects rather than big-tech dominance ([Fluid Intelligence](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/06/fluid-intelligence-simulating-solutions-with-tim-duignan/)).
