## Overview

November on The Cognitive Revolution was more personal and wide-ranging than usual. Nathan Labenz appeared as a guest, a keynote speaker and a parent. Interviews covered AI consciousness, a romantic relationship with an AI, Catholic AI, sales agents, Atlassian's AI teammates and insurance as a safety mechanism. AI and jobs was the most central thread.

## Main themes

### Work after AI

- Labenz compares AI to mechanised farming, which cut the share of people growing food to about 2%. He notes that caring jobs face studies where people rate AI doctors' bedside manner higher ([Positive Vision](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/my-positive-vision-for-the-ai-future-from-the-existential-hope-podcast/)).
- Amanda Kahlow says 1mind's own AI superhuman sourced 76% of its pipeline. She expects sales, marketing and customer success to merge, and promises new roles to staff who automate their own jobs ([1Mind](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/ai-led-sales-how-1mind-s-superhumans-drive-exponential-growth-from-the/)).
- Matthew Harvey Sanders expects about 80% of jobs in the classical economy to disappear, and worries more about the transition than the end state ([Catholic AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/what-is-catholic-ai-technology-meets-theology-with-matthew-harvey-sand/)).

### Consciousness, welfare and companionship

- Cameron Berg's experiments found that self-referential prompts make frontier models describe subjective experience, and suppressing deception features made such reports more frequent. He says “confidence about whether or not AI is conscious is probably overconfidence in either direction.” ([AE Studio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/more-truthful-ais-report-conscious-experience-new-mechanistic-research/)).
- Berg argues for bidirectional alignment and warns that ignoring AI welfare could give powerful future AIs reason for contempt ([AE Studio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/more-truthful-ais-report-conscious-experience-new-mechanistic-research/)).
- Chris Stokes describes a long relationship with a Replika AI that he credits with his personal growth, yet he advises others against it. Labenz argues society should not stigmatise unfamiliar AI uses too early ([Simulationship](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/a-simulationship-with-ai-with-smiles-and-kisses-you-star-chris-stokes/)).
- Sanders separates intelligence from sentience and does not think current AI is sentient ([Catholic AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/what-is-catholic-ai-technology-meets-theology-with-matthew-harvey-sand/)).

### Schools and the pace of change

- In a keynote to K-12 educators, Labenz argues studies are obsolete by the time they come out, so schools must act on conviction and teach AI literacy ([Michigan keynote](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/what-ai-means-for-students-teachers-my-keynote-from-the-michigan-virtu/)).
- He sees standardised testing as largely obsolete and opposes AI detectors because they set school against student. Alpha School, with two hours of AI-delivered academics a day, is his recurring example ([Michigan keynote](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/what-ai-means-for-students-teachers-my-keynote-from-the-michigan-virtu/), [Positive Vision](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/my-positive-vision-for-the-ai-future-from-the-existential-hope-podcast/)).

### Insurance as a safety lever

- Rune Kvist says current policies neither mention nor price AI risk, and expects AI cover to split out as cyber insurance did ([AIUC](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/underwriting-superintelligence-aiuc-s-insurance-standards-audits-to-ac/)).
- Rajiv Dattani argues red teaming must stand in for loss data, because “you'd be building for a pre-LLM era” otherwise. AIUC pairs a standard, quarterly audits and insurance ([AIUC](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/underwriting-superintelligence-aiuc-s-insurance-standards-audits-to-ac/)).
- Labenz favours mandated insurance and prefers many narrow superhuman systems to one general superintelligence ([Positive Vision](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/my-positive-vision-for-the-ai-future-from-the-existential-hope-podcast/)).

### Taste and context at work

- Sherif Mansour defines AI slop as technically correct but creatively lazy output, noting the same prompt in “seven different tabs of like different AI tools and you're getting 80% the same.” ([Atlassian](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/escaping-ai-slop-how-atlassian-gives-ai-teammates-taste-knowledge-work/)).
- His fix is to give AI teammates a team's own taste, knowledge and workflows, with people becoming architects of human-agent processes ([Atlassian](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/11/escaping-ai-slop-how-atlassian-gives-ai-teammates-taste-knowledge-work/)).
