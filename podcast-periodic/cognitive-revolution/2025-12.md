## Overview

December on The Cognitive Revolution leaned towards policy. A debate on banning superintelligence and the second part of the year-end live show both featured Dean Ball and both turned on how AI should be regulated. Around that sat a careful look at AI's energy and water use, two episodes on model builders outside the US, and two on security and alignment.

## Main themes

### Regulating AI, and the politics around it

- Max Tegmark wants pre-approval safety standards, with tiered AI safety levels much like drug classes. Dean Ball counters that a ban cannot be defined sensibly in law and that “when there are demonstrated harms, the first thing we do is we deal with that through the liability system” ([Ban or Not to Ban](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/superintelligence-to-ban-or-not-to-ban-max-tegmark-dean-ball-join-liro/)).
- Liron Shapira concludes the split comes from risk estimates: Tegmark puts loss of control above 90% without regulation, Ball says 0.01%. Ball concedes liability fails for tail risks ([Ban or Not to Ban](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/superintelligence-to-ban-or-not-to-ban-max-tegmark-dean-ball-join-liro/)).
- In the live show, Ball describes public sentiment as negative but low salience, and sees an emerging anti-AI camp beside the safety and pro-AI camps ([Live Show Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-2025-2026-live-show-part-2/)).
- Assemblymember Alex Boris says New York's RAISE Act targets only extreme harms. Peter Wildeford expects data centers to become an easy target for populist anger before the midterms ([Live Show Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-2025-2026-live-show-part-2/)).

### Energy and water, sorted by scale

- Andy Masley puts a median ChatGPT prompt at 0.3 to 0.6 watt-hours, about a second of a microwave. His top local worry is air pollution from on-site gas turbines ([Energy & Water](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-s-energy-water-demands-sorting-fact-from-fiction-with-andy-masley/)).
- He says electricity, not water, is the real data center challenge, noting that “total American electricity use is rising for the first time since the financial crisis” ([Energy & Water](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-s-energy-water-demands-sorting-fact-from-fiction-with-andy-masley/)).

### Model builders outside the US

- Zixuan Li describes Zhipu AI (Z.ai) as an upstart chasing US labs, with a core training team of 100 to 200 people. It open-sources GLM models within hours of training, and Li argues open weights are needed for global reach ([Z.ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/china-s-ai-upstarts-how-z-ai-builds-benchmarks-ships-in-hours-from-chi/)).
- Marek Kozlowski says Poland's PLLuM adapts Llama and Mistral base models to Polish, matching much larger frontier models on local tasks at lower cost ([Sovereign AI in Poland](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/sovereign-ai-in-poland-language-adaptation-local-control-cost-advantag/)).
- US–China competition ran through three episodes. Wildeford proposes renting compute to China rather than selling chips, so access can be cut off ([Live Show Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-2025-2026-live-show-part-2/)).

### Security proofs and the alignment question

- Kathleen Fisher says AI helps attackers at every skill level. Byron Cook expects generative AI plus formal verification to make provably safer code cheap, with each incident improving future systems ([Security Update](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/the-great-security-update-ai-formal-methods-with-kathleen-fisher-of-ra/)).
- Emmett Shear argues alignment-as-control is a dead end whether AI is a tool or a being. He says “I get lower predictive loss when I treat ChatGPT or Claude as a being”, while Séb Krier defends the tool view ([Tools or Creatures](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/controlling-tools-or-aligning-creatures-emmett-shear-softmax-s-b-krier/)).

### Taking stock of 2025

- Zvi Mowshowitz argues AI is nearly past normal technology for coding and that augmentation precedes automation ([Live Show Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-2025-2026-live-show-part-1/)).
- Guests named the open gaps: sample-efficient learning (ARC Prize), layered memory and judgment on messy real-world tasks. Eugenia Kuyda wants companion apps measured by human flourishing rather than engagement ([Live Show Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/12/ai-2025-2026-live-show-part-1/)).
