## Overview

October on The Cognitive Revolution moved from safety research to deployment. AI and jobs ran through five of seven episodes, led by Intercom's account of its Fin support agent. Other episodes covered how to build agents, whether capabilities are stalling, China's approach to AI, schools, and AI scientists that learn from real experiments.

## Main themes

### AI customer service and jobs

- Fergal Reid says Fin's resolution rate rose from about 35% to about 65%, roughly a point a month. He also says “I haven't met a support team that isn't underwater by like 30% versus the capacity they wished they had.” ([Intercom](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/the-customer-service-revolution-building-fin-with-eoghan-mccabe-fergal/)).
- Fin mostly brings teams to parity rather than replacing staff. Outsourced BPO contracts are the exception, and Intercom's own support headcount stayed flat against a projected 25% rise ([Intercom](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/the-customer-service-revolution-building-fin-with-eoghan-mccabe-fergal/)).
- Eoghan McCabe says a human-handled resolution cost Intercom $26 all in, against a $0.99 Fin price, and he does not see big layoffs coming ([Intercom](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/the-customer-service-revolution-building-fin-with-eoghan-mccabe-fergal/)).
- Nathan Labenz expects fewer engineers in five years and labour-market effects that are undeniable by 2027 or 2028 ([Is AI Stalling Out?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/is-ai-stalling-out-cutting-through-capabilities-confusion-w-erik-toren/)).

### Building around the model

- Reid says almost all of Fin's improvement came from the retrieval layer around the core models, tested through large production A/B tests rather than evals ([Intercom](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/the-customer-service-revolution-building-fin-with-eoghan-mccabe-fergal/)).
- Andrew Lee takes the opposite architectural bet: one model-driven agent plans the whole task instead of following a workflow. He prefers Anthropic's models for long tool-calling loops ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/always-bet-on-the-models-how-tasklet-puts-the-agency-in-agents-with-ce/)).
- Lee gave each agent its own SQL database after an editable JSON memory broke down, and reports no complaints of agents going rogue ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/always-bet-on-the-models-how-tasklet-puts-the-agency-in-agents-with-ce/)).

### Is progress stalling?

- Labenz argues against Cal Newport's stall claim, citing IMO gold from pure reasoning models and FrontierMath scores rising from about 2% to about 25% in a year ([Is AI Stalling Out?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/is-ai-stalling-out-cutting-through-capabilities-confusion-w-erik-toren/)).
- He blames much of the GPT-5 disappointment on naming and a broken router launch. Citing METR, he says agents handle about two-hour tasks today ([Is AI Stalling Out?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/is-ai-stalling-out-cutting-through-capabilities-confusion-w-erik-toren/)).

### China's pragmatic AI path

- Brian Tse says the AI Plus directive never mentions AGI, and that “the real AI race for China isn't about beating the US to AGI supremacy” ([Concordia AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/chinese-ai-they-re-just-like-us-with-beijing-based-concordia-ai-ceo-br/)).
- He argues export controls breed resentment and have accelerated China's homegrown chips. Public chatbots must pass pre-deployment testing against a national standard ([Concordia AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/chinese-ai-they-re-just-like-us-with-beijing-based-concordia-ai-ceo-br/)).

### Schools, science and governance

- Johan Falk argues schools should urgently teach about AI, but not yet push it on all teachers as a learning tool. On detection he is blunt: “AI detectors, no, they don't work. Dead end.” ([Johan Falk](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/education-in-the-ai-age-a-teacher-rethinks-learning-purpose-w-johan-fa/)).
- Liam Fedus and Ekin Dogus Cubuk say AI scientists need real experiments as their reward signal. Periodic Labs aims for a high-temperature superconductor ([Periodic Labs](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/training-an-ai-scientist-with-feedback-from-reality-w-liam-fedus-ekin/)).
- Illia Polosukhin describes NEAR's move to AI delegates in governance. Labenz disputes his view that AIs have no interests of their own ([NEAR](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/a-positive-vision-for-the-future-part-2-with-illia-polosukhin-of-near/)).
