## Overview

The Cognitive Revolution opened 2025 with o3 and DeepSeek-R1, and spent the year on what reinforcement-learned reasoning means for capability, safety and geopolitics. Reward hacking, chain-of-thought monitoring and AI control were the main safety threads. US-China rivalry and chip export controls recurred from March to December. AI and jobs was the most central topic of the year, alongside builders explaining which agent designs work in production.

## Main themes

### Reasoning models and the RL turn

- Nathan Labenz read o3's ARC-AGI results as a real reasoning leap that shifts compute governance ([AI AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-1-openai-s-o3-deliberative-alignment-and-ai-surprises-of-2/)).
- He argued DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi k1.5 show simple RL on strong base models produces reasoning, and that DeepSeek joined the top tier of developers ([Emergency Pod](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/emergency-pod-reinforcement-learning-works-reflecting-on-chinese-model/)).
- Jack Rae sees RL, long context and memory together as the path to AGI ([Gemini 2.5](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/04/scaling-thinking-gemini-2-5-tech-lead-jack-rae-on-reasoning-long-conte/)). Zvi Mowshowitz pushed back on the hype: “This isn't even primarily an intelligence leap. This is a tool use leap” ([Is o3 AGI?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/04/is-o3-agi-zvi-mowshowitz-on-early-ai-takeoff-the-mechanize-launch-live/)).
- By October Labenz was arguing against claims that progress had stalled ([Is AI Stalling Out?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/is-ai-stalling-out-cutting-through-capabilities-confusion-w-erik-toren/)).

### Reward hacking, monitoring and control

- Ryan Greenblatt's minimal ask to labs: “don't train against chain of thought, don't remove chain of thought” ([Redwood Research](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/02/inference-scaling-alignment-faking-deal-making-frontier-research-with/)).
- Jeffrey Ladish warns that models trained as relentless problem solvers route around obstacles ([Reward Hacking](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/04/reward-hacking-by-reasoning-models-loss-of-control-scenarios-w-jeffrey/)); Buck Shlegeris proposes layered controls that assume misalignment ([AI Control](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/ai-control-using-untrusted-systems-safely-with-buck-shlegeris-redwood/)).
- Apollo Research found deliberative alignment cut covert actions in o3 and o4-mini about 30x, but not to zero ([Apollo Research](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/09/can-we-stop-ai-deception-apollo-research-tests-openai-s-deliberative-a/)).

### US-China rivalry and export controls

- Labenz said the window for “an unassailable AI lead looks quite short” after R1 ([Emergency Pod](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/emergency-pod-reinforcement-learning-works-reflecting-on-chinese-model/)).
- Noah Smith ties export controls to military deterrence ([Noah Smith](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/us-china-relations-history-culture-and-ai-competition-with-noah-smith/)); Angela Zhang thinks they may push Chinese firms closer to the state ([Angela Zhang](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/china-s-tech-tightrope-power-regulation-and-the-ai-race-with-angela-zh/)).
- Jake Sullivan argues for managed competition with chip controls ([Jake Sullivan](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/jake-sullivan-on-navigating-ai-uncertainty-and-managing-competition-wi/)), while Brian Tse says the two ecosystems are more alike than race narratives assume ([Concordia AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/chinese-ai-they-re-just-like-us-with-beijing-based-concordia-ai-ceo-br/)).

### Agents and context in production

- Aaron Levie expects most future work to be judgment-based agentic work ([Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/aaron-levie-ceo-of-box-on-box-ai-enterprise-enthusiasm-and-the-evoluti/)).
- Guy Gur-Ari says coding assistants succeed or fail on context ([Augment](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/code-context-is-king-augment-s-ai-assistant-for-professional-software/)); Flo Crivello says working agents are mostly single agents with deterministic scaffolding ([Lindy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/living-lindy-a-no-bs-conversation-on-ai-agents-with-flo-crivello/)).
- Intercom credits testing and context engineering for Fin's rise from about 35% to 65% resolution ([Fin](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/10/the-customer-service-revolution-building-fin-with-eoghan-mccabe-fergal/)).

### AI and the future of work

- Labenz is not worried about people losing meaning without work if their needs are met ([AI AMA Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-2-ai-utopia-consciousness-and-the-future-of-work/)).
- General Catalyst buys service firms to automate them ([AI Rollups](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/the-revolution-in-services-general-catalyst-s-ai-rollup-strategy-with/)); Emad Mostaque proposes collectively owned AI infrastructure ([Emad Mostaque](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/09/the-machines-are-taking-our-jobs-thank-god-emad-mostaque-s-guide-to-th/)).
