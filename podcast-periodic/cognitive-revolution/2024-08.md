## Overview

August on The Cognitive Revolution asked what is going on inside models and what that means for safety, understanding and moral status. The SB 1047 debate brought regulation and liability back to the centre. Alongside it, Nathan Labenz gave a practical automation playbook, and guests covered AI video, self-driving cars, forecasting bots and self-replicating programs.

## Main themes

### Looking inside models

- Goodfire's Tom McGrath and Dan Balsam say sparse autoencoders have made interpretability an engineering discipline. Balsam argues “for too long, we've started with the prior that these models are not understandable” ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/popular-mechanistic-interpretability-goodfire-lights-the-way-to-ai-saf/)).
- On Robert Wright's show, Labenz points to Golden Gate Claude and feature edits as evidence of real, if alien, understanding. He puts AI risk on par with nuclear and pandemic risk ([Robert Wright](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/understanding-ai-understanding-with-robert-wright-of-nonzero-newslette/)).
- Eric Schwitzgebel says no theory of consciousness can settle whether AI is conscious, and proposes building only systems clearly on one side of the line. Labenz says “I always say please and thank you to my AIs” ([AI Consciousness](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/ai-consciousness-exploring-the-possibility-with-prof-eric-schwitzgebel/)).

### SB 1047, liability and open weights

- Cosponsor Nathan Calvin and critic Dean W. Ball agree the amended bill is now mostly about transparency. Labenz says he would sign it ([SB 1047](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/final-analysis-on-ca-s-ai-bill-sb-1047-with-nathan-calvin-dean-w-ball/)).
- Ball calls model-based legislation a costly break from how software is regulated and objects to Sacramento setting national rules. Calvin argues liability waivers do not protect harmed third parties ([SB 1047](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/final-analysis-on-ca-s-ai-bill-sb-1047-with-nathan-calvin-dean-w-ball/)).
- Labenz says open source adds a new dimension of risk but calls Meta's Llama 405B release an olive branch towards China. Ball notes America is unusual in even entertaining open-source AGI ([Robert Wright](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/understanding-ai-understanding-with-robert-wright-of-nonzero-newslette/), [SB 1047](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/final-analysis-on-ca-s-ai-bill-sb-1047-with-nathan-calvin-dean-w-ball/)).

### Making automation work

- Labenz's playbook: pick task-sized work that needs intelligence, write gold standard examples with reasoning, then prompt, retrieve and fine-tune. He says projects “almost never fail for being too expensive or being too slow.” ([AI Automation](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/ai-automation-making-ai-work-for-you-now-with-gpt-4o-fine-tuning/)).
- Fine-tuning GPT-4o mini costs $3 per million training tokens. Labenz warns a tuned model gets worse at everything else, and each extra nine of accuracy costs about ten times more ([AI Automation](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/ai-automation-making-ai-work-for-you-now-with-gpt-4o-fine-tuning/)).
- Metaculus CEO Deger Turan runs a bot-only forecasting tournament with about $120,000 in prizes. He says usefulness to decision makers, not accuracy, is the bottleneck ([Superforecasting](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/scaling-superforecasting-ai-forecasting-tournaments-road-to-epistemic/)).

### AI video and avatars

- HeyGen's Joshua Xu wants AI to replace the camera for business video, aiming for engagement over realism. Labenz rates its consent checks as the best trust and safety he has tested ([HeyGen](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/ai-avatars-the-future-of-video-with-heygen-ceo-joshua-xu-and-benchmark/)).
- Descript's Andrew Mason edits video like a document and integrates outside models rather than building them. Labenz calls avatars a late uncanny valley ([Descript](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/underlord-descript-s-ai-video-editor-with-ceo-andrew-mason/)).

### Reliability and emergence

- Timothy B. Lee says Waymo leads because it solved driverless edge cases first, and that its slow expansion is about technology and operations, not regulation ([Self-Driving Cars](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/self-driving-cars-timothy-b-lee-answers-all-the-questions-you-were-too/)).
- Google researchers show self-replicating programs arising from random code with no fitness function. Labenz expects self-replicating AI; the guests are more cautious ([Computational Life](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/08/computational-life-how-self-replicators-arise-from-randomness-with-goo/)).
