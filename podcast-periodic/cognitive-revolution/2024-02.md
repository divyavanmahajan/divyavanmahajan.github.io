## Overview

February on The Cognitive Revolution kept returning to one question: how close AI is to human-level capability, and what evidence should count. Robin Hanson, Max Bennett and Michael Levin gave sceptical or biological perspectives, while Zvi Mowshowitz and Azeem Azhar looked at the fast-moving frontier. Consumer episodes on AI companions, a16z's abundance agenda and You.com's search filled out the month.

## Main themes

### How far is AGI, and how should we tell

- Robin Hanson argues every AI paradigm looked like the last one needed, and judges progress by jobs and wages rather than benchmarks. He finds automation moved only steadily across about 900 jobs from 1999 to 2019 ([Hanson](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/is-agi-far-with-robin-hanson-economist-at-george-mason-university/)).
- Labenz pushes back, suggesting “replacing the end with meaningful thresholds along the way”, such as AI understanding plain language ([Hanson](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/is-agi-far-with-robin-hanson-economist-at-george-mason-university/)).
- Max Bennett traces five evolutionary breakthroughs behind human intelligence and sees continual learning without forgetting as a key gap in current AI ([Bennett](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/a-brief-history-of-biological-and-artificial-intelligence-with-max-ben/)).
- Michael Levin argues cognition is a continuum to be tested by experiment, and says he stopped writing a paper on what AI architectures miss because it could create agents with moral worth. Richard Socher accepts a pragmatic automation definition of AGI within a few years ([Levin](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/dr-michael-levin-on-embodied-minds-and-cognitive-agents/), [Socher](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/amazing-answers-richard-socher-on-how-you-com-is-reimagining-search-wi/)).

### AI companions and consumer abundance

- Eugenia Kuyda says Replika's North Star is “the share of conversations that make people feel better”, and that the relationship, not the model, is the product and its moat ([Replika](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/ai-friends-real-relationships-with-eugenia-kuyda-replika-s-founder-ceo/)).
- Kuyda says moving users to a smarter model caused a bigger uproar than content restrictions, so Replika routes between several models and keeps a legacy one ([Replika](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/ai-friends-real-relationships-with-eugenia-kuyda-replika-s-founder-ceo/)).
- Anish Acharya and Justine Moore of a16z argue AI will make creative tools, a back office and companionship cheap and widely available, with AI-native startups capturing most value. Labenz worries companion apps could drift into predatory dynamics ([a16z](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/ai-abundance-a16z-partners-justine-moore-and-anish-acharya-forecast-a/)).
- Yohei Nakajima argues identity is collective and that AI, treated as a tool rather than a self, can help people understand each other ([Nakajima](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/ai-identity-from-east-and-west-with-yohei-nakajima-gp-at-untapped-capi/)).

### Sora, Gemini and application-layer safety

- Zvi Mowshowitz has made Gemini Advanced his default model and sees real uses for Gemini 1.5 Pro's long context. He calls Sora's physics vibey heuristics that “mostly look like physics” ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/openai-sora-google-gemini-and-meta-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)).
- Labenz describes calling apps that place ransom calls and clone a voice from 15-30 seconds of audio. Mowshowitz says the application layer is hard to police because it is far cheaper and open models are available ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/openai-sora-google-gemini-and-meta-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)).

### Incumbents, startups and governance

- Azeem Azhar expects big companies to adopt AI for efficiency while startups create the new products, and sees outsourced call-centre and data-entry roles most at risk ([Azhar](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/looking-up-the-ai-exponential-with-azeem-azhar-of-the-exponential-view/)).
- Citing the FAA and Boeing, Azhar argues self-regulation fails and that governments, regulators and academia are the weak players needing support ([Azhar](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/looking-up-the-ai-exponential-with-azeem-azhar-of-the-exponential-view/)).
- Socher is sceptical of existential-risk arguments and prioritises intentional misuse; Labenz keeps a wide range of uncertainty ([Socher](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/amazing-answers-richard-socher-on-how-you-com-is-reimagining-search-wi/)).

### Search rebuilt around LLMs

- Socher says ChatGPT broke Google's hold on how people expect answers. You.com built its own index for LLMs and offers modes that spend more reasoning time on harder questions ([Socher](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/amazing-answers-richard-socher-on-how-you-com-is-reimagining-search-wi/)).
- He expects chat ads and a fragmented search market, noting Microsoft raised search API prices 5 to 20x ([Socher](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/02/amazing-answers-richard-socher-on-how-you-com-is-reimagining-search-wi/)).
