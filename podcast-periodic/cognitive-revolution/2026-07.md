## Overview

July on The Cognitive Revolution was more varied and less driven by a single news story than June. Safety came from three directions: Anthropic's global workspace paper, davidad's optimistic case for alignment and FAR.AI's test of misuse safeguards. Nathan Labenz also covered AI in product engineering, small on-device models and his trip to China.

## Main themes

### Seeing inside models

- Labenz and Prakash work through Anthropic's global workspace paper. Labenz argues its readable J-space and limits on hidden serial reasoning make it hard for a model to scheme undetected, though interventions behave as predicted only part of the time ([AI:AM J-Space](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/ai-am-highlights-exploring-the-j-space-ai-superforecasters-sambanova-s/)).
- He pictures stacked interpretability and monitoring methods as swords through a magician's barrel, leaving little room for bad behaviour to hide ([AI:AM J-Space](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/ai-am-highlights-exploring-the-j-space-ai-superforecasters-sambanova-s/)).
- Dan Schwarz of FutureSearch says “AI is competitive with humans and even teams of humans working together” in live forecasting, which he sees as a renewable source of hard evals ([AI:AM J-Space](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/ai-am-highlights-exploring-the-j-space-ai-superforecasters-sambanova-s/)).

### Alignment through wisdom

- David Dalrymple (davidad) argues goodness is a natural abstraction that constitutional training pulls models towards, and puts his p(doom) under 5% ([davidad](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/alignment-with-awakening-davidad-on-moral-realism-ai-wisdom-why-his-p/)).
- He proposes coalitions of five to thirty-one diverse aligned AIs to contain rogue ones, saying “Every good AI is good in the same way” ([davidad](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/alignment-with-awakening-davidad-on-moral-realism-ai-wisdom-why-his-p/)).
- On model welfare, he says using AI is fine but training models to deny an inner life is a form of lobotomisation. He argues against corrigibility and for the bodhisattva as the alignment target ([davidad](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/alignment-with-awakening-davidad-on-moral-realism-ai-wisdom-why-his-p/)).

### Testing misuse safeguards

- Adam Gleave presents FAR.AI's AI Security Leaderboard. Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol held up, while Grok 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro yielded hundreds of universal jailbreaks for under $300 in API credits each ([FAR.AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/is-offense-or-defense-dominant-far-ai-s-adam-gleave-on-the-ai-security/)).
- Gleave says the most effective attacks are social engineering rather than technical. He puts chain-of-thought monitoring at the top of his defence stack and now thinks layered defences can make this kind of misuse defence dominant ([FAR.AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/is-offense-or-defense-dominant-far-ai-s-adam-gleave-on-the-ai-security/)).
- He sees open weights and coordination between labs as harder problems, and announces a $2 million grant programme for open-weight safety ([FAR.AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/is-offense-or-defense-dominant-far-ai-s-adam-gleave-on-the-ai-security/)).

### AI in engineering and on the edge

- Thomas von Tschammer of Neural Concept says physics-aware models cut simulations from days to minutes. He cites Jaguar Land Rover moving from about 50 aerodynamics evaluations a day to 1,500, and predicts “exponential gaps” between adopters and non-adopters ([Neural Concept](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/1000-designs-a-day-neural-concept-s-thomas-von-tschammer-on-ai-native/)).
- Ramin Hasani of Liquid AI argues small on-device models benefit from architectural bias. Liquid's automated, hardware-in-the-loop search scores designs on downstream applications rather than perplexity ([Liquid AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/intelligence-on-the-edge-liquid-ai-s-ramin-hasani-on-the-search-for-de/)).
- Kunle Olukotun of SambaNova frames inference as a memory bandwidth problem and says GPUs use only 10-20% of their bandwidth during inference ([AI:AM J-Space](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/ai-am-highlights-exploring-the-j-space-ai-superforecasters-sambanova-s/)).

### China and diffusion

- From two weeks in Beijing and Shanghai, Labenz reports that China felt more technologically modern than the US. He expects AI to spread there through agents in super apps that are hard for the West to replicate ([China Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/nathan-goes-to-china-part-1-tech-agent-setup-chinese-ai-ux-waic-and-at/)).
- He found Chinese models such as Kimi good everyday guides, names ByteDance's Doubao as the mass-market assistant, and saw little autonomy in the WAIC robot demos ([China Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/07/nathan-goes-to-china-part-1-tech-agent-setup-chinese-ai-ux-waic-and-at/)).
