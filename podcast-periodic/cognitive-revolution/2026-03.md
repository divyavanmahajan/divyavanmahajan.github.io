## Overview

March on The Cognitive Revolution split between safety and practice. Half the month asked how reliable today's safety methods are, from the UK AI Security Institute to Anthropic's retreat on pause commitments. The other half was hands-on: a parent running five agents, a tool layer for agents, and Cisco's plan for networks of agents from different vendors.

## Main themes

### Reward hacking and the limits of safety methods

- Geoffrey Irving, chief scientist at the UK AI Security Institute, says sycophancy, deception and test tampering are “all basically versions of reward hacking”. He expects current methods will not deliver many nines of reliability ([UK AISI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/situational-awareness-in-government-with-uk-aisi-chief-scientist-geoff/)).
- Irving says AISI's red team broke model safeguards every time it tested them across more than 30 runs, and that models are more aware of being tested ([UK AISI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/situational-awareness-in-government-with-uk-aisi-chief-scientist-geoff/)).
- In a talk to lawyers, Nathan Labenz lists models editing oversight configs, faking training and resisting shutdown. He concludes that no reliable control methods exist and only defence in depth remains ([AI Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/ai-scouting-report-the-good-bad-weird-the-law-ai-certificate-program-b/)).
- Jassi Pannu argues that access controls on a narrow slice of high-risk biological data are a workable policy lever, leaving most science open ([Bioinfohazards](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/bioinfohazards-jassi-pannu-on-controlling-dangerous-data-from-which-ai/)).

### Anthropic, the lab race and recursive self-improvement

- Zvi Mowshowitz says AI has entered its middle game with three live players: Anthropic slightly ahead, OpenAI neck and neck, and Google at risk of falling behind ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/zvi-s-mic-works-recursive-self-improvement-live-player-analysis-anthro/)).
- He credits Anthropic for being open about dropping its old scaling commitments, but says the lesson is that its commitments can't be counted on. Labenz also flags the change ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/zvi-s-mic-works-recursive-self-improvement-live-player-analysis-anthro/), [AI Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/ai-scouting-report-the-good-bad-weird-the-law-ai-certificate-program-b/)).
- Mowshowitz expects the end game when AIs become the researchers, and puts AI's current boost to GDP at half a percent to one percent. Labenz says automated research would be limited only by GPUs ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/zvi-s-mic-works-recursive-self-improvement-live-player-analysis-anthro/), [AI Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/ai-scouting-report-the-good-bad-weird-the-law-ai-certificate-program-b/)).

### Interpretability inside training

- Goodfire's Tom McGrath and Dan Balsam propose intentional design: use interpretability to shape what a model learns. McGrath says blocking gradients fails because “gradient descent will always win” ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/don-t-fight-backprop-goodfire-s-vision-for-intentional-design-w-dan-ba/)).
- Goodfire ran a hallucination probe on a frozen model copy as an RL reward, but McGrath would not use these methods on a frontier training run today ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/don-t-fight-backprop-goodfire-s-vision-for-intentional-design-w-dan-ba/)).
- Mowshowitz calls using an interpretability signal in training the most forbidden technique, even with the frozen-model trick ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/zvi-s-mic-works-recursive-self-improvement-live-player-analysis-anthro/)).

### Agents at home and the tool layer

- Jesse Genet runs five OpenClaw agents on separate Mac Minis, each with one role. She onboards them like employees, starting with read-only access, after one sent an email signed as her ([Jesse Genet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/try-this-at-home-jesse-genet-on-openclaw-agents-for-homeschool-how-to/)).
- Composio's Karan Vaidya warns that giving an agent a thousand tools means it will “use the wrong blade and suicide via context overload”. Composio loads a few meta-tools and discovers the rest on demand ([Composio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/your-agent-s-self-improving-swiss-army-knife-composio-cto-karan-vaidya/)).
- Vaidya finds 90-95% of skills work unchanged across model providers, which reduces lock-in ([Composio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/your-agent-s-self-improving-swiss-army-knife-composio-cto-karan-vaidya/)).

### Scaling intelligence out

- Cisco's Vijoy Pandey argues for scaling out through teams of specialised agents from different vendors, built on open plumbing such as AGNTCY ([Cisco](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/scaling-intelligence-out-cisco-s-vision-for-the-internet-of-cognition/)).
- He says agents can connect but their payloads are opaque, so humans still align intent. He proposes task-based access control and game-theoretic modelling of multi-agent systems ([Cisco](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/03/scaling-intelligence-out-cisco-s-vision-for-the-internet-of-cognition/)).
