## Overview

March on The Cognitive Revolution leaned heavily on China: two guests examined the US-China relationship, export controls and what DeepSeek really represents. Alongside that, Nathan Labenz and Allan Dafoe looked at where AI capabilities and safety work are heading, and two founders explained how they build working AI products.

## Main themes

### US-China rivalry and export controls

- Noah Smith argues Xi Jinping moved China towards rivalry first, and that the US is mostly reacting. He says “the purpose of export controls is to maintain a military technological edge over China” ([Noah Smith](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/us-china-relations-history-culture-and-ai-competition-with-noah-smith/)).
- Smith calls controls on equipment and people effective but sees the ban on selling Nvidia chips as largely cosmetic. He does not see DeepSeek as the threat ([Noah Smith](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/us-china-relations-history-culture-and-ai-competition-with-noah-smith/)).
- Angela Zhang says China is strong at diffusion rather than breakthroughs: “I don't see DeepSeek as a technology breakthrough in a way”. She expects export controls to align Chinese firms with the state and speed up self-sufficiency ([Angela Zhang](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/china-s-tech-tightrope-power-regulation-and-the-ai-race-with-angela-zh/)).
- Labenz criticises US AI leaders for embracing a race and worries the two countries' tech trees could diverge, making safety warnings harder to share ([Superintelligence](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/historic-ai-developments-the-emerging-shape-of-superintelligence-from/)).

### Safety, governance and the shape of superintelligence

- Labenz says reinforcement learning on strong base models is simple and cheap, and that distributed training weakens compute-based governance ([Superintelligence](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/historic-ai-developments-the-emerging-shape-of-superintelligence-from/)).
- He reads alignment faking and emergent misalignment results as signs the problem is still poorly understood ([Superintelligence](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/historic-ai-developments-the-emerging-shape-of-superintelligence-from/)).
- Allan Dafoe argues competition forces adoption of useful technologies, so safety work should target what the market will not supply. He describes Google DeepMind's staged releases and dangerous-capability evals ([Allan Dafoe](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/agency-over-ai-allan-dafoe-on-technological-determinism-deepmind-s-saf/)).
- Zhang calls China's current AI regulation very lax and supports an IPCC-style body to share evidence on capabilities ([Angela Zhang](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/china-s-tech-tightrope-power-regulation-and-the-ai-race-with-angela-zh/)).

### Context, evals and agent loops in products

- Guy Gur-Ari says enterprise coding help is mainly a context problem. Augment built its own retrievers and vector database, and starts research with 10 to 20 hand-labelled samples ([Augment](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/code-context-is-king-augment-s-ai-assistant-for-professional-software/)).
- Andrew Lee rebuilt Shortwave around Claude calling tools in a loop. He says working agents come down to iteration, “if they actually have a working solution, is iteration” ([Shortwave](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/shortwave-rides-the-tidal-wave-inbox-agents-hyper-growth-hiring-ai-man/)).
- Lee is sceptical of multi-agent handoffs, relies on prompt caching for cost, and keeps light evals with more than 100 golden prompts ([Shortwave](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/shortwave-rides-the-tidal-wave-inbox-agents-hyper-growth-hiring-ai-man/)).

### Voice AI and jobs

- Olivia Moore and Anish Acharya of a16z say voice has crossed the latency and quality bar, with vertical B2B voice agents growing fastest ([a16z on AI Voices](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/a16z-on-ai-voices-call-centers-coaches-and-companions-with-olivia-moor/)).
- Pressed by Labenz on a 90% call-centre headcount cut, Acharya says they have not seen headcount cuts yet ([a16z on AI Voices](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/a16z-on-ai-voices-call-centers-coaches-and-companions-with-olivia-moor/)).
- Gur-Ari thinks Dario Amodei's timeline for models writing most code is too quick, and Augment still hires juniors ([Augment](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/code-context-is-king-augment-s-ai-assistant-for-professional-software/)).

### AI as a research collaborator

- Immunologist Derya Unutmaz treats reasoning models as peers that generate novel hypotheses, and argues mindset matters more than prompt engineering ([Derya Unutmaz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/frontier-models-for-frontier-science-with-prof-derya-unutmaz-immunolog/)).
- Stanford postdoc Siyu He describes purpose-built generative models that can stand in for parts of slow lab work, with results still needing experimental validation ([Siyu He](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/03/unlocking-cells-secrets-diffusion-deconvolution-discovery-with-siyu-he/)).
