## Overview

September on The Cognitive Revolution split between the business of AI apps and the security of the models underneath them. Nathan Labenz and Zvi Mowshowitz talked about fine-tuning costs, underpriced subscriptions and a possible app bundle, while three episodes looked at jailbreaks, firewalls and cryptographic proofs. The creative industries got two episodes: the Hollywood strikes and a short film made from DALL-E 2 stills.

## Main themes

### Fine-tuning, pricing and the app bundle

- Labenz fine-tuned GPT-3.5 on GPT-4's chain-of-thought outputs for Waymark's scripts, cutting cost by roughly 90% and latency to under 10 seconds. He dropped plans to fine-tune Llama 2 because hosting was harder ([Fine-Tuning Update](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/openai-fine-tuning-update-acceleration-debate-and-bundling-ai/)).
- He proposes a cable-style bundle of AI apps. Erik Torenberg doubts rivals would cooperate, and Labenz suggests rival bundles instead, such as OpenAI-Microsoft against Anthropic-Google ([Fine-Tuning Update](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/openai-fine-tuning-update-acceleration-debate-and-bundling-ai/)).
- Mowshowitz likes the bundle idea but doubts agreement on revenue splits. On pricing he says “So at $20 I think it's a joke” given what GPT-4 is worth to him ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/openai-amazon-s-anthropic-investment-and-the-roman-empire-with-zvi-mow/)).

### OpenAI, Anthropic and the frontier race

- Mowshowitz says OpenAI has sat on capability advances before, holding GPT-4 for 8 months, and now faces pressure from Claude 2, Llama 2 and Gemini to add features ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/openai-amazon-s-anthropic-investment-and-the-roman-empire-with-zvi-mow/)).
- He calls Amazon's $4 billion investment in Anthropic a bargain, and calls Anthropic the big puzzle of AI safety: a safety organisation or a capabilities lab ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/openai-amazon-s-anthropic-investment-and-the-roman-empire-with-zvi-mow/)).

### Attacks, defences and verification

- Zico Kolter and Andy Zou show that gradient-guided suffixes found on open models transfer to ChatGPT, Bard and Claude. Kolter notes that a decade of adversarial research has not produced robust models ([Universal Jailbreaks](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/universal-jailbreaks-with-zico-kolter-andy-zou-and-asher-trockman/)).
- Adam Wenchel says “You're really moving from a deterministic world into a probabilistic world” and argues for an external firewall, task-specific benchmarks and deployer liability ([Arthur.ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/the-future-of-ai-security-with-adam-wenchel-ceo-of-arthur-ai/)).
- Daniel Kang argues zero-knowledge proofs can show that a specific model ran without revealing its weights. Cost is the obstacle, at about 10,000 times a normal forward pass ([Daniel Kang](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/cryptography-will-revolutionize-ai-data-privacy-with-daniel-kang/)).

### Hollywood, copyright and AI filmmaking

- Trey Kollmer explains the Writers Guild's AI terms and the copyright suits against most labs. Labenz says generative AI is “both transformative and derivative at the same time” ([Hollywood Strike](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/hollywood-strike-update-and-ai-roundup-with-trey-kollmer/)).
- Labenz argues RLHF pushes models toward a neutral centre, while creative work benefits from polarising reception ([Hollywood Strike](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/hollywood-strike-update-and-ai-roundup-with-trey-kollmer/)).
- Stephen Parker and Josh Rubin made The Frost from DALL-E 2 stills, treating the model almost as an artist they could not fully control. They say character-driven films remain very hard ([AI Filmmaking](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/ai-powered-filmmaking-with-waymark-s-stephen-parker-and-josh-rubin/)).

### Simulated worlds

- Yoko Li, Martin Casado and James Cowling describe AI Town, an open-source town of LLM agents with memory and reflection, built in about two weeks ([AI Town](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/why-a16z-built-a-town-for-ai-people/)).
- Labenz expects AI-populated worlds to spread and worries people may lose themselves in them; Li compares the worry to past fears about TV and novels ([AI Town](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/why-a16z-built-a-town-for-ai-people/)).
