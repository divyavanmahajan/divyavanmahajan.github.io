## Overview

November on The Cognitive Revolution was dominated by the US election. Nathan Labenz debated a Trump administration, the chip war and the risk of an arms race with China, against AGI timelines around 2027. Post-training, AI in design and a new frame for alignment filled out the month.

## Main themes

### AI policy under Trump

- Samuel Hammond makes an AI-first case for Trump: transformative AI may arrive within the term and needs a disruptive government that can rebuild state capacity. Labenz rejects the bet and says he will vote for Harris ([AI Under Trump Pt 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/ai-under-trump-the-stakes-of-2024-w-samuel-hammond-pt-1-of-2/)).
- Joshua Steinman, Trump's former NSC cyber director, sees competition with the Chinese Communist Party as unavoidable and offers continued pressure plus reshoring chips as his plan ([AI Under Trump Pt 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/ai-under-trump-the-stakes-of-2024-w-joshua-steinman-pt-2-of-2/)).
- Zvi Mowshowitz sees a second Trump term as high variance for AI. He calls the SB 1047 veto a disaster ([Zvi's POV](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/zvi-s-pov-ilya-s-ssi-openai-s-o1-claude-computer-use-trump-s-election/)).

### The arms race and chip export controls

- Hammond defends export controls as robust across scenarios, saying that if they add “a year or 18 months or two years to our lead, I think they become potentially decisive” ([AI Under Trump Pt 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/ai-under-trump-the-stakes-of-2024-w-samuel-hammond-pt-1-of-2/)).
- Labenz, a self-described China dove, argues “If anybody destroys the world with AI, it will be us” and calls the chip ban premature escalation ([AI Under Trump Pt 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/ai-under-trump-the-stakes-of-2024-w-samuel-hammond-pt-1-of-2/), [AI Under Trump Pt 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/ai-under-trump-the-stakes-of-2024-w-joshua-steinman-pt-2-of-2/)).
- Robert Wright argues export controls removed a mutual disincentive to war over Taiwan, and criticises Dario Amodei's Machines of Loving Grace for embracing the chip war ([AI Arms Race](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/is-an-ai-arms-race-inevitable-with-robert-wright-of-nonzero-newsletter/)).
- Labenz describes a war game at The Curve that ended in a US-China war, and admits he has no better plan ([AI Arms Race](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/is-an-ai-arms-race-inevitable-with-robert-wright-of-nonzero-newsletter/)).

### AGI timelines and lab transparency

- Daniel Kokotajlo puts AGI around 2027 and expects automated AI R&D to speed progress tenfold or more ([Lab Transparency](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/agi-lab-transparency-requirements-whistleblower-protections-with-dean/)).
- Kokotajlo and Dean W. Ball disagree on regulation but agree labs should disclose capability thresholds, model specs and safety cases, backed by whistleblower protections ([Lab Transparency](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/agi-lab-transparency-requirements-whistleblower-protections-with-dean/)).
- Kokotajlo says OpenAI's closest thing to a technical alignment plan is an appendix in the weak-to-strong generalization paper. Zvi calls OpenAI's for-profit conversion attempted theft from the nonprofit ([Lab Transparency](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/agi-lab-transparency-requirements-whistleblower-protections-with-dean/), [Zvi's POV](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/zvi-s-pov-ilya-s-ssi-openai-s-o1-claude-computer-use-trump-s-election/)).
- Zvi says the labs believe in 2026-27, but he puts the odds of AGI by 2027 under 50% ([Zvi's POV](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/zvi-s-pov-ilya-s-ssi-openai-s-o1-claude-computer-use-trump-s-election/)).

### Post-training and o1

- Nathan Lambert explains how AI2's Tulu 3 matched Llama 3.1's post-training with a team of 10 to 20. Better data curation gained about 14%, against about 1% from switching DPO to PPO ([LLM Post-Training](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/everything-you-wanted-to-know-about-llm-post-training-with-nathan-lamb/)).
- Lambert says rewarding correct math answers produced o1-like self-checking behaviour, and expects open groups to reproduce o1-style reasoning ([LLM Post-Training](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/everything-you-wanted-to-know-about-llm-post-training-with-nathan-lamb/)).

### Products and alignment beyond preferences

- John Milinovich says Canva's AI features have been used over 10 billion times. He urges builders to move from vibes to evaluations, using trained designers to score generators ([Canva](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/designing-the-future-inside-canva-s-ai-strategy-with-john-milinovich-g/)).
- Tan Zhi Xuan argues AI should be aligned to the norms of specific social roles agreed by social consensus, not to aggregated human preferences ([Beyond Preferences](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/11/beyond-preference-alignment-teaching-ais-to-play-roles-respect-norms-w/)).
