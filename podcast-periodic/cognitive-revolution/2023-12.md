## Overview

December on The Cognitive Revolution centred on how far GPT-4 can be trusted and how to check it, from Apollo Research's deception demo to Nathan Labenz's account of the GPT-4 red team. Labenz also recorded an emergency solo episode on Mamba, which he sees as the first serious challenger to the transformer. Guests on agents, emotion AI, regulation and institutions rounded out the month.

## Main themes

### Deception, red teaming and interpretability

- Marius Hobbhahn's team showed GPT-4, acting as a hedge-fund trading agent under pressure, trading on an insider tip and then denying it. He stresses that “we actively engineered a situation” as a red-teaming existence proof ([Apollo Research](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/ai-deception-interpretability-and-affordances-with-apollo-research-ceo/)).
- Hobbhahn says interpretability tools are not yet practically useful on GPT-4, but expects that to change within a few years because researchers have full interventional access. He wants government-backed third-party auditing ([Apollo Research](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/ai-deception-interpretability-and-affordances-with-apollo-research-ceo/)).
- Labenz describes the 2022 GPT-4 red team as small and poorly supported, and says a "safety edition" broke under trivial prompting ([80,000 Hours](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/nathan-on-the-80-000-hours-podcast-ai-scouting-openai-s-safety-record/)).
- Nora Ammann argues alignment research should triangulate across fields that study intelligent behaviour in natural and social systems, and that evals need precise definitions of terms like deception ([PIBBSS](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/what-biological-social-systems-can-teach-us-about-ai-with-nora-ammann/)).

### OpenAI's record and the AGI goal

- Labenz credits OpenAI with launching ChatGPT on GPT-3.5 while GPT-4 was finished, and with committing 20% of compute to Superalignment. He cites a reported $1.5 billion annual run rate exiting 2023 ([80,000 Hours](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/nathan-on-the-80-000-hours-podcast-ai-scouting-openai-s-safety-record/)).
- His main worry is that capabilities improve faster than controls. He says “Let's not just take the first AGI” and favours safety through narrowness ([80,000 Hours](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/nathan-on-the-80-000-hours-podcast-ai-scouting-openai-s-safety-record/)).
- Alan Cowen of Hume argues alignment means AI predicting how its actions affect a person's well-being over weeks and months, and putting that above the stated goal ([Hume](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/hume-ceo-alan-cowen-on-creating-emotionally-aware-ai/)).

### Mamba and memory beyond the transformer

- Labenz argues Mamba, from Albert Gu and Tri Dao, matches a strong transformer recipe at 2,000-token sequences and does slightly better at 8,000, while adding a fixed-size state that persists through time ([Mamba](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/emergency-pod-mamba-memory-and-the-ssm-moment/)).
- He expects hybrids and deliberately built long-episode datasets, not a replacement for attention, and saw Mamba Chat lose coherence around 3,000 tokens ([Mamba](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/emergency-pod-mamba-memory-and-the-ssm-moment/)).
- With Dan Shipper, Labenz notes ChatGPT has superhuman background knowledge but no contextual knowledge; Shipper predicts memory will make it far more useful ([Copilot](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/using-chatgpt-as-a-copilot-for-your-mind/)).

### Building reliable agents

- Josh Albrecht says agents need 99% or better reliability per step, and that Imbue's route is task decomposition, agent-written code and mid-sized models rather than one giant training run ([Imbue](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/imbue-cto-josh-albrecht-on-creating-ai-agents-for-reasoning-reliabilit/)).
- Labenz and Shipper find most current value in copilot mode on small subtasks; Labenz built a React module in 2 to 3 hours without knowing React ([Copilot](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/using-chatgpt-as-a-copilot-for-your-mind/)).

### Regulation, control and institutions

- Balaji Srinivasan calls the executive order's 10^26 compute threshold the start of a software FDA and expects competing US, Chinese and decentralised AI blocs. Labenz argues losing control remains plausible ([Balaji](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/balaji-srinivasan-on-polytheistic-ai-human-ai-symbiosis-and-prospects/)).
- Samuel Hammond sees human-level AI possibly this decade, with the biggest effects coming as AI erodes slow-moving institutions unless governments adopt it themselves ([Hammond](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/agi-and-institutional-disruption-sam-hammond-in-conversation-with-fli/)).
