## Overview

April was a wide month for The Cognitive Revolution, with 12 episodes. The densest cluster looked inside models and inside brains: interpretability research, latent-space interfaces and a run of neurotech conversations. Alongside that, Nathan Labenz asked whether there is enough data to keep scaling, talked to builders about making AI products reliable and cheap, and reviewed reinforcement learning in robotics and circuit board design.

## Main themes

### Looking inside the model

- David Bau argues that trained networks have to be reverse engineered, asking whether there is “a physical reality to that knowledge”. His students show a hidden state carries future tokens, and a few attention heads carry the task in a few-shot prompt ([Opening AI's Black Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/opening-ai-s-black-box-with-prof-david-bau-koyena-pal-and-eric-todd-of/)).
- Bau says the field does not yet know the right level of abstraction for interpretability, and suggests function vectors may be one useful unit ([Opening AI's Black Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/opening-ai-s-black-box-with-prof-david-bau-koyena-pal-and-eric-todd-of/)).
- Linus Lee of Notion wants interfaces that let people edit text in a model's space of meaning. He learned by adding print statements to GPT-2 and tests AI products by whether they amplify human agency ([Linus Lee](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/interfacing-with-ai-with-linus-lee-of-notion/)).

### Neurotech heads for consumers

- Dean W. Ball describes neurotech as sensor fusion and signal processing, and Labenz places brain reading around GPT-1 or GPT-2. Both expect reading the brain to improve much faster than writing to it ([Neurotech Frontier](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/merging-man-and-machine-the-neurotech-frontier-with-dean-w-ball/)).
- Ball's main policy concern is the courts, where lie detection would change what being under oath means ([Neurotech Frontier](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/merging-man-and-machine-the-neurotech-frontier-with-dean-w-ball/)).
- Paul Scotti's MindEye2 reconstructs viewed images from one hour of a person's scans instead of 30 to 40, with compute provided by Stability AI ([Reading Minds](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/reading-minds-from-shared-latent-space/)).
- Prophetic AI pitches a lucid-dreaming headband as a consumer product, reporting $2.4 million in refundable reservations and no medical claims ([Organic Holodeck](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/organic-holodeck-with-prophetic-ai-co-founders-eric-wollberg-and-wesle/)).

### Data, scaling and China

- Nick Gannon estimates GPT-4 used about 10 trillion tokens, so a GPT-5 run would need about 100 trillion. The raw volume exists, but high-quality text may run short before a trillion-dollar run ([Data Everywhere](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/data-data-everywhere-enough-for-agi/)).
- Gannon argues that superhuman systems mean “we have to throw most of the human generated data in the trash” in favour of self-play and synthetic data ([Data Everywhere](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/data-data-everywhere-enough-for-agi/)).
- A Beijing-based guest, L-squared, sees China's AI ecosystem as a near mirror of America's, with loosely enforced rules and chip export controls likely to bite in two to three years ([China's AI Ecosystem](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/inside-china-s-ai-ecosystem-a-view-from-beijing/)).

### Engineering reliable products

- Elicit's founders split research into small, checkable subtasks; splitting summaries into separate claims cut hallucinations by about two thirds ([Elicit](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/unbounded-ai-assisted-research-with-elicit-founders-andreas-stuhlm-lle/)).
- Andrew Lee says Shortwave gave up multi-step planning for heavy retrieval feeding a single GPT-4 call, and loses money on purpose while costs fall ([Shortwave](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/the-ai-email-assistant-i-ve-been-waiting-for-with-andrew-lee-of-shortw/)).
- Fireworks AI's Lin Qiao and Dmytro Ivchenko compete on latency, quality and total cost rather than price. Qiao calls fine-tuning one of their fastest-growing features ([Fireworks AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/ai-inference-good-fast-and-cheap-with-lin-qiao-dmytro-ivchenko-of-fire/)).

### Reinforcement learning in the physical world

- Sergiy Nesterenko frames PCB layout as a game scored on physics and manufacturability, arguing that learning from human boards would cap Quilter near human performance ([Quilter](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/automating-circuit-board-design-using-reinforcement-learning-w-sergiy/)).
- Ted Xiao says foundation models moved robotics from the GPT-2 era towards GPT-3. He and Keerthana Gopalakrishnan still see robot data and safety as bottlenecks ([DeepMind Robotics](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/04/robotics-research-update-with-keerthana-gopalakrishnan-and-ted-xiao-of/)).
