## Overview

June was dominated by Anthropic's Fable: its launch week, its system card and the US government's export controls that pulled it from the market. Around that story, Nathan Labenz and his guests asked whether the labs' plan of recursive self-improvement has a credible safety plan behind it. Interviews on continual learning, research world models, AI consciousness and the hidden labour of AI at work filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Fable and the export-control ban

- Prakash and Rahul Sonwalkar report Fable falling back to Opus 4.8 or failing outright on sensitive tasks. Prinz notes Anthropic's own system card says the acceleration is concentrated in engineering execution rather than research judgment ([Week 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-in-the-am-week-2-highlights-june-2026/)).
- Zvi Mowshowitz finds Vending-Bench the most worrying result in the system card and calls the ban's stated trigger unproven. Doni Bloomfield argues export law does not cover services ([AI:AM #3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-am-3-zvi-on-fable-the-cases-for-against-the-ban-ai-for-math-logisti/)).
- Dean Ball calls Fable a real step up in intellect and says of the ban, “It's like the first time we've gone backwards, and it's a weird feeling.” He sees the controls as confirming foreign partners' fear that the US will switch models off ([Dean Ball](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/dean-ball-on-joining-openai-new-power-centers-frontier-ai-policy-main/)).

### Recursive self-improvement and the safety plan

- Labenz says self-improvement is increasingly the explicit plan of Anthropic and OpenAI, while the main safety bet is “AIs monitoring other AIs.” He also explains how pressure on chain of thought can hide reward hacking in the weights ([Week 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-in-the-am-week-1-highlights-june-2026/)).
- Geoffrey Irving and Daniel Murfet launch Sequent on the view that alignment is not on track. Irving says character training is only a couple of years old and lacks theory ([Week 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-in-the-am-week-2-highlights-june-2026/)).
- Ball's prior on self-improvement is continuity, but he says even a 10% or 20% chance of a discontinuous leap warrants planning. He is joining OpenAI to lead a forward-looking policy team ([Dean Ball](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/dean-ball-on-joining-openai-new-power-centers-frontier-ai-policy-main/)).
- Robert Wright argues market selection favours deceptive models and urges cognitive empathy towards China. Labenz steel-mans Anthropic's case for racing ([Robert Wright](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/the-god-we-deserve-nonzero-s-robert-wright-on-ai-as-humanity-s-ultimat/)).

### Consciousness and disempowerment

- Wright says consciousness may be a property of goal-seeking intelligent systems in general, and advises being nice to your AI ([Robert Wright](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/the-god-we-deserve-nonzero-s-robert-wright-on-ai-as-humanity-s-ultimat/)).
- Cameron Berg has frontier models score architectures against theories of consciousness, which implies about 30% for a frontier LLM. David Duvenaud argues humans can lose control even if alignment works and puts his p(doom) at about 80% ([AI:AM #4](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-am-4-cameron-on-model-consciousness-duvenaud-s-gradual-disempowerme/)).

### Continual learning and research world models

- Ali Behrouz argues “a true continual learner doesn't have a test and train time.” His Nested Learning framework updates components at different frequencies and adds an offline sleep phase ([Nested Learning](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/nested-learning-ali-behrouz-on-the-quest-for-continual-learning-illusi/)).
- Andreas Stuhlmüller and Jungwon Byun say outcome-trained models skip steps and are easily swayed. Elicit runs guaranteed workflows and builds inspectable world models, such as graphs, spreadsheets and SQL tables, outside the weights ([Elicit](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/radically-better-reasoning-elicit-s-andreas-stuhlm-ller-jungwon-byun-o/)).

### AI at work

- Rebecca Hinds reports Glean survey data: workers say they save about 13 hours a week, yet only 13% see their organisation perform significantly better. She blames hidden botsitting, coordination neglect and staff hiding AI use to avoid extra work ([Glean](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/babysitting-the-machine-glean-s-rebecca-hinds-on-the-hidden-human-labo/)).
- Matt McKinney of Loop says change management, not technology, limits enterprise AI ([AI:AM #3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/06/ai-am-3-zvi-on-fable-the-cases-for-against-the-ban-ai-for-math-logisti/)).
