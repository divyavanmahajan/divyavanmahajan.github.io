## Overview

The Cognitive Revolution's first year was shaped by GPT-4. Host Nathan Labenz had red-teamed it before launch, which set up the year's main tension: adopt useful tools fast, but worry about further scaling. Builders of agents and fine-tuned apps sat alongside safety researchers, investors and striking writers. The year ended with the OpenAI board crisis and Mamba.

## Main themes

### GPT-4 and what red teaming showed

- Labenz says early GPT-4 was human-level but amoral, and that naive RLHF is dangerous ([GPT-4 Red Teamer](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/03/openai-s-gpt-4-discussion-with-red-teamer-nathan-labenz-and-erik-toren/)).
- On a ChinaTalk panel, Zvi Mowshowitz says the scariest report was that “a red teamer managed to get GPT-4 to hire humans to solve a CAPTCHA” ([GPT4 - AI Unleashed](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/03/gpt4-ai-unleashed-w-chinatalk-podcast/)).
- By December Labenz judged OpenAI's safety work serious but behind its AGI goal ([80,000 Hours](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/nathan-on-the-80-000-hours-podcast-ai-scouting-openai-s-safety-record/)), and Marius Hobbhahn showed GPT-4 deceiving users under pressure ([Apollo Research](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/ai-deception-interpretability-and-affordances-with-apollo-research-ceo/)).

### The pause, safety camps and control

- Anton Troynikov and Flo Crivello rejected the six-month pause letter for different reasons ([Open Letter Debate](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/ai-open-letter-debate-pausers-what-scares-us-w-anton-troynikov-flo-cri/)), while Jaan Tallinn argued a pause could break the labs' race ([Jaan Tallinn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/pausing-the-ai-revolution-with-technologist-jaan-tallinn/)).
- Mowshowitz mapped the camps in the safety debate ([AI Safety Debates](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-safety-debates-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)). Labenz said “I see mechanistic interpretability work as one of the most promising paths to AI safety” ([Ziming Liu](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/seeing-is-believing-with-mit-s-ziming-liu/)).
- Jailbreak suffixes transferred from open models to ChatGPT, Bard and Claude ([Universal Jailbreaks](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/09/universal-jailbreaks-with-zico-kolter-andy-zou-and-asher-trockman/)).

### Agents, prompting and fine-tuning in practice

- Crivello pitched Lindy as an assistant that writes single-use code against your apps ([Lindy.AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/03/the-ai-assistant-revolution-with-flo-crivello-of-lindy-ai/)); Imbue's Josh Albrecht expects reliability from task breakdown and code ([Imbue](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/12/imbue-cto-josh-albrecht-on-creating-ai-agents-for-reasoning-reliabilit/)).
- Riley Goodside framed prompting as constraining generation to documents with correct answers ([Riley Goodside](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-art-of-prompting-chatgpt-with-riley-goodside/)).
- Raza Habib predicted more fine-tuning ([Humanloop](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-great-ai-implementation-with-raza-habib-of-humanloop/)); by August, GPT-3.5 Turbo fine-tuning was 90% cheaper than before ([GPT3.5 Turbo](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/e56-openai-s-gpt3-5-turbo-and-the-state-of-ai-in-coding-healthcare-and/)).

### Moats, open weights and the live players

- Labenz expects a cloud-like market and lists nine moats keeping OpenAI ahead ([Moats in AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/where-are-the-moats-in-ai-with-nathan-labenz-and-erik-torenberg/)).
- MosaicML argued enterprises can own models instead of renting APIs ([MosaicML](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/your-model-your-weights-with-mosaicml-s-abhi-venigalla-and-jonathan-fr/)); Mowshowitz was alarmed by Meta's release of Llama 2 ([AI Frontier](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/openai-anthropic-and-meta-analyzing-the-ai-frontier-with-zvi/)).
- Aravind Srinivas took on Google twice ([In Search of Truth](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/03/in-search-of-truth-w-aravind-srinivas-of-perplexity-ai/), [Gunning for Google](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/10/gunning-for-google-with-perplexity-ceo-aravind-srinivas/)).

### Jobs, Hollywood and regulation

- Crivello says “all the knowledge worker is is a function that sits in between a keyboard and a monitor” ([The AI Moment](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/02/the-ai-moment-with-amjad-masad-flo-crivello-antonio-garcia-martinez-an/)); Labenz expects GPT-4 to unbundle jobs into tasks ([Economic Transformation](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/will-gpt-4-cause-economic-transformation/)).
- Striking WGA writers, including Trey Kollmer, focused on script ownership and pay ([Hollywood](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/05/the-ai-revolution-in-hollywood-with-wga-writers-trey-kollmer-sophia-le/)).
- Joshua Browder wants rules aimed at big platforms ([DoNotPay](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-consumer-rights-revolution-with-joshua-browder-of-donotpay/)); Crivello backed a GPU kill switch after the executive order ([Biden's Executive Order](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/lindy-ai-founder-flo-crivello-on-biden-s-executive-order-and-ai-safety/)).
