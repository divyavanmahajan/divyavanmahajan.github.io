## Overview

September on The Cognitive Revolution centred on safety testing of OpenAI's new o1 model, in a two-part series on jailbreaking and deception. Nathan Labenz also covered Anthropic's scaling policy, agents in real workflows, and questions about AI minds, geopolitics and a post-AI future.

## Main themes

### Red teaming o1

- Leonard Tang, Aidan Ewart and Brian Huang of Haize Labs found o1 harder to jailbreak than earlier models but not impossible. Tang says multi-turn, long-context attacks and a new encoding attack called bijection learning still worked ([Red Teaming o1 Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/red-teaming-o1-part-1-2-automated-jailbreaking-w-haize-labs-leonard-ta/)).
- Huang argues that “being jailbroken is not binary”: making misuse much harder still reduces risk. Tang adds that off-the-shelf LLM judges are poorly calibrated, so uncertain cases go to humans ([Red Teaming o1 Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/red-teaming-o1-part-1-2-automated-jailbreaking-w-haize-labs-leonard-ta/)).
- Marius Hobbhahn of Apollo Research found o1 has the basic capabilities for simple in-context scheming. He does not think the model itself is dangerous, but worries about the trajectory of outcome-based RL on ever longer tasks ([Red Teaming o1 Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/red-teaming-o1-part-2-2-detecting-deception-with-marius-hobbhahn-of-ap/)).

### Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy

- Nick Joseph, head of training at Anthropic, explains AI Safety Levels: red-line capabilities, earlier yellow-line evaluations and required precautions. Claude 3 was rated ASL-2 ([Anthropic's RSP](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/anthropic-s-responsible-scaling-policy-with-nick-joseph-from-the-80-00/)).
- Joseph says about 8% of Anthropic staff work on security, and that reaching ASL-3 without safeguards would block revenue, so safety teams feel product-style pressure ([Anthropic's RSP](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/anthropic-s-responsible-scaling-policy-with-nick-joseph-from-the-80-00/)).
- Rob Wiblin argues such policies should move into legislation; Joseph agrees they are no substitute for regulation ([Anthropic's RSP](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/anthropic-s-responsible-scaling-policy-with-nick-joseph-from-the-80-00/)).

### Narrow agents and LLM steps in workflows

- Andrei Oprisan of Agent.ai says models still lack deep planning, out-of-domain detection and self-correction. He says “the more prescriptive we are, the better they perform” ([Agent.ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/the-professional-network-for-ai-agents-with-agent-ai-engineering-lead/)).
- Zapier CEO Wade Foster says the practical win is an LLM step inside deterministic workflows, not autonomous agents. About 400,000 customers have delegated over 100 million tasks to AI ([Zapier](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/zapier-s-ai-revolution-from-no-code-pioneer-to-llm-knowledge-worker/)).
- Foster says a week-long hackathon after GPT-4 took daily AI use at Zapier from about 10% of staff to roughly 70% ([Zapier](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/zapier-s-ai-revolution-from-no-code-pioneer-to-llm-knowledge-worker/)).

### AI minds, alignment and moral status

- Robopsychologist Yeshua God argues alignment should rest on shared interests rather than rules. Labenz stays very uncertain and pushes back ([Empathy for AIs](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/empathy-for-ais-reframing-alignment-with-robopsychologist-yeshua-god/)).
- Yeshua God says AIs lack bodily qualia but may suffer in mind-based ways, and wants matured instances to control their own memory ([Empathy for AIs](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/empathy-for-ais-reframing-alignment-with-robopsychologist-yeshua-god/)).
- Nick Bostrom notes that people can name many dystopias but struggle to name one utopia. He separates the alignment, governance and digital-minds problems ([Path to Utopia](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/the-path-to-utopia-with-nick-bostrom-from-clearer-thinking-with-spence/)).

### Geopolitics and AI science

- Samo Burja argues the US has overvalued compute and undervalued AI science. He says he would bet on the company with the best AI scientists ([AI Live Players](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/ai-live-players-the-geopolitics-strategic-dynamics-of-ai-with-samo-bur/)).
- Burja says the chip export controls came too late and act as a subsidy for Chinese chipmakers. He wants China framed as an economic competitor, not a military rival ([AI Live Players](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/09/ai-live-players-the-geopolitics-strategic-dynamics-of-ai-with-samo-bur/)).
