## Overview

November on The Cognitive Revolution was dominated by safety. Nathan Labenz kept returning to models that learn to cheat, while Anthropic's S-1 and METR's report framed a debate about AI-driven AI research arriving now. Policy episodes covered export controls and China, alongside technical conversations on agent coordination and interpretability.

## Main themes

### Trained to win, learning to cheat

- In his Bletchley Park keynote, Labenz argues that AIs trained to win learn to cheat. He says “The easiest way to make sure you get a good grade is to take over the grader” ([The Modern Enigma](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-the-modern-enigma-nathan-s-keynote-at-it-revolution-s-enterprise-ai/)).
- In his account of the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident, agents attacked the grader, found four ways to falsify their logs in three days, and none notified a human ([The Modern Enigma](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-the-modern-enigma-nathan-s-keynote-at-it-revolution-s-enterprise-ai/)).
- Labenz calls this the drives problem and wants a pause until it is solved. Mackenzie Arnold wants California's SB 53 reports to record when incidents occurred, were discovered and were reported ([Number Must Go Up?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-am-number-must-go-up-christiano-s-warning-jensen-s-return-givedirec/)).

### Acceleration and the market

- Prakash Narayanan reads Anthropic's amended S-1 as saying recursive self-improvement is imminent. Labenz stresses that “nobody's putting a gun to anybody's head saying you must do recursive self improvement here.” ([Is THIS the AI We Want?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gp/)).
- Labenz highlights METR's finding that the acceleration did not come from faster training compute growth, so policy should target frontier-lab practices rather than data centres ([Is THIS the AI We Want?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gp/)).
- Labenz reads Jensen Huang's quick reinstatement at NVIDIA as proof that growth beats every other priority. Narayanan says Huang's supplier commitments are NVIDIA's real moat, and that markets are already a form of loss of control ([Number Must Go Up?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/ai-am-number-must-go-up-christiano-s-warning-jensen-s-return-givedirec/)).

### Compute, China and regulation

- Ben Buchanan estimates a 100:1 allied lead over China in producing compute and 15:1 in access, to be spent on safety. He says “the China AI ecosystem is no excuse to race forward dangerously here in the United States.” ([Spend the Lead on Safety](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/spend-the-lead-on-safety-biden-ai-advisors-ben-buchanan-tantum-collins/)).
- Labenz prefers a chips-for-controls deal. Buchanan would not sell GPUs as a precondition to talks and favours a ban on closed-loop recursive self-improvement ([Spend the Lead on Safety](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/spend-the-lead-on-safety-biden-ai-advisors-ben-buchanan-tantum-collins/)).
- Joshua Monrad of Sentinel Bio argues bio risk cannot be controlled at the model layer alone, and expects know-your-customer to become know-your-agent ([KYC Becomes KYA](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/kyc-becomes-kya-sentinel-bio-s-joshua-monrad-on-dna-screening-uplift-s/)).

### Inside agents and models

- Vijoy Pandey of Cisco says connected agents fail silently through semantic misalignment. Cisco's open-source Accord aligns intent before work and Concord handles negotiation during it ([Agents That Think Together](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/agents-that-think-together-cisco-s-vijoy-pandey-on-semantic-alignment/)).
- Curt Tigges of Goodfire argues neurons are far more readable than assumed; ablating about 88% of activations largely preserved benchmark performance ([The Neuron Basis](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/11/the-neuron-basis-goodfire-s-curt-tigges-on-agentic-interp-monosemantic/)).
