## Overview

November on The Cognitive Revolution was dominated by OpenAI: its DevDay launches, a startup it backed in self-driving, and the board's attempt to fire Sam Altman. Alongside that ran a debate on safety and regulation, set off by the White House executive order. Two builder episodes covered synthetic data and AI-generated user interfaces.

## Main themes

### Safety and where to regulate

- Flo Crivello, an accelerationist on most issues, treats advanced AI as the exception. He welcomes the executive order and says “it's important to regulate the silicon layer” through chip choke points ([Flo Crivello](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/lindy-ai-founder-flo-crivello-on-biden-s-executive-order-and-ai-safety/)).
- Crivello sees GPT-4 as the core of AGI, gives a more-than-even chance of AGI in 2 to 8 years, and thinks the safety side is making real progress ([Flo Crivello](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/lindy-ai-founder-flo-crivello-on-biden-s-executive-order-and-ai-safety/)).
- Nathan Labenz argues a toy DeepMind paper was misread as proof that LLMs cannot generalise, and that attacking voluntary VC commitments will invite heavier regulation ([AI Discourse Deranged](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/ai-discourse-deranged-assessing-llm-generalization-takes-and-polarizin/)).
- Alex Watson of Gretel also welcomed the executive order as a sign of attention to the right things ([Gretel AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/synthetic-data-with-alex-watson-founder-of-gretel-ai/)).

### OpenAI DevDay and the road to agents

- Logan Kilpatrick says “Assistants and GPTs are really our first shot at and moving in the direction of agents”, deliberately without self-planning yet ([DevDay](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/openai-devday-beyond-the-headlines-with-logan-kilpatrick-openai-s-dev/)).
- He rejects the idea that DevDay killed startups, describes a ladder of fine-tuning options, and repeats that OpenAI does not train on API data ([DevDay](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/openai-devday-beyond-the-headlines-with-logan-kilpatrick-openai-s-dev/)).
- Guillermo Rauch compares building on GPTs to selling on Amazon, with no direct customer link, and urges companies to build AI into their own products ([Vercel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/vercel-ceo-guillermo-rauch-on-v0-ai-powered-coding-and-software-2-0/)).

### The OpenAI board and Sam Altman

- Labenz tells how his time on the GPT-4 red team ended after he took his concerns to a board member who had seen only a demo ([Board Context](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/new-context-on-the-openai-board-s-initial-decision-to-fire-sam-altman/)).
- He reads the board's complaint about candour as consistent with capabilities outpacing control, but says the board made an own goal by not explaining itself. He calls the possible triggers speculation ([Board Context](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/new-context-on-the-openai-board-s-initial-decision-to-fire-sam-altman/)).
- He also credits OpenAI's later steps, including the Superalignment team and the White House commitments ([Board Context](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/new-context-on-the-openai-board-s-initial-decision-to-fire-sam-altman/)).

### Multimodal models in the car

- John Hayes of Ghost Autonomy splits driving into a fast in-car safety system and a slow multimodal LLM that chooses manoeuvres, backed by OpenAI's startup fund ([Ghost Autonomy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/openai-invests-in-the-self-driving-race-with-john-hayes-founder-of-gho/)).
- Hayes admits GPT-4V calls take 10 to 30 seconds but expects models to get 100 to 1000 times faster and cheaper over a five-year planning cycle ([Ghost Autonomy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/openai-invests-in-the-self-driving-race-with-john-hayes-founder-of-gho/)).

### Builders: synthetic data and AI-written code

- Watson says synthetic data serves both safe data sharing and better training sets, and must be measured against the real distribution ([Gretel AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/synthetic-data-with-alex-watson-founder-of-gretel-ai/)).
- Rauch says v0 output was more accessible than his own hand-written code. He predicts writing code will matter less as attention moves elsewhere, and Labenz reports a multiple-X speed-up from GPT-4 ([Vercel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/11/vercel-ceo-guillermo-rauch-on-v0-ai-powered-coding-and-software-2-0/)).
