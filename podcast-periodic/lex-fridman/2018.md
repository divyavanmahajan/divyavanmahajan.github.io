## Overview

In 2018 the podcast ran from August to December with twelve long conversations. It opened with consciousness and the nature of intelligence, then moved to a debate about AI risk that ran from Pinker's scepticism to Russell's control problem. Machine learning researchers questioned what deep learning still lacks. Programming and industry episodes with van Rossum, Atwood and Schmidt rounded out the year.

## Main themes

### Consciousness and what intelligence is

- Max Tegmark says “I define intelligence simply as the ability to accomplish complex goals”, and treats consciousness as a pattern of information processing, not something only carbon can do ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/08/max-tegmark-life-3-0/)).
- Christof Koch separates the two: digital machines may reach human-level intelligence, but without brain-like causal power they will not experience anything ([Christof Koch](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/09/christof-koch-consciousness/)).
- Tegmark calls AGI potentially the biggest transition in the history of life on Earth, so aligning its goals with ours is the main task ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/08/max-tegmark-life-3-0/)).

### How worried to be about AI

- Steven Pinker calls both standard existential-risk scenarios, takeover and collateral damage, incoherent, and rejects the comparison with nuclear weapons ([Steven Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- Yoshua Bengio sees existential risk as very unlikely but worth study, and wants public debate on near-term harms such as surveillance, autonomous weapons, jobs and concentration of power ([Yoshua Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).
- Stuart Russell works on the control problem and says “in practice it's extremely unlikely that we could specify correctly in advance the full range of concerns of humanity”, so machines should stay uncertain about our objectives ([Stuart Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).
- Eric Schmidt treats existential risk as a far-off question; the near-term job is to spread AI widely, with healthcare and education first ([Eric Schmidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/eric-schmidt-google/)).

### What deep learning still lacks

- Bengio argues current networks have only a basic, low-level understanding and need causal and disentangled representations and agents that learn by acting ([Yoshua Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).
- Vladimir Vapnik dismisses deep learning as fantasy: “Mathematics does not know deep learning. Mathematics does not know neurons; it is just functions.” The hard problem is choosing good predicates that shrink the admissible set of functions ([Vladimir Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/vladimir-vapnik-statistical-learning/)).
- Russell notes AlphaGo looks 40 to 60 moves ahead but is very selective about what it considers ([Stuart Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).

### Learning to learn, curiosity and games

- Juergen Schmidhuber calls meta-learning and recursive self-improvement the pinnacle, and describes artificial curiosity and compression progress as intrinsic rewards ([Juergen Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).
- Schmidhuber predicts a bigger wave of active AI that shapes data through its actions, beyond today's ad-driven pattern recognition ([Juergen Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).
- Pieter Abbeel points to self-play, demonstrations and simulator ensembles to give robots more learning signal ([Pieter Abbeel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/pieter-abbeel-deep-reinforcement-learning/)), while Tuomas Sandholm explains how Libratus beat poker pros with game theory ([Tuomas Sandholm](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/tuomas-sandholm-poker-and-game-theory/)).

### Software, communities and scale

- Guido van Rossum explains Python's focus on programmer productivity and why he stepped down as BDFL without naming a successor ([Guido van Rossum](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/guido-van-rossum-python/)).
- Jeff Atwood traces Stack Overflow back to his Coding Horror blog and argues communities need strict rules and software they own ([Jeff Atwood](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/jeff-atwood-stack-overflow-and-coding-horror/)).
- Schmidt describes Google's 20% time and predictable cash generation as what lets it make bets ([Eric Schmidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/eric-schmidt-google/)).
