## Overview

July had two long conversations about where software and AI are heading. David Heinemeier Hansson made the case for simple tools, small teams and code written by hand. Demis Hassabis laid out why he thinks learning systems can model much of the natural world, and what AGI might look like.

## Main themes

### How programmers should use AI

- DHH uses AI every day as a tutor and pair programmer but will not let it write his code. He says letting Cursor-style tools drive made him feel slightly dumber while building his Omakub setup ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- He doubts the claim from Anthropic's CEO that 90% of code will be AI-written by year end. Lex Fridman counters that vibe coding with careful correction could become a real skill ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).

### Small software and owned infrastructure

- DHH points to Basecamp and HEY at about 100,000 lines of Ruby each, and a default 37signals team of one programmer and one designer. He cites Shopify's large Rails monolith as proof that the stack scales ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- 37signals' AWS bill peaked at about $3.2-3.4 million a year. The company moved seven applications to Dell servers in just over six months without new hires and projects about $10 million in savings over five years ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- He also covers platform fights: the HEY dispute over Apple's 30% cut, credit to Epic for forcing open link-out payments, and criticism of Matt Mullenweg's demands on WP Engine ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).

### Learnable nature and world models

- Hassabis argues that patterns shaped by evolution or other selection have structure a classical learning algorithm can find. AlphaFold, AlphaGo and Veo 3 are his evidence ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- He says Veo 3's handling of liquids, lighting and materials shows intuitive physics can be learned from passive observation, which changed his earlier view that embodiment was needed. Interactive video is his next step toward a world model ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- He describes a long-term ambition to simulate a cell so that most lab experiments could run in silico ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).

### AGI timelines, testing and risk

- Hassabis puts the odds of AGI by 2030 at about 50%. To test for it he would run tens of thousands of cognitive tasks and give top experts a month or two to find flaws ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- He says pre-training, post-training and inference-time scaling all have room left, while DeepMind puts roughly half its resources into blue-sky research ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- He expects about 10 times the impact of the Industrial Revolution, arriving 10 times faster. He calls the risk non-zero and non-negligible and wants 10 times more safety research ([Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
