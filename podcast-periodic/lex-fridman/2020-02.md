## Overview

February 2020 was a month of computing fundamentals. A chip architect and a quantum computing theorist talked about where hardware is heading, three theorists offered competing ideas of what intelligence is, and Andrew Ng and Michael I. Jordan brought the talk back to how machine learning is used in practice.

## Main themes

### The future of computing hardware

- Jim Keller argues Moore's law is thousands of innovations, each with diminishing returns, that add up to an exponential. He expects 10 to 20 more years of shrinking ([Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).
- Keller says that to make real progress on computer architecture “every five years you should do one from scratch”, since rewrites are often faster and simpler than tweaks ([Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).
- Scott Aaronson calls Google's 53-qubit supremacy experiment a genuine milestone, but says breaking public-key cryptography would need millions of physical qubits ([Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/scott-aaronson-quantum-computing/)).
- Aaronson expects chemistry and materials simulation to be the first useful application, and warns that many quantum machine learning claims have since been matched by classical algorithms ([Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/scott-aaronson-quantum-computing/)).

### Theories of intelligence

- Vladimir Vapnik argues intelligence means finding a few good predicates that shrink the set of admissible functions, so learning needs far less data. He challenges researchers to match the best digit recognisers with about 100 times fewer examples ([Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/vladimir-vapnik-predicates-invariants-and-the-essence-of-intelligence/)).
- Marcus Hutter presents AIXI, which joins Solomonoff induction with long-term planning, as a provably optimal but uncomputable gold standard for AGI. He sees no difference between compression, understanding and prediction ([Hutter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/marcus-hutter-universal-artificial-intelligence-aixi-and-agi/)).
- John Hopfield argues “a large part of intelligent behavior is actually just large associative memories at work”, and that brains rely on feedback and collective dynamics that artificial networks ignore ([Hopfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/john-hopfield-physics-view-of-the-mind-and-neurobiology/)).

### Machine learning as engineering for decisions and markets

- Michael I. Jordan says today's AI is a new engineering discipline, not a step towards human-like intelligence, and calls the term AI a bad choice that led to false promises ([Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).
- He argues consequential decisions need error bars and causal questions, not just prediction, and estimates about half the people at Amazon work on decision making ([Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).
- Jordan blames advertising models for fake news, saying “you're monetizing with click-through rate and that is the core problem”. He would rather build markets connecting producers and consumers, such as a market for young musicians ([Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).

### AI education and adoption beyond software

- Andrew Ng expects nearly all developers to have some appreciation of machine learning, and compares the spread of coding to the spread of literacy ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/)).
- Ng sees the next wave in manufacturing, agriculture and healthcare, where small, messy data and deployment matter more than the model ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/)).
- On risk, Ng compares AGI worries to overpopulation on Mars and points to bias, job displacement and deepfakes as more urgent, while Keller says he is not worried about superintelligence ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/), [Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).
