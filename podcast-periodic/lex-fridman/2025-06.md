## Overview

June brought AI back to the centre of the show. Sundar Pichai explained Google's AI strategy and Terence Tao described how AI and formal proofs are changing mathematics. The month closed with a heated debate on the US strikes on Iran.

## Main themes

### Google's AI strategy

- Sundar Pichai calls AI the most important technology humanity has built and says Google was well placed even when commentators wrote it off. He names merging Brain and DeepMind, early TPU investment and a central AI infrastructure team as the key decisions ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- Pichai says Google is compute-limited and skips Ultra models because each new Pro matches the previous Ultra at lower serving cost. Monthly Gemini tokens grew 50x in a year, to 480 trillion ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- He uses Karpathy's term artificial jagged intelligence for today's uneven models and expects dramatic progress by 2030, with AGI slightly after ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- About 30% of Google's code uses AI suggestions, but Pichai says the metric that counts is a 10% gain in engineering velocity, and Google still plans to hire more engineers ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).

### AI and the practice of mathematics

- Terence Tao argues that Lean proof assistants combined with AI will enable large-scale, trustless collaboration. His Equational Theories Project checked about 22 million implications with about 50 contributors ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He is cautious about current tools: LLM proofs can look flawless but hide basic errors, and literature searches often return hallucinated papers. AlphaProof needed about three days of server time per IMO problem ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Tao predicts research-level human-AI collaborations by 2026, and explains why problems like Navier-Stokes and twin primes still resist proof ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).

### Iran, the bomb and deterrence

- Mark Dubowitz defends the US strikes on Fordow as deterrence that should force a deal with no enrichment and full dismantlement ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
- Scott Horton argues Iran wanted only a latent deterrent, that demanding zero enrichment is a poison pill, and that attacking Iran makes a bomb more likely ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
- Both agree Trump should not have left the JCPOA without first negotiating longer sunsets, and both doubt Iran would strike first. Fridman moderates and asks each to steelman the other ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
