## Overview

August 2021 kept returning to consciousness and to how minds, natural or artificial, build models of the world. Joscha Bach and Jeff Hawkins gave two engineering views of the brain, and Wojciech Zaremba explained OpenAI's path from GPT-3 to Codex and Copilot. The rest of the month covered physics and the history of science, scientific publishing, sleep, strength training and ancient ritual.

## Main themes

### Consciousness as a model

- Joscha Bach argues the world we experience is a predictive model the brain builds, and that consciousness is the attention mechanism that keeps it coherent. He pictures it as a monkey riding an elephant: “we're basically like a little monkey sitting on top of an elephant” ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/joscha-bach-nature-of-reality-dreams-and-consciousness/)).
- Bach says free will is the model a system makes of its own decisions under uncertainty, not an illusion. Fridman pushes back that this leaves the felt sense of control unexplained ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/joscha-bach-nature-of-reality-dreams-and-consciousness/)).
- Brian Muraresku argues that a ritual experience, not doctrine, was at the core of early Western religion, and Fridman brings in simulation and consciousness as his own lenses ([Brian Muraresku](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/brian-muraresku-the-secret-history-of-psychedelics/)).

### GPT-3, Codex and Copilot

- Zaremba describes GPT-3 as next-word prediction at scale. He says Codex grew from noticing that GPT-3 had rudimentary coding abilities, and that GitHub Copilot is the first product built on it ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He calls compute, algorithms and data multiplicative levers and says “so far we have seen that more gains came from compute than algorithms” ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Bach credits GPT-3's 2,048-token context but calls the model amnesiac. He thinks Codex could prove revolutionary because most industry programming is not creative ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/joscha-bach-nature-of-reality-dreams-and-consciousness/)).
- Zaremba backs iterative deployment to avoid a capability overhang and wants control of AGI spread widely ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).

### A thousand brains and AI risk

- Jeff Hawkins says the neocortex is about 150,000 cortical columns, each a complete model of the world, which reach one perception by voting ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jeff-hawkins-the-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Hawkins argues a machine built this way will have no drives of its own, so the real danger is self-replication. He accepts that “all technology is dangerous”, while Fridman cites recommender systems as harm that only shows up at scale ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jeff-hawkins-the-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).

### How science moves forward

- Barry Barish traces gravitational waves from Einstein's 1916 prediction to LIGO's 2015 detection, and insists the hard part was engineering. He also recalls Enrico Fermi as both experimentalist and theorist ([Barry Barish](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/barry-barish-gravitational-waves-and-the-most-precise-device-ever-buil/)).
- Jed Buchwald argues new theories win because they let scientists build and discover new things, using Newton's prism work and his rivalries with Hooke and Leibniz as examples ([Jed Buchwald](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jed-buchwald-isaac-newton-and-the-philosophy-of-science/)).
- The Batalha brothers say the impact-factor journal system holds science back and see room to build annotation and collaboration on top of arXiv ([Fermat's Library](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/luis-and-joao-batalha-lu-s-and-jo-o-batalha-fermat-s-library-and-the-a/)).

### Body and mind

- Matt Walker argues almost no system in body or mind escapes damage from lost sleep, and calls dreaming emotional first aid. Fridman defends trading sleep for passion as an informed choice ([Matt Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/matt-walker-sleep/)).
- Chris Duffin treats strength as an engineering problem of stability and carefully managed load, and links it to his move from corporate turnarounds to Kabuki Strength ([Chris Duffin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/chris-duffin-the-mad-scientist-of-strength/)).
