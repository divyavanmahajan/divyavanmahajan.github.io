## Overview

June paired opposing views more than once. Jonathan Haidt answered Fridman's earlier Zuckerberg interview on social media, and back-to-back episodes with Richard Wolff and Douglas Murray took Marxism from both sides. Robin Hanson and Donald Hoffman offered large theories of aliens and reality, while Tony Fadell and Jonathan Reisman brought the month down to products and medicine.

## Main themes

### Social media's business model

- Jonathan Haidt argues the problem is not the internet but an ad-driven model in which users create viral content and are themselves the product, and that it harms pre-teen girls most ([Jonathan Haidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/jonathan-haidt-the-case-against-social-media/)).
- Haidt proposes keeping children off these platforms until 16, verifying users for posting privileges and hiding the most hostile users through an AI- or crowd-rated aggression score ([Jonathan Haidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/jonathan-haidt-the-case-against-social-media/)).
- Fridman agrees with much of the data but argues platforms could be built to maximise long-term well-being, and defends Elon Musk's authenticity against Haidt's criticism ([Jonathan Haidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/jonathan-haidt-the-case-against-social-media/)).

### Marxism and the West, from both sides

- Richard Wolff argues that Marxism is about who controls the workplace surplus, not state control, and proposes democratic worker co-ops ([Richard Wolff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/richard-wolff-marxism-and-communism/)).
- Fridman, drawing on his family's Soviet history, argues hierarchy is natural and that competition keeps firms accountable. Wolff replies that competition leads to monopoly and that automation could have shortened the working day ([Richard Wolff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/richard-wolff-marxism-and-communism/)).
- Douglas Murray argues Western history is judged by a double standard and that gratitude is the answer to resentment. Fridman plays devil's advocate, suggesting Marx's critique may matter once machines do more labour ([Douglas Murray](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/douglas-murray-racism-marxism-and-the-war-on-the-west/)).

### Big theories of aliens and reality

- Robin Hanson argues that humanity's early arrival implies fast-expanding grabby aliens, and that expansion means accepting competition rather than central control ([Robin Hanson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/robin-hanson-alien-civilizations-ufos-and-the-future-of-humanity/)).
- Hanson calls AI progress modestly chunky, expects human-level AI at least a decade away, and proposes prediction markets to make research institutions more informative ([Robin Hanson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/robin-hanson-alien-civilizations-ufos-and-the-future-of-humanity/)).
- Donald Hoffman argues evolution tuned perception for fitness rather than truth, so spacetime is an interface and consciousness is fundamental. Fridman presses him on the limits of his models ([Donald Hoffman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/donald-hoffman-reality-is-an-illusion-how-evolution-hid-the-truth/)).

### Building products and judging evidence

- Tony Fadell says great products start with a customer pain and a clear why, written down early as a press release, and that first versions rest on opinions leaders must own ([Tony Fadell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/tony-fadell-iphone-ipod-and-nest/)).
- Fadell says companies such as Philips stall by turning opinion-based decisions into data-driven ones to protect jobs, and describes the go-to-market as the hard part of Nest ([Tony Fadell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/tony-fadell-iphone-ipod-and-nest/)).
- Physician Jonathan Reisman and Fridman agree medicine knows less than it thinks. Fridman argues AI should flag issues for doctors from long-term data that consumer devices already collect ([Jonathan Reisman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/06/jonathan-reisman-the-human-body-from-sex-sperm-to-hands-heart/)).
