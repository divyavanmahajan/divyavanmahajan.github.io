## Overview

In 2020 the show ran 84 episodes and reached well beyond AI research. Consciousness and the simulation argument recurred all year. GPT-3 arrived mid-year and became the test case for whether scaling leads to understanding. Later conversations with Joe Rogan, Michael Malice and Eric Weinstein turned to meaning, power and cancel culture.

## Main themes

### Consciousness and the simulation argument

- David Chalmers says that if “the simulation is designed well enough, it'll be indistinguishable from a non simulated reality” ([David Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/)); Nick Bostrom sets out his three-way simulation argument ([Nick Bostrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/nick-bostrom-simulation-and-superintelligence/)).
- Roger Penrose argues consciousness is not a computation ([Roger Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)); Joscha Bach sees the self as a simulation the brain runs ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).
- Alex Garland and Peter Singer ask whether machines could be conscious and what that would mean morally ([Alex Garland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/alex-garland-ex-machina-devs-annihilation-and-the-poetry-of-science/), [Peter Singer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/peter-singer-suffering-in-humans-animals-and-ai/)).

### GPT-3 and the paths to AGI

- Ilya Sutskever argues the field keeps underestimating deep learning ([Ilya Sutskever](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/ilya-sutskever-deep-learning/)), while Ben Goertzel bets on hybrid symbolic-neural systems ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Dileep George finds GPT-3 intriguing but says “it is not really carrying a world state around” ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/)).
- David Silver frames intelligence as the reinforcement learning problem ([David Silver](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/04/david-silver-alphago-alphazero-and-deep-reinforcement-learning/)); François Chollet defines it as skill-acquisition efficiency ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).

### Self-driving and robots around people

- Ayanna Howard warns that drivers drift from vigilance into over-reliance on Tesla Autopilot ([Ayanna Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).
- Jim Keller calls driving an attention problem; Lex Fridman counters that people complicate it ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).
- George Hotz doubts Waymo's product: “users already have the choice to trade off money for speed it's called uberpool” ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)). Dmitri Dolgov answers with lidar and a repeatable deployment process ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).

### Moore's law and the craft of computing

- Jim Keller sees Moore's law as a cascade of innovations, far from dead ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)); David Patterson backs domain-specific accelerators as it slows ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- Brian Kernighan and Chris Lattner credit simple mechanisms and good defaults for tools that last ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/), [Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).

### Meaning, power and cancel culture

- Stephen Kotkin argues unconstrained power compounds mistakes ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).
- Joe Rogan sees many meanings of life resting on love ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)); Manolis Kellis says the search for meaning is the point ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).
- Ryan Hall calls cancel culture poisonous ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)); Eric Weinstein says stalled institutions turn on outliers ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
