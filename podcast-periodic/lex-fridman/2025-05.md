## Overview

May had three episodes and no AI focus. Cosmologist Janna Levin explained black holes, historian James Holland retold World War II through logistics, and Oliver Anthony talked about the working people behind his viral song.

## Main themes

### Black holes and the limits of physics

- Janna Levin argues a black hole is best understood as an event horizon, an empty region of spacetime, not a crushed star. That makes black holes the main testing ground for quantum gravity ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).
- On Hawking's information paradox she rejects information loss, fuzzballs and firewalls and favours ER = EPR, which suggests gravity emerges from quantum entanglement ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).
- She recounts LIGO's roughly 50-year path to its first signal on 14 September 2015, from a collision more than 1.5 billion years ago ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).

### World War II as a war of supply

- James Holland argues the war was decided at the operational level of supply chains, factories, shipping and training. Germany invaded the Soviet Union with 2,000 different vehicle types ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
- He cites 49,000 Shermans against 1,347 Tigers, and Britain building 496 single-engine fighters to Germany's 240 in July 1940 ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
- Holland defends Chamberlain's 1938 choice as understandable, rejects Darryl Cooper's claim that Churchill was the chief villain, and says the story of a weak Britain was shaped after the war ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).

### Working people, fame and technology

- Oliver Anthony wrote Rich Men North of Richmond from eight years of selling to industrial job sites. After release he got more than 50,000 messages about suicide, addiction and unemployment and turned down $8 million offers ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
- He argues corporate, HR-style politeness strips people of their humanity and that blue-collar workers are the least represented. Fridman adds that some rudeness is needed to break bureaucracy ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
- Both worry that algorithmic feeds make digital life more appealing as real life degrades. Anthony plans non-profit venues outside cities, free of Ticketmaster and Live Nation ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
