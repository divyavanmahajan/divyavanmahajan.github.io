## Overview

September 2020 mixed technology with some of the show's most personal episodes. Eugenia Kuyda and James Gosling covered AI companions and the origins of Java, while Stephen Wolfram and David Fravor took on physics and an unexplained encounter at sea. Much of the rest of the month, including a solo episode in memory of Lex Fridman's grandmother, was about struggle, love and how to live well.

## Main themes

### AI that people talk to

- Eugenia Kuyda argues that conversational AI matters most for emotional connection, not task completion. Replika asks users after each chat whether they feel better, and she says about 80% of conversations help ([Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/)).
- Kuyda built the first bot from about 10,000 text messages with a friend who had died, which led to Replika. GPT-3, fine-tuned with OpenAI, now supplies about one in five responses ([Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/)).
- She admits a perfect AI friend could isolate people further and says the product has to lead users back to other humans ([Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/)).
- David Fravor argues AI works within hard limits, while a pilot can act in grey areas, such as departing controlled flight on purpose ([David Fravor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/david-fravor-ufos-aliens-fighter-jets-and-aerospace-engineering/)).

### Physics, origins and the unexplained

- Stephen Wolfram proposes that a simple rule rewriting a hypergraph could generate space and time, with general relativity and quantum mechanics as facets of one theory ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/stephen-wolfram-fundamental-theory-of-physics-life-and-the-universe/)).
- Wolfram doubts quantum computers will deliver an exponential speed-up, and links causal invariance to eventual consistency in distributed databases ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/stephen-wolfram-fundamental-theory-of-physics-life-and-the-universe/)).
- Manolis Kellis frames life as a chain of transformations that resist physics, from self-reinforcing chemistry to brains that can override evolution ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/manolis-kellis-origin-of-life-humans-ideas-suffering-and-happiness/)).
- Fravor describes the 2004 Tic Tac encounter as a real object beyond known technology and wants it studied without stigma. Fridman is more sceptical of the videos than of his account ([David Fravor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/david-fravor-ufos-aliens-fighter-jets-and-aerospace-engineering/)).

### Struggle, grief and a meaningful life

- In a solo episode, Fridman honours his grandmother through five lessons: mental toughness, physical strength, thinking before speaking, belief in himself and love ([In Memory of My Grandmother](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/lex-solo-grandmother-lex-solo-3-in-memory-of-my-grandmother/)).
- Kellis says meaning comes from the journey and its struggles, and tells his mother he aims to be fulfilled rather than happy ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/manolis-kellis-origin-of-life-humans-ideas-suffering-and-happiness/)).
- Joe Rogan says hard training keeps him grounded and guards against arrogance. For him a good life rests on love first, then real passions ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).

### Martial arts, power and free speech

- Ryan Hall says jiu jitsu teaches humility and persistence, and compares techniques to vocabulary and principles to grammar ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)).
- Hall argues for grace over cancel culture and says silencing people is worse than the problem it targets. Fridman worries about conspiracy theories spread by anonymous trolls ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)).
- Rogan rates grappling as the best self-defence base and explains why he avoids a co-host in long-form conversation ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).

### Building software that lasts

- James Gosling traces Java to pointer bugs that caused security holes and to buyers locked into one CPU vendor. The JVM grew from a grad-school bytecode hack ([James Gosling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/james-gosling-java-jvm-emacs-and-the-early-days-of-computing/)).
- Gosling says Kodak saw the digital shift coming but could not accept the quarterly hit, while founders with large stakes gave Amazon and Tesla patient money ([James Gosling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/james-gosling-java-jvm-emacs-and-the-early-days-of-computing/)).
