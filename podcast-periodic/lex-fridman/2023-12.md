## Overview

December swung from geopolitics back to science, space and AI. Guillaume Verdon made the e/acc case against safety-led regulation, Lee Cronin dismissed AI doom, and Jeff Bezos explained how Blue Origin plans to cut the cost of reaching orbit. Large language models came up in three episodes, though none was centred on them.

## Main themes

### Regulation, power and e/acc

- Verdon argues markets reach AI reliability more efficiently than heavy-handed rules written by incumbents, and that whoever deploys an AI system should be liable for it ([Verdon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/guillaume-verdon-beff-jezos-e-acc-movement-physics-computation-agi/)).
- He calls concentrated AI power one of the biggest existential risks and says safety is “the perfect cover for centralization of power”. He wants separation of AI and state ([Verdon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/guillaume-verdon-beff-jezos-e-acc-movement-physics-computation-agi/)).
- Citing the OpenAI board crisis, Verdon argues centralised control is fragile. He names OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Meta and Mistral as evidence competition works. Fridman pushes back on whether markets can handle unintended consequences ([Verdon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/guillaume-verdon-beff-jezos-e-acc-movement-physics-computation-agi/)).

### How worried to be about AI

- Cronin puts the chance of AGI doom at zero because there is no mechanism, but worries about fake data and fake users now ([Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lee-cronin-controversial-nature-paper-on-evolution-of-life-and-univers/)).
- Verdon rejects p(doom) estimates as biased sampling of scary futures and ranks a government-corporate AI oligopoly as the darker timeline ([Verdon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/guillaume-verdon-beff-jezos-e-acc-movement-physics-computation-agi/)).
- Lisa Randall has become more concerned about AI losing control, and sees LLMs as generalisations of existing knowledge that have not yet found new physics ([Randall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lisa-randall-dark-matter-theoretical-physics-and-extinction-events/)).
- Bezos calls LLMs discoveries rather than inventions and expects them to help more than harm ([Bezos](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/jeff-bezos-amazon-and-blue-origin/)).

### Bezos on space and decisions

- Bezos says reaching orbit was solved decades ago and “The only interesting problem is dramatically reducing the cost of access to orbit” ([Bezos](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/jeff-bezos-amazon-and-blue-origin/)).
- For Blue Origin that means rate manufacturing: 24 New Glenn launches a year needs an upper stage every two weeks. He wants to build infrastructure, such as Blue Ring, that others can start companies on ([Bezos](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/jeff-bezos-amazon-and-blue-origin/)).
- He separates reversible two-way doors from irreversible one-way doors, distrusts metrics as proxies and runs meetings on six-page memos rather than slides ([Bezos](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/jeff-bezos-amazon-and-blue-origin/)).

### Science at the edge

- Cronin defends assembly theory, with Sara Walker, as a way to measure selection: complex objects found in many copies signal a process like evolution. Critics came from many fields ([Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lee-cronin-controversial-nature-paper-on-evolution-of-life-and-univers/)).
- Randall says eight or nine independent methods agree on how much dark matter exists. She stresses that her dark disk idea linking it to extinction events is speculative but testable ([Randall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lisa-randall-dark-matter-theoretical-physics-and-extinction-events/)).
- Both guests touched on nuclear weapons; Randall says people worry about them less than they should ([Randall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lisa-randall-dark-matter-theoretical-physics-and-extinction-events/), [Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/lee-cronin-controversial-nature-paper-on-evolution-of-life-and-univers/)).
