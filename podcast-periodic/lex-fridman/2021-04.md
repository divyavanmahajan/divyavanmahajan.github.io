## Overview

Bitcoin was central in three of April's seven episodes, with Nic Carter, Robert Breedlove and Tyler Cowen each giving a different view. A second thread ran through history and power, from Yannis Pappas to Jeremi Suri on the American presidency. Risto Miikkulainen on evolutionary computation and Georges St-Pierre on the science of fighting completed the month.

## Main themes

### Bitcoin as encoded values

- Nic Carter says “the key number one value that's embedded in bitcoin is the notion of non-discretionary monetary policy”. Running a full node lets anyone check that no covert inflation occurred ([Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/)).
- Carter argues the small-block side won the blocksize wars rightly: Bitcoin should be a slow settlement layer with faster payments built on top, as Fedwire sits under Visa and PayPal ([Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/)).
- He sees Ethereum as a deliberately different, more adaptive trade-off and calls the two mutualistic, with over a billion dollars of bitcoin tokenized on Ethereum ([Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/)).

### Inflation, scarcity and money

- Robert Breedlove calls inflation a technology backdoor and says “counterfeiting is criminalized inflation inflation is legalized counterfeiting” ([Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/)).
- He describes Bitcoin as the discovery of absolute scarcity, with a supply schedule that ends in 2140, and expects network effects to make it winner-take-all ([Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/)).
- Fridman, who holds no cryptocurrency, says certainty makes him sceptical and asks for kindness instead of toxicity. Tyler Cowen sees Bitcoin taking over some of gold's role but is less sure about transaction uses such as DeFi ([Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/), [Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).

### Growth, big business and conformity

- Cowen has turned optimistic about growth, pointing to green energy, batteries and other advances rooted in cheaper computation. He argues big business has mostly been a hero in American history ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).
- He disagrees with Eric Weinstein on immigration, trade and academia, which he calls only partially bankrupt. He sees the worst conformity at schools just below the top dozen ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).

### Power, history and the presidency

- Yannis Pappas says of power, “i don't think it changes anyone it just reveals your darkest”. He argues Crete's resistance in 1941 delayed Hitler's invasion of Russia ([Yannis Pappas](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/yannis-pappas-history-and-comedy/)).
- Jeremi Suri argues the presidency now concentrates more power in one person than is healthy. He names Lincoln the greatest president and praises Washington for giving power up ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/jeremi-suri-history-of-american-power/)).

### Search, strategy and surprise

- Risto Miikkulainen argues evolutionary algorithms are creative because they lack human biases. One evolved network won a tic-tac-toe tournament by making rival programs run out of memory ([Risto Miikkulainen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/risto-miikkulainen-neuroevolution-and-evolutionary-computation/)).
- He says evolution can tune deep learning architectures and loss functions, and reports a 15 percent improvement on a winning image-captioning design ([Risto Miikkulainen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/risto-miikkulainen-neuroevolution-and-evolutionary-computation/)).
- Georges St-Pierre says he loved the science of fighting, not fighting. Frame-by-frame analysis showed BJ Penn had a fast reaction time but a slow reset, which St-Pierre exploited with constant feints ([Georges St-Pierre](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/georges-st-pierre-the-science-of-fighting/)).
