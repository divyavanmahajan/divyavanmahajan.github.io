## Overview

September 2021 was a busy month of 12 episodes, many with veterans of computing and AI. Douglas Lenat, Jay McClelland and Jeffrey Shainline offered three very different routes to machine intelligence, while Rodney Brooks, Jaron Lanier and Donald Knuth warned against over-reading demos and tools. Education, philosophy, sport, 9/11 and pandemic policy filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Competing routes to machine intelligence

- Douglas Lenat argues AI stays brittle without common sense, and says Cyc now holds tens of millions of hand-built assertions after about 37 years of work ([Douglas Lenat](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/douglas-lenat-cyc-and-the-quest-to-solve-common-sense-reasoning-in-ai/)).
- Jay McClelland, co-author of the PDP books, says cognition emerges from connection weights between simple units, and recounts how backpropagation came out of the PDP group ([Jay McClelland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jay-mcclelland-neural-networks-and-the-emergence-of-cognition/)).
- Jeffrey Shainline proposes brain-scale hardware that computes with superconducting electronics and communicates with single photons, accepting 4 kelvin cooling as the cost ([Jeffrey Shainline](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jeffrey-shainline-neuromorphic-computing-and-optoelectronic-intelligen/)).

### Sceptics of demos and automation

- Rodney Brooks says people have over-read self-driving demos for 35 years, and expects early deployments in campuses and gated communities. Fridman cites Tesla and Waymo as surprises ([Rodney Brooks](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/rodney-brooks-robotics/)).
- Brooks recounts iRobot's 14 failed business models before the Roomba, and how Rethink Robotics lost $150 million ([Rodney Brooks](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/rodney-brooks-robotics/)).
- Jaron Lanier says there is no AI, only algorithms people should control. He blames social media harms on crude engagement incentives and proposes data unions ([Jaron Lanier](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jaron-lanier-virtual-reality-social-media-the-future-of-humans-and-ai/)).
- Donald Knuth worries that tools like Codex and Copilot cost society its understanding of what machines do; Fridman sees automation freeing people for harder work ([Donald Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/donald-knuth-programming-algorithms-hard-problems-the-game-of-life/)).

### Software craft and open source

- Knuth calls literate programming the most significant result of TeX, and explains premature optimisation with a compiler that spent most of its time reading comment cards ([Donald Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/donald-knuth-programming-algorithms-hard-problems-the-game-of-life/)).
- Travis Oliphant describes building SciPy, NumPy, Numba and conda to scratch his own itch, and argues open source still lacks a way to pay its maintainers ([Travis Oliphant](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/travis-oliphant-numpy-scipy-anaconda-python-scientific-programming/)).

### Learning, meaning and resilience

- Jo Boaler argues maths is visual and creative, that anyone can learn it, and that teachers' and parents' messages shape whether students persist ([Jo Boaler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jo-boaler-how-to-learn-math/)).
- Sean Kelly says meaning comes from responding well to the situations we are thrown into, and warns against treating ourselves as resources to optimise ([Sean Kelly](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/sean-kelly-existentialism-nihilism-and-the-search-for-meaning/)).
- Travis Stevens describes how strategy, accepting reality and a change of mindset took him from a disputed 2012 loss to Olympic silver in 2016 ([Travis Stevens](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/travis-stevens-judo-olympics-and-mental-toughness/)).

### Institutions under strain

- Vincent Racaniello argues the tools for pandemics exist, but funding dried up after SARS. Fridman's main concern is leaders who spoke with unearned certainty and damaged trust in science ([Vincent Racaniello](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/vincent-racaniello-viruses-and-vaccines/)).
- Retired firefighter Niels Jorgensen recounts September 11 and its aftermath, and says responders who fell ill were later let down by politicians and institutions ([Niels Jorgensen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/niels-jorgensen-new-york-firefighters-and-the-heroes-of-9-11/)).
