## Overview

September 2021 brought in several founders of the field, from Rodney Brooks and Douglas Lenat to Jay McClelland and Donald Knuth, each with a different view of how intelligence should be built. Programming and scientific software, brain-like hardware and the search for meaning filled out the month. Two episodes looked at how institutions handled the pandemic and the aftermath of 9/11.

## Main themes

### Many routes to AI

- Rodney Brooks says people keep over-reading demos, and that perception and deployment in messy environments stay hard. He still holds that “machines can think because i believe you're a machine and i'm a machine” ([Rodney Brooks](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/rodney-brooks-robotics/)).
- Douglas Lenat argues AI stays brittle without common sense. After about 37 years, Cyc holds tens of millions of hand-built assertions and can now pair with machine learning ([Douglas Lenat](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/douglas-lenat-cyc-and-the-quest-to-solve-common-sense-reasoning-in-ai/)).
- Jay McClelland takes the opposite route: cognition emerges from connection weights between simple units. He recounts how the PDP group's work led to backpropagation ([Jay McClelland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jay-mcclelland-neural-networks-and-the-emergence-of-cognition/)).
- Jaron Lanier rejects the idea of AI as a creature: “there's just algorithms we make them we control them”. He blames social media's harms on crude engagement incentives ([Jaron Lanier](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jaron-lanier-virtual-reality-social-media-the-future-of-humans-and-ai/)).

### Programming and scientific software

- Donald Knuth argues programs should be written for human readers and optimised only after measuring. He worries that tools like Codex and Copilot cost society control and understanding; Fridman is more optimistic ([Donald Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/donald-knuth-programming-algorithms-hard-problems-the-game-of-life/)).
- Travis Oliphant says array programming changes how you think about data: “you start to think at that level you think in end dimensions” ([Travis Oliphant](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/travis-oliphant-numpy-scipy-anaconda-python-scientific-programming/)).
- Oliphant traces SciPy, NumPy, Numba and conda to his own research needs. His companies Anaconda, Quansight and OpenTeams try to pay open source maintainers, and he says Microsoft offered to buy Anaconda in 2014 ([Travis Oliphant](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/travis-oliphant-numpy-scipy-anaconda-python-scientific-programming/)).

### Brain-like hardware and the universe

- Jeffrey Shainline argues brain-scale hardware should compute with superconducting electronics at 4 kelvin and communicate with single photons ([Jeffrey Shainline](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jeffrey-shainline-neuromorphic-computing-and-optoelectronic-intelligen/)).
- Shainline extends Lee Smolin's cosmological natural selection to suggest the universe may be tuned to allow technology. Lanier describes a paper with Smolin linking physics to machine learning ([Jeffrey Shainline](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jeffrey-shainline-neuromorphic-computing-and-optoelectronic-intelligen/), [Jaron Lanier](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jaron-lanier-virtual-reality-social-media-the-future-of-humans-and-ai/)).

### Meaning, learning and toughness

- Sean Kelly argues meaning comes from responding well to the situations we are thrown into, not from radical freedom or self-optimisation. He also argues AI cannot be an artist because art is social communication ([Sean Kelly](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/sean-kelly-existentialism-nihilism-and-the-search-for-meaning/)).
- Jo Boaler says maths is a visual, creative subject anyone can learn, and that the belief in a fixed maths brain does lasting harm ([Jo Boaler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jo-boaler-how-to-learn-math/)).
- Travis Stevens describes how accepting what he could not change carried him from a disputed loss at London 2012 to silver in Rio ([Travis Stevens](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/travis-stevens-judo-olympics-and-mental-toughness/)).

### Institutions under strain

- Vincent Racaniello argues the tools for pandemics exist but underinvestment after SARS left the world unprepared. Fridman's main concern is public trust after overconfident official messaging ([Vincent Racaniello](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/vincent-racaniello-viruses-and-vaccines/)).
- Niels Jorgensen recounts 9/11 and the years-long fight to get Congress to cover responders' illnesses, which he says still leaves gaps ([Niels Jorgensen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/niels-jorgensen-new-york-firefighters-and-the-heroes-of-9-11/)).
