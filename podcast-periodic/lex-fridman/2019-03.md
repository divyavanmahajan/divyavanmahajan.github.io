## Overview

March 2019 had two very different guests. MIT roboticist Leslie Kaelbling made a pragmatic engineering case for how to build intelligent robots, while mathematician Eric Weinstein ranged across AI risk, physics, academia and the economy. Both were critical of how research institutions set their incentives.

## Main themes

### Building intelligent robots pragmatically

- Kaelbling treats robot intelligence as an engineering question: the open problem is deciding what to build in and what to learn. She rejects both hand-written expert rules and giant end-to-end networks trained from scratch ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- She argues spatial and temporal abstraction make long-horizon planning possible, using the example of planning a flight to New York without planning the walk through the airport ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- Planning in belief space lets a robot choose information-gathering actions, like a driver glancing over a shoulder. She wants more built-in priors, such as the idea that objects exist ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- She says expert systems failed because experts cannot articulate how they decide; their explanations are post-hoc stories ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).

### Danger without general intelligence

- Weinstein argues the near-term risk is software that evolves under selective pressure and exploits people without understanding anything, which he calls artificial outtelligence ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
- He says a generation that never felt existential danger treats nuclear brinkmanship lightly, and asks why devices lack physical camera shutters and unbypassable indicator lights ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).

### Research incentives and the economy

- Kaelbling founded the open-access Journal of Machine Learning Research after most of an editorial board resigned over publisher prices. She worries short research horizons discourage hard multi-year problems ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- Weinstein says string theory has not delivered and that little theory since 1973-74 has predicted an experiment well enough to win a Nobel. He argues academics must stay loyal to consensus while outsiders often lose their way ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
- He warns that repetitive jobs are exposed to automation and that capitalists will invite revolution unless they support people whose labour no longer pays enough ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
