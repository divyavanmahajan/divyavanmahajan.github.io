## Overview

December paired one strong AI episode, Noam Brown on poker and Diplomacy bots, with conversations on fraud, political systems and the search for life beyond Earth. Coffeezilla dissected the FTX collapse weeks after it happened, while Michael Malice and Bhaskar Sunkara gave opposing readings of socialism. Two astrobiologists and a jiu jitsu champion rounded out the month.

## Main themes

### AI that plans, negotiates and earns trust

- Noam Brown argues that real-time search and planning, not just bigger neural nets, drove the poker breakthroughs. Libratus approximated a Nash equilibrium instead of exploiting opponents and beat four top pros in 2017 ([Brown](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/noam-brown-ai-vs-humans-in-poker-and-games-of-strategic-negotiation/)).
- Brown says depth-limited search let the six-player bot Pluribus train for under $150 on AWS, against roughly $100,000 for Libratus ([Brown](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/noam-brown-ai-vs-humans-in-poker-and-games-of-strategic-negotiation/)).
- For Diplomacy, Meta's Cicero pairs a language model with intents from a planning model anchored to human play. Brown says lying made the bot score worse because people stopped trusting it ([Brown](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/noam-brown-ai-vs-humans-in-poker-and-games-of-strategic-negotiation/)).
- Nathalie Cabrol calls AI a very smart tool built by humans, pushing back on Fridman's view that it may out-understand us ([Cabrol](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/nathalie-cabrol-search-for-alien-life/)).

### Fraud, trust and corruption

- Coffeezilla argues FTX's collapse was designed deception, not negligence, pointing to about 50 entities and customer deposits routed through Alameda Research ([Coffeezilla](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/coffeezilla-sbf-ftx-fraud-scams-fake-gurus-money-fame-and-power/)).
- He says Sequoia, BlackRock and the media endorsed Sam Bankman-Fried without due diligence, and links FTX to rug pulls, MLMs and online gurus that sell a dream ([Coffeezilla](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/coffeezilla-sbf-ftx-fraud-scams-fake-gurus-money-fame-and-power/)).
- Ed Calderon says Mexico's drug war persists because corruption runs through police, politicians and the military, with US guns and Chinese fentanyl precursors making it a shared regional problem ([Calderon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/ed-calderon-mexican-drug-cartels/)).

### Socialism and its history

- Michael Malice presents The White Pill, arguing that Soviet history shows evil is real but defeat is never certain, and credits Gorbachev most for the peaceful collapse of 1989 ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/michael-malice-christmas-special/)).
- Malice says central planning without prices, and a state news monopoly, necessarily bring authoritarianism. Both he and Fridman praise the Twitter Files as a check on power ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/michael-malice-christmas-special/)).
- Bhaskar Sunkara argues for market socialism of worker-owned firms and public banks, beyond Nordic social democracy. Fridman presses him on incentives, authoritarianism and tax ([Sunkara](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/bhaskar-sunkara-the-case-for-socialism/)).

### Life beyond Earth

- Cabrol says the search should target the nature of life, framed as gathering, storing and exchanging information, and expects simple life to be common in the universe ([Cabrol](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/nathalie-cabrol-search-for-alien-life/)).
- Cabrol notes that SETI searches for messages, not UFOs, and that UFO folklore has cost it government funding ([Cabrol](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/nathalie-cabrol-search-for-alien-life/)).
- Betül Kaçar describes life as chemistry that explores solutions and remembers what works, and Fridman keeps returning to computing analogies. She prefers studying other planets' chemistry to seeding life there ([Kaçar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/betul-kacar-bet-l-ka-ar-origin-of-life-ancient-dna-panspermia-and-alie/)).

### Mastery through practice

- Roger Gracie says his simple game took years of live practice against resisting opponents, and that most people train to get tough rather than good ([Gracie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/roger-gracie-greatest-jiu-jitsu-competitor-of-all-time/)).
- Calderon teaches adversarial thinking: to train a counter-ambush team, you make its members good ambushers ([Calderon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/12/ed-calderon-mexican-drug-cartels/)).
