## Overview

October 2020 leaned towards computing. Scott Aaronson, Chris Lattner and George Hotz covered complexity theory, programming languages and self-driving, with GPT-3 coming up in two of those conversations. Alongside them, Lisa Feldman Barrett explained the brain as a prediction engine, and Michael Malice and Eric Weinstein argued about the state, institutions and cancel culture in the run-up to the US election.

## Main themes

### Computation, complexity and consciousness

- Scott Aaronson says the evidence so far fits the Church-Turing thesis, so nothing outside computation is needed to explain physics ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- He argues integrated information theory fails a basic test: a grid of XOR gates could score as more conscious than a human. He puts P equalling NP at two or three percent ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- Aaronson calls GPT-3 a major advance that can write a B-minus essay but struggles with arithmetic. Chris Lattner says GPT-style models lack a way to express intent and check correctness ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/), [Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).

### Languages, compilers and chips

- Lattner describes a programming language as a user interface between humans and hardware, valued for the productivity it gives people. He uses Swift's value semantics and progressive disclosure as examples ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).
- He presents MLIR as infrastructure for domain-specific compilers and RISC-V as an open instruction set. With single-threaded speed-ups over, he says the burden moves to languages, libraries and custom hardware ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).
- George Hotz criticises Nvidia's pricing for the A100 and says Google's cloud terms ruled out TPUs for training autonomous vehicles ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)).

### Learning to drive end to end

- Hotz argues self-driving will be solved by neural networks learning from real drivers, and calls Tesla's multi-task approach a higher-level form of feature engineering ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)).
- He says openpilot's unplanned disengagements fell from one every 10 miles to one every 100 in a year, still far from the 100,000 needed. Fridman stresses that driver attention and user experience are the hard part ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)).
- Hotz positions Comma as a self-funding, open-source level 2 system that sells hardware and rejects acquisition offers ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)).

### How the brain and the body work

- Lisa Feldman Barrett argues the brain predicts the world rather than reacting to it, and that its main job is running the body's energy budget ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/lisa-feldman-barrett-counterintuitive-ideas-about-how-the-brain-works/)).
- She calls the triune brain a myth and describes emotions as constructed concepts shaped by culture, with no single biomarker for any emotion ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/lisa-feldman-barrett-counterintuitive-ideas-about-how-the-brain-works/)).
- Manolis Kellis says disease research is becoming a data-driven, systems discipline, where automation and machine learning shorten the path from discovery to therapy. He wants computer scientists in biology ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/manolis-kellis-biology-of-disease/)).

### The state, institutions and discourse

- Michael Malice makes the case for anarchism and for mocking the powerful. Fridman plays angel's advocate, arguing that trolling deepens division ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/michael-malice-anarchy-democracy-libertarianism-love-and-trolling/)).
- Eric Weinstein argues that US institutions have stalled and punish innovators, and that nobody is looking for new orchards. Fridman counters that good people and new platforms can still win ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
- Malice and Aaronson both criticise cancel culture. Fridman defends MIT's science faculty, and Aaronson calls the US COVID response an institutional failure ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/michael-malice-anarchy-democracy-libertarianism-love-and-trolling/), [Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
