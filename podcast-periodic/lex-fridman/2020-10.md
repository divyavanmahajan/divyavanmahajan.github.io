## Overview

October ran up to the 2020 US election, and politics framed two of the month's biggest conversations: Michael Malice on anarchism and trolling, and Eric Weinstein on failing institutions. The rest leaned technical, with Scott Aaronson on computation and consciousness, Chris Lattner on languages and chips, George Hotz on self-driving and Lisa Feldman Barrett on the predictive brain.

## Main themes

### Institutions, cancel culture and the election

- Eric Weinstein argues that ageing US institutions punish innovators and can destroy anyone's reputation. He says “every single person is signed up right now to be ruined by the system” ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
- Fridman pushes back that pockets of creative play survive at MIT, proposes giving young faculty more power than administrators, and says he is worried about the months around the election ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
- Weinstein says the federal government stopped funding risk-taking science, leaving a gap Jeffrey Epstein filled. He speculates Epstein was a government construct; Fridman suspects weakness rather than conspiracy ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
- Scott Aaronson also criticises cancel culture and argues the silent supporters of open discourse need to speak up ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).

### Anarchism, trolling and Trump

- Michael Malice argues political authority is illegitimate and that mockery is the best way to strip the powerful of authority. Fridman counters that trolling escalates conflict and erodes the love needed for nuanced conversation ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/michael-malice-anarchy-democracy-libertarianism-love-and-trolling/)).
- Malice calls cancel culture a form of Maoism and the universities the real villains. He reads Trump's refusal to send troops into Portland without the mayor's request as restraint ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/michael-malice-anarchy-democracy-libertarianism-love-and-trolling/)).

### Consciousness, Penrose and complexity

- Aaronson says Penrose “wants the brain to be exploiting as yet unknown laws of quantum gravity” and counts several separate speculations in that view, including a Gödel argument he considers unsound ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- Aaronson argues integrated information theory fails because a simple grid of XOR gates could score as more conscious than a human. He puts two or three percent odds on P equalling NP ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- Barrett argues the brain predicts rather than reacts, and that emotions are concepts it constructs, shaped by culture ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/lisa-feldman-barrett-counterintuitive-ideas-about-how-the-brain-works/)).

### GPT-3, self-driving and learned systems

- Aaronson calls GPT-3 a major advance over ELIZA that can write a B-minus student essay, though nobody knows where scaling hits its ceiling ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- George Hotz argues end-to-end networks trained on real driver data will beat Tesla's feature-engineered approach, yet still thinks Tesla is on track to beat Comma. Openpilot disengagements fell from one every 10 miles to one every 100 ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/)).
- Chris Lattner sees machine learning as one programming paradigm among others and says GPT-style models lack a way to express intent and check correctness ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).

### Languages, compilers and chips

- Lattner treats a language as a user interface between humans and hardware, and explains Swift's value semantics and progressive disclosure ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).
- He presents MLIR as infrastructure for domain-specific compilers and RISC-V as an open instruction set, and says single-threaded speed doubling is over ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).
- Manolis Kellis argues that computation, automation and large pooled experiments are turning disease research into a data-driven discipline ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/manolis-kellis-biology-of-disease/)).
