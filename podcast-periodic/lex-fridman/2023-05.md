## Overview

May leaned towards law, politics and the university. Two lawyers, Harvey Silverglate and Randall Kennedy, argued about free speech, race and affirmative action, and David Pakman gave a progressive reading of Trump and Biden. Stephen Wolfram supplied the month's main AI conversation, while Anna Frebel and Neil Gershenfeld covered the early universe and the future of making things.

## Main themes

### ChatGPT as an interface to computation

- Stephen Wolfram calls ChatGPT wide and shallow, and says one of its big features is that “it's a linguistic user interface” that should hand precise work to Wolfram Language ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- He warns against expecting facts from it: asked for the song HAL sang in 2001, it played Mary Had a Little Lamb ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- Wolfram ties computational irreducibility to AI risk, where he is an optimist, and to physics, arguing that relativity, quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics come from computationally bounded observers ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- AI in education came up twice: Wolfram proposes teaching "computational X" to everyone, and Pakman discussed ChatGPT as a pressure on traditional university teaching ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/), [Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).

### Free speech and the campus

- Harvey Silverglate, a FIRE co-founder, says free speech matters most on campuses because that is where young citizens are educated, and blames administrators who enforce speech codes ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).
- His remedy is blunt: “Get rid of 95% of the administrators, get rid of their speech codes, reduce tuition by 40%” ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).
- Silverglate also argues the FBI should be abolished because its culture under J. Edgar Hoover cannot be reformed ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).

### Race, law and affirmative action

- Randall Kennedy argues the N-word's meaning depends on context and that people should be educated about it, not shielded from it ([Kennedy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/randall-kennedy-the-n-word-history-of-race-law-politics-and-power/)).
- He calls state bans on critical race theory “a frightening encroachment on freedom”, while rejecting the movement's view that racism is permanent ([Kennedy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/randall-kennedy-the-n-word-history-of-race-law-politics-and-power/)).
- Kennedy sets out reparative, distributive and diversity justifications for affirmative action and thinks its benefits outweigh stigma and resentment. Silverglate opposes it and predicted a 6-3 Supreme Court ruling against it ([Kennedy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/randall-kennedy-the-n-word-history-of-race-law-politics-and-power/), [Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).

### Trump, Biden and the left

- David Pakman, a self-described social democrat, calls Trump's strengths mostly presentational and says the China tariffs cost about a quarter million American jobs ([Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).
- Pakman argues Trump would have been re-elected had he handled COVID differently. Fridman pushes back on wokeism and merit at universities ([Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).
- Silverglate thinks the Alvin Bragg prosecution of Trump is improper, while believing Trump has committed tax crimes ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).

### Stars and fabrication

- Anna Frebel reads the chemistry of the Milky Way's oldest, metal-poor stars as a record of the first stars, supernovae and neutron star mergers ([Frebel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/anna-frebel-origin-and-evolution-of-the-universe-galaxies-and-stars/)).
- Neil Gershenfeld says fabrication is the next digital revolution, with machines assembling reusable parts until assemblers can make assemblers. He cites about 2,500 Fab Labs in 125 countries ([Gershenfeld](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/neil-gershenfeld-self-replicating-robots-and-the-future-of-fabrication/)).
