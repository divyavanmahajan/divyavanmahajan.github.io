## Overview

May split evenly between science and society. Three guests talked about free speech, race and American politics, while the other three covered language models, the first stars and machines that build machines. Stephen Wolfram's episode was the month's only direct look at ChatGPT.

## Main themes

### ChatGPT needs computation underneath

- Stephen Wolfram describes LLMs as a wide, shallow linguistic interface that should hand off to Wolfram Language for anything that must be correct, as in the ChatGPT plugin workflow ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- He gives examples of plausible but wrong output, such as a word problem solved correctly except for the last two lines, and warns against expecting facts from a language model ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- On AI risk he calls himself an optimist: computational irreducibility means no sandbox is perfect, but no single catastrophe is certain either. He expects boilerplate programming to fade while far more people use computation ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).

### Free speech and campus censorship

- Harvey Silverglate, co-founder of FIRE, calls himself a free speech absolutist and blames university administrators for speech codes. He says Harvard should fire 95% of its administrators ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).
- Randall Kennedy argues people should be educated about the N-word rather than shielded from it. He objects to required DEI statements and to state bans on critical race theory ([Kennedy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/randall-kennedy-the-n-word-history-of-race-law-politics-and-power/)).
- Fridman raised campus censorship and merit with David Pakman too. Pakman agrees excesses exist but says the problem is smaller than the right claims ([Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).

### Race, law and American politics

- Kennedy sees affirmative action as costly in stigma but worth it. Silverglate opposes it and predicted a 6-3 Supreme Court ruling against it ([Kennedy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/randall-kennedy-the-n-word-history-of-race-law-politics-and-power/), [Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).
- Pakman, a progressive social democrat, expects Biden to beat Trump in a 2024 rematch. He reads Tucker Carlson's firing as legal risk management after the Dominion settlement ([Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).
- Silverglate says the FBI cannot be reformed and should be abolished, and calls the Alvin Bragg prosecution of Trump improper ([Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/)).

### Stars, atoms and fabrication

- Anna Frebel reads the chemistry of the Milky Way's oldest, most metal-poor stars as a record of the first stars, supernovae and neutron star mergers ([Frebel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/anna-frebel-origin-and-evolution-of-the-universe-galaxies-and-stars/)).
- Neil Gershenfeld argues fabrication is the next digital revolution after communication and computation: machines assembling reusable parts until assemblers can make assemblers ([Gershenfeld](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/neil-gershenfeld-self-replicating-robots-and-the-future-of-fabrication/)).
- He says computing has caught up with the brain, but chip fabs place parts about eight orders of magnitude slower than biology. Fab Labs have grown to about 2,500 in 125 countries ([Gershenfeld](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/neil-gershenfeld-self-replicating-robots-and-the-future-of-fabrication/)).
