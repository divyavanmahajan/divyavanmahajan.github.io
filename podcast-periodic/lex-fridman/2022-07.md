## Overview

July 2022 opened and nearly closed with DeepMind: Demis Hassabis on the company's mission and Oriol Vinyals on Gato as a first general agent. Between them, Joe Rogan's 300th-episode conversation and a former KGB spy brought Putin and the war in Ukraine to the fore. The rest of the month ranged over intelligence research, heterodox economics, Catholic theology, cosmology and crypto regulation.

## Main themes

### DeepMind's route to general intelligence

- Hassabis restates the 2010 mission as “step one solve intelligence step two use it to solve everything else”. He says he tried to build DeepMind as a new kind of Bell Labs, mixing neuroscience, machine learning, engineering and mathematics ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- He traces a line from AlphaGo through AlphaZero and MuZero that steadily removed human knowledge, with games as the training ground ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- Vinyals describes Gato as a roughly one-billion-parameter transformer with a single brain for text, images and actions, trained by imitation. It is not yet as good as the specialist agents it learned from, and he hopes scale brings synergy between tasks ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Vinyals argues for reusing trained weights, as Flamingo did with Chinchilla, and for teaching models new tasks through interaction ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/)).

### AI as a tool for science

- Hassabis calls AlphaFold the most complex and meaningful system DeepMind has built so far. He says DeepMind open-sourced it after consulting about 30 bioethicists ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- He also points to reinforcement learning control of plasma in a fusion tokamak and learned models for quantum chemistry ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- Martin Rees suggests string theory may be too hard for human brains but workable for AI. He warns that such theories could predict correctly without giving humans real insight ([Martin Rees](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/martin-rees-black-holes-alien-life-dark-matter-and-the-big-bang/)).

### Consciousness, sentience and responsible release

- Hassabis guesses that “consciousness and intelligence are double dissociable”. He would rather build AI as non-conscious tools for now, and says large language models are not ready to deploy at scale ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- Both DeepMind guests dismiss the claim that Google's LaMDA was sentient. Fridman predicts a civil rights movement for robots as people form relationships with AI ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/), [Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).

### Putin, spies and the war in Ukraine

- Recorded days before Fridman left for Ukraine and Russia, the Rogan episode covers how to question Putin. Fridman thinks Putin would focus on legacy and genuinely wants the best for Russia ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/joe-rogan-comedy-controversy-aliens-ufos-putin-cia-and-freedom/)).
- Former KGB illegal Jack Barsky says Putin was open about restoring a strong Russia. He blames US policy: “We were engaged in wishful thinking, and we didn't stop it with sanctions before the shooting started.” ([Jack Barsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/jack-barsky-kgb-spy/)).
- Barsky also describes KGB training and why love for his daughter made him quit ([Jack Barsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/jack-barsky-kgb-spy/)).

### Economics, crypto and belief

- Steve Keen argues mainstream economics wrongly assumes equilibrium and ignores money, private debt and energy, and underestimates climate risk ([Steve Keen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/steve-keen-marxism-capitalism-and-economics/)).
- Brian Armstrong presents Coinbase as a regulated on-ramp and proposes a simple legal test to sort crypto assets between regulators ([Brian Armstrong](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/brian-armstrong-coinbase-cryptocurrency-and-government-regulation/)).
- Bishop Robert Barron argues God is the ground of being rather than a being, and that love means willing the good of the other ([Robert Barron](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/robert-barron-bishop-robert-barron-christianity-and-the-catholic-churc/)).
