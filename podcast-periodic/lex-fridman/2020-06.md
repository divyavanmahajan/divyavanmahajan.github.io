## Overview

June 2020 split between big questions about minds and AGI and practical lessons from long careers. Joscha Bach and Ben Goertzel argued about what intelligence and consciousness are and how to build them. David Patterson, Robert Langer and Steven Pressfield described what decades of disciplined work in hardware, medicine and writing look like.

## Main themes

### Minds, consciousness and the self

- Bach argues the mind is software that models both the world and a self living in it, and that only a simulation, not a physical system, can be conscious ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).
- He calls consciousness a model of the contents of attention, and says transformer attention captures only one aspect of it ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).
- Pressfield frames creative struggle as ego against the greater self, with Resistance as the ego's fearful voice ([Steven Pressfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/steven-pressfield-the-war-of-art/)).

### Different roads to AGI

- Goertzel argues current deep networks will not reach AGI alone. OpenCog puts logic, neural and evolutionary learning into one shared hypergraph, the Atomspace ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- He wants AI built on open, decentralized infrastructure such as SingularityNET, which ran about 5,200 AIs at the time, so early AGIs do not serve only a few corporations or governments ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Goertzel uses a transformer only as a sentence-probability oracle guiding a symbolic learner, and recounts how the term AGI was coined around 2001-2002 ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).

### Hardware after Moore's law

- Patterson says RISC won once the case was made quantitative: perhaps 25-50% more instructions, but about two cycles per instruction against roughly ten for CISC ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- He says general-purpose processors now improve only a few percent a year, calls machine learning a godsend for hardware designers, and points to MLPerf and Intel's purchase of Habana ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- He predicts RISC-V may become the most popular instruction set, starting with embedded devices ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).

### Long careers and disciplined work

- Langer says big advances come from platform ideas, surviving early rejection and years of costly development, and has helped launch about 40 companies. He calls the business people the most important factor ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
- He argues patents are what fund the cost of bringing new products to market, while admitting they can limit access ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
- Pressfield says there is no shortcut: mundane daily discipline over decades, with about four hours of writing a day as his maximum ([Steven Pressfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/steven-pressfield-the-war-of-art/)).
- Patterson judges his career by people rather than papers, and Langer is proudest of his former students ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/), [Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
