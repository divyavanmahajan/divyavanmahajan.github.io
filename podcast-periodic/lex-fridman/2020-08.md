## Overview

August 2020 was the month GPT-3 arrived in the conversation: it came up in four of seven episodes, and most guests doubted that scaling text models leads to real understanding. Dileep George and François Chollet offered alternatives built on world models and on measuring how efficiently systems learn. Robotics, neuroscience, mathematics teaching, mortality and the search for life on other planets filled out the month.

## Main themes

### GPT-3 and the limits of scaling

- Dileep George calls GPT-3 intriguing but says “it doesn't have a world model uh and it can't run simulations in its head”. He argues scaling alone will not give AGI ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/)).
- François Chollet says GPT-3 likely pattern-matches new tasks to ones seen in training, and expects training data, not model size, to be the bottleneck ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- David Eagleman calls GPT-3 a good impersonator whose best outputs are picked out by humans. Grant Sanderson expects it to struggle with maths, which needs hypothesis testing. Fridman stays more open to scale surprising people ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/), [Grant Sanderson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/grant-sanderson-math-manim-neural-networks-teaching-with-3blue1brown/)).

### Other routes to intelligence

- George turns neuroscience findings into functional models and tests them on tasks. His Recursive Cortical Network broke text CAPTCHAs from few examples ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/)).
- Chollet defines intelligence as the efficiency of acquiring new skills. His ARC benchmark has about a thousand grid tasks that need only core knowledge priors ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- Chollet thinks L4 driving is probably achievable but L5 probably not by pure end-to-end learning; Fridman pushes back on edge cases ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).

### Robots, physics and touch

- Russ Tedrake argues robots should work with their natural dynamics, and that contact is central to manipulation: “we mostly in robotics are afraid of contact on the rest of our body which is crazy” ([Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).
- From the DARPA Robotics Challenge with Boston Dynamics' Atlas humanoid, he concludes that robustness depends on simulation, testing and rigorous thinking ([Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).
- Tedrake worries deep learning makes it easy to skip rigorous thinking, and describes Drake and rare-event simulation at Toyota Research Institute ([Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).

### The adaptable brain and brain interfaces

- Eagleman describes the brain as livewired, constantly reconfiguring around whatever data matters. His company Neosensory sells a wristband that turns sound into vibration ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/)).
- Neuralink came up in three episodes. Eagleman doubts healthy people will accept surgery to type faster, George calls its intermediate steps reasonable, and Chollet does not see bandwidth as the bottleneck ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/), [Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/), [François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- Sanderson argues real understanding comes from rebuilding ideas yourself, and wants teachers to publish the best version of a few topics online ([Grant Sanderson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/grant-sanderson-math-manim-neural-networks-teaching-with-3blue1brown/)).

### Mortality, meaning and life elsewhere

- Sheldon Solomon, following Ernest Becker, argues awareness of death drives most human behaviour, and that facing it directly can lead to care for others ([Sheldon Solomon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/sheldon-solomon-death-and-meaning/)).
- Fridman suggests a sense of mortality may be needed for AI that humans can love ([Sheldon Solomon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/sheldon-solomon-death-and-meaning/)).
- Sara Seager expects signs of life to come as gases in exoplanet atmospheres, within one or two decades if the right telescopes are built, and as “a hint it's not going to be a 100 percent yay we found it” ([Sara Seager](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/sara-seager-search-for-planets-and-life-outside-our-solar-system/)).
