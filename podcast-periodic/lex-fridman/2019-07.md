## Overview

July ranged widely. Jeff Hawkins and Sean Carroll asked what intelligence and consciousness are, while Kai-Fu Lee and Chris Urmson dealt with industry questions: China's tech culture, jobs and self-driving safety. Gustav Söderström closed the month with how Spotify uses machine learning in a consumer product.

## Main themes

### Where intelligence comes from

- Jeff Hawkins argues the neocortex runs one common algorithm, with thousands of cortical columns each modelling whole objects in reference frames and voting on an answer ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- He says Numenta found enforced sparsity makes convolutional networks harder to fool, and rejects Richard Sutton's view that scale alone is enough ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Sean Carroll says understanding particle physics gives no direct insight into the mind, because higher levels emerge and need their own descriptions. He expects a gradual approach to apparently conscious agents rather than one breakthrough ([Sean Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/)).
- Carroll argues against the simulation hypothesis and leans towards zero other intelligent species in the observable universe ([Sean Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/)).

### China, Silicon Valley and jobs

- Kai-Fu Lee contrasts Chinese teams that win on data, data cleaning and fast iteration with American teams that look for new algorithms ([Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- He describes Chinese companies moving from copying to original products such as Alipay and TikTok, with the state funding incubators and guiding funds ([Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- Lee expects routine white-collar work to be automated first and argues for retraining over a plain basic income ([Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).

### Self-driving: sensors, safety and deployment

- Chris Urmson says full self-driving is a different technology and business from Level 2 driver assistance, and warns that people will over-trust driver assistance ([Chris Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).
- Responding to Elon Musk, Urmson argues that with about 37,000 US road deaths a year any useful sensor, including lidar, should be used. Lee questions Tesla's decision to skip lidar too ([Chris Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/), [Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- Urmson proposes per-task failure comparisons with humans instead of disengagement counts, and explains why Aurora chose moderate-speed urban driving before highways ([Chris Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).

### Machine learning in a consumer product

- Gustav Söderström says Spotify won by beating piracy on speed and pairing free and paid tiers ([Gustav Soderstrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/gustav-soderstrom-spotify/)).
- He explains how some 3 billion user-made playlists made collaborative filtering work, and why Discover Weekly can take risks while Daily Mix must stay safe ([Gustav Soderstrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/gustav-soderstrom-spotify/)).
