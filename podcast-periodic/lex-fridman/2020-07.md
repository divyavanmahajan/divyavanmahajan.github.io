## Overview

July 2020 was a busy month of eight episodes. Robotics and vision researchers argued that machines must learn from experience the way children do, and a DeepMind neuroscientist described ideas moving between brains and AI. Computer science pioneers looked back on Unix and complexity theory, while philosophers and physicists took on suffering, energy and faith.

## Main themes

### Learning from experience, not just data

- Sergey Levine says robot hardware is close to good enough and the gap is intelligence. He sees offline reinforcement learning on large logged datasets as the key open problem ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/)).
- Jitendra Malik argues vision should be learned the way children learn: multimodal, incremental and tied to action. He estimates video recognition is about 10 years behind object recognition ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/)).
- Malik's fallacy of the first step (90% in a day, 99.99% perhaps never) makes him a pessimist on near-term full self-driving ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/)).

### Brains and AI informing each other

- Matt Botvinick describes a virtuous circle at DeepMind: meta-learning and distributional reinforcement learning suggest how the brain works, and brain evidence validates the methods ([Matt Botvinick](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/matt-botvinick-neuroscience-psychology-and-ai-at-deepmind/)).
- He suggests an AI that people trust as warm, not only competent, would be the ultimate Turing test ([Matt Botvinick](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/matt-botvinick-neuroscience-psychology-and-ai-at-deepmind/)).
- Manolis Kellis argues the genome's defining trait is resilience, and that engineers and AI designers can learn robustness from how evolution builds and trims complexity ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/manolis-kellis-human-genome-and-evolutionary-dynamics/)).

### Foundations of computing

- Brian Kernighan says Unix succeeded as an environment built for programmers on modest hardware, inside a cooperative Bell Labs community, and that its spread owed more to luck than inevitability ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/)).
- Kernighan worries that bias in training data gets propagated, and that pip and npm leave programmers not knowing what they installed ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/)).
- Richard Karp would bet that P is not equal to NP, and notes that deep learning works empirically without theoretical understanding ([Richard Karp](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/richard-karp-algorithms-and-computational-complexity/)).

### Suffering, ethics and belief

- Peter Singer argues suffering matters regardless of species and that robots deserve rights only if they become conscious. Fridman pushes back that a convincing display of pain might be enough ([Peter Singer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/peter-singer-suffering-in-humans-animals-and-ai/)).
- Singer supports some AI control research but questions treating extinction risk as infinite ([Peter Singer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/peter-singer-suffering-in-humans-animals-and-ai/)).
- Ian Hutchinson argues against scientism, defines faith as belief, trust and loyalty, and calls transhumanism a nightmare ([Ian Hutchinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/ian-hutchinson-nuclear-fusion-plasma-physics-and-religion/)).

### Energy and the limits of technology

- Hutchinson wants more fusion funding and calls fission clean and underrated, but says energy, population and pollution are human problems with no technological fix ([Ian Hutchinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/ian-hutchinson-nuclear-fusion-plasma-physics-and-religion/)).
- Malik and Levine both put present-day misuse and harms ahead of AGI fears ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/), [Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/)).
