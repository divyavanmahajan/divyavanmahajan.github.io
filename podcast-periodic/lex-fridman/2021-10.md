## Overview

October 2021 had little direct AI content. Brian Greene and Stephen Wolfram took on the deepest physics questions, Alex Gladstein made the human rights case for Bitcoin, and two guests challenged how US institutions regulate drugs and tests. The rest of the month looked at violence and war, and at how champions and artists are made.

## Main themes

### Physics, computation and meaning

- Brian Greene accepts that entropy means everything eventually decays, but argues that a universe without built-in purpose leaves people free to make their own meaning ([Brian Greene](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/brian-greene-quantum-gravity-big-bang-aliens-life-death-and-meaning/)).
- Greene says string theory has made strong theoretical progress but no contact with experiment, and thinks the simplest answer to the Fermi paradox is that there are no advanced civilisations. Fridman believes there are many ([Brian Greene](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/brian-greene-quantum-gravity-big-bang-aliens-life-death-and-meaning/)).
- Stephen Wolfram models the universe as a rewritten hypergraph and argues that the laws we see follow from us being computationally bounded observers ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/stephen-wolfram-complexity-and-the-fabric-of-reality/)).
- Wolfram calls consciousness narrower than intelligence; Fridman asks whether it could be more fundamental. Both guests also discussed machines and consciousness ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/stephen-wolfram-complexity-and-the-fabric-of-reality/), [Brian Greene](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/brian-greene-quantum-gravity-big-bang-aliens-life-death-and-meaning/)).

### Bitcoin against authoritarianism

- Alex Gladstein of the Human Rights Foundation says about 4.3 billion people live under authoritarian regimes, and that money has been left out of human rights debates ([Alex Gladstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/alex-gladstein-bitcoin-authoritarianism-and-human-rights/)).
- He argues Bitcoin's fixed 21 million supply makes it a savings account and payments network that regimes cannot stop, and calls almost all other tokens scams ([Alex Gladstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/alex-gladstein-bitcoin-authoritarianism-and-human-rights/)).
- Fridman warns that cult-like certainty looks like a scam signal, and counters Gladstein's fear of government big data with user-owned, distributed AI ([Alex Gladstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/alex-gladstein-bitcoin-authoritarianism-and-human-rights/)).

### Regulation and public health policy

- Michael Mina argues cheap rapid tests are held back because the FDA treats them as medical devices, and wants them designated public health tools ([Michael Mina](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/michael-mina-rapid-covid-testing/)).
- Carl Hart argues addiction is driven mainly by mental illness and social conditions, and that adults should have legal, regulated access to drugs. Fridman calls the ideas controversial and pushes back ([Carl Hart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/carl-hart-heroin-cocaine-mdma-alcohol-the-role-of-drugs-in-society/)).
- Hart lists law enforcement, prisons, media and politicians of both parties among those who benefit from the war on drugs ([Carl Hart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/carl-hart-heroin-cocaine-mdma-alcohol-the-role-of-drugs-in-society/)).

### Violence, war and recovery

- Richard Wrangham says humans pair low impulsive aggression with high planned, group violence, and that cooking created the genus Homo ([Richard Wrangham](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/richard-wrangham-role-of-violence-sex-and-fire-in-human-evolution/)).
- Wrangham welcomes falling violent death rates but warns that long peace builds dominance tensions, naming China and Taiwan ([Richard Wrangham](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/richard-wrangham-role-of-violence-sex-and-fire-in-human-evolution/)).
- Artillery veteran Kelsi Sheren recounts her Afghanistan deployment and argues militaries must train soldiers' mental resilience instead of defaulting to medication and discharge ([Kelsi Sheren](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/kelsi-sheren-war-artillery-ptsd-and-love/)).

### Building champions and craft

- Jimmy Pedro says the US must build judo champions through a deliberate system, constant competition and visualisation, and that they must hate losing more than they love winning ([Jimmy Pedro](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/jimmy-pedro-judo-and-the-forging-of-champions/)).
- RZA says creativity comes from absorbing life and studying masters, crediting mentors such as Quentin Tarantino and Ridley Scott ([RZA](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/rza-wu-tang-clan-kung-fu-chess-god-life-and-death/)).
