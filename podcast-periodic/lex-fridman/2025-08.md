## Overview

August leaned towards history and geopolitics. Three episodes looked at power from different angles: the Mongol Empire, China's economy under US trade pressure, and three decades of US military intervention. The month closed with a former Microsoft engineer's stories from the Windows NT era.

## Main themes

### Empire, conquest and how to judge it

- Jack Weatherford argues Genghis Khan's conquest lasted about 20 years, and that the institutions that followed (religious freedom, protected trade, merit-based promotion) let a nation of one million rule hundreds of millions ([Weatherford](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/jack-weatherford-genghis-khan-and-the-mongol-empire/)).
- He describes a cavalry army that probably never exceeded 100,000, organised in units of ten, and a leader who learned from failure ([Weatherford](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/jack-weatherford-genghis-khan-and-the-mongol-empire/)).
- Asked about an estimate of 40 million deaths, Weatherford says Americans should not condemn the Mongols while excusing their own wars, a theme Scott Horton picks up at length later in the month ([Weatherford](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/jack-weatherford-genghis-khan-and-the-mongol-empire/), [Horton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/scott-horton-the-case-against-war-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).

### China's economy and the trade war

- Keyu Jin argues the West misreads China as centrally run. She describes a mayor economy in which local officials competed on GDP growth for promotion, driving industrialisation and later a real estate boom ([Keyu Jin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/keyu-jin-china-s-economy-tariffs-trade-trump-communism-capitalism/)).
- She calls DeepSeek crisis innovation and says export controls and sanctions sped up Chinese self-sufficiency, as with Huawei. Leverage such as rare earths, she warns, loses force each time it is used ([Keyu Jin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/keyu-jin-china-s-economy-tariffs-trade-trump-communism-capitalism/)).
- Jin says tariffs do not work and that US trade deficits widened because the US saves less than it invests. China's real problems, in her view, are weak consumption, the property downturn and eroding meritocracy ([Keyu Jin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/keyu-jin-china-s-economy-tariffs-trade-trump-communism-capitalism/)).

### The case against US interventions

- Lex Fridman opens with Brown University's Cost of War estimates: 900,000 to 940,000 direct deaths and $8 trillion spent on the post-9/11 wars ([Horton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/scott-horton-the-case-against-war-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).
- Horton argues these wars were sold on false pretexts and served bureaucrats, think tanks, foreign lobbies and arms makers, creating the terrorism they claimed to fight ([Horton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/scott-horton-the-case-against-war-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).
- On Ukraine, Horton says NATO expansion broke promises made in 1990 and that the US provoked the war. Fridman puts chief responsibility for the escalation on Vladimir Putin ([Horton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/scott-horton-the-case-against-war-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).

### Old-school software engineering

- Dave Plummer shipped Task Manager in NT 4.0 at about 87k by avoiding the C runtime. He says it is now used about two billion times a month ([Plummer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/dave-plummer-programming-autism-and-old-school-microsoft-stories/)).
- He spent about half his day debugging raw assembly across four instruction sets, and argues robustness, small size and disciplined asserts matter more than features ([Plummer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/dave-plummer-programming-autism-and-old-school-microsoft-stories/)).
- Plummer describes autism as a source of intense focus with real social costs, and advises autistic people to sell their work rather than their personality ([Plummer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/08/dave-plummer-programming-autism-and-old-school-microsoft-stories/)).
