## Overview

September mixed AI researchers, roboticists and one physicist. Several guests questioned how far pure deep learning can go and argued for hybrids with models, programs or human judgement. Two robotics episodes looked at what it takes for robots to work outside the lab, in homes and in the air.

## Main themes

### What deep learning can and cannot do

- François Chollet argues neural networks only interpolate between nearby training points and need dense sampling of the input space. He expects progress from combining deep learning with program synthesis ([Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)).
- Chollet rejects the intelligence explosion, saying intelligence is bounded by body and environment. He is building a benchmark that measures skill-acquisition efficiency rather than task skill ([Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)).
- Vijay Kumar says machine learning has worked in perception but not in fielded action, and that higher accuracy will need far more data and energy. He favours learning combined with model-based methods ([Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).
- Leonard Susskind says nobody really understands why machine learning generalises so well ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).

### Robots outside the lab

- Kumar defines true autonomy for aerial robots as flying with no GPS, no base-station link, no pilot and no map. He sees battery energy density, not control, as the limit for swarms and delivery ([Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).
- Colin Angle says a home robot must solve a frequent, disliked chore and be clearly worth more than it costs. iRobot has sold 25 million robots and bet on cheap cameras over lasers ([iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/)).
- Angle now wants robots that act on context, such as cleaning one room, using a map the user labels. Kumar worries swarms are easy to weaponise ([iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/), [Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).

### Trust, fairness and data

- Peter Norvig says the new edition of his textbook treats choosing the utility function as the hard part. He argues trust should rest on testing and dialogue, not single explanations ([Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)).
- Norvig explains that equal calibration and equal error rates across groups cannot both be achieved, so humans must decide the trade-off ([Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)).
- Regina Barzilay, discussing machine learning in cancer care, says data access and hospital adoption, not algorithms, are now the main barriers. She proposes an opt-in scheme for donating medical records ([Barzilay](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/regina-barzilay-deep-learning-for-cancer-diagnosis-and-treatment/)).
- Chollet and Angle both raise concerns about data use: Chollet about engagement-driven feeds, Angle about privacy standards for home robots ([Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/), [iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/)).

### Physics and quantum computing

- Susskind expects quantum computers to matter most for simulating chemistry, materials and quantum gravity, not for factoring-style speedups ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).
- He sees string theory's main achievement as showing that gravity and quantum mechanics can coexist ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).
