## Overview

June had two episodes with little overlap. Rajat Monga told the story of TensorFlow from Google Brain's early days to the 2.0 release. Rosalind Picard argued that emotion-sensing AI is less a technical problem than a question of consent, control and purpose.

## Main themes

### Building and simplifying a machine learning framework

- Rajat Monga traces TensorFlow from Google Brain's DistBelief to its open-sourcing in November 2015. He credits Jeff Dean with pushing for openness after Hadoop and HBase became standards built on Google papers ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/)).
- He says the graph design came from production needs, and acknowledges that PyTorch's research-first approach helped push TensorFlow towards eager execution ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/)).
- With several competing high-level APIs confusing users, the team chose Keras as the standard for 2.0. François Chollet joined for a quarter and stayed about two years ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/)).
- Monga says enterprises mostly want stability, structured-data predictions and full pipelines rather than the newest models. He moved governance towards RFCs and special interest groups ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/)).

### Emotion AI, privacy and regulation

- Rosalind Picard uses Clippy as a lesson: it understood context but kept smiling and dancing at angry users ([Rosalind Picard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rosalind-picard-affective-computing-emotion-privacy-and-health/)).
- She says face-reading technology used for surveillance made her pull back on some research, and that Affectiva turned away money for reading affect without informed consent ([Rosalind Picard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rosalind-picard-affective-computing-emotion-privacy-and-health/)).
- Picard wants people to own their data, limits on emotion recognition in hiring, and a firewall between agents that read emotion and agents that sell things ([Rosalind Picard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rosalind-picard-affective-computing-emotion-privacy-and-health/)).
- She also covers wearables for health through Empatica, and says getting FDA clearance was far harder than publishing a paper. She prefers AI as a helper for people left behind rather than a way to make the rich richer ([Rosalind Picard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rosalind-picard-affective-computing-emotion-privacy-and-health/)).
