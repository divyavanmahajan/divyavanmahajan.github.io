## Overview

May was a quiet two-episode month with no politics. Paul Rosolie spoke from deep in the Peruvian Amazon about predators, conservation and uncontacted tribes, and neuroscientist Charan Ranganath explained why memory is built for the future rather than the past.

## Main themes

### Protecting the Amazon

- Rosolie argues the jungle's dangerous animals are mostly defensive and the real threat is human: logging, gold mining and road building ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
- He says Junglekeepers' protected area has grown to 66,000 acres, helped by donations from listeners of his first episode. He says “what we need is $30 million in the next two years to protect the whole thing” ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
- That money would fund a corridor linking uncontacted-tribe reserves with Manu National Park and Tambopata. Rosolie traces the tribes' hostility to rubber-boom violence a century ago ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
- Much of the forest is still unexplored: half of rainforest life is in the canopy, which scientists have barely reached ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).

### Memory as a tool for the future

- Ranganath argues the brain keeps a biased, economical sample of experience, captured at moments of surprise, and rebuilds memories each time they are recalled ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- He says “there’s no such thing as true or false memories”. Repeated recall plus outside suggestion can produce vivid memories of events that never happened ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- His practical advice is to remember better, not more: spaced study and self-testing work because they expose errors ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).

### What AI and alien talk miss

- Asked about OpenAI adding memory, Ranganath says it is easy on the surface but very hard underneath, because we do not understand episodic memory ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- He will not accept that AI replicates human intelligence until it makes the same characteristic mistakes people make ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- Rosolie says aliens are “about as real as Santa Claus” and that protecting the only known life matters more. Fridman counters that thinking about aliens teaches humility ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
