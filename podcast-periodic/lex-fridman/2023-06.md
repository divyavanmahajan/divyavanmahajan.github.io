## Overview

June was the show's open-source AI month. Mark Zuckerberg, Marc Andreessen and George Hotz each argued that releasing models openly is safer than concentrating AI in a few companies, and each recast AI risk in more human terms. Jimmy Wales and Chris Lattner added views on ChatGPT and programming, while Bert Kreischer and Matthew McConaughey brought comedy and life philosophy.

## Main themes

### Open-source AI against concentration

- Zuckerberg says he is “quite confident in the assessment that open sourcing models now is net positive”, citing Llama and community efficiency work, but has not decided for systems near superintelligence ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Andreessen sketches two futures: a few "God models" protected by regulatory capture, or a billion LLMs built in the open. He says a Google Brain contact told him Google could have had a GPT-4 equivalent by 2019 ([Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)).
- Hotz argues open models such as LLaMA guard against a single controlled AI, and pitches his $15,000 tinybox as a way to keep AI aligned with its owner rather than a cloud provider ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).

### Reframing AI risk

- Zuckerberg separates intelligence from autonomy and says governance should focus on how much autonomy systems get. He worries that tail-risk debates crowd out near-term harms such as fraud and scams ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Andreessen calls doom claims untestable, applies the Baptists-and-bootleggers metaphor to AI regulation, and names China winning AI dominance as the risk he takes most seriously ([Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)).
- Hotz says “half of these AI alignment problems are just human alignment problems” and blames human misuse rather than rebellious machines. Fridman pushes back on lowering the barrier to harm ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).

### ChatGPT, Wikipedia and search

- Jimmy Wales says GPT-4 invented three plausible athlete quotes for him. Wikipedia's rule is that “you the human are responsible for what you put into Wikipedia” ([Wales](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/jimmy-wales-wikipedia/)).
- Wales still uses ChatGPT for programming help more than Stack Overflow, and sees promise in retrieval tools that answer only from cited Wikipedia entries ([Wales](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/jimmy-wales-wikipedia/)).
- Andreessen expects search to give way to direct answers and warns that if people stop making web pages, models lose training data ([Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)).

### Moderation and business models

- Zuckerberg wants removal reserved for broadly agreed harms, with labels for disputed claims, and says Meta draws a harder line on government data requests than on takedowns ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Wales argues advertising pushes platforms towards engagement and outrage, while Wikipedia runs on small donations with no ads ([Wales](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/jimmy-wales-wikipedia/)).
- Hotz, after his Twitter internship, says the company needed refactors before features and could be run by 50 people ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).

### Software and hardware for AI

- Chris Lattner says AI is held back by complexity and presents Mojo, a Python superset aimed at hardware-level performance, with Modular's engine under PyTorch and TensorFlow ([Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/chris-lattner-future-of-programming-and-ai/)).
- Hotz says tinygrad uses about 25 primitive operations and that AI chip startups fail on software ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).
