## Overview

August was the busiest month so far, with seven guests. Jeremy Howard and Yann LeCun gave two very different views of where deep learning should go next, while Kevin Scott and Keoki Jackson spoke for large companies deploying AI. George Hotz, Pamela McCorduck and Paola Arlotta added self-driving, AI history and brain research.

## Main themes

### Where deep learning goes next

- Jeremy Howard argues deep learning works with small datasets and one GPU, so domain experts should use it rather than leave it to big labs. He calls multi-GPU training largely a waste of time for iteration ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).
- Howard says most research is minor incremental work while transfer learning and active learning are understudied ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).
- Yann LeCun says the next step is self-supervised learning of predictive world models, noting that model-free RL needs far more practice than humans ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
- LeCun rejects the term AGI because humans are not general, and wants reasoning made compatible with gradient-based learning ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).

### AI in large organisations and the policy gap

- Kevin Scott wants AI treated as a platform that creates more value for builders than for its owner, and says it should not be a product's first-order feature ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/kevin-scott-microsoft-cto/)).
- Scott wants democratically set rules on face recognition and suggests cryptographically signed content to counter deepfakes ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/kevin-scott-microsoft-cto/)).
- Howard says he is deeply concerned about labour displacement and wants data scientists to build in human oversight and appeals ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).

### Autonomy on the road and in the air

- George Hotz says comma.ai is happy to be Level 2, focusing on very good lane-keeping with driver monitoring that cannot be cheated ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Hotz argues end-to-end learning will beat hand-coded perception, agrees with Musk that lidar is a crutch, but says Tesla should add driver monitoring ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Keoki Jackson says AI-enabled autonomy will be built into everything Lockheed Martin fields, with verification of learning systems as the hard problem ([Keoki Jackson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/keoki-jackson-lockheed-martin/)).
- Pressed on the Boeing 737 MAX, Jackson argues safety comes from culture and human control as much as from technology ([Keoki Jackson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/keoki-jackson-lockheed-martin/)).

### AI's history and the brain

- Pamela McCorduck sees AI as an ancient wish to build intelligence outside the skull, and calls AI winters a commercial rather than scientific event ([Pamela McCorduck](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/pamela-mccorduck-machines-who-think-and-the-early-days-of-ai/)).
- LeCun recalls how a patent on convolutional nets and AT&T's lawyers held back his work in the 1990s ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
- Paola Arlotta presents brain organoids as a new research platform, far simpler than a brain, and wants ethicists, lawyers and philosophers involved in decisions on their use ([Paola Arlotta](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/paola-arlotta-brain-development-from-stem-cell-to-organoid/)).
