## Overview

December stepped away from technology. British Museum curator Irving Finkel talked about cuneiform, the oldest flood story and an ancient board game. Set theorist Joel David Hamkins walked through infinity, Gödel's incompleteness theorems and why he thinks there are many mathematical universes.

## Main themes

### Ancient writing and what survives of it

- Finkel says the oldest evidence of writing is around 3500 BC, but argues that Sumerian pictographs sit at the end of a long earlier tradition. He admits the view is controversial: “Many Assyriologists would leave the room.” ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/))
- He credits early lexicography, inertia and scribes guarding their power for cuneiform lasting about 3,000 years ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- The British Museum holds about 130,000 cuneiform tablets, and Finkel thinks millions more are still in the ground. He defends keeping most of the collection off display as stockpiling for future study ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).

### The flood story and the Royal Game of Ur

- A visitor brought Finkel a tablet from about 1700 BC with instructions for a giant round boat. He says it established the primacy of the Mesopotamian flood story, about a thousand years older than the Bible ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- Finkel argues the Genesis account shows literary dependence, written during the Babylonian exile with sin replacing noise as the cause. He thinks a real local catastrophe lies behind it, not a global flood ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- He reconstructed the rules of the Royal Game of Ur from a second-century BC tablet. The race game was played for nearly 3,000 years and is played again today ([Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).

### Infinity and the diagonal argument

- Hamkins says almost all mathematicians before Cantor thought actual infinity incoherent. Cantor showed the real numbers form a strictly larger infinity than the natural numbers ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- Hamkins argues one diagonal argument links Cantor's power set theorem, Russell's paradox, the halting problem and Gödel. He says “almost every major result in mathematical logic” uses it in an abstract way ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- He sees incompleteness as eye-opening rather than traumatic, and compares a theory proving its own consistency to a used car salesman vouching for himself ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).

### The continuum hypothesis and a multiverse of set theories

- The continuum hypothesis stayed open until Gödel in 1938 and Cohen's forcing in 1963 showed it is independent of ZFC. Hamkins notes no large cardinal axiom settles it ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- He argues the hypothesis can be switched on and off by forcing, which points to many set-theoretic universes. This puts him against his former supervisor Hugh Woodin ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- Hamkins says current LLMs have given him basically zero help with his mathematics ([Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
