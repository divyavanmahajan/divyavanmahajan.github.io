## Overview

November turned to politics and geopolitics. Five episodes discussed Trump and Putin, with Fiona Hill, Ben Shapiro and Destiny at the centre, while Abbas Amanat put Iran's protests in historical context. The month ended with technology and craft: Python's speed-up, the Silk Road takedown and how Bethesda builds open worlds.

## Main themes

### Putin, Trump and the war in Ukraine

- Fiona Hill says Trump often asked valid questions, such as why Europe relied on NATO while paying Russia for gas, but undercut them with insults. She sees a straight line from the Ukraine pressure campaign to the war ([Hill](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/fiona-hill-vladimir-putin-and-donald-trump/)).
- Hill credits Putin's first two terms with stabilising Russia and says “The annexation of Crimea in 2014 is the beginning of the end” of him as a positive force ([Hill](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/fiona-hill-vladimir-putin-and-donald-trump/)).
- Shapiro expects an off-ramp leaving Russia with Crimea and parts of Luhansk and Donetsk. Destiny rejects claims that NATO expansion caused the invasion and praises Biden's limits on US involvement ([Shapiro](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/ben-shapiro-politics-kanye-trump-biden-hitler-extremism-and-war/), [Destiny](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/destiny-politics-free-speech-controversy-sex-war-and-relationships/)).
- Chamath Palihapitiya argues high US interest payments make a long war hard to finance, so a negotiated settlement is more likely ([Chamath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/chamath-palihapitiya-money-success-startups-energy-poker-happiness/)).

### Polarisation, speech and Twitter

- Shapiro welcomes Musk's purchase of Twitter and wants Trump restored, but calls the platform “an ego machine” and keeps it off his phone ([Shapiro](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/ben-shapiro-politics-kanye-trump-biden-hitler-extremism-and-war/)).
- Destiny says the Trump ban was understandable but permanent bans are wrong. Fridman presses him on his use of slurs, which he says undercuts better debate ([Destiny](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/destiny-politics-free-speech-controversy-sex-war-and-relationships/)).
- Fridman and Shapiro both criticise Biden's MAGA Republicans speech as divisive; Destiny says it targeted election deniers ([Shapiro](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/ben-shapiro-politics-kanye-trump-biden-hitler-extremism-and-war/), [Destiny](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/destiny-politics-free-speech-controversy-sex-war-and-relationships/)).
- Palihapitiya argued both sides of Twitter's Trump ban, then said he would not have removed him ([Chamath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/chamath-palihapitiya-money-success-startups-energy-poker-happiness/)).

### Iran between protest and history

- Amanat reads the protests after Mahsa Amini's death as a leaderless, youth-led break with the Islamic Republic, driven by a young, educated population ([Amanat](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/abbas-amanat-iran-protests-mahsa-amini-history-cia-nuclear-weapons/)).
- He says the 1953 coup is seen as one of the earliest and most successful CIA operations, later used by Khomeini for his great Satan rhetoric ([Amanat](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/abbas-amanat-iran-protests-mahsa-amini-history-cia-nuclear-weapons/)).

### Energy, climate and cost

- Bjørn Lomborg and Andrew Revkin both accept human-caused warming but argue that cutting vulnerability and funding innovation beat costly emission cuts ([Climate debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/climate-change-debate-bj-rn-lomborg-and-andrew-revkin/)).
- Lomborg and Shapiro both point to nuclear energy as a realistic route ([Climate debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/climate-change-debate-bj-rn-lomborg-and-andrew-revkin/), [Shapiro](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/ben-shapiro-politics-kanye-trump-biden-hitler-extremism-and-war/)).
- Palihapitiya bets the marginal cost of energy and compute is heading to zero, with rooftop solar and batteries competing with utilities ([Chamath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/chamath-palihapitiya-money-success-startups-energy-poker-happiness/)).

### Software, security and games

- Guido van Rossum says Python 3.11's speed-up rests on an adaptive specializing interpreter, and he remains wary of removing the GIL ([Van Rossum](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/guido-van-rossum-python-and-the-future-of-programming/)).
- Chris Tarbell explains how basic mistakes, such as a personal Gmail address, exposed Ross Ulbricht ([Tarbell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/chris-tarbell-fbi-agent-who-took-down-silk-road/)).
- Todd Howard describes Bethesda's games as simulations that keep running out of sight, built to be played and modded for decades ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/11/todd-howard-skyrim-elder-scrolls-6-fallout-and-starfield/)).
