## Overview

January's one deep AI conversation was Yann LeCun's case for self-supervised learning of world models. Around it, the month returned to how institutions shape behaviour: Nobel incentives in cosmology, open debate in pandemic policy, and a debate on nationalism. Thomas Tull, comedian Mark Normand and three combat-sports figures talked about how hard work and the right strategy compound.

## Main themes

### The dark matter of intelligence

- Yann LeCun argues that the background knowledge humans pick up by observing the world is what machines lack, and that self-supervised predictive world models are the route to common sense ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- LeCun contrasts teenagers learning to drive in about 20 hours with self-driving systems that fail after millions of simulated hours. He says reinforcement learning and labels give far less signal than predicting video ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- He has switched from pixel prediction to non-contrastive joint embedding methods such as VICReg, and argues text alone cannot teach basic physical common sense ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- Thomas Tull thinks AI is arriving more slowly than people assume, while Fridman sees home AI that forms relationships as underexplored ([Thomas Tull](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/thomas-tull-from-batman-dark-knight-trilogy-to-ai-and-the-rolling-ston/)).

### Incentives, humility and trust in science

- Brian Keating recounts how BICEP2's claimed detection of inflation turned out to be galactic dust, and argues the Nobel Prize fuels ego and premature announcements ([Brian Keating](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/brian-keating-cosmology-astrophysics-aliens-losing-the-nobel-prize/)).
- Keating proposes that funders set aside money to publicise retractions, and wants theories of everything judged on testable predictions ([Brian Keating](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/brian-keating-cosmology-astrophysics-aliens-losing-the-nobel-prize/)).
- Jay Bhattacharya argues, at the policy level, that lockdowns caused wide collateral harm and that focused protection of the elderly would have been better. He and Fridman frame the deeper problem as a breakdown of open scientific debate ([Jay Bhattacharya](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/jay-bhattacharya-the-case-against-lockdowns/)).

### Tradition versus the individual

- In a moderated debate, Yoram Hazony argues that people naturally form loyalty groups and that independent nations are the best political order ([Nationalism Debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/nationalism-debate-yaron-brook-and-yoram-hazony/)).
- Yaron Brook counters that reason yields universal principles, chief among them individual rights, and that relationships should be chosen consciously. Both oppose a single world government ([Nationalism Debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/nationalism-debate-yaron-brook-and-yoram-hazony/)).

### Craft, capital and the long game

- Thomas Tull says patient capital, the right people and some constraint explain success, from Legendary's films with Christopher Nolan to Tulco adding data science teams to low-innovation industries ([Thomas Tull](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/thomas-tull-from-batman-dark-knight-trilogy-to-ai-and-the-rolling-ston/)).
- Mark Normand says comedy is learned through hardship and repeated bombing, and that the best jokes are simple misdirects refined over a long time ([Mark Normand](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/mark-normand-comedy/)).
- John Danaher argues the edge in a mature sport comes from undervalued skills such as leg locks, and that confidence is built through repeated success in training. Georges St-Pierre and Gordon Ryan agree ([The Greatest of All Time](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/georges-st-pierre-john-danaher-gordon-ryan-the-greatest-of-all-time/)).
