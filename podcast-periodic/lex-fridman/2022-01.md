## Overview

January's one deep AI episode was Yann LeCun on self-supervised learning, with Thomas Tull adding an investor's view of AI in old industries. Brian Keating told the story of a cosmology result that fell apart. The rest of the month covered political debate, pandemic policy, comedy and the craft of elite grappling.

## Main themes

### Self-supervised learning and machine common sense

- Yann LeCun argues the dark matter of intelligence is background knowledge gained by observing the world, and that today's systems lack the common sense of a house cat ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- He notes teenagers learn to drive in about 20 hours, and says reinforcement learning and labels give far less signal than predicting video ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- LeCun has moved to non-contrastive joint embedding methods such as VICReg and argues text alone can never teach how objects behave ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).
- He calls FAIR an unqualified success that produced PyTorch, and says Meta would crumble without deep learning ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/yann-lecun-dark-matter-of-intelligence-and-self-supervised-learning/)).

### AI and patient capital in old industries

- Thomas Tull's Tulco buys companies in large, low-innovation industries and adds a labs team of data scientists and AI engineers. He says quarterly pressure makes that reinvention hard for public incumbents ([Tull](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/thomas-tull-from-batman-dark-knight-trilogy-to-ai-and-the-rolling-ston/)).
- Tull thinks AI is “actually happening slower than most people realize”, while Fridman sees home AI that forms relationships as an underexplored opportunity ([Tull](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/thomas-tull-from-batman-dark-knight-trilogy-to-ai-and-the-rolling-ston/)).

### Cosmology, the Nobel Prize and aliens

- Brian Keating recounts how BICEP2's claimed detection of inflation turned out to be galactic dust, admitting “we weren't peer reviewed at the point where we made the announcement” ([Keating](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/brian-keating-cosmology-astrophysics-aliens-losing-the-nobel-prize/)).
- He argues the Nobel fuels ego and premature claims, and proposes that funders also pay to publicise retractions ([Keating](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/brian-keating-cosmology-astrophysics-aliens-losing-the-nobel-prize/)).
- Keating doubts technological life exists elsewhere, putting the odds at 10^24 against roughly 10^22 planets. Fridman cites Lee Cronin's opposite view ([Keating](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/brian-keating-cosmology-astrophysics-aliens-losing-the-nobel-prize/)).

### Debating political order and pandemic policy

- Yoram Hazony argues tradition and independent nations carry tested knowledge, citing Burke. Yaron Brook argues reason yields universal principles, with individual rights first ([Nationalism debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/nationalism-debate-yaron-brook-and-yoram-hazony/)).
- Both agree the Declaration's truths are not self-evident, though Brook calls it the greatest political document and both oppose world government ([Nationalism debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/nationalism-debate-yaron-brook-and-yoram-hazony/)).
- Jay Bhattacharya argues lockdowns caused wide collateral harm and that focused protection of the most vulnerable was the better policy. He calls NIH's role in setting pandemic policy an institutional failure ([Bhattacharya](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/jay-bhattacharya-the-case-against-lockdowns/)).

### Mastery through failure and skill

- John Danaher argues the edge in a mature sport comes from undervalued skills such as leg locks, and that confidence is built through repeated success in training ([GOAT](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/georges-st-pierre-john-danaher-gordon-ryan-the-greatest-of-all-time/)).
- Danaher says “the vast majority of people who watch a professional fight have almost no technical understanding of what's going on in front of them” ([GOAT](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/georges-st-pierre-john-danaher-gordon-ryan-the-greatest-of-all-time/)).
- Mark Normand says comedians are built through hardship and bombing, and that the best jokes are simple misdirects refined over time ([Normand](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/01/mark-normand-comedy/)).
