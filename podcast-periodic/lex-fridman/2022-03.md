## Overview

March on the Lex Fridman Podcast ranged from the history of science to the edges of space. A historian, a chemist and a space architect each argued about how knowledge gets made and used, while a rabbi and a former cybercriminal brought the month back to belief, trust and personal responsibility. AI came up mostly as a side question: who funds it, whether it can be conscious and whether we can control it.

## Main themes

### Science is never value-free

- Stanford historian Robert Proctor argues that German science was a full collaborator in Nazi exclusion, and that physicians joined the party and the SS in higher proportions than any other profession ([Robert Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).
- Proctor describes how the tobacco industry manufactured doubt for decades, a practice he studies as agnometrics, and how he pushed Philip Morris to withdraw research funding at Stanford ([Robert Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).
- Fridman links this to his own field, raising worries about DARPA and car-company money in AI research, and argues that distrust of institutions must be fixed at its cause rather than through censorship ([Robert Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).

### What life is, and how to detect it

- Chemist Lee Cronin argues that selection existed before biology and proposes assembly theory: the minimum number of steps needed to build an object signals whether evolution made it ([Lee Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- Cronin thinks the universe is full of life but that we cannot recognise aliens until we understand life itself. Fridman is more open than Cronin to some UFO sightings being significant ([Lee Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- Cronin's chemputer turns written chemical recipes into executable code. He pitches licensed, on-demand manufacturing through his company Chemify and discusses safety controls ([Lee Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).

### Building homes in orbit

- MIT's Ariel Ekblaw argues that large human communities will live in floating cities in orbit rather than on Mars, using modular structures bigger than any rocket fairing ([Ariel Ekblaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/ariel-ekblaw-space-colonization-and-self-assembling-space-megastructur/)).
- Her TESSERAE tiles use magnets to find each other in microgravity and self-correct bad bonds; small versions were tested on the ISS ([Ariel Ekblaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/ariel-ekblaw-space-colonization-and-self-assembling-space-megastructur/)).
- Ekblaw credits commercial launch with letting her buy flights directly, which led to the Aurelia Institute spin-out, and raises open questions on lunar property rights under the 1967 Outer Space Treaty ([Ariel Ekblaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/ariel-ekblaw-space-colonization-and-self-assembling-space-megastructur/)).

### Trust, belief and responsibility

- Former ShadowCrew founder Brett Johnson says cybercrime runs on social engineering, and that the forum's real innovation was reviews, vouching and escrow so criminals could trust each other ([Brett Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/brett-johnson-us-most-wanted-cybercriminal/)).
- Johnson urges companies to prosecute fraud, share information as criminals do and fix known vulnerabilities, citing the figure that 90% of attacks use known exploits ([Brett Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/brett-johnson-us-most-wanted-cybercriminal/)).
- Rabbi David Wolpe presents the Torah as a human, divinely inspired text and warns that believing we can control intelligences greater than ours is hubris. Fridman suggests humans and robots may one day form deep bonds; Wolpe resists ([David Wolpe](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/david-wolpe-judaism/)).
