## Overview

March 2022 had five conversations with little overlap: a historian of science, a chemist, a rabbi, a space architect and a former cybercriminal. The common threads were how institutions shape what people know and trust, and two recurring questions, consciousness and free will, which came up with both Lee Cronin and David Wolpe.

## Main themes

### Science, ideology and manufactured doubt

- Robert Proctor argues that science and ideology are woven together rather than separate, and that German science was a full collaborator in the Nazi regime ([Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).
- He calls the study of produced ignorance agnotology. The tobacco industry, in his account, made doubt as a product, and its playbook later served climate denial ([Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).
- Proctor describes cigarettes as the deadliest object in history. He says US consumption peaked at 640 billion in 1981 and is now about 240 billion, while about six trillion are smoked globally each year ([Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).
- He says “almost everything that has been known has been forgotten”, and warns that narrow training lets scientists be used by whoever pays. Fridman raises the same worry about military and car-company funding of AI research ([Proctor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/robert-proctor-nazi-science-and-ideology/)).

### Life, assembly theory and programmable chemistry

- Lee Cronin proposes assembly theory: count the minimum steps needed to build an object while reusing parts. Objects with high counts, especially in abundance, point to evolution or an information process ([Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- He thinks the universe is full of life, but alien life is so different that we cannot yet recognise it, just as “you would not go and talk to your cat about calculus” ([Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).
- His chemputer reduces chemistry to a few repeatable steps run as code. Cronin says his lab has 15 of them and pitches on-demand manufacture through his company Chemify, with safety controls ([Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).

### Religion, free will and consciousness

- David Wolpe argues that Judaism is a religious family rather than a single idea, and that one can doubt its theology and stay Jewish. He sums up one difference this way: “Christianity has an ideal life. Judaism doesn't have an ideal life. Judaism has an ideal book.” ([Wolpe](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/david-wolpe-judaism/)).
- Wolpe recalls Christopher Hitchens convincing him that the claim that religion makes people better is more complicated than he wished ([Wolpe](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/david-wolpe-judaism/)).
- Free will and consciousness ran through two episodes. Wolpe says a thorough materialist cannot have free will, while Cronin ties limited freedom to assembly theory and argues any AGI will carry human consciousness in its causal chain ([Wolpe](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/david-wolpe-judaism/), [Cronin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/lee-cronin-origin-of-life-aliens-complexity-and-consciousness/)).

### Building homes in orbit

- Ariel Ekblaw argues that large human communities will live in self-assembling structures in orbit rather than on Mars. Her magnetic TESSERAE tiles have been tested on the ISS ([Ekblaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/ariel-ekblaw-space-colonization-and-self-assembling-space-megastructur/)).
- She names radiation and mental health as the two big problems on long missions, and says a Mars round trip takes about three years ([Ekblaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/ariel-ekblaw-space-colonization-and-self-assembling-space-megastructur/)).

### Cybercrime runs on trust

- Brett Johnson says cybercrime is mostly social engineering. ShadowCrew's real innovation was giving criminals reviews, vouching and escrow so they could trust each other ([Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/brett-johnson-us-most-wanted-cybercriminal/)).
- He urges companies to report fraud, share information as criminals do and fix known vulnerabilities ([Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/03/brett-johnson-us-most-wanted-cybercriminal/)).
