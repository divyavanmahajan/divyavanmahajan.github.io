## Overview

May 2020 was a strongly technical month for the show. Ilya Sutskever and Dawn Song covered what deep learning can do and how easily it breaks, Sertac Karaman discussed autonomy for drones and cars, and Karl Friston set out a theory of what it means for anything to exist. Stephen Schwarzman and Daphne Koller added the view from business, education and AI policy.

## Main themes

### Deep learning and the path to AGI

- Sutskever argues the ideas behind deep learning existed long before it worked; large datasets, enough compute and conviction were missing ([Ilya Sutskever](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/ilya-sutskever-deep-learning/)).
- He says the field keeps underestimating neural networks, expects AGI to be deep learning plus a few more ideas such as self-play, and explains GPT-2's staged release as a response to misinformation concerns ([Ilya Sutskever](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/ilya-sutskever-deep-learning/)).
- Koller sees AGI as far off and puts the nearer risks in complex, poorly understood systems and in misuse of powerful tools ([Daphne Koller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/daphne-koller-biomedicine-and-machine-learning/)).

### Fragile and leaky machine learning

- Song argues deep learning systems do not learn rich enough representations, which makes them easy to fool. Her examples range from perturbed stop signs to an attack that changed Google Translate's output ([Dawn Song](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/dawn-song-adversarial-machine-learning-and-computer-security/)).
- She showed a language model trained on the Enron emails could leak social security and credit card numbers, and that differential privacy blocked the attack. She argues clear data ownership should come first ([Dawn Song](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/dawn-song-adversarial-machine-learning-and-computer-security/)).
- Koller warns that neural networks grow more confident as inputs drift from the training data, which is dangerous in diagnosis or driving ([Daphne Koller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/daphne-koller-biomedicine-and-machine-learning/)).

### Robots that fly and drive

- Karaman expects autonomy to arrive first in dense, geofenced areas, with a few human controllers supervising many vehicles; Optimus Ride aims for ten people running 50 vehicles ([Sertac Karaman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/sertac-karaman-robots-that-fly-and-robots-that-drive/)).
- He says predicting human behaviour is still the hard part, expects early deployments to use lidar, and contrasts Waymo's research approach with Tesla's product ([Sertac Karaman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/sertac-karaman-robots-that-fly-and-robots-that-drive/)).
- Song says attacks on Tesla's perception are feasible, against Elon Musk's dismissal, and proposes fusing many sensors as a defence ([Dawn Song](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/dawn-song-adversarial-machine-learning-and-computer-security/)).

### Data, institutions and scale

- Koller says machine learning has barely helped medicine because the right data never existed. Her company insitro sets out to generate data built for predictive models in drug discovery ([Daphne Koller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/daphne-koller-biomedicine-and-machine-learning/)).
- Schwarzman explains his $350 million gift to MIT's College of Computing as a bid for US competitiveness in AI and for an ethics framework, and calls for a federal moonshot on AI ([Stephen Schwarzman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/stephen-schwarzman-going-big-in-business-investing-and-ai/)).
- Koller's Coursera lessons: short videos, short courses and fast feedback keep learners engaged ([Daphne Koller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/daphne-koller-biomedicine-and-machine-learning/)).

### Brains, life and the free energy principle

- Friston argues that anything existing apart from its environment must look as if it minimizes variational free energy, the quantity machine learning calls the negative ELBO ([Karl Friston](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/karl-friston-neuroscience-and-the-free-energy-principle/)).
- He says active data sampling has reached robotics but not deep learning, and that self-awareness follows from living among similar creatures ([Karl Friston](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/karl-friston-neuroscience-and-the-free-energy-principle/)).
