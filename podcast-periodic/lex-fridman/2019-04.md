## Overview

April brought four of the field's best-known names to the show: Greg Brockman of OpenAI, Elon Musk, Ian Goodfellow and Oriol Vinyals of DeepMind. The month moved between the long view (AGI, safety and how to release powerful models) and concrete systems: Tesla Autopilot, GANs and AlphaStar. Adversarial examples and AI safety came up in more than one conversation.

## Main themes

### AGI, safety and the GPT-2 release

- Greg Brockman argues that if AI can be built, “it will be the most transformative technology that humans will ever create”, and puts AGI within our lifetimes ([Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- He describes OpenAI's three arms (capabilities, technical safety and policy) and says the safety team is finding at an early stage that human preferences can be learned from data ([Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- Brockman calls withholding the full GPT-2 a test case for responsible disclosure: when it is unclear whether a release is beneficial, he says, default to caution. He notes GPT-2 was a scale-up of a model released the previous June ([Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- He warns that competitive races create pressure to drop safety, explains the capped-profit OpenAI LP, and tells lawmakers this is the time for measurement rather than regulation ([Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).

### Tesla Autopilot and driver monitoring

- Elon Musk says it is “just obvious that cars will drive themselves completely”, and credits Tesla's fleet of nearly half a million camera-equipped cars and its own redundant self-driving computer ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- Fridman opens by disagreeing with Musk on camera-based driver monitoring. His MIT team found drivers stayed functionally vigilant across about 18,900 Autopilot disengagements ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- Musk calls the question moot. He predicts human intervention will soon decrease safety, while expecting hands-on-wheel detection for at least six more months ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).

### GANs and adversarial robustness

- Ian Goodfellow defines deep learning as learning the parameters of more than one consecutive step, and names hunger for labelled data as a main limit ([Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).
- He explains GANs as a two-player game between a generator and a discriminator, and admits he still cannot name one factor that explains why they worked ([Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).
- Goodfellow now sees adversarial examples “more of as a security liability” than proof that machine learning is uniquely flawed. Musk, by contrast, says such attacks can be blocked by training on what is definitely not a car ([Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/), [Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- On deepfakes, he expects authentication such as cryptographically signing phone images to matter more than pixel-level detectors ([Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).

### AlphaStar and StarCraft

- Oriol Vinyals treats StarCraft like machine translation: AlphaStar is a single neural network policy that first imitates human replays, conditioned on the skill level it should mimic ([Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/)).
- The AlphaStar League builds a population of agent personalities, including cheesy strategies, because pure self-play got stuck ([Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/)).
- Vinyals says the claim is a first win over a professional, not an unbeatable agent, and concedes the action-rate cap was probably set too high. He calls generalisation deep learning's main challenge ([Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/)).
