## Overview

April brought four guests from the centre of 2019 AI: OpenAI, Tesla, Google Brain and DeepMind. Greg Brockman and Elon Musk talked about building and governing powerful systems, while Ian Goodfellow and Oriol Vinyals explained how GANs and AlphaStar work. All four came back to the same gap: deep learning scales well but still struggles to generalise and reason.

## Main themes

### Steering the road to AGI

- Greg Brockman argues that AGI cannot be stopped, so the real choice is its initial conditions. OpenAI's charter commits it to help, not race, a value-aligned project that is ahead ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- He says building AGI will take many billions of dollars, which is why OpenAI LP is a capped-return company governed by the nonprofit board ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- Brockman calls withholding the full GPT-2 model a test case for disclosure norms, and tells lawmakers this is the time for measurement, through bodies like NIST, before regulation ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).

### Self-driving, data and driver monitoring

- Elon Musk says nearly half a million Teslas give the company about 99% of all such driving data, and that its own redundant self-driving computer makes the rest a software problem ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- Fridman opens by disagreeing with Musk on camera-based driver monitoring. His MIT team found drivers stayed functionally vigilant across about 18,900 disengagements; Musk calls the question moot and expects human intervention to soon reduce safety ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- Musk suggests regulators may want Autopilot to be 200 or 300% safer than a person, measured in incidents per mile ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).

### Generative models and adversarial thinking

- Ian Goodfellow explains GANs as a two-player game between a generator and a discriminator, and admits he still cannot name one factor that explains why they worked ([Ian Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).
- He traces progress from blurry 2014 samples to semi-supervised GANs that need far fewer labels, and applies adversarial ideas to privacy and fairness ([Ian Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).
- On deepfakes, Goodfellow expects authentication, such as phones that sign images cryptographically, to matter more than pixel-level fake detectors ([Ian Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).

### Scale, games and the generalisation gap

- Brockman cites OpenAI Five, where PPO at massive scale produced long-term planning nobody expected, as evidence that progress needs both compute and ideas ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- Oriol Vinyals describes AlphaStar as a sequence model that first imitated human replays, then improved in a league of agent personalities because pure self-play got stuck ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/)).
- Vinyals concedes the action-rate cap was probably set too high and calls generalisation deep learning's main challenge. Goodfellow and Musk also point to missing pieces for general intelligence ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/), [Ian Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/), [Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
