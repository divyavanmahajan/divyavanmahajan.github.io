## Overview

March leaned towards big questions. A filmmaker, a philosopher and a physicist each took on machine consciousness and simulation, while two physicists argued that fundamental physics is incomplete. One roboticist, one cryptocurrency founder and one leadership author kept the month grounded in practice.

## Main themes

### Consciousness, simulation and superintelligence

- Nick Bostrom separates the simulation hypothesis from the simulation argument, in which one of three propositions must be true, and declines to put numbers on them ([Bostrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/nick-bostrom-simulation-and-superintelligence/)).
- Bostrom says the upside of superintelligence now gets too little attention, and that he puts more weight on an intelligence explosion than the average AI researcher ([Bostrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/nick-bostrom-simulation-and-superintelligence/)).
- Roger Penrose argues that understanding is not rule-following, so consciousness cannot come from computation alone. He points to the cerebellum, which computes precisely yet seems unconscious ([Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)).
- Alex Garland doubts appearance proves consciousness because humans project sentience onto everything. He worries more about the money and power of technology builders than about AI itself ([Garland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/alex-garland-ex-machina-devs-annihilation-and-the-poetry-of-science/)).

### Unfinished physics

- Lee Smolin says quantum mechanics is consistent but incomplete and not yet joined with general relativity. He treats time and causality as fundamental and space as emergent ([Smolin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/lee-smolin-quantum-gravity-and-einstein-s-unfinished-revolution/)).
- Penrose also calls quantum mechanics incomplete, linking state reduction to a missing gravity-based theory, and outlines conformal cyclic cosmology ([Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)).
- Smolin hopes rival quantum gravity camps will collaborate, and sees younger researchers already bringing them together ([Smolin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/lee-smolin-quantum-gravity-and-einstein-s-unfinished-revolution/)).

### Robots that model people

- Anca Dragan argues robots must model humans as roughly rational under their own assumptions, and treat specified rewards as evidence of what people want, not as law ([Dragan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/anca-dragan-human-robot-interaction-and-reward-engineering/)).
- She says driving is hard because of people, and warns that driver-assist systems may wrongly assume a supervising human is as safe as a driving one. Fridman counters with his own driver data ([Dragan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/anca-dragan-human-robot-interaction-and-reward-engineering/)).

### Money, coordination and leadership

- Vitalik Buterin describes money as a coordination game and Satoshi's real invention as cryptoeconomics. He outlines Ethereum 2.0, with proof of stake replacing energy-hungry mining, plus sharding ([Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-cryptocurrency-and-the-future-of-money/)).
- Buterin explains quadratic funding for public goods and the composability behind DeFi projects such as DAI and Uniswap ([Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-cryptocurrency-and-the-future-of-money/)).
- Simon Sinek argues business and life are infinite games, and that leaders should offer a just cause rather than pressure to hit arbitrary numbers ([Sinek](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/simon-sinek-leadership-hard-work-optimism-and-the-infinite-game/)).

### Science as a shared story

- Ann Druyan calls science a form of love for reality that must belong to everyone. She ranks climate change above AI as a concern ([Druyan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/ann-druyan-cosmos-carl-sagan-voyager-and-the-beauty-of-science/)).
- Garland sees himself as a layperson translating scientists' ideas into stories and calls science essentially poetic ([Garland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/alex-garland-ex-machina-devs-annihilation-and-the-poetry-of-science/)).
