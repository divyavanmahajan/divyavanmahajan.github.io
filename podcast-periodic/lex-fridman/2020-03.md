## Overview

March 2020 leaned towards physics and philosophy. A filmmaker, a philosopher and a mathematical physicist each asked whether machines can be conscious and whether reality might be simulated, while Lee Smolin and Roger Penrose argued that quantum mechanics is incomplete. Robotics, cryptocurrency and leadership filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Machine consciousness and simulated worlds

- Alex Garland says appearing conscious proves little because “humans are so quick to project sentience into things that don't have sentience”. He calls Ex Machina a Turing test with the machine in plain sight ([Garland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/alex-garland-ex-machina-devs-annihilation-and-the-poetry-of-science/)).
- Roger Penrose argues understanding is not rule-following, so computation alone cannot produce consciousness. He notes the cerebellum does precise computation yet appears unconscious ([Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)).
- Nick Bostrom separates the simulation hypothesis from his simulation argument, in which one of three propositions must be true. He notes a simulation need not render every detail, as games show ([Bostrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/nick-bostrom-simulation-and-superintelligence/)).
- Garland, by contrast, says we live in a dream state built from the brain's best guesses, but does not think the universe is simulated ([Garland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/alex-garland-ex-machina-devs-annihilation-and-the-poetry-of-science/)).

### Einstein's unfinished revolution

- Lee Smolin says the revolution is unfinished because “general relativity describes big things quantum theory describes little things” and the two are not yet joined ([Smolin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/lee-smolin-quantum-gravity-and-einstein-s-unfinished-revolution/)).
- Smolin, like Einstein, sees quantum mechanics as consistent but incomplete. He wants principles first, with time and causality fundamental and space emergent ([Smolin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/lee-smolin-quantum-gravity-and-einstein-s-unfinished-revolution/)).
- Penrose links the missing physics to wavefunction collapse and a gravity-based theory, and outlines conformal cyclic cosmology, where the remote future of one universe becomes the next Big Bang ([Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)).

### Robots that work among people

- Anca Dragan says driving would be essentially solved without humans on the road. Merging is hard because “people change what they do depending on what the car does” ([Dragan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/anca-dragan-human-robot-interaction-and-reward-engineering/)).
- She models people as roughly rational agents and treats specified rewards as evidence of what humans want, not as law ([Dragan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/anca-dragan-human-robot-interaction-and-reward-engineering/)).

### Science, long horizons and existential risk

- Ann Druyan describes science's error-correcting mechanism as what makes it deliver, and argues it must belong to everyone ([Druyan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/ann-druyan-cosmos-carl-sagan-voyager-and-the-beauty-of-science/)).
- Druyan ranks climate change above AI as a concern. Bostrom, meanwhile, says AI alignment research has grown since 2014 and the upside of superintelligence now gets too little attention ([Druyan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/ann-druyan-cosmos-carl-sagan-voyager-and-the-beauty-of-science/), [Bostrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/nick-bostrom-simulation-and-superintelligence/)).

### Money and leadership as long games

- Vitalik Buterin calls money a coordination game and credits Satoshi with inventing cryptoeconomics. He describes Ethereum 2.0's move to proof of stake as a way to avoid proof-of-work energy use ([Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-cryptocurrency-and-the-future-of-money/)).
- Simon Sinek argues business and life are infinite games, and that leaders must offer a cause bigger than profit rather than pressure to hit arbitrary numbers ([Sinek](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/simon-sinek-leadership-hard-work-optimism-and-the-infinite-game/)).
