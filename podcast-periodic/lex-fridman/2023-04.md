## Overview

April continued March's AI debate. Max Tegmark made the case for the six-month pause letter, and Manolis Kellis argued against it a week later, with AI and jobs coming up in three episodes. The rest of the month moved to building things: Boston Dynamics' robots, Simone Giertz's products, plus a mathematician on paradox and a conservationist in the Amazon.

## Main themes

### To pause or not to pause

- Max Tegmark argues labs are stuck in a Moloch race where no one can slow down alone. He calls it “a suicide race, where everybody loses if anybody's AI goes out of control” ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Tegmark says the open letter's six-month pause on models beyond GPT-4 gives labs cover to coordinate. He says “it's not GPT-4 that's terrifying” but where the technology goes next, and opposes open-sourcing it now ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- He frames alignment as getting AI to understand, adopt and retain our goals, and proposes running only AI code that can prove itself to a simple checker ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Manolis Kellis opposes the pause, wants Google and Meta models opened up, and prefers rules on use rather than training. He sees alignment as two-way and suggests giving AI rights ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).

### AI, jobs and vocations

- Kellis expects AI to take the routine part of work, so that we no longer have jobs but vocations. He says hiring only humans when AI is better would be discrimination ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).
- Fridman expects AI tools to make him 5 to 10 times faster at coding, which Tegmark calls an early form of recursive self-improvement ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Robert Playter says former manual unloaders now operate Boston Dynamics' Stretch robots in warehouses ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- Kellis also sees AI tutors fitted to each child, and describes, at a high level, using AI models to speed up drug discovery ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).

### From prototypes to products

- Playter says a robotics company must sell thousands of units, and that selling only hundreds is where small robot companies die. Spot needs 1,000 to 1,500 sales a year, mainly for industrial inspection ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- He welcomes Tesla's humanoid effort as validation and describes a pledge, co-signed by other robot makers, not to arm general-purpose robots ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- Simone Giertz describes moving from deliberately failing robots to product design. A serious health scare showed her how fragile an ad-funded YouTube business is, so she now runs a self-funded store and uses the channel for R&D ([Simone Giertz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/simone-giertz-queen-of-sh-tty-robots-innovative-engineering-and-design/)).

### Mathematics, paradox and the natural world

- Edward Frenkel no longer believes everything reduces to computation. He cites Gödel's incompleteness theorem and doubts language models could make leaps of mathematical imagination ([Edward Frenkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/edward-frenkel-reality-is-a-paradox-mathematics-physics-truth-love/)).
- Frenkel describes the Langlands program and doubts any theory of everything exists ([Edward Frenkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/edward-frenkel-reality-is-a-paradox-mathematics-physics-truth-love/)).
- Paul Rosolie says Junglekeepers pays loggers more to guard the forest than to cut it. He describes artisanal gold mining that has destroyed hundreds of thousands of acres in Peru's Madre de Dios ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/paul-rosolie-amazon-jungle-uncontacted-tribes-anacondas-and-ayahuasca/)).
