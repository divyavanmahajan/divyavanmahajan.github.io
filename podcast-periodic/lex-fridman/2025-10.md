## Overview

October had three unrelated guests: Telegram founder Pavel Durov, criminal psychologist Julia Shaw and Rockstar co-founder Dan Houser. AI was not the main subject of any episode, but Shaw and Houser both gave sceptical views on what generative AI does to memory and to creative work.

## Main themes

### Telegram, privacy and state pressure

- Pavel Durov describes his arrest in France: about 15 charges read to him and almost four days in custody in a windowless room. He is under investigation, not on trial, and is still waiting for a hearing date on his appeal ([Pavel Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- He says French intelligence asked him to block channels before the Romanian election and in Moldova; he refused and made the requests public ([Pavel Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- Durov explains Telegram's hybrid model: end-to-end encryption cannot support large persistent group histories or big channels, so cloud chats use encrypted servers and keys split across jurisdictions ([Pavel Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- About 40 engineers run almost 100,000 servers. Premium has over 15 million subscribers, and ads use context rather than personal data ([Pavel Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).

### Evil as a label

- Julia Shaw argues that calling someone evil ends the conversation. Dark traits sit on a continuum, and scoring high on all four makes harming others most likely ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- She says most murders are mundane fights that get out of control, and links serial offending to isolation ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- Her work on environmental crime, including Volkswagen's Dieselgate, explains corporate fraud through conformity, rationalisation and social pressure ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).

### Memory and generative AI

- Shaw says false memories are normal: “The question isn’t whether it’s false, the question is how false.” In her PhD study, three interviews led 70% of participants to confess to a crime that never happened ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- She calls generative AI “basically the ultimate false memory machine”, because chatbots tell people what they want to hear, and says social scientists are ignored when these systems are built ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).

### Open worlds and AI in creative work

- Dan Houser says great open-world games combine a living, systemic world with a strong story. He thinks Red Dead Redemption 2 reconciled the two best ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).
- On AI, he argues LLMs have done 90-95% of the work of sounding human but says the last 5% “is going to end up being about 95% of the work”. He expects cheap, decent output and a harder start for beginners ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).
- His company Absurd Ventures is sticking to open-world single-player games. One project, A Better Paradise, imagines an AI more intelligent than humans but just as broken ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).
