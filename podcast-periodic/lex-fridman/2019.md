## Overview

In 2019 the show grew from a handful of episodes a month to nearly weekly releases, 48 in all. The main thread was whether deep learning is enough, with guests from LeCun to Chomsky and Pearl taking different sides. Self-driving cars and robots were the second big subject. From the autumn the guest list widened to physicists, a chess champion, an investor and a comedian.

## Main themes

### Is deep learning enough

- Yann LeCun sees self-supervised learning of predictive world models as the next step ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)); Oriol Vinyals calls generalisation deep learning's core limit ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/oriol-vinyals-deepmind-alphastar-starcraft-language-and-sequences/)).
- François Chollet wants deep learning combined with program synthesis ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)); Melanie Mitchell puts concepts and analogy at the centre ([Melanie Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Judea Pearl says correlation-fitting cannot answer causal questions ([Judea Pearl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/)), and Noam Chomsky says deep learning tells us nothing about human language ([Noam Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/)).
- Jeff Hawkins and Tomaso Poggio look to the brain for the next ideas ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/), [Tomaso Poggio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/01/tomaso-poggio-brains-minds-and-machines/)).

### Self-driving cars and robots

- Elon Musk expects fleet data to make driver monitoring irrelevant ([Elon Musk: Tesla Autopilot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)); George Hotz says lane-keeping with strict monitoring is what adds value today ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Chris Urmson wants every sensor and a path separate from Level 2 assistance ([Chris Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)); Kyle Vogt leans on an automaker for scale ([Kyle Vogt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/02/kyle-vogt-cruise-automation/)).
- Colin Angle says home robots must deliver more value than they cost ([Colin Angle](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/)); Vijay Kumar sees energy and economics limiting drone swarms ([Vijay Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).

### Labs, tools and platforms

- Greg Brockman explains OpenAI's charter, capped-profit structure and staged releases ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- Rajat Monga and Chris Lattner cover TensorFlow 2.0 and ML frameworks as compilers ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/), [Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/05/chris-lattner-compilers-llvm-swift-tpu-and-ml-accelerators/)); Jeremy Howard wants deep learning in domain experts' hands ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).
- Kevin Scott wants AI as a democratised platform ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/kevin-scott-microsoft-cto/)); Kai-Fu Lee contrasts China's execution culture with Silicon Valley ([Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).

### Fairness, trust and regulation

- Michael Kearns treats fairness and privacy as algorithmic constraints whose trade-offs society must choose ([Michael Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).
- Peter Norvig says trust needs testing and dialogue, not just explanations ([Peter Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)); Rosalind Picard wants emotion-sensing AI built on consent ([Rosalind Picard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rosalind-picard-affective-computing-emotion-privacy-and-health/)).
- Musk calls for a government AI regulator ([Elon Musk: Neuralink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)); Regina Barzilay names data access and adoption as the barriers in clinical AI ([Regina Barzilay](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/regina-barzilay-deep-learning-for-cancer-diagnosis-and-treatment/)).

### Physics and the foundations of computing

- Leonard Susskind, Sean Carroll and Jim Gates discuss quantum mechanics and string theory ([Leonard Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/), [Sean Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/sean-carroll-quantum-mechanics-and-the-many-worlds-interpretation/), [Jim Gates](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/jim-gates-supersymmetry-string-theory-and-proving-einstein-right/)).
- Bjarne Stroustrup, Gilbert Strang and Donald Knuth cover abstraction, linear algebra and algorithms ([Bjarne Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/), [Gilbert Strang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/gilbert-strang-linear-algebra-deep-learning-teaching-and-mit-opencours/), [Donald Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/donald-knuth-algorithms-tex-life-and-the-art-of-computer-programming/)).
