## Overview

In 2019 the show grew to 48 episodes and widened well beyond AI research. Self-driving cars were the most concrete thread, with Elon Musk, George Hotz, Chris Urmson and Sebastian Thrun. Many guests probed what deep learning cannot do. In the second half, physicists brought string theory, many-worlds and the simulation question.

## Main themes

### Self-driving cars and Tesla Autopilot

- Elon Musk argues that “any car that does not have autonomy would be about as useful as a horse”, and that Tesla's fleet gives it almost all the data ([Tesla Autopilot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/)).
- Musk says driver monitoring only makes sense for systems at or below human reliability; George Hotz counters that strict monitoring with good lane-keeping is what adds value today ([Tesla Autopilot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/elon-musk-tesla-autopilot/), [George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Kyle Vogt and Thrun trace the field back to the DARPA challenges ([Kyle Vogt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/02/kyle-vogt-cruise-automation/), [Sebastian Thrun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/)), while Urmson argues full autonomy needs every sensor and a separate path from driver assistance ([Chris Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).

### What deep learning can and cannot do

- Yann LeCun is surprised that huge networks trained on little data work at all, and sees self-supervised learning as the next step ([Yann LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
- François Chollet says “a deep neural network can only make sense of points in experience space that are very close to things that it has already” seen ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)).
- Noam Chomsky, Judea Pearl and Melanie Mitchell each name a missing piece: innate language structure, causal models, and concepts formed by analogy ([Noam Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/), [Judea Pearl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/), [Melanie Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Ian Goodfellow now treats adversarial examples mainly as a security liability ([Ian Goodfellow](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/ian-goodfellow-generative-adversarial-networks-gans/)).

### AGI, jobs and fairness

- Greg Brockman calls AGI potentially the most transformative technology humans will create, and describes OpenAI's charter and capped-profit structure ([Greg Brockman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/04/greg-brockman-openai-and-agi/)).
- Eric Weinstein warns that capitalism which abandons the median worker flirts with revolution; Kai-Fu Lee favours retraining over plain basic income ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/), [Kai-Fu Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- Peter Norvig says “i prefer to talk about trust and uh validation and verification rather than just about explainability” ([Peter Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)). Michael Kearns treats fairness and privacy as measurable trade-offs that society must choose ([Michael Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).

### The deep learning toolchain

- Rajat Monga explains how TensorFlow grew from Google Brain into an open ecosystem, with Keras chosen as its standard API ([Rajat Monga](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/06/rajat-monga-tensorflow/)).
- Chris Lattner argues ML frameworks are compilers needing LLVM-style shared infrastructure ([Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/05/chris-lattner-compilers-llvm-swift-tpu-and-ml-accelerators/)); Jeremy Howard wants deep learning in the hands of domain experts ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).

### Physics, string theory and simulation

- String theory was central in four episodes: Weinstein says it has not delivered, while Leonard Susskind, Michio Kaku and Jim Gates defend it ([Leonard Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/), [Michio Kaku](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/10/michio-kaku-future-of-humans-aliens-space-travel-physics/), [Jim Gates](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/jim-gates-supersymmetry-string-theory-and-proving-einstein-right/)).
- Sean Carroll calls the universe a computation, not a computer, doubts we live in a simulation, and defends many-worlds ([Sean Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/), [Many-Worlds](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/sean-carroll-quantum-mechanics-and-the-many-worlds-interpretation/)).
