## Overview

February on the Lex Fridman Podcast was entirely about AI. Nathan Lambert and Sebastian Raschka gave a broad state-of-the-field review, and Peter Steinberger told the story of OpenClaw, the open-source personal agent that went viral. Anthropic, Claude Code and OpenAI ran through both conversations.

## Main themes

### Anthropic, OpenAI and the coding race

- Lambert credits Claude Opus 4.5 and Claude Code for Anthropic's lead in coding, saying “culturally Anthropic is known for betting very hard on code” ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Fridman splits his time between Cursor and Claude Code, and cautions that the coding hype on X may not reflect actual use, where ChatGPT and Gemini dominate ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert still sees OpenAI as the most likely source of a new paradigm, citing Deep Research, Sora and o1, and expects Gemini to keep gaining on ChatGPT ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Steinberger prefers Codex because it reads a lot of code by default, and calls Opus the best general model ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).

### Scaling, open weights and AGI timelines

- Both guests say gains now come from post-training (RLVR) and inference-time scaling rather than new architectures, and that no lab keeps a lasting lead because researchers move ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert says DeepSeek is losing its crown to Z.ai's GLM, MiniMax and Kimi K2 Thinking. His ATOM Project argues about $100 million could produce competitive American open models ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert thinks big tech will spend $100 billion long before an automated AI researcher arrives. Raschka expects “amplification, but not a paradigm change” ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).

### Personal agents as the new operating system

- Steinberger built the first prototype in an hour by hooking WhatsApp to the Claude Code CLI. The agent later handled a voice message it was never built for by converting it and sending it to OpenAI ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- He argues personal agents could make 80% of apps obsolete, naming MyFitnessPal, Eight Sleep and Sonos, and that every app is now a slow API ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- He favours composable CLIs over MCP and is talking to Meta and OpenAI on condition that OpenClaw stays open source ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).

### Agent security

- Steinberger says many early security reports came from users exposing the local debug interface to the internet against the docs ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- He admits prompt injection is unsolved, warns against cheap models such as Haiku, and now scans skills through a VirusTotal partnership ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).

### What happens to programmers

- Steinberger calls his practice agentic engineering and treats vibe coding as a slur. He expects hand-coding to become like knitting, but says developers are still builders ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- Lambert expects software engineering to shift towards system design and outcomes. Raschka asks how people become experts if they never do the work themselves ([State of AI in 2026](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
