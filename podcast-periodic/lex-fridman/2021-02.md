## Overview

February 2021 was shaped by the GameStop squeeze and its fallout, with Richard Craib and Jason Calacanis explaining WallStreetBets and Robinhood from the inside. Elon Musk came up in seven of eight episodes. The rest of the month covered the tools of the web and AI (Brendan Eich, Jim Keller), free speech with Eric and Zev Weinstein, satellite propulsion and the science of sleep.

## Main themes

### GameStop, WallStreetBets and Robinhood

- Richard Craib says redditors bought GameStop because it was shorted beyond its float, and that karma and trade screenshots made anonymous claims credible. He warns that “wall street bets needs to get a little bit more powerful and it'll be pretty dangerous” ([Richard Craib](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/richard-craib-wallstreetbets-numerai-and-the-future-of-stock-trading/)).
- Jason Calacanis, an early Robinhood investor, says the trading pause came from a liquidity crisis and clearinghouse collateral demands, not hedge fund pressure. Robinhood raised about three and a half billion dollars in a week ([Jason Calacanis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jason-calacanis-startups-angel-investing-capitalism-and-friendship/)).

### Startups, investing and Elon Musk

- Calacanis says “the way to do really well as an angel investor or just in technology or in life is to not say what could go wrong”. He judges startups on a well-built product and delighted customers ([Jason Calacanis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jason-calacanis-startups-angel-investing-capitalism-and-friendship/)).
- He recalls Musk telling him in 2008 that Tesla had two weeks of cash, and says SpaceX would have been over had its third rocket failed ([Jason Calacanis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jason-calacanis-startups-angel-investing-capitalism-and-friendship/)).
- Natalya Bailey of Accion Systems says customers now want thrusters for around 10,000 dollars instead of 20-25 million. She sees chip-scale manufacturing as the answer and SpaceX as a future customer ([Natalya Bailey](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/natalya-bailey-rocket-engines-and-electric-spacecraft-propulsion/)).

### Building the web and AI hardware

- Brendan Eich traces JavaScript's 10-day origin to Brave. He says Brave blocks third-party cookies almost without exception and matches ads locally with simple ML, giving users 70% of gross ad revenue through the Basic Attention Token ([Brendan Eich](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/brendan-eich-javascript-firefox-mozilla-and-brave/)).
- Jim Keller argues GPUs emulate graphs, while neural networks need hardware that runs graphs natively. Tenstorrent compiles PyTorch into graphs and aims to scale from 100 milliwatts to a megawatt ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jim-keller-the-future-of-computing-ai-life-and-consciousness/)).

### Free speech, deplatforming and stagnation

- Eric Weinstein argues that digital speech has outgrown a constitutional frame built for local speech. He says “it is better that we have bad people in our system than this idea of no platforming people who are beyond the pale” ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/eric-weinstein-difficult-conversations-freedom-of-speech-and-physics/)).
- Fridman says banning Trump from Twitter feels wrong, and that Amazon removing Parler from AWS troubled him more because AWS is infrastructure ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/eric-weinstein-difficult-conversations-freedom-of-speech-and-physics/)).
- Zev Weinstein argues that deep thinking becomes more dangerous when growth stalls but is the only way out. He is less sure than his father that the current period must end ([Zev Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/zev-weinstein-the-next-generation-of-big-ideas-and-brave-minds/)).

### Sleep, effort and new science media

- Andrew Huberman argues that sleep, fatigue and motivation rest on understandable mechanisms, and that knowing them beats anxious optimisation ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/andrew-huberman-sleep-dreams-creativity-the-limits-of-the-human-mind/)).
- Fridman plans a 48-mile run with David Goggins, and both argue that science communication should not be left to existing institutions ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/andrew-huberman-sleep-dreams-creativity-the-limits-of-the-human-mind/)).
