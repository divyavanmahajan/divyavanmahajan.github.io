## Overview

January opened 2021 with a mix of AI, science and culture. Max Tegmark and Dan Kokotov covered how AI should be built and trusted, Avi Loeb and Dmitry Korkin argued about how science handles anomalies and breakthroughs, and Tim Dillon brought the month's deplatforming debates onto the show. Lex Fridman also recorded his first solo AMA.

## Main themes

### AI that people can understand and trust

- Max Tegmark wants AI held to a physics standard: systems that affect lives should be understood, and safety-critical ones provably correct. He cites the Boeing 737 MAX and Knight Capital as cases of over-trust ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/max-tegmark-ai-and-physics/)).
- Tegmark calls scaling ever bigger black boxes reckless and prefers combining neural networks with symbolic methods and formal verification. His AI Feynman project rediscovered all 100 equations it was tested on ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/max-tegmark-ai-and-physics/)).
- He extends alignment to corporations and news algorithms, says engagement optimisation deepened polarisation, and calls 2020 a turning point for autonomous weapons ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/max-tegmark-ai-and-physics/)).
- In his AMA, Fridman argues that AI meant to live among humans will need the possibility of suffering, while task-specific systems will not ([AMA](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/ama1-lex-fridman-ask-me-anything-ama-january-2021/)).

### Data as the hard part of machine learning

- Dan Kokotov of Rev says data is the hardest part of speech recognition. Rev's human transcription business produces the labelled data its models learn from ([Dan Kokotov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dan-kokotov-speech-recognition-with-ai-and-humans/)).
- Kokotov reports about a 14% word error rate on Rev's internal tests against a human level of 2-3%, and says the edits transcribers make are still unused signal ([Dan Kokotov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dan-kokotov-speech-recognition-with-ai-and-humans/)).
- Both want searchable podcasts and meetings, and Fridman criticises exclusive deals for cutting shows off from open RSS ([Dan Kokotov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dan-kokotov-speech-recognition-with-ai-and-humans/)).
- Dmitry Korkin ranks DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 among the top AI achievements for scientific impact, but says much of the problem remains open. He puts Deep Blue first among AI milestones ([Dmitry Korkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dmitry-korkin-evolution-of-proteins-viruses-life-and-ai/)).

### Anomalies, taboo and open-minded science

- Avi Loeb argues that 'Oumuamua is best explained as an artificial light sail, citing its shape, lack of a tail and non-gravitational push ([Avi Loeb](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/avi-loeb-aliens-black-holes-and-the-mystery-of-the-oumuamua/)).
- Loeb says ego and groupthink make the search for technological signatures taboo, and proposes looking for industrial pollutants in exoplanet atmospheres ([Avi Loeb](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/avi-loeb-aliens-black-holes-and-the-mystery-of-the-oumuamua/)).
- Fridman says in his AMA that he now sees extraterrestrial life as worth scientific study, citing Loeb among others ([AMA](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/ama1-lex-fridman-ask-me-anything-ama-january-2021/)).
- Tegmark takes the opposite bet and argues we may be the only technological civilisation in the observable universe ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/max-tegmark-ai-and-physics/)).

### Platforms, power and speech

- Tim Dillon argues the bigger danger is a handful of tech companies removing dissenting voices. Both he and Fridman call Amazon removing Parler from AWS troubling ([Tim Dillon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/tim-dillon-comedy-power-conspiracy-theories-and-freedom/)).
- Fridman explains that moderation at YouTube and Twitter scale is mostly automated and title-driven, and suggests a transparent, jury-like review ([Tim Dillon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/tim-dillon-comedy-power-conspiracy-theories-and-freedom/)).
- In the AMA, Fridman says he is not afraid of being cancelled but worries about handling difficult conversations poorly ([AMA](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/ama1-lex-fridman-ask-me-anything-ama-january-2021/)).

### Toughness and preparation

- Dan Gable says champions join American conditioning with Russian technical science, and that a loss to Larry Owings pushed him to study the Russians ([Dan Gable](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dan-gable-olympic-wrestling-mental-toughness-the-making-of-champions/)).
- Gable argues losses are never fully overcome but can be turned into purpose, and that luck mostly comes from preparation ([Dan Gable](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/dan-gable-olympic-wrestling-mental-toughness-the-making-of-champions/)).
