## Overview

November had only three episodes, and two of them dealt with the wars in Ukraine and Gaza. Elon Musk added AI: xAI's Grok, the shift from chips to electrical power as the limit, and his views on regulation. Michael Malice closed the month with a lighter Thanksgiving conversation about gratitude.

## Main themes

### Musk on AI, power and open models

- Musk says xAI's Grok is modelled on The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy. He argues a useful model must be grounded in physics and checked against reality ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/elon-musk-war-ai-aliens-politics-physics-video-games-and-humanity/)).
- He predicts a transformer shortage in about a year and wider electricity shortages in about two, and contrasts Tesla's 100-watt self-driving computer with a 10 megawatt GPU cluster ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/elon-musk-war-ai-aliens-politics-physics-video-games-and-humanity/)).
- Musk wants an objective third-party referee for leading AI labs, leans towards open sourcing models with a delay of about six months, and says Grok was trained on 8,000 A100s ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/elon-musk-war-ai-aliens-politics-physics-video-games-and-humanity/)).

### Ukraine and Gaza

- Musk recommends that Israel pursue Hamas members while making highly visible humanitarian efforts, and expects a Ukraine truce close to the current lines ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/elon-musk-war-ai-aliens-politics-physics-video-games-and-humanity/)).
- John Mearsheimer argues NATO expansion, especially the 2008 Bucharest pledge, was the main cause of the Ukraine war, and expects at best a frozen conflict ([Mearsheimer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/john-mearsheimer-israel-palestine-russia-ukraine-china-nato-and-ww3/)).
- Mearsheimer traces October 7 to the occupation and believes a two-state solution, though the only workable outcome, is now out of reach ([Mearsheimer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/john-mearsheimer-israel-palestine-russia-ukraine-china-nato-and-ww3/)).

### Realism, China and American power

- Mearsheimer explains offensive realism: with no higher authority, states maximise relative power to survive. Lex Fridman pushes back with the case for leadership, trust and empathy ([Mearsheimer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/john-mearsheimer-israel-palestine-russia-ukraine-china-nato-and-ww3/)).
- He says the US should defend Taiwan to protect its alliances, and that deterrence only requires making a Chinese victory costly ([Mearsheimer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/john-mearsheimer-israel-palestine-russia-ukraine-china-nato-and-ww3/)).
- Malice argues against Mearsheimer that American influence comes more from exported culture than from military bases ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/michael-malice-thanksgiving-pirate-special/)).

### Gratitude and human nature

- Malice argues cynicism is a form of giving up: most things are mediocre, but the good art, books and people are what matter ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/michael-malice-thanksgiving-pirate-special/)).
- He and Fridman disagree on human nature. Malice sees a thin veneer of civilisation, while Fridman points to a century of cooperation and creation ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/michael-malice-thanksgiving-pirate-special/)).
