## Overview

December 2018 was the show's busiest month so far, with four researchers and one executive. Stuart Russell, Pieter Abbeel, Juergen Schmidhuber and Tuomas Sandholm covered AI control, reinforcement learning, self-improving systems and game solving. Eric Schmidt brought a business view of scale and long-range planning, and the guests split sharply on how much to worry about AI risk.

## Main themes

### How seriously to take AI risk

- Russell argues the standard model of AI, optimising a fixed objective, is the root of the control problem. A machine uncertain about what humans want becomes deferential and willing to be corrected ([Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).
- He lists three failure modes: loss of control, misuse and overuse, and compares unregulated software to pharmaceuticals before the FDA ([Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).
- Schmidt disagrees with Elon Musk's focus on existential risk and says the next five to ten years are about deploying AI in health and education ([Schmidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/eric-schmidt-google/)).
- Sandholm calls value misalignment a fairly theoretical worry and names climate change and nuclear war as bigger concerns ([Sandholm](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/tuomas-sandholm-poker-and-game-theory/)).

### More signal for learning robots

- Abbeel says reinforcement learning's bottleneck is sparse reward, and points to self-play, human comparisons, demonstrations and meta-learning as ways to get more signal per experience ([Abbeel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/pieter-abbeel-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- He proposes training across many simulators so the real world becomes just another sample, and notes robots still lack a driving test ([Abbeel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/pieter-abbeel-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Russell warns self-driving cars may be the flagship of the next hype bust, since driving needs about eight nines of reliability ([Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).

### Self-improving and curious machines

- Schmidhuber argues general intelligence will be a short program that learns to improve its own learning, driven by curiosity measured as compression progress ([Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).
- He calls much of today's meta-learning simple transfer learning, while Abbeel sees meta-learning as a route to the hierarchy that long-horizon credit assignment needs ([Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/), [Abbeel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/pieter-abbeel-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Schmidhuber expects the next wave to be active machines that shape their own data, reaching far beyond the advertising uses of current AI ([Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).

### Game theory and strategy at scale

- Sandholm explains that Libratus beat top heads-up no-limit pros through Nash-equilibrium reasoning from the rules alone, without opponent data ([Sandholm](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/tuomas-sandholm-poker-and-game-theory/)).
- He wants to show business and military strategy can be done with computational game theory, citing kidney exchange and sourcing auctions at CombineNet ([Sandholm](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/tuomas-sandholm-poker-and-game-theory/)).
- Schmidt's own strategy lesson is scale: build general platforms for hundreds of millions of users and keep a real five-year model of platform change ([Schmidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/eric-schmidt-google/)).
