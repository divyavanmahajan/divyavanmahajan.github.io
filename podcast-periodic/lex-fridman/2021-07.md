## Overview

July 2021 was one of the show's broadest months, with ten guests and little AI. Yeonmi Park and Michael Malice, in the 200th episode, discussed totalitarianism and freedom, while three scientists covered Planet Nine, string theory and the origin of life. Drug policy came up repeatedly, from a former cartel pilot to the head of MAPS.

## Main themes

### Totalitarianism, freedom and the state

- Yeonmi Park argues that political systems, not culture, separate North and South Korea, and that the outside world is choosing not to act. She holds the Chinese Communist Party largely accountable for sustaining Kim Jong-un ([Yeonmi Park](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/yeonmi-park-north-korea/)).
- Fridman pushes back on casting China as an enemy, arguing that demonising it makes leaders harden rather than reform ([Yeonmi Park](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/yeonmi-park-north-korea/)).
- Michael Malice argues that much totalitarian evil comes from mediocre people enjoying small amounts of power, and that the state is illegitimate force. Fridman defends government as an improvable collective ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).

### Leadership, discipline and endurance

- Jocko Willink says harsh feedback works only when the team knows the leader cares, and that humility and owning mistakes matter most. Fridman tests this against Elon Musk, Steve Jobs and Sundar Pichai ([Jocko Willink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/jocko-willink-war-leadership-and-discipline/)).
- On autonomous weapons, Fridman worries about an AI race with China. Willink would accept more surgical machines with protocols and a kill switch, while admitting he does not know AI's shortfalls ([Jocko Willink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/jocko-willink-war-leadership-and-discipline/)).
- Zach Bitter says ultramarathon success rests on a large aerobic base and a big-picture reason to keep going. He treats his low-carb diet as personal, not universal ([Zach Bitter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/zach-bitter-ultramarathon-running/)).

### The outer solar system and fundamental physics

- Konstantin Batygin argues that clustered orbits in the distant Kuiper belt point to Planet Nine, about five Earth masses, and that our solar system is a rare outcome ([Konstantin Batygin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/konstantin-batygin-planet-9-and-the-edge-of-our-solar-system/)).
- Batygin favours compressed understanding over ever-larger simulations, and says Starlink interferes with observing ([Konstantin Batygin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/konstantin-batygin-planet-9-and-the-edge-of-our-solar-system/)).
- Cumrun Vafa concedes string theory has no experimental evidence yet, but argues its unplanned links between gravity, particle physics and mathematics are strong theoretical evidence ([Cumrun Vafa](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/cumrun-vafa-string-theory/)).

### Life, origins and the planet

- Sara Walker argues life is best understood through a physics of information and causal history. Her assembly theory work with Lee Cronin's lab offers a biosignature that does not depend on single molecules ([Sara Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/sara-walker-the-origin-of-life-on-earth-and-alien-worlds/)).
- Walker expects AGI to emerge as a planetary-scale phenomenon that includes humans ([Sara Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/sara-walker-the-origin-of-life-on-earth-and-alien-worlds/)).
- Anya Fernald argues regenerative grazing can make meat part of the climate solution, and that AI and better data could help scale the human attention it needs ([Anya Fernald](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/anya-fernald-regenerative-farming-and-the-art-of-cooking-meat/)).

### Drug policy

- Roger Reaves recounts flying loads for the Medellin Cartel and decades in prison, says the money was not worth it, and argues drugs should be treated as a health issue ([Roger Reaves](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/roger-reaves-smuggling-drugs-for-pablo-escobar-and-the-medellin-cartel/)).
- Rick Doblin describes MAPS's non-profit route to bringing MDMA-assisted therapy through regulators, funded by donations and a public benefit corporation, with licensed legal access as a longer-term goal ([Rick Doblin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/rick-doblin-psychedelics/)).
- Malice and Fridman both criticise the war on drugs ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).
