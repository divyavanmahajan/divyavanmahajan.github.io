## Overview

January ranged widely, from Stalin and Putin to the YouTube recommender. The AI thread ran through a psychologist, a philosopher and a roboticist: what deep learning lacks, whether machines could be conscious, and who is responsible when automated systems fail people. Economics and mathematics filled out the month.

## Main themes

### What deep learning lacks, and what consciousness is

- Daniel Kahneman frames deep learning as a System 1 achievement: fast pattern matching without reasoning, causality or grounded meaning. He sides with those who expect neural networks to hit limits, while Fridman relays Yann LeCun's more optimistic view ([Kahneman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/daniel-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow-deep-learning-and-ai/)).
- David Chalmers separates the hard problem of subjective experience from the easy problems of explaining behaviour. His working hypothesis is that computation, not biology, matters, so a simulated brain would also be conscious ([Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/)).
- Chalmers argues consciousness is the line for moral status and warns of highly intelligent but unconscious AGI. Fridman predicts a civil rights movement for robots ([Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/)).
- Cristos Goodrow estimates that understanding video content is less than a quarter solved after eight years, and doubts next-frame prediction alone will be enough ([YouTube](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/cristos-goodrow-youtube-algorithm/)).

### Trust and responsibility in automated systems

- Ayanna Howard says developers of safety-critical algorithms carry ethical responsibility, much as programmers once tested their own code. She proposes bug-bounty-style rewards for finding ethics holes ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).
- Howard defines trust by behaviour: people say they would not follow a robot in a burning building, then do. Kahneman suggests self-driving cars may need a model of the human mind to deal with pedestrians ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/), [Kahneman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/daniel-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow-deep-learning-and-ai/)).
- Goodrow describes YouTube's move from views to watch time to next-day satisfaction surveys, because watch time alone rewards regret viewing. Human reviewers label borderline content, and their labels train models ([YouTube](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/cristos-goodrow-youtube-algorithm/)).

### Automation, wages and safety nets

- Paul Krugman argues current technological change is no faster than earlier shocks such as containerisation, and that productivity data do not show robots taking jobs ([Krugman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/paul-krugman-economics-of-innovation-automation-safety-nets-universal/)).
- He blames stagnant wages on political choices and union decline, and prefers longer unemployment insurance, food stamps and family support to universal basic income ([Krugman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/paul-krugman-economics-of-innovation-automation-safety-nets-universal/)).
- Howard is more hopeful about workforce retraining than early education, but worries about people without strong schooling ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).

### Power, history and mathematics

- Stephen Kotkin argues the central question in politics is whether executive power is constrained, and applies it to Stalin's contingent rise and Putin's Russia ([Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).
- Grant Sanderson says mathematics is understood from concrete examples upward and that notation shapes thought. He sees math as alternating between discovery and invention ([Sanderson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/grant-sanderson-3blue1brown-and-the-beauty-of-mathematics/)).
