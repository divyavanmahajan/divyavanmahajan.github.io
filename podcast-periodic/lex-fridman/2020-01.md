## Overview

January 2020 ranged widely: a historian on power, an economist on automation, a psychologist and a philosopher on the minds of machines. Two guests, Ayanna Howard and Cristos Goodrow, spoke from inside the systems they build, on robot ethics and on YouTube's recommender. Grant Sanderson rounded out the month with a conversation about how mathematics is understood and taught.

## Main themes

### Power and its constraints

- Stephen Kotkin argues the central question in politics is whether executive power is limited, because “people make more mistakes when they're not challenged” ([Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).
- He calls Stalin's rise contingent, and says every attempt to abolish markets, private property and parliaments produced tyranny and shortages. He presents this as history, not as his own politics ([Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).
- On Putin, Kotkin credits growth of about 7% a year in the first two terms, but says support rests partly on the lack of alternatives. He suggests the Russian wish for a strong hand may come from failed post-Soviet institutions rather than culture ([Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).

### Bias, trust and responsibility in AI systems

- Ayanna Howard separates bias from prejudice and notes that training data is historical, so it carries past discrimination into areas such as predictive policing ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).
- She compares coding to medicine and says developers should test the ethical outcomes of their own work. She also proposes bug-bounty-style rewards for finding ethics holes ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).
- Cristos Goodrow says YouTube's top priority is responsibility: human reviewers label misinformation and borderline videos, and machine learning extends their decisions across billions of videos. Borderline content is demoted rather than removed ([YouTube](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/cristos-goodrow-youtube-algorithm/)).

### Machine minds: System 1 and consciousness

- Daniel Kahneman sees deep learning as “more like a system one product than like a system two product”: predictive pattern matching without causality or meaning ([Kahneman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/daniel-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow-deep-learning-and-ai/)).
- Kahneman suggests self-driving cars may need a model of the human mind to handle pedestrians, while Howard expects early success in slow, fixed settings such as closed campuses ([Kahneman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/daniel-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow-deep-learning-and-ai/), [Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).
- David Chalmers holds that computation, not biology, matters for consciousness, and that consciousness decides moral status. A machine genuinely puzzled by its own experience would be some evidence ([Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/)).
- On the simulation hypothesis, Chalmers says “even if we are in a simulation, all of this is real” ([Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/)).

### Automation, jobs and safety nets

- Paul Krugman argues current technological change is no bigger than earlier shocks such as containerisation, and that productivity data do not show robots taking jobs ([Krugman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/paul-krugman-economics-of-innovation-automation-safety-nets-universal/)).
- He blames stagnant wages on political choices and union decline, and prefers stronger targeted programmes to universal basic income. Howard, more hopeful about retraining, worries that people without strong education will struggle to adapt ([Krugman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/paul-krugman-economics-of-innovation-automation-safety-nets-universal/), [Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/)).

### Recommendation and explanation

- Goodrow traces YouTube's recommender from collaborative filtering and a related graph, through watch time, to survey-based satisfaction meant to avoid regret viewing ([YouTube](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/cristos-goodrow-youtube-algorithm/)).
- Grant Sanderson argues understanding builds from concrete examples upward, which is why he uses video, and admits his 3Blue1Brown videos are only one part of learning. Goodrow cites the channel as explaining linear algebra in a way a whiteboard cannot ([Sanderson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/grant-sanderson-3blue1brown-and-the-beauty-of-mathematics/), [YouTube](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/cristos-goodrow-youtube-algorithm/)).
