## Overview

June 2021 had two clear clusters. Vitalik Buterin and Charles Hoskinson set out competing visions for proof-of-stake blockchains, and three episodes dealt with risk, institutions and trust in the shadow of the pandemic. Mathematics, longevity as a business and the Venus phosphine debate filled the rest of the month.

## Main themes

### Proof of stake and blockchain design

- Vitalik Buterin says Ethereum's next stage is rolling out step by step: proof of stake cuts energy use and new issuance, while sharding plus rollups could bring about 100x times 100x in scale ([Ethereum 2.0](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-2-0/)).
- Buterin calls himself a medium blocker, expects ZK rollups to beat optimistic rollups, and credits multiple client implementations with getting Ethereum through past attacks and bugs ([Ethereum 2.0](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-2-0/)).
- Charles Hoskinson presents Cardano as a financial operating system built on 105 peer-reviewed papers and Haskell, with a treasury of over a billion dollars spent through community voting ([Cardano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/charles-hoskinson-cardano/)).
- Hoskinson criticises Bitcoin's culture as a religion and calls Polkadot Ethereum 1.5. Fridman defends fun and risk-taking as sources of innovation ([Cardano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/charles-hoskinson-cardano/)).

### Civilizational risk and coordination

- Daniel Schmachtenberger argues that humanity has god-like technology but Stone Age game theory, and that the Bretton Woods order cannot handle many actors with cheap catastrophic weapons ([Schmachtenberger](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/daniel-schmachtenberger-steering-civilization-away-from-self-destructi/)).
- He uses Facebook's news feed as an example of harm through side effects, and warns that any single metric becomes a paperclip maximizer. Fridman is more optimistic about markets and platforms like Wikipedia ([Schmachtenberger](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/daniel-schmachtenberger-steering-civilization-away-from-self-destructi/)).
- Rob Reid ranks engineered pathogens above AI and nuclear weapons as a 30-year risk, and calls for an international agreement against risky pathogen research plus affordable detection systems ([Rob Reid](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/rob-reid-the-existential-threat-of-engineered-viruses-and-lab-leaks/)).

### Institutions, trust and censorship

- Reid and Fridman criticise public-health messaging and argue that stating probabilities would have built more trust ([Rob Reid](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/rob-reid-the-existential-threat-of-engineered-viruses-and-lab-leaks/)).
- Bret Weinstein argues that scientific institutions were captured and that platforms such as YouTube amplified this by censoring dissent. Fridman frames the episode as a defence of open discussion and says he disagrees with Weinstein on several points ([Bret Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/bret-weinstein-truth-science-and-censorship-in-the-time-of-a-pandemic/)).
- On running YouTube, Weinstein would limit content only by law, while Fridman proposes recommendations that show a wider range of views ([Bret Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/bret-weinstein-truth-science-and-censorship-in-the-time-of-a-pandemic/)).

### Science, health data and the search for life

- David Sinclair predicts that wearables, cheap home tests and AI will give individuals better health data, and that hospital and insurer economics will drive adoption. Fridman presses him on privacy ([David Sinclair](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/david-sinclair-extending-the-human-lifespan-beyond-100-years/)).
- Jordan Ellenberg says much of modern mathematics asks when two things count as the same, and compares unusual notions of distance to word embeddings ([Jordan Ellenberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/jordan-ellenberg-mathematics-of-high-dimensional-shapes-and-geometries/)).
- Clara Sousa-Silva, co-author of the Venus phosphine paper, calls the detection exciting but unconfirmed, and says only about 4% of the molecular fingerprints needed for remote spectroscopy are understood ([Clara Sousa-Silva](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/clara-sousa-silva-searching-for-signs-of-life-on-venus-and-other-plane/)).
