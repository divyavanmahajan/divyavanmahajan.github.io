## Overview

November 2019 ranged widely across seven episodes. Elon Musk made the case for regulating AI and for Neuralink, Michael Kearns treated fairness and privacy as engineering constraints, and Bjarne Stroustrup defended abstraction as the route to performance. Noam Chomsky and Gilbert Strang offered contrasting views of deep learning, while Sean Carroll and Dava Newman covered physics and space.

## Main themes

### AI risk, regulation and Neuralink

- Musk argues there is heavy investment in AI but little in AI safety. He wants a regulatory agency comparable to the FAA, warning that agencies usually form only after “something terrible happens, there's a huge public outcry” ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).
- He says Neuralink will first target brain-related conditions, but is ultimately meant to address the existential risk of digital superintelligence by letting humans keep pace ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).
- Asked about Neuralink, Chomsky says a machine could expand cognition only the way a book does, not beyond native capacities ([Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/)).
- Musk also says highway Autopilot rarely needs intervention, while complex traffic lights and winding roads remain unsolved ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).

### Fairness, privacy and markets as algorithms

- Kearns notes a proof that three reasonable fairness notions cannot all hold at once. He calls fairness “a harder problem” than privacy, where differential privacy is the accepted definition ([Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).
- He wants a Pareto curve of error against unfairness to become the interface with policymakers, who must choose the trade-off ([Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).
- Kearns says algorithms first took over trade execution, and sees statistical arbitrage over seconds to days as the current sweet spot for quant trading ([Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).

### Abstraction and engineering discipline in C++

- Stroustrup says C++ is for people who want to use hardware well and manage that complexity through abstraction, which he calls his main tool for performance ([Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/)).
- He describes concepts as compile-time predicates arriving in C++20 after a failed attempt for C++0x ([Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/)).
- Standardisation began in 1989 at the request of IBM and HP. The committee works by consensus with one vote per organisation and now ships every three years ([Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/)).

### What deep learning is, and is not

- Strang argues linear algebra deserves pride of place because data arrives as matrices. He frames neural networks as linear algebra plus a simple folding non-linearity ([Strang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/gilbert-strang-linear-algebra-deep-learning-teaching-and-mit-opencours/)).
- Chomsky calls deep learning useful engineering but not science. A system that learns a language based on linear order as easily as a real one is, in his view, a scientific failure ([Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/)).
- Carroll and Chomsky both returned to Newton and Einstein; Chomsky argues Newton's action without contact ended the search for an intelligible world ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/sean-carroll-quantum-mechanics-and-the-many-worlds-interpretation/), [Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/)).

### Physics and space

- Carroll argues many-worlds is the simplest version of quantum mechanics and the best starting point for explaining how spacetime emerges ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/sean-carroll-quantum-mechanics-and-the-many-worlds-interpretation/)).
- Dava Newman says “we really need a decade on the moon” as a testbed before people reach Mars in the 2030s. Her BioSuit is technically feasible but still in research and development ([Newman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/dava-newman-space-exploration-space-suits-and-life-on-mars/)).
