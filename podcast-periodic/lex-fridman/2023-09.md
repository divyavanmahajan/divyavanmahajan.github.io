## Overview

September ranged widely, but technology was framed through people: Mark Zuckerberg recorded as a photorealistic avatar, and Walter Isaacson explained what drives Elon Musk. The other three episodes covered free speech on campus, how marriages fail, and a designer who wants products grown rather than built.

## Main themes

### Presence, mixed reality and many AIs

- Zuckerberg and Lex Fridman recorded from different states as Codec Avatars. The headset sends only an encoded expression, and Meta's goal is a phone scan of 2 to 3 minutes ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).
- Zuckerberg calls the $500 Quest 3 the first mainstream mixed reality device. He predicts rooms could hold as many holograms as physical objects by about the end of the decade ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).
- He prefers many specialised AIs to one superintelligence: a Meta AI assistant, business AIs and character AIs. He says the value of open-sourcing Llama 2 is significantly greater than the risks ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).

### Musk, biography and real-world AI

- Isaacson argues that Musk, like many driven innovators, is harnessing the demons of a difficult childhood, and that his faults and achievements cannot be separated ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)).
- He links Einstein, Leonardo, Jobs and Musk through visual thinking, and predicts Musk will be remembered for real-world AI such as FSD 12 and Optimus ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)).
- On Twitter, Isaacson describes staff cuts of about 85% and judges that Musk probably overdid it by roughly 20% ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)).

### Free speech and cancel culture

- Greg Lukianoff of FIRE counts well over 1,000 attempts to punish professors between 2014 and July 2023, with around 190 firings, and compares this with the Red Scare ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).
- He describes rhetorical fortresses on both left and right that dismiss people by label instead of engaging their claims ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).
- He compares social media to the printing press, a period of epistemic anarchy that top-down bans cannot fix ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).

### Relationships and design with nature

- Divorce lawyer James Sexton argues marriages end through slow disconnection, not one big event. He calls prenups an excellent idea and warns against weaponising children ([Sexton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/james-sexton-divorce-lawyer-on-marriage-relationships-sex-lies-love/)).
- Neri Oxman argues design should grow products with nature rather than assemble them against it. Her company OXMAN works on guiding natural systems until they act on their own ([Oxman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/neri-oxman-biology-art-and-science-of-design-engineering-with-nature/)).
- Oxman draws a parallel with AI: what we fear from AGI, systems that set their own rules, is what she wants from her design work ([Oxman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/neri-oxman-biology-art-and-science-of-design-engineering-with-nature/)).
