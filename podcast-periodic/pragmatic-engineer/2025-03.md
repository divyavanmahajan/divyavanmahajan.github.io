## Overview

March on The Pragmatic Engineer looked at how software gets built inside four very different organisations: a small design-led app company, Uber's developer platform, the Linux kernel community and Figma. The common thread was engineering culture and tooling rather than AI, although AI coding tools came up in passing. Rust was the one item that recurred across episodes.

## Main themes

### Design-first engineering and owning the canvas

- Balint Orosz argues that a standout product needs both a backend and a UI-first engineering culture. He says an amazing product “cannot really have one engineering culture inside of the company” ([Craft](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/design-first-software-engineering-craft-with-balint-orosz/)).
- He says design-focused engineers are undervalued: making a click take 2 ms instead of 10 ms is harder to credit than a $2 million infrastructure saving, and big-company promotion frameworks favour measurable impact ([Craft](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/design-first-software-engineering-craft-with-balint-orosz/)).
- Craft avoids Auto Layout and SwiftUI and builds its own components for control. One codebase, about 99% shared, covers iPhone, iPad, Mac and Vision Pro, and the product team is around 20 people with no product managers ([Craft](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/design-first-software-engineering-craft-with-balint-orosz/)).
- Figma Slides also builds on its own canvas: a C++ codebase compiled to WebAssembly, with TypeScript and React for panels. Noah Finer says teams on Figma's editors are half C++ or moving towards more ([Figma Slides](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/building-figma-slides-with-noah-finer-and-jonathan-kaufman/)).

### Developer experience run as a product

- Gautam Korlam says Uber's developer platform treated internal developers as customers, with published SLOs and funnel analytics. Developer NPS went from about -50 to about +8 during his time ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).
- He found code review time, not CI, was the biggest bottleneck, and argues metrics should come from what engineers say slows them rather than an industry framework ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).
- Uber built SubmitQueue to keep main green and DevPods cloud environments that booted in about six seconds. Korlam calls DevPods one of the most loved internal products ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).
- At Figma, Finer describes 30-minute eng crits on a FigJam board, which he says lower the stakes compared with sending out a document. Risky changes go behind more than 2,000 feature flags ([Figma Slides](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/building-figma-slides-with-noah-finer-and-jonathan-kaufman/)).

### How Linux is built, and where Rust fits

- Greg Kroah-Hartman says the kernel runs on trust between people, a nine-week release cycle and companies contributing for their own reasons. About 80% of contributors are paid by employers ([Linux](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/how-linux-is-built-with-greg-kroah-hartman/)).
- He says there are no project managers: planning happens inside companies before patches arrive, and maintainership follows the person, not the employer ([Linux](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/how-linux-is-built-with-greg-kroah-hartman/)).
- The kernel has about 25,000 lines of Rust, mostly bindings. Kroah-Hartman says “the Red Hat developers are starting to write the new NVIDIA GPU drivers in Rust”, and estimates Rust would prevent about half the bugs he has seen ([Linux](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/how-linux-is-built-with-greg-kroah-hartman/)).
- Rust also came up with Korlam, who says it has been his favourite language since starting Gitar ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).

### AI across the development lifecycle

- Korlam says autocomplete “helps you less type less but think more”, while taste stays with the developer. He argues current tools focus too narrowly on completion or review ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).
- He expects agents to check other agents' work but wants a human in the loop before check-in. Gitar's agent targets maintenance work such as library upgrades ([Uber DevEx](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/developer-experience-at-uber-with-gautam-korlam/)).
- Balint Orosz says OpenAI's o1 helped him build Apple Pencil shape recognition in three hours, while Kroah-Hartman sees little impact from LLMs on kernel code ([Craft](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/design-first-software-engineering-craft-with-balint-orosz/), [Linux](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/03/how-linux-is-built-with-greg-kroah-hartman/)).
