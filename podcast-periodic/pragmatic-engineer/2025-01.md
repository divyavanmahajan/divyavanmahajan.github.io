## Overview

January on The Pragmatic Engineer covered careers inside large tech companies from two sides: the recruiter's and the senior engineer's. Charity Majors made the case for a new model of observability. The month closed with an indie game team whose success came from skipping most of the practices the other guests take for granted.

## Main themes

### How Big Tech hires

- Blake Stockman, a recruiter at Google, Meta, Uber and Flexport, says larger companies add committee after committee, but the hiring manager should own the decision. He estimates only about 20% of hires were unanimous yeses ([Big Tech recruiter](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/confessions-of-a-big-tech-recruiter/)).
- Stockman reminds candidates that recruiters steward the company, so they should let the company name a number first. He says almost every offer has room to negotiate and multiple offers are the strongest lever ([Big Tech recruiter](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/confessions-of-a-big-tech-recruiter/)).
- Michael Novati describes Meta's 45-minute screen, whiteboard onsites and hiring committees of at least three directors or VPs that focused mostly on level ([Coding Machine](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/the-coding-machine-at-meta-with-michael-novati/)).

### Meta's culture and the IC career path

- Novati says Meta's coding machine archetype was created for him around 2015 to reward impact, such as one engineer launching a product that might need a team of 5 to 10 ([Coding Machine](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/the-coding-machine-at-meta-with-michael-novati/)).
- He says Meta wanted to break the idea that you must manage to progress, yet there were about 10 E8s against 20 to 30 directors. He now accepts that “you cannot replace 30,000 engineers with one engineer” ([Coding Machine](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/the-coding-machine-at-meta-with-michael-novati/)).
- At Facebook, internal tools were products on the same codebase as the public site. Novati says external senior hires often struggled to have impact and left ([Coding Machine](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/the-coding-machine-at-meta-with-michael-novati/)).

### Observability beyond the three pillars

- Charity Majors defines observability as understanding the intersection of code, systems and users. She argues for moving from separate metrics, logs and traces to wide structured events in columnar storage ([Observability](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/observability-the-present-and-future-with-charity-majors/)).
- Cardinality drives cost: Majors says teams on traditional tools “spend an outright majority of their time trying to govern cardinality”, though unique data is the most useful for debugging ([Observability](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/observability-the-present-and-future-with-charity-majors/)).
- She says the best time to instrument is while writing the code, and that SLOs should be the entry point. Honeycomb uses its error budget to kill its oldest Kafka node every Monday ([Observability](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/observability-the-present-and-future-with-charity-majors/)).

### Practices that depend on context

- Jonas Tyroller says the two-person Thronefall team wrote no unit tests, did no code review and pushed straight to main. Gergely Orosz argues such practices may not apply to a product that ships rarely ([Thronefall](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/building-a-best-selling-game-with-a-tiny-team-with-jonas-tyroller/)).
- Tyroller built one- or two-day prototypes for about two months and suggests scaling prototyping to project length. Dropping a card-game direction after a month felt like relief ([Thronefall](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/building-a-best-selling-game-with-a-tiny-team-with-jonas-tyroller/)).
- He says the challenge is the overlap between what you want to make and what will sell, since “games don't automatically sell better if they're bigger” ([Thronefall](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/01/building-a-best-selling-game-with-a-tiny-team-with-jonas-tyroller/)).
