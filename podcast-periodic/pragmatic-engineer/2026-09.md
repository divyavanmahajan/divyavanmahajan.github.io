## Overview

September on The Pragmatic Engineer was about working with coding agents day to day. Tibo Sottiaux explained how OpenAI builds Codex, and Matt Pocock and Maggie Appleton described how engineers and designers steer agents. Peter Mattis closed the month with distributed databases and his return to coding with AI.

## Main themes

### Codex and the shrinking harness

- Tibo Sottiaux says Codex was built in Rust for robustness, security and efficiency, and open-sourced for onboarding and community energy, though that also brings low-quality contributions ([Building Codex](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/building-codex-with-tibo-sottiaux/)).
- He says “the harness, in a sense, is always a little bit ahead of the model”. The developer message and the harness both shrink as models improve ([Building Codex](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/building-codex-with-tibo-sottiaux/)).
- Sottiaux says the first cloud Codex had too much friction, which is why the CLI launched too. ChatGPT Work now runs the full Codex harness on a cloud computer, and Gergely Orosz notes Codex crossed 20 million active users ([Building Codex](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/building-codex-with-tibo-sottiaux/)).
- Codex also came up with Maggie Appleton and Peter Mattis, alongside Claude Code in Pocock's and Mattis's episodes ([Design Engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/design-engineering-with-maggie-appleton/), [Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/), [AI Skills](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/ai-skills-with-matt-pocock/)).

### Code review moves to machines

- Sottiaux says AI security review is mandatory on OpenAI pull requests and blocks merges automatically. He expects correctness review to be fully automated, with discussion of intent moving earlier ([Building Codex](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/building-codex-with-tibo-sottiaux/)).
- Mattis says agents need less and less scrutiny as they improve. He predicts that within a year or two engineers will stop reading code much as they stopped reading assembly ([Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/)).
- Mattis argues it is easier to make agents disciplined about testing than humans, and urges property-based, metamorphic and deterministic simulation testing ([Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/)).

### Steering agents with skills and sketches

- Matt Pocock says agents now handle tactical programming, leaving engineers the strategic layer. In his words, “Skills really, they're just a folder of markdown files” that the model or the user can invoke ([AI Skills](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/ai-skills-with-matt-pocock/)).
- Pocock keeps sessions under roughly 150k tokens: grill the user, write a spec, then one ticket per session. Terms from classic books, such as tracer bullets and deep modules, change how agents behave ([AI Skills](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/ai-skills-with-matt-pocock/)).
- Maggie Appleton sketches on paper because agents are weak at spatial reasoning, then has them build live prototypes she calls jigs. She sees planning and team decisions as the new bottleneck ([Design Engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/design-engineering-with-maggie-appleton/)).
- Appleton's term capability gaslighting describes models that do well on a task one day and fail it the next ([Design Engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/design-engineering-with-maggie-appleton/)).

### AI amplifies domain experts

- Mattis says AI stretches the range of engineering ability: “your ability before is now amplified.” A non-expert asking for a distributed database gets something hollow ([Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/)).
- Opus-class models brought him back to coding. He built a B-tree of about 10,000 lines of Rust in 30 minutes and runs five to 10 sessions at once ([Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/)).
- He traces B-trees from Gmail and Colossus to CockroachDB's range index, and says non-engineers at Cockroach Labs built 500 to 1,000 internal apps in a few months ([Distributed databases](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/09/distributed-databases-with-peter-mattis/)).
