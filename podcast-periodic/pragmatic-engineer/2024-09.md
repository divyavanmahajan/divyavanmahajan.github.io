## Overview

September 2024 is the launch month of The Pragmatic Engineer podcast. Gergely Orosz introduced the show with a short trailer, and the first full episode was a long conversation with Simon Willison on using AI tools for software engineering without the hype.

## Main themes

### A new show about building software from the inside

- Orosz frames the podcast as a conversational complement to his newsletter, blog and book. He says it covers “software engineering at big tech and startups from the inside” ([Trailer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/the-pragmatic-engineer-podcast-trailer/)).
- He promises deep dives with experienced engineers on what worked, what did not and why, aimed at both engineers and engineering managers ([Trailer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/the-pragmatic-engineer-podcast-trailer/)).

### LLMs as power-user tools

- Simon Willison argues LLMs have no manual. He says “There's a lot of intuition you have to build up in order to use them effectively” ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- He knows they are bad at current events, maths, logic puzzles and counting, and good at Python and JavaScript. He recommends side projects, the free top models and local models, which hallucinate enough to show how LLMs really behave ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- His stack: Claude 3.5 Sonnet as the default, which he calls the first time a company other than OpenAI had the clearly best model; GPT-4o for Code Interpreter and voice mode on dog walks; GitHub Copilot for autocomplete ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- He estimates he is two to three times faster at typing code, about 10% of his job. The bigger gain is ambition: he shipped Go to production without being a Go programmer, relying on experience to judge the code ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).

### Fine-tuning, RAG and what actually works

- Willison says “Fine-tuning on these things is mostly a waste of time” for adding knowledge such as company docs, and that it can make models hallucinate more. It does work for tasks such as SQL generation ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- He calls RAG a simple trick and the Hello World of LLM apps. A basic version is about 30 lines of Python, but a good one can take six months, because picking the right context is a long-studied information retrieval problem ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- He describes Code Interpreter answering a question about an uploaded SQLite database flawlessly, which felt like it solved the problem his tool Datasette exists for. He responded by adding LLM features to his own software ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).

### Hype, AGI and jobs

- Willison doubts AGI can come from auto-completing sentences and would welcome a plateau after two exhausting years. He argues LLMs write convincingly without being intelligent, which misleads people ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- On jobs he is optimistic: a custom CRM that once needed 20 people and six months might need five people and two months, which could raise demand for engineers. He hopes many more people learn basic programming ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
- He respects people who refuse models trained on unlicensed data, comparing it to veganism, but worries it could leave them at a professional disadvantage ([Simon Willison](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/09/ai-tools-for-software-engineers-but-without-the-hype-with-simon-willis/)).
