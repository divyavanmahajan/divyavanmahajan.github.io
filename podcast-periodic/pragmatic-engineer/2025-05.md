## Overview

May on The Pragmatic Engineer had three long interviews with Gergely Orosz. Two were about building with AI: Windsurf's CEO on the hard engineering behind an AI coding IDE, and Janvi Kalra on how she moved into AI engineering. The third looked at how Kubernetes, a very large open source project, organises releases, documentation and its volunteers.

## Main themes

### The engineering inside an AI coding IDE

- Varun Mohan says open models lacked fill-in-the-middle, a capability that needs substantial training rather than light post-training. That forced Windsurf to build its own models and training recipes early ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).
- Mohan targets time to first token under a couple of hundred milliseconds. GPUs have far more compute than memory bandwidth, so batching trades against latency, and he says a 10 millisecond increase materially reduces use ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).
- Embedding search alone had low recall, he says, so Windsurf fuses embeddings, keyword search, a knowledge graph and AST dependencies, then spends compute ranking snippets ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).
- The agent work behind Cascade failed for months. Mohan credits better retrieval and fast edit models, but says “if the models hadn't gotten better, we wouldn't have been able to release it” ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).

### AI and the demand for engineers

- Mohan argues that cheaper software should lead companies to build more. He calls employers who cut engineers because of AI short-sighted next to competitors who keep hiring ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).
- He expects engineers who only want to build boilerplate websites to see their skills depreciate, while problem solvers stay in demand. Inside Windsurf, he says, engineers now jump into unfamiliar code more readily ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/)).
- Vibe coding came up in both AI episodes. Mohan describes a partnerships lead with no software background building internal apps such as a quoting tool ([Windsurf](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/building-windsurf-with-varun-mohan/), [AI Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/from-software-engineer-to-ai-engineer-with-janvi-kalra/)).

### How Kubernetes is built

- Kat Cosgrove calls Kubernetes the second-largest open source project after Linux, with roughly 150 to 200 maintainers and more than a thousand contributors a month ([Kubernetes](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/how-kubernetes-is-built-with-kat-cosgrove/)).
- Everything is written down, including governance. Features go through a KEP modelled on Python's PEPs, and any user-facing change without documentation is reverted at the docs freeze ([Kubernetes](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/how-kubernetes-is-built-with-kat-cosgrove/)).
- To prevent burnout, release leads must sit out a cycle, and the SIG Release charter says “we will delay a release” rather than ask anyone to work nights or weekends ([Kubernetes](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/how-kubernetes-is-built-with-kat-cosgrove/)).
- Cosgrove gives her personal view that most generative AI tools are a scam; SIG Docs bans them. She advises startups to use a managed service and an experienced SRE ([Kubernetes](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/how-kubernetes-is-built-with-kat-cosgrove/)).

### From software engineer to AI engineer

- Janvi Kalra was first turned down for Coda's new two-engineer AI team. She taught herself deep learning basics, blogged and entered hackathons, and was accepted five months later ([AI Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/from-software-engineer-to-ai-engineer-with-janvi-kalra/)).
- She glued a search index, LLM infrastructure and a chat feature into a chatbot in a couple of days. It grew into Coda Brain, with 20 people working on it in the first half of 2024 ([AI Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/from-software-engineer-to-ai-engineer-with-janvi-kalra/)).
- Kalra says AI engineering rests on software engineering and often means building workarounds for model limits, then scrapping them. Coda moved from parsing JSON to function calling to MCP ([AI Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/05/from-software-engineer-to-ai-engineer-with-janvi-kalra/)).
