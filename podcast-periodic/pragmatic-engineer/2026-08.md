## Overview

August on The Pragmatic Engineer asked what engineers still own once agents write much of the code. Charity Majors and Addy Osmani answered with verification, production feedback and accountability. Casey Muratori made the case that performance is designed in, not tuned in later, and that reading assembly makes the gap visible.

## Main themes

### Skeptics, enthusiasts and shipping unread code

- Charity Majors says skepticism about AI was reasonable in 2025 but no longer is. Her own shift came with Opus 4.5 in November 2025, and she credits harnesses and tooling more than the model ([Charity Majors](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for-development-with-charity-majors/)).
- On the two camps, Majors says “neither side is making it up”. People on call see slop and melting mental models, while enthusiasts see real wins in rewrites and removing toil ([Charity Majors](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for-development-with-charity-majors/)).
- She calls code review overloaded and puts line-by-line validation at the bottom of its purposes. Trust lost in generation has to be rebuilt through tests, evals and production feedback ([Charity Majors](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for-development-with-charity-majors/)).
- Majors says 20 years of DevOps tried to connect people writing code to production and failed. She argues code should ship as soon as it is merged, and that OpenTelemetry auto-instrumentation now makes instrumenting faster than not ([Charity Majors](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for-development-with-charity-majors/)).

### Accountability and cognitive surrender

- Addy Osmani separates cognitive debt, the erosion of your understanding as you use AI more, from cognitive surrender, where the AI's answer becomes your own ([Addy Osmani](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering-with-addy-osmani/)).
- With 20 to 30 sub-agents running, he no longer reads every trajectory. He reads or asks for a summary of decisions and has agents log what they learned ([Addy Osmani](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering-with-addy-osmani/)).
- Osmani says an engineer's edge is taste, since an agent can check a spec but not judge what is good. Even if models catch up, he says “We still need engineers to be answerable for these different systems.” ([Addy Osmani](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering-with-addy-osmani/)).
- He expects engineering, product and UX roles to unbundle, and advises engineers to learn product, evangelism and go-to-market ([Addy Osmani](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/from-chrome-devtools-to-ai-engineering-with-addy-osmani/)).

### Performance as architecture

- Casey Muratori argues most software runs 10 to 100 times slower than it needs to because performance is not designed into the architecture. Profiling and tweaking only finds a local minimum ([Casey Muratori](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/why-performant-code-matters-but-gets-widely-ignored-with-casey-murator/)).
- On assembly he says “writing it, no. Reading it, essential.” About 20 to 30 instructions cover most compiler output ([Casey Muratori](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/why-performant-code-matters-but-gets-widely-ignored-with-casey-murator/)).
- He says the real cost of clean-code polymorphism is that the compiler cannot inline and optimize, not the virtual call itself ([Casey Muratori](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/why-performant-code-matters-but-gets-widely-ignored-with-casey-murator/)).
- Muratori calls licensable game engines the games industry's AI transition: new creativity, then so many releases that organic discovery is close to impossible ([Casey Muratori](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/08/why-performant-code-matters-but-gets-widely-ignored-with-casey-murator/)).
