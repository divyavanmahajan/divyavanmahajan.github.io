## Overview

July on The Pragmatic Engineer asked how engineering practice holds up when AI writes much of the code. Kent Beck looked back on TDD, XP and Agile, Dex Horthy explained context engineering, and Hillel Wayne made the case for formal methods. Gergely Orosz answered listener questions on hiring and Big Tech's AI efforts.

## Main themes

### Old practices, new pace

- Beck warns “we're accumulating code faster than we're accumulating trust”, and says development has sped up while the pace of business has not ([Kent Beck](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/how-kent-beck-shapes-the-software-engineering-industry/)).
- On the Chrysler C3 payroll project he threw away the code and restarted on three-week cycles, which became Extreme Programming. He objected to the word agile because nobody will say they are not agile ([Kent Beck](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/how-kent-beck-shapes-the-software-engineering-industry/)).
- His explore, expand, extract model explained how Facebook could be large and innovative at once. He argues AI has wiped the extract playbook clean, so everyone is exploring again, and that an AI manifesto is too soon ([Kent Beck](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/how-kent-beck-shapes-the-software-engineering-industry/)).

### Context engineering and the limits of unread code

- Horthy named context engineering after talking to about 100 AI engineers who had dropped frameworks for hand-written API calls. Factor three of his 12-Factor Agents repo, own your context window, is the one he says endures ([Context engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy/)).
- Because attention is quadratic, more context spreads the model's attention thin. He suggests about 100K tokens for smaller models and 200K for Opus 4.8-class models, beyond which models try increasingly extreme fixes ([Context engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy/)).
- HumanLayer ran a lights-off software factory from July 2025 and shut it down by November, after a mis-routed primary key took weeks to untangle. Horthy now keeps humans at design review in a research, design, plan workflow ([Context engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy/)).

### Formal methods meet AI

- Wayne argues engineers are bad at race conditions because feedback takes months, while TLA+ reports time-of-check-to-time-of-use bugs in seconds ([Formal methods](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/formal-methods-with-hillel-wayne/)).
- He says full specs of real-world software are impractical and recommends property-based testing for most engineers ([Formal methods](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/formal-methods-with-hillel-wayne/)).
- LLMs fix spec syntax and explain long error traces, but Wayne says AI is “very bad at coming up with properties”. He sees adoption rising from about 0.1% to 0.3% ([Formal methods](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/formal-methods-with-hillel-wayne/)).

### Hiring and AI adoption in companies

- Orosz says he has not seen any company retrofit an AI-native SDLC; most are building AI infrastructure such as internal coding agents, and he wonders whether AI-native is overrated next to traction ([AMA](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/the-pragmatic-engineer-ama/)).
- Engineers who have built AI systems are hard to hire, while those with no AI exposure at work struggle. Wordsmith's Volodymyr Giginiak tests whether candidates can correct the AI's choices ([AMA](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/the-pragmatic-engineer-ama/)).
- Orosz rates Google as trying hardest and codes with Codex, Claude Code and Cursor; Horthy also discussed Claude Code. Wayne worries software work may become lower-paid ordinary white-collar work ([AMA](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/the-pragmatic-engineer-ama/), [Context engineering](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy/), [Formal methods](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/07/formal-methods-with-hillel-wayne/)).
