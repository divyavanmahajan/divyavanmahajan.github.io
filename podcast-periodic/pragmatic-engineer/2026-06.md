## Overview

June on The Pragmatic Engineer covered infrastructure and delivery practice, then turned to careers. Kelsey Hightower explained why Kubernetes won and how he retired early, Robert Erez of Octopus Deploy walked through CI/CD and GitOps, and Navdeep Singh of NeetCode discussed interviews and effort in an age of cheap AI code.

## Main themes

### Kubernetes and infrastructure as data

- Hightower says “the number one success criteria was Docker”: Kubernetes reused a runtime that already had global consensus, plus etcd ([Kelsey Hightower](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/kubernetes-and-retiring-at-the-top-with-kelsey-hightower/)).
- He argues Kubernetes moved the industry from infrastructure as code to infrastructure as data, gave infrastructure a type system with control loops, and, crediting Brendan Burns, made the API extensible with custom objects ([Kelsey Hightower](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/kubernetes-and-retiring-at-the-top-with-kelsey-hightower/)).
- At CoreOS, Hightower got Kubernetes running overnight after the embargo notice, and the company deprecated its own Fleet scheduler to go all in. At Google he had the Kubernetes team install it without scripts, which led to fixes such as kubeadm ([Kelsey Hightower](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/kubernetes-and-retiring-at-the-top-with-kelsey-hightower/)).
- Erez says many Octopus customers run Kubernetes on their own hardware, including clusters on research vessels that update only when ships return to port ([CI/CD](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/ci-cd-with-robert-erez/)).

### Shipping safely: toggles, rolling forward and GitOps

- Erez describes maturity stages from YOLO deploys to continuous integration, continuous delivery and continuous deployment. He says continuous delivery, which also tests the deployment process, is enough for many regulated companies ([CI/CD](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/ci-cd-with-robert-erez/)).
- He prefers feature toggles over canaries: a toggle's unit of change can be one line and can be switched off in seconds. With databases involved he advises rolling forward rather than back ([CI/CD](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/ci-cd-with-robert-erez/)).
- Erez notes that none of GitOps' four pillars mention Git, and that most customers just want to ship software. He expects AI-generated code to shift pipeline priorities from speed to reducing risk ([CI/CD](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/ci-cd-with-robert-erez/)).

### AI, careers and effort

- Hightower says engineers who saw their job as only writing code are most afraid, and should expect little sympathy from professions software disrupted. He adds that decision making can benefit from going slow ([Kelsey Hightower](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/kubernetes-and-retiring-at-the-top-with-kelsey-hightower/)).
- He treated money as “freedom tokens”, kept his lifestyle flat and retired three years ago ([Kelsey Hightower](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/kubernetes-and-retiring-at-the-top-with-kelsey-hightower/)).
- Singh says coding interviews persist because nothing better scales, and that Google has largely returned to onsite interviews because of AI cheating tools ([NeetCode](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/tech-interviews-with-neetcode/)).
- Most NeetCode code is now written by AI. Singh warns that shipping fast without measuring impact can make products worse, and blames weak AI-made redesign entries on a lack of effort and caring rather than intelligence ([NeetCode](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/06/tech-interviews-with-neetcode/)).
