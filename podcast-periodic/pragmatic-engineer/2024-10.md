## Overview

October's two episodes looked at how software companies run once the 2021 boom is over. Sourcegraph CEO Quinn Slack reflected on growth mistakes and an incremental approach to AI coding, and the Bending Spoons leadership described buying products like Evernote, rebuilding them and running the company on radical simplicity.

## Main themes

### From 2021 growth to an efficient scaleup

- Quinn Slack says Sourcegraph's revenue grew more than 30x during the pandemic years, but managers ran the growth with a peacetime mindset and some 2021 products should not have made the cut ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).
- He says many people joined companies, Sourcegraph included, for the rocket-ship story rather than a love of code. Today the company ships many times a day and expects engineers to tie their work to business value ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).
- To pivot to Cody, everyone regrouped every month or two around stack-ranked priorities. Slack says this job fair worked for about six months but hurt ownership and camaraderie, so long-lived teams returned ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).
- Sourcegraph, at about 180 people, moved from location-independent to zone-based pay. Slack says the old model created odd incentives and hiring limits ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).

### AI coding by small increments

- Slack compares fully autonomous agents to concept cars with no steering wheel. He argues that what worked for self-driving was “going from the human driving 100% to 99 to 98 to 97” ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).
- He prefers handing AI small tasks such as updating a changelog, checked against ground truth like type checks, tests and deploys. Agent-first tools may suit toy projects but, he says, fail in existing codebases ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).
- He predicts more investment in very fast builds so AI can iterate, and sees new graduates as more fluent with AI than senior engineers ([Sourcegraph](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/efficient-scaleups-in-2024-vs-2021-sourcegraph-with-ceo-co-founder-qui/)).

### Buying products to run for the long term

- Luca Ferrari says Bending Spoons is not a fund: “We're using our own money.” He cites about $700 million in revenue and profit in every one of its 11 years ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
- Each deal starts with a learning phase of weeks to months, often followed by a smaller, more autonomous organisation. Ferrari calls layoffs painful and accepts that critics will disagree ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
- Francesco Mancone says Evernote ran as a Java 11 monolith on 750 manually provisioned VMs. The team moved data to managed databases, replaced client polling with event-driven sync and aimed to retire the monolith by year end ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
- Federico Simionato says the Evernote team shipped visible features such as collapsible sections and slash commands alongside the long migrations ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).

### Radical simplicity

- Ferrari puts the burden of proof on whoever wants to add complexity. Orosz names radical simplicity as one of his top takeaways ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
- In practice that means aiming for no on-call on many products, fixed pay without bonuses and few titles or levels. Ferrari argues that without an on-call fallback engineers think harder about corner cases ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
- He says the company prefers hiring talented new graduates and reports about 1% unwanted churn per year ([Bending Spoons](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/10/twisting-the-rules-of-building-software-bending-spoons-the-team-behind/)).
