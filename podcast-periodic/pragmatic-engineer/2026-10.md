## Overview

October on The Pragmatic Engineer looked at how established engineering practice holds up as AI agents write more of the code. Uber's AI infrastructure and Titus Winters on Google both argued that tests, tooling and platforms built before AI now decide who benefits from it. Sam Newman covered resilience and microservices, and Will Larson argued that managers must be technical again.

## Main themes

### AI agents on a pre-AI developer platform

- Uday Kiran Medisetty says Uber's agent tooling moved fast because DevPods, API registries, tracing and test environments were already in place. Gergely Orosz concludes that companies with strong pre-AI platform teams gain the most ([Uber's AI infra](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside/)).
- DevPods are cloud developer boxes, one per monorepo, with Claude Code and Codex preinstalled. A mega DevPod with all repositories runs Minion, the background agent that Medisetty says produces around 15% of production PRs ([Uber's AI infra](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside/)).
- Gateways sit in front of model and tool calls, and AIFX lets skills follow engineers across harnesses. Orosz says the harness is the easy part and the hard part is everything around it ([Uber's AI infra](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside/)).

### Measuring output and the review bottleneck

- Medisetty says Uber now tracks “not necessarily just diff velocity, but feature velocity”. Tagged PRs show the feature share rising and maintenance falling ([Uber's AI infra](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside/)).
- Reviews have become a big bottleneck at Uber. Every PR still gets human review because incidents are costly, though low-risk PRs may later auto-land in a green lane ([Uber's AI infra](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/ubers-ai-infra-from-the-inside/)).
- Winters cites senior engineers reporting code up 70% and outages up 30% while feature counts stay flat. He argues index metrics miss work that changes what is possible ([Titus Winters](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/software-engineering-at-google-cs-education-with-titus-winters/)).

### Resilience and microservices

- Newman defines microservices first by independent deployability and second by business-domain boundaries. He says the best reason to adopt them is wanting team autonomy ([Sam Newman](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/building-resilient-systems-with-sam-newman/)).
- He says most outages come from resource saturation and that business context decides failure modes. Orosz describes Uber moving from failing open to failing closed on payments ([Sam Newman](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/building-resilient-systems-with-sam-newman/)).
- On AI, Newman advises being multi-vendor and multi-model and warns of cognitive surrender, which he says is “basically LGTM, right? But for whatever the AI tells me” ([Sam Newman](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/building-resilient-systems-with-sam-newman/)).

### Safe change at scale and CS education

- Winters says Google's mandatory tests made a 10-12 person cleanup of about 250 million lines of C++ for Abseil possible. He calls it “probably the largest refactoring task in human history” ([Titus Winters](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/software-engineering-at-google-cs-education-with-titus-winters/)).
- He argues AI should generate deterministic rewrite tools and fill in the gaps, rather than running inference forever ([Titus Winters](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/software-engineering-at-google-cs-education-with-titus-winters/)).
- Winters says the CS curriculum has barely changed since about 2000 and needs more design, specification and testing. He asks whether CS enrollment will fall as software engineering programmes grow ([Titus Winters](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/software-engineering-at-google-cs-education-with-titus-winters/)).

### Managers back in the details

- Larson argues that ZIRP-era norms kept managers out of technical decisions, and that AI tools now expect leaders to be hands-on. He says he has shipped over 500 PRs in about 18 months at Imprint ([Will Larson](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/the-revised-rules-of-engineering-management-with-will-larson/)).
- He sees line managers as better future executives than middle managers, and says durable, high-ownership teams are the only way to build skilled organisations ([Will Larson](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/10/the-revised-rules-of-engineering-management-with-will-larson/)).
