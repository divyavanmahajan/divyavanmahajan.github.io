## Overview

August on Practical AI kept returning to responsibility: who decides about AI, who protects the data, and who tests the models. Mozilla guests and a hosts-only privacy episode framed that from two sides. The rest of the month covered model delivery in CI, an AI pilot for crowded airspace, consented deepfakes and DeepMind's AlphaFold.

## Main themes

### Power and accountability in AI

- Solana Larsen explains that Mozilla's 2022 Internet Health Report is the first devoted to one subject, built around who has power over AI. She says treating AI as magic serves those who use it to exert power ([Mozilla](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/ai-irl-mozilla-s-internet-health-report/)).
- Bridget Todd cites Shmyla Khan's point that technology is built without imagining users in places like Pakistan. Larsen warns against claims that AI is “good for Africa, or this is good for women or whatever, but it's not always true” ([Mozilla](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/ai-irl-mozilla-s-internet-health-report/)).
- Daniel Whitenack says big tech mines language communities for data without building systems that benefit them. Larsen describes gig workers tracking their own data and argues for community-built datasets ([Mozilla](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/ai-irl-mozilla-s-internet-health-report/)).

### Privacy is now the practitioner's job

- Whitenack recalls the early data science habit of storing everything, and says the easiest way to handle a privacy concern is not to have the data. Chris Benson argues practitioners must now justify what they collect and why ([Privacy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/privacy-in-the-age-of-ai/)).
- Whitenack describes de-identification with named entity recognition and pseudonyms, using tools such as Scrubadub. Benson warns that removing too much can make a model useless, and that combining data sources can compromise privacy ([Privacy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/privacy-in-the-age-of-ai/)).
- Benson notes that in Georgia only one party needs to know a call is recorded, so laws do not enforce good practice. Whitenack says tool builders should make misuse hard by design ([Privacy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/privacy-in-the-age-of-ai/)).

### Shipping models and flying autonomously

- Whitenack describes Baseten's open-source Truss, which packages a model and its dependencies into a Docker image. His team runs minimum functionality tests in GitHub Actions so a bad model fails the build; Benson calls this the exception rather than the rule ([CMU AI pilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/cmu-s-ai-pilot-lands-in-the-news/)).
- Benson describes Carnegie Mellon's AI pilot, tested in simulation, that avoids collisions, predicts other aircraft's intent and talks on the radio using NLP, with a six-camera vision system ([CMU AI pilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/cmu-s-ai-pilot-lands-in-the-news/)).
- He thinks “all of us will be getting on airliners that are almost entirely automated”, but says coordination at scale and cybersecurity remain open problems ([CMU AI pilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/cmu-s-ai-pilot-lands-in-the-news/)).

### Deepfakes with consent

- Lior Hakim of Hour One presents deepfakes as virtual humans. He expects a character economy where people are paid to lend their face or voice ([Deepfakes](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/practical-positive-uses-for-deep-fakes/)).
- Hakim says temporal stability is the hard technical part, and advises practitioners to treat the end-to-end data pipeline as the main challenge ([Deepfakes](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/practical-positive-uses-for-deep-fakes/)).

### AlphaFold and AI for science

- The hosts discuss the release that expanded the AlphaFold database from about 1 million to over 200 million predicted structures. Benson compares it to the release of the human genome ([AlphaFold](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/alphafold-is-revolutionizing-biology/)).
- Whitenack notes the inference pipeline is open source and the work already has more than 4,000 citations. He highlights a self-distillation training trick anyone can borrow to augment a dataset ([AlphaFold](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/08/alphafold-is-revolutionizing-biology/)).
