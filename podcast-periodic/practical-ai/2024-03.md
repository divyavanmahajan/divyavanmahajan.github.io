## Overview

March on Practical AI split between building with LLMs and asking what they mean for developers. Devin, the AI coding agent, and prompt engineering were the two threads that ran across episodes. The other two shows looked at efficient computer vision and language models, and at how Runway makes video generation controllable for artists.

## Main themes

### AI coding agents and developer jobs

- Kevin Ball reads Devin's published benchmark of 13.86% of issues resolved as about one in seven, and asks how a team would know which one succeeded ([AI vs software devs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/ai-vs-software-devs/)).
- Ball says Devin could be useful in skilled hands but is nowhere near trustworthy enough to replace a developer. He would not give a hallucination-prone LLM raw access to spin up cloud resources ([AI vs software devs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/ai-vs-software-devs/)).
- Nick Nisi thinks the excitement comes from “what it represents and the progress that it's made”, not from current capability. He asks whether three to five times faster means three to five times fewer engineers ([AI vs software devs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/ai-vs-software-devs/)).
- Ball expects short-term disruption but argues cheaper software opens new markets. The Go Time panel sees code generation as a productivity tool, and Johnny Boursiquot warns that companies banning AI coding tools will lose engineers ([AI vs software devs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/ai-vs-software-devs/)).

### Prompt engineering as a discipline

- Jared Zoneraich of PromptLayer defines prompt engineering as tuning every input to the LLM: the prompt, the model choice and parameters such as temperature ([Prompting the future](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/prompting-the-future/)).
- He says tricks like offering a $100 tip “work today. They're not going to last forever”, while differences between models will persist. Teams should track inputs against outputs and treat the model as a black box ([Prompting the future](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/prompting-the-future/)).
- Zoneraich recommends a small ground-truth dataset, backtesting on old responses, regression tests and slow releases, with prompts moved out of code into a registry ([Prompting the future](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/prompting-the-future/)).
- Jerod Santo takes a different view, calling prompt engineering a leaky abstraction that products will hide ([AI vs software devs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/ai-vs-software-devs/)).

### Efficient models for the edge

- Daniel Whitenack explains YOLOv9's programmable gradient information, an auxiliary reversible branch that preserves gradients in training and is removed at inference ([YOLOv9](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/yolov9-computer-vision-is-alive-and-well/)).
- He cites 42% fewer parameters and 21% less compute than YOLOv7 at comparable accuracy, and covers Microsoft's BitNet b1.58 with ternary weights ([YOLOv9](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/yolov9-computer-vision-is-alive-and-well/)).
- Whitenack notes that model shrinking that took five or six years in computer vision is happening in about one year for LLMs. He expects real deployments to mix local and cloud inference ([YOLOv9](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/yolov9-computer-vision-is-alive-and-well/)).

### Controllable video generation

- Anastasis Germanidis describes Runway's Gen-2 as a text-to-video and image-to-video model. With text-only prompts, users generated tens or hundreds of outputs to get one result ([Runway](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/generating-the-future-of-art-entertainment/)).
- He says Runway never saw negative effects from adding more controls, such as camera and object motion. Working daily with artists, not briefly having the best model, is what he sees as the differentiator ([Runway](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/03/generating-the-future-of-art-entertainment/)).
