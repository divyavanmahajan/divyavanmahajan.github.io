## Overview

October on Practical AI ranged widely. Keith Lynn described Lockheed Martin's AlphaPilot drone racing challenge, and the hosts looked into Chinese AI companies placed on the US Entity List. Two practitioner interviews covered running models in the browser and analysing time series data.

## Main themes

### Autonomous drone racing

- Lynn describes AlphaPilot, run with the Drone Racing League, as an open innovation challenge with $2 million for top teams and a $250,000 bonus for beating a professional human pilot ([AlphaPilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/flying-high-with-ai-drone-racing-at-alphapilot/)).
- About 430 teams entered and a simulation-based qualifier picked nine. Every team flies the same drone because the organisers wanted winners to be “the best programmers, not because they had better motors or better batteries” ([AlphaPilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/flying-high-with-ai-drone-racing-at-alphapilot/)).
- Decisions run on board an NVIDIA Xavier, and teams have no physical involvement on race day. Lynn stresses modelling wind, glare and sensor error to close the sim-to-real gap ([AlphaPilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/flying-high-with-ai-drone-racing-at-alphapilot/)).

### Surveillance firms on the US blacklist

- Whitenack and Benson, who say they are not trade experts, look up four Chinese AI companies added to the US Commerce Department's Entity List, against the background of reports on Uyghur detention camps ([Blacklisted](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/blacklisted-facial-recognition-and-surveillance-companies/)).
- Benson reads a Bloomberg report that Hikvision is government-controlled and uses AI for facial recognition at scale. iFlytek, with a voice platform for over 400,000 developers, struck both hosts as less ominous ([Blacklisted](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/blacklisted-facial-recognition-and-surveillance-companies/)).
- Megvii is described as a facial recognition unicorn backed by Alibaba, Ant Financial and the Bank of China. Benson says China is “in a classification all by themselves” on surveillance ([Blacklisted](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/blacklisted-facial-recognition-and-surveillance-companies/)).
- Benson argues the judgement turns on intent and use case, contrasting Lockheed Martin's openly known government work with ties he calls unclear in China ([Blacklisted](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/blacklisted-facial-recognition-and-surveillance-companies/), [AlphaPilot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/flying-high-with-ai-drone-racing-at-alphapilot/)).

### Machine learning in the browser

- Victor Dibia names privacy, ease of distribution and interactivity as the benefits of TensorFlow.js. Privacy matters most to him, because the service never sees user data ([Browser](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/ai-in-the-browser/)).
- He describes online, offline and hybrid workflows, and says in-browser training suits small models. He cites Airbnb checking profile photos on the device ([Browser](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/ai-in-the-browser/)).
- Models are downloadable web assets, and his Handtrack.js is about 18 MB, so he sees model compression as the key obstacle ([Browser](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/ai-in-the-browser/)).

### Time series data and forecasting

- Anais Dotis-Georgiou of InfluxData lists industrial sensors, DevOps monitoring, greenhouses, solar panels and CERN as time series users. InfluxDB is append-only and built for very high write volumes ([Time series](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/it-s-time-to-talk-time-series/)).
- She says to check a method's assumptions first. Citing the M competitions, she reports pure statistical methods beat pure machine learning on univariate series ([Time series](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/it-s-time-to-talk-time-series/)).
- Neural nets earn their cost on multivariate problems. For many users, simple standard-deviation thresholds are enough for anomaly detection ([Time series](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/10/it-s-time-to-talk-time-series/)).
