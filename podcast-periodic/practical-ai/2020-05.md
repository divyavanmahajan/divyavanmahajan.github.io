## Overview

May on Practical AI had three episodes with little overlap. One guest episode covered an AI for Good project predicting which water points in Africa will fail. The hosts filled the other two on their own, answering listener questions and reading through NVIDIA's GTC 2020 hardware announcements.

## Main themes

### AI for Good and clean water

- Chandler McCann of DataRobot says roughly one in four people rely on sources such as hand pumps and wells, and about 25% of water points in the data are broken ([Clean water access](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ai-for-good-clean-water-access-in-africa/)).
- Brian Banks of the Global Water Challenge built the first standardised database of water points. McCann says about 1,600 free-text technology values reduced to 12-14 real categories, and that age was “the most predictive kind of across the board” ([Clean water access](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ai-for-good-clean-water-access-in-africa/)).
- McCann reports that Sierra Leone's Ministry of Water used the models in budgeting and passed a policy requiring data in water-service decisions ([Clean water access](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ai-for-good-clean-water-access-in-africa/)).
- He argues hackathon apps fail because small nonprofits cannot maintain code, and that models “are sort of living things that go stale over time” ([Clean water access](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ai-for-good-clean-water-access-in-africa/)).

### NVIDIA Ampere from data centre to edge

- Daniel Whitenack explains that GPUs suit neural networks because layers apply element-wise operations to matrices, as graphics does to pixels ([NVIDIA Ampere](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/exploring-nvidia-s-ampere-the-a100-gpu/)).
- The hosts cite NVIDIA's A100 claims: 20x more FLOPS for AI, a 3x to 6x speed-up over the V100 for BERT-large training and 7x for inference. Each A100 splits into seven isolated instances ([NVIDIA Ampere](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/exploring-nvidia-s-ampere-the-a100-gpu/)).
- Chris Benson notes the DGX A100 drops to eight GPUs but can run 56 GPU applications, and warns that organisations underestimate the data architecture needed to use one well ([NVIDIA Ampere](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/exploring-nvidia-s-ampere-the-a100-gpu/)).
- Whitenack argues a model shipped on an edge device is valuable IP that could be copied, so encryption at the edge makes sense ([NVIDIA Ampere](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/exploring-nvidia-s-ampere-the-a100-gpu/)).

### Listener questions: tools, products and jobs

- Benson sees research turning beyond deep learning since early 2020. Whitenack counters that deep neural networks still have plenty of unsolved problems ([Ask us anything](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ask-us-anything-about-ai/)).
- Benson warns against making AI a product requirement; start from the problem. Whitenack describes a trained model as a function with a large parameter set, saved to a file and served like other software ([Ask us anything](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ask-us-anything-about-ai/)).
- Both see AI as more augmentation than replacement. Benson thinks specialised or mundane jobs are most exposed ([Ask us anything](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ask-us-anything-about-ai/)).
