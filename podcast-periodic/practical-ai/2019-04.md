## Overview

April on Practical AI moved between the practical and the experimental. The hosts mapped out AI infrastructure, GIPHY showed a deep learning model built for a real internal need, and the AI for Good Foundation explained how to point researchers at unsolved social problems. Two guests covered the research edge: deep reinforcement learning at GPU scale and a neural interface worn on the arm.

## Main themes

### Building and running AI infrastructure

- Daniel Whitenack frames AI infrastructure as compute and storage at the base, with experimentation tools, automated pipelines and serving layered on top ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/the-landscape-of-ai-infrastructure/)).
- Chris Benson describes a crossover point: owning GPUs such as NVIDIA DGX systems pays off only when training runs around the clock. Whitenack says aspiring practitioners do not need a personal GPU workstation ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/the-landscape-of-ai-infrastructure/)).
- Whitenack says his biggest blockers have been data issues such as privacy and GDPR audit trails, not compute. He sketches a CI/CD flow where a GitHub push retrains a model and updates it in TensorFlow Serving or Seldon ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/the-landscape-of-ai-infrastructure/)).

### Applied deep learning at GIPHY

- Nick Hasty says the celebrity detector was GIPHY's first big deep learning project, built to annotate tens of millions of GIFs that human taggers could no longer handle ([GIPHY](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/giphy-s-celebrity-detector/)).
- Its classes come from GIPHY's top 50,000 searched celebrities, because commercial services often missed newer pop-culture names. A center loss approach improved face clustering a lot, and the team chose PyTorch for easier iteration ([GIPHY](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/giphy-s-celebrity-detector/)).
- GIFs bring their own problems: 256 colours per frame and 90 to 120 frames, so the team samples frames and aggregates them into one prediction. The open source release includes Docker code to show how GIPHY deploys ([GIPHY](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/giphy-s-celebrity-detector/)).

### AI for good: incentives, data and food security

- James Hodson says researchers rarely work on social problems because those problems lack funding and data. His foundation focuses on “solutions to problems that currently don't have any viable solution” ([AI for Good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/making-the-world-a-better-place-at-the-ai-for-good-foundation/)).
- Volunteers work in small task forces, each overseen by a faculty mentor, a structure added after early projects lost volunteers ([AI for Good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/making-the-world-a-better-place-at-the-ai-for-good-foundation/)).
- In the Food Security AI Challenge, Hodson says models raised annual seed-yield improvement from about 1% to 1.5%. He warns that without doubling productive capacity, “by 2050, we basically run out of food” ([AI for Good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/making-the-world-a-better-place-at-the-ai-for-good-foundation/)).

### Research frontiers: deep RL and neural input

- Adam Stooke of UC Berkeley used a DGX-1 donated by NVIDIA to spread training across eight GPUs, cutting Pong training from 10-15 hours to about four minutes ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Stooke covers StarCraft II, Dota 2 and robotic-hand work, and advises reimplementing algorithms until you reproduce published learning curves ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Adam Berenzweig says CTRL-labs reads motor-neuron signals through the skin of the forearm, so a neural interface needs no electrodes in the brain. The CTRL-kit band has 16 channels and decodes hand joint angles ([CTRL-labs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/ctrl-labs-lets-you-control-machines-with-your-mind/)).
- He sees neural input as the interface for AR, VR and wearables once displays move into glasses. The hardest ML problem is generalising across users and sessions ([CTRL-labs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/04/ctrl-labs-lets-you-control-machines-with-your-mind/)).
