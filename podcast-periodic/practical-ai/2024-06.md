## Overview

June on Practical AI was about where models run and how they find information. Two episodes covered running AI locally and at the edge, from AI PCs to compact models on hospital machines. The other two looked at retrieval: Perplexity's answer engine and the techniques that take RAG beyond its naive form.

## Main themes

### Local AI and the AI PC

- Daniel Whitenack says AI PCs build processing optimised for running models into the chip architecture. Chris Benson expects buying a laptop that is not an AI laptop to soon seem “a ridiculous thing” ([AI PC & local LLMs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/rise-of-the-ai-pc-local-llms/)).
- Whitenack says the answer to local versus cloud is both, much as databases run embedded, at the edge and in the cloud. Local suits private, air-gapped or poorly connected settings ([AI PC & local LLMs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/rise-of-the-ai-pc-local-llms/)).
- He sorts tools into local runtimes such as Ollama, LM Studio and llama.cpp, and hardware-specific optimisation libraries. A quantised 7B model runs on a CPU, only more slowly than on a GPU ([AI PC & local LLMs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/rise-of-the-ai-pc-local-llms/)).
- He warns AI PCs are expensive for now, which creates a gap for new and indie developers ([AI PC & local LLMs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/rise-of-the-ai-pc-local-llms/)).

### Edge models for sensitive data

- Ramin Mohammadi of Tausight says healthcare data cannot go to the cloud, “That means everything needs to happen at the edge” ([Edge models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/using-edge-models-to-find-sensitive-data/)).
- He cites 133 million breached healthcare records in 2023 and argues regex rules miss hidden health data, since a regex is only as good as its author ([Edge models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/using-edge-models-to-find-sensitive-data/)).
- Hospital machines often have at most 4 GB of RAM, so Tausight uses distillation, quantisation and pruning. Mohammadi expects edge hardware to arrive slowly because hospitals refresh machines about every five years ([Edge models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/using-edge-models-to-find-sensitive-data/)).
- Federated learning came up in both edge episodes, as a way to train where data cannot move ([Edge models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/using-edge-models-to-find-sensitive-data/), [AI PC & local LLMs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/rise-of-the-ai-pc-local-llms/)).

### Answer engines and model agnosticism

- Denis Yarats, CTO of Perplexity, describes retrieving documents and synthesising them into a cited answer, with accuracy and speed as the two priorities ([Perplexity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/the-perplexities-of-information-retrieval/)).
- Citations were a first-class feature from the start, answering the main complaint that ChatGPT users could not verify answers ([Perplexity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/the-perplexities-of-information-retrieval/)).
- Yarats says “There is not going to be one model that rules them all”. A single request touches at least 10 models, and simpler queries can be routed to smaller ones ([Perplexity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/the-perplexities-of-information-retrieval/)).

### Apple Intelligence, closed models and advanced RAG

- Benson likes that Apple presents AI as a device feature rather than the product, and that users are asked each time before a request goes to OpenAI's model ([Apple Intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/apple-intelligence-advanced-rag/)).
- Whitenack argues closed APIs are productised systems that make opinionated choices. Benson says partner data agreements push towards self-hosted open models ([Apple Intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/apple-intelligence-advanced-rag/)).
- Whitenack covers advanced RAG techniques: context enrichment, hierarchical search over summaries, hybrid search, re-ranking, HyDE and query transformation ([Apple Intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/06/apple-intelligence-advanced-rag/)).
