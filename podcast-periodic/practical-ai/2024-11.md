## Overview

November on Practical AI was about keeping AI grounded. BlackBerry's security leaders played down attacker AI and vendor hype, Daniel Whitenack made the case for testing AI workflows like ordinary software, and Probabl argued that classic machine learning still does most of the paid work. The month closed with a news roundup on US policy under a second Trump term, open models and AI agents that shop or attend meetings for you.

## Main themes

### AI in cybersecurity, minus the hype

- Ismael Valenzuela says “AI is just one more tool in the arsenal of any of these people.” He sees better phishing and more deepfake deception, but no autonomous hacking agents ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/ai-is-changing-the-cybersecurity-threat-landscape/)).
- Valenzuela cites BlackBerry's report of a 53% rise in unique malware, over 11,000 samples a quarter, as the reason signatures do not scale and predictive ML does ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/ai-is-changing-the-cybersecurity-threat-landscape/)).
- Gregory Richardson criticises vendors who promise SOCs that need no analysts. He sees the real value in classifying scattered alerts across endpoint, network and cloud into a few attack cases to cut alert fatigue ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/ai-is-changing-the-cybersecurity-threat-landscape/)).
- Richardson describes BlackBerry's managed XDR, which ingests whatever stack a customer already runs and pairs human analysts with predictive AI ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/ai-is-changing-the-cybersecurity-threat-landscape/)).

### Testing AI applications

- Whitenack's hot take is that low-code workflow tools such as Flowise, Gumloop and Dify repeat the old notebook-to-production problem. Workflows that must scale should become functions or services with tests ([Tested AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/creating-tested-reliable-ai-applications/)).
- He borrows three test types from behavioural testing: minimum functionality tests that must always pass, invariant tests and variant tests. He says people “don't realize how sensitive the models are to small changes in the input” ([Tested AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/creating-tested-reliable-ai-applications/)).
- With GPT-5 not arriving on schedule, Whitenack says current models are good enough as an orchestration layer over purpose-built tools. Agents, which choose their steps at run time, are harder to test and left for later ([Tested AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/creating-tested-reliable-ai-applications/)).

### Classical ML and sustaining open source

- Yann Lechelle says people tell him most production ML use cases, such as bank fraud detection, run on scikit-learn. He calls it frugal, CPU-friendly and easier to inspect than deep learning ([scikit-learn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/scikit-learn-data-science-you-own/)).
- He cites 1.5 billion cumulative downloads and about 80 million a month ([scikit-learn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/scikit-learn-data-science-you-own/)).
- Probabl's bylaws lock in an open-source mission, and the scikit-learn brand stays with INRIA. Lechelle argues purely VC-funded companies face return demands that push them to change licences; Probabl funds about 10 open-source staff through sponsorships, certification and a planned product ([scikit-learn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/scikit-learn-data-science-you-own/)).

### Policy, open models and agents

- Daniel and Chris expect US AI policy to change under Trump but are unsure how. Chris argues repealing the AI executive order or the CHIPS Act would be a mistake ([Clones](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/clones-commerce-campaigns/)).
- Daniel says open models, many from China such as Qwen 2.5, are now hard to tell apart from closed ones for most tasks. He separates self-hosting downloaded weights from sending data to a Chinese API ([Clones](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/clones-commerce-campaigns/)).
- On meeting clones, Daniel worries about more isolation in remote work, while Chris fears a return to projecting a perfect image. Daniel also covers Stripe's agent toolkit and Perplexity's shopping assistant ([Clones](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/11/clones-commerce-campaigns/)).
