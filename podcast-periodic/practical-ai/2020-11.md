## Overview

November on Practical AI covered three practical layers of machine learning work: the tools that record training runs, the hardware that runs them, and the statistics needed to draw sound conclusions. Lukas Biewald of Weights & Biases, host Daniel Whitenack and statistician Lucy D'Agostino McGowan each took one layer.

## Main themes

### ML tooling and experiment tracking

- Lukas Biewald argues that every step of the ML tooling pipeline is broken, from visualising data to tracking experiments, which leaves room for many new companies ([Killer developer tools](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/killer-developer-tools-for-machine-learning/)).
- He says the most striking problem is that deep learning runs take days and “it's really hard to go back and look at a run that you did in the past” ([Killer developer tools](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/killer-developer-tools-for-machine-learning/)).
- Weights & Biases captures metrics passively, saves the commit plus a diff of uncommitted code, and adds features such as Sweeps and Artifacts at customer request. Biewald advises against encoding hyperparameters in run names ([Killer developer tools](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/killer-developer-tools-for-machine-learning/)).
- John Deere's Blue River team attaches a report to each pull request for its weed-spraying tractors. Biewald also argues the industry must accept that 100% accuracy is not attainable and plan for errors ([Killer developer tools](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/killer-developer-tools-for-machine-learning/)).

### Owning GPUs versus renting them

- Daniel Whitenack moved to multi-day speech training runs and estimated a summer of cloud GPUs at $8,000-12,000, which put his team right at the break-even point with owning hardware ([Deep learning workstation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/building-a-deep-learning-workstation/)).
- He built a two-GPU machine (an RTX 2080 Ti and a Titan RTX) on a board with room for four, running headless with Ubuntu, Docker and CUDA ([Deep learning workstation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/building-a-deep-learning-workstation/)).
- His regrets: rigid power cables that stop the case closing, a one-terabyte drive that fills quickly with speech data, and a Threadripper he would swap for an Intel CPU to match his Intel deployment target ([Deep learning workstation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/building-a-deep-learning-workstation/)).

### Causal inference and statistical communication

- Lucy D'Agostino McGowan argues that most data questions are causal, so analysts working with observational data should state the assumptions under which an effect is causal ([Causal inference](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/a-casual-conversation-concerning-causal-inference/)).
- She warns against confusing adjusting for many variables with having everything that matters. Her R package tipr estimates how strong an unmeasured confounder would need to be to overturn a result ([Causal inference](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/a-casual-conversation-concerning-causal-inference/)).
- Her four checks for communication are that a result is mathematically correct, marketed correctly, disseminated correctly and interpreted correctly. Her lab finds that “people do tend to prefer the certain recommendation” ([Causal inference](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/a-casual-conversation-concerning-causal-inference/)).
