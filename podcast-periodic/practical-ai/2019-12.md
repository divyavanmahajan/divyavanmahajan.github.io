## Overview

December on Practical AI moved from model families to the systems around them. Guests covered GANs, practical NLP with spaCy, deep learning infrastructure at Determined AI and search ranking at Etsy. A common thread was that data, tooling and engineering often matter more than the model itself.

## Main themes

### Data and tooling beat bigger models

- Matthew Honnibal of Explosion argues that about $4,000 spent annotating four times as much data might add 10-12% accuracy, against about 1% from a longer hyperparameter search ([spaCy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/modern-nlp-with-spacy/)).
- Ines Montani says the real problem with labeling is the tooling. Honnibal says organisational boundaries around annotation teams are “where the innovation or iteration speed goes to die”, so Prodigy lets data scientists label data themselves ([spaCy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/modern-nlp-with-spacy/)).
- Honnibal walks through his embed, encode, attend, predict formula. spaCy uses a convolutional architecture so it runs well without a GPU, and Montani recommends a rule-based baseline before training anything ([spaCy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/modern-nlp-with-spacy/)).
- Explosion bootstrapped with about six months of consulting rather than venture funding, then funded itself by selling Prodigy as a one-off licence ([spaCy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/modern-nlp-with-spacy/)).

### Escaping ad hoc AI infrastructure

- Evan Sparks of Determined AI says TensorFlow and PyTorch are good at model definition but leave tuning, scheduling, reproducibility and deployment to “bash scripts and duct tape in many cases” ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/escaping-the-dark-ages-of-ai-infrastructure/)).
- Determined's scheduler is AI-aware: it knows jobs are iterative and checkpointable, so it can reschedule work. Sparks says the company differentiates by being neutral across hardware vendors ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/escaping-the-dark-ages-of-ai-infrastructure/)).
- He frames AutoML as power tools for experts, not a replacement, and advises beginners to start with data versioning and the simplest model that could work ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/escaping-the-dark-ages-of-ai-infrastructure/)).
- On reproducibility, Sparks contrasts software builds with ML, where a peer might have only a 2% chance of getting the same artifact ([AI infrastructure](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/escaping-the-dark-ages-of-ai-infrastructure/)).

### Search ranking for a one-of-a-kind marketplace

- Andrew Stanton says Etsy has over 60 million listings, many handmade, with no manufacturer SKUs; a search for jewelry returns 18 million results ([Etsy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/ai-for-search-at-etsy/)).
- Etsy ends its cascade of rankers with a neuroevolution layer that balances goals such as helping new sellers without hurting established ones. Stanton notes that when a gradient can be computed, SGD is almost always better ([Etsy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/ai-for-search-at-etsy/)).
- Fitness functions get gamed: optimising precision at 10, the model often put the purchased item at exactly position 10. Stanton compares writing them to being a lawyer ([Etsy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/ai-for-search-at-etsy/)).
- Etsy's two Rust systems serve hundreds of billions of predictions a day, and moving neuroevolution from Python to Rust gave roughly a 100x speedup ([Etsy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/ai-for-search-at-etsy/)).

### GANs move from research to specialists

- Jakub Langr explains GANs as a generator, like an amateur painter, competing against a discriminator, like an art critic. Vladimir Bok says training is better described as a game than an optimisation ([GANs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/making-gans-practical/)).
- Bok calls GANs the state of the art for image and video generation, but Langr says unstable training produces hundreds of papers and advises starting from a proven architecture ([GANs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/making-gans-practical/)).
- Langr expects growth in audio and in GANs as components of larger pipelines, and notes that many deepfake methods are not GAN-based ([GANs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/12/making-gans-practical/)).
