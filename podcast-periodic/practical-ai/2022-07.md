## Overview

July on Practical AI had three episodes that each took a different angle on AI moving out of the lab. Coqui's Josh Meyer talked about voice cloning that anyone can use from a browser. The hosts then pushed back on claims of AI sentience after DALL-E 2, and closed the month on AI as a source of national and corporate power.

## Main themes

### Voice cloning for everyone, built in the open

- Josh Meyer says the main change in voice cloning is access: the technique once needed command-line skills, and Coqui's web app now works from about five seconds of audio ([Coqui](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/cloning-voices-with-coqui/)).
- He argues flat synthetic speech is now hard to tell from a human, so the next problem is emotion. He is weighing interfaces from drop-downs to free-text directions, asking “do we want an emotional color wheel of sorts?” ([Coqui](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/cloning-voices-with-coqui/)).
- Coqui's speech projects grew out of about five years of work with Mozilla. Meyer credits open source with attracting strong contributors and points to BibleTTS with the Masakhane community, which adds six sub-Saharan African languages ([Coqui](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/cloning-voices-with-coqui/)).
- On misuse, Meyer splits cases into accidental and deliberate. Coqui runs an open GitHub discussion on safeguards and now requires sign-in with a real email address ([Coqui](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/cloning-voices-with-coqui/)).

### Coherence is not sentience

- Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson reject a Google engineer's claim that a dialogue model is sentient. Benson says people “infer a bigger jump than it actually is” when something impressive happens ([DALL-E](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/dall-e-is-one-giant-leap-for-raccoons/)).
- Whitenack reads from the stochastic parrots paper by Timnit Gebru and co-authors: generated text is not grounded in communicative intent, and readers project meaning onto it ([DALL-E](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/dall-e-is-one-giant-leap-for-raccoons/)).
- He explains DALL-E 2 as a combination of existing parts: transformers, CLIP text-image embeddings and diffusion models. Benson calls it a normal path of innovation, not a surprise ([DALL-E](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/dall-e-is-one-giant-leap-for-raccoons/)).
- Whitenack expects more work mixing modalities, such as translating between sign languages and searching sign language video by signing ([DALL-E](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/dall-e-is-one-giant-leap-for-raccoons/)).

### AI as a power resource

- Benson calls AI “an incredibly valuable national resource or corporate resource” that shifts power through prestige, economics, academia and military capability ([Geopolitics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/the-geopolitics-of-artificial-intelligence/)).
- He finds most national and corporate AI strategies look alike, and holds up the show's AI in Africa series as research aimed at real local needs ([Geopolitics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/the-geopolitics-of-artificial-intelligence/)).
- Whitenack points to IBM, Dell, Meta and Apple leaving Russia, and to Starlink, as signs that technology holders can rival nation states. Both hosts see regulation lagging deployment, with GDPR a limited first step ([Geopolitics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/the-geopolitics-of-artificial-intelligence/)).

### Autonomy and coding assistants

- Benson expects autonomy to become pervasive. He notes the military has led autonomy in aviation because civilians remain wary of machines flying airliners ([Geopolitics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/the-geopolitics-of-artificial-intelligence/)).
- Whitenack has moved from Vim to VS Code for GitHub Copilot but thinks programming will not be automated; Benson is less sure and notes the criticism of training on open-source code ([Geopolitics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/07/the-geopolitics-of-artificial-intelligence/)).
