## Overview

December on Practical AI had two episodes, both recorded around events. One came from a Purdue-led student case competition on multilingual image captioning, the other from EMNLP 2022 with Unbabel's researchers on how to judge machine translation.

## Main themes

### Competitions and cloud resources for students

- Matthew Lanham says 172 teams from 36 universities registered for a competition run by Purdue, Microsoft, INFORMS and SIL International. Teams captioned images in Thai, Hausa and Kyrgyz from SIL's Bloom captioning data ([AI competitions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/ai-competitions-cloud-resources/)).
- Mark Tabladillo says competitions teach what classes don't: “putting into practice and working as a team.” Judging followed the seven domains of the INFORMS CAP framework, from problem framing to deployment ([AI competitions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/ai-competitions-cloud-resources/)).
- Daniel Whitenack recalls a student group that bought more laptop RAM before realising it needed the cloud. Tabladillo separates Azure Machine Learning Studio from pre-trained cognitive services exposed as REST APIs ([AI competitions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/ai-competitions-cloud-resources/)).
- The graduate winners from Georgia Tech used a multilingual CLIP model to match some images to existing captions. The undergraduate winners from Butler augmented a small Hausa dataset ([AI competitions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/ai-competitions-cloud-resources/)).

### Judging machine translation

- José Souza says Unbabel pioneered using quality estimation to decide whether a machine translation needs post-editing by a professional ([Unbabel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/sota-machine-translation-at-unbabel/)).
- Ricardo Rei explains that BLEU weights all words equally, so a missed punctuation mark costs as much as a wrong named entity. COMET instead uses XLM-RoBERTa embeddings fine-tuned on human judgments ([Unbabel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/sota-machine-translation-at-unbabel/)).
- Rei notes the blind spots: the embeddings for 50 and 500 are very similar, so a critical number error can slip through. Souza adds that model-based metrics have “orders of magnitude less label data for this fine-tuning process.” ([Unbabel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/sota-machine-translation-at-unbabel/)).
- Bigger models score better but are hard to serve. Unbabel presented a smaller COMET called Cometinho and is exploring kNN-MT and quality-aware decoding ([Unbabel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/12/sota-machine-translation-at-unbabel/)).
