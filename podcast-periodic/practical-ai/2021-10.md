## Overview

October on Practical AI mixed fundamentals with field work. One episode covered the maths behind neural networks and another explained federated learning. The show also opened its Spotlight on AI in Africa series with Makerere AI Lab and heard how a startup builds personal NLP models on a user's own data.

## Main themes

### Maths as the language of ML

- Tivadar Danka says “Mathematics is the language in which algorithms are written.” He argues the maths behind neural networks is not that complicated; mathematicians make it so ([Mathematics of ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/the-mathematics-of-machine-learning/)).
- How much you need depends on role: MLOps engineers need little, data scientists a lot of probability and statistics. Beginners should pick a problem, start building and study when they get stuck ([Mathematics of ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/the-mathematics-of-machine-learning/)).
- His early-access book follows two rules: every topic has an ML motivation, and every concept is as visual as possible. It is aimed at computer scientists, not mathematicians ([Mathematics of ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/the-mathematics-of-machine-learning/)).

### Training where the data lives

- Daniel Whitenack defines federated learning as training a centralised model on decentralised data. Clients train locally and send weights back for federated averaging ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/federated-learning/)).
- Chris Benson says the idea dates to 2016-2017 but only now has workable tooling. Legal limits on cross-border data and the logistics of moving large datasets matter more than privacy ideals ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/federated-learning/)).
- Updates still need encryption and secure aggregation with differential privacy. Whitenack walks through PyGrid and mentions TensorFlow Federated, Open Federated Learning and Flower ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/federated-learning/)).

### Local problems, local data

- Joyce Nabende says the Open for Good Alliance formed in 2020 because there was a “lack of localized training data that was of sufficient quality.” ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/ai-in-africa-makerere-ai-lab/)).
- Mutembesa describes the lab's ethos: find a good local problem, match it to a computational toolkit, and attach a local beneficiary. Work spans crop disease, credit scoring for unbanked smallholder farmers, health, infrastructure and languages ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/ai-in-africa-makerere-ai-lab/)).
- The lab collects data itself, from community-deployed air quality devices to radio monitoring of crop crises, and runs a call centre to follow up with farmers ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/ai-in-africa-makerere-ai-lab/)).

### Personal models instead of a black box

- Nicholas Mohnacky says bundleIQ aims to engineer eureka moments by vectorising a user's notes, email and documents and nudging them towards related material as they work ([Eureka moments](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/eureka-moments-with-natural-language-processing/)).
- bundleIQ used GPT-3 in beta mainly as a benchmark but is moving off it because it is a black box. Mohnacky and Benson agree a narrow model tied to one user's data does not need GPT-3's breadth ([Eureka moments](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/eureka-moments-with-natural-language-processing/)).
