## Overview

June on Practical AI ranged widely, with no item recurring across episodes. The hosts discussed DeepMind's Gato and the AI-recreated voice of Val Kilmer, NTT DATA's Theresa Kushner covered emotion AI and digital humans, and two former Instacart engineers argued for running machine learning inside Postgres. Immunai closed the month with a data-first view of AI in biotech.

## Main themes

### Generalist models and generative media

- Daniel Whitenack describes Gato as “a single model that can operate in a multimodal, multitask, multi-embodiment generalization policy”, in the same spirit as prompt engineering ([Gato and Iceman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/generalist-models-iceman-s-voice/)).
- Whitenack says Gato is general only across tasks AI researchers have defined, a small part of the world's complexity, so the AGI goal line keeps moving ([Gato and Iceman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/generalist-models-iceman-s-voice/)).
- Kilmer's voice in Top Gun: Maverick was rebuilt from old film audio. Chris Benson expects AI-produced media to become pervasive, and the hosts ask who gets paid and whether voices become estate assets ([Gato and Iceman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/generalist-models-iceman-s-voice/)).
- Benson argues practical value sits at the intersection of disciplines, which academia does not reward ([Gato and Iceman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/generalist-models-iceman-s-voice/)).

### Emotion AI and digital humans

- Theresa Kushner calls emotion AI early and says the right modality depends on the problem: a contact centre may get more from voice than from visuals ([Digital humans](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/digital-humans-detecting-emotions/)).
- NTT greets lobby visitors in Japan with a kiosk avatar, first built for Kia. Kushner admits crowds cannot yet be handled and that some avatar work “can be fairly creepy” ([Digital humans](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/digital-humans-detecting-emotions/)).
- She says the hard part is fast access to good customer data, and that consent checkboxes must be understandable. Cultural differences such as eye contact complicate detection ([Digital humans](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/digital-humans-detecting-emotions/)).

### Machine learning inside the database

- Montana Low and Lev Kokotov argue most business ML is predicting one column from relational data, so it can run in Postgres without ETL. Their PostgresML extension trains and serves models through SQL functions ([In your database](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/machine-learning-in-your-database/)).
- Low recounts how Instacart went through a year of planned growth in the first week of the pandemic and moved live traffic to a Postgres prototype that ran at about 4% CPU ([In your database](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/machine-learning-in-your-database/)).
- Low says moving thousands of rows to the application costs more than inference itself. Kokotov built PgCat for sharding and failover, and the two have started a company around both tools ([In your database](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/machine-learning-in-your-database/)).

### Data-led AI in biotech

- Drausin Wulsin of Immunai says progress in biological AI has come more from new ways of measuring data than from new algorithms, and that biology has many small datasets rather than one web-scale source ([Reprogramming immunity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/ai-s-role-in-reprogramming-immunity/)).
- He spent his first six months without training a model, says simple methods cover most classification needs, and warns that “There's a lot of like AI marketing that happens in the biotech space” ([Reprogramming immunity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/06/ai-s-role-in-reprogramming-immunity/)).
