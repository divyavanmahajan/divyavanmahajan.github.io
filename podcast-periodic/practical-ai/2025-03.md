## Overview

March on Practical AI covered three layers of putting AI to work: employees building their own small assistants, companies picking efficient open models, and developers handing more of the software lifecycle to coding agents. Across all three, guests argued that clear instructions and good fit with existing work matter more than raw model size.

## Main themes

### Bottom-up adoption and no-code assistants

- Scott Meyer of Chipp.ai argues that AI adoption is not driven from the top. He says “it's not the CIO or CTO saying, I bought this thing” and that individuals find their own tasks ([Workspace of AI agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/build-a-workspace-of-ai-agents/)).
- He recommends a monthly lunch and learn so staff share practices, and clear privacy rules so fear does not block use. He cites admin users saving about sixty minutes a day ([Workspace of AI agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/build-a-workspace-of-ai-agents/)).
- Meyer says Chipp's cost per API call fell 90% in eighteen months, so value sits in the experience and customer relationship rather than the model ([Workspace of AI agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/build-a-workspace-of-ai-agents/)).

### Small, efficient open models

- Kate Soule says IBM centralised Granite development in IBM Research and released it under Apache 2.0 so customers keep their IP ([IBM Granite](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/optimizing-for-efficiency-with-ibm-s-granite/)).
- She believes about 80% of use cases can run on models of 8 billion parameters or fewer, and says Granite 2B beats Llama 2 70B on several benchmarks ([IBM Granite](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/optimizing-for-efficiency-with-ibm-s-granite/)).
- Granite includes 1B and 3B mixture-of-experts models for CPUs and edge devices, switchable reasoning in Granite 3.2, and Granite Guardian guardrails. About 60,000 IBM consultants use Granite-based assistants ([IBM Granite](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/optimizing-for-efficiency-with-ibm-s-granite/)).

### Coding assistants become agents

- Kyle Daigle, COO of GitHub, describes Copilot moving from completions to model choice, chat and agent mode in VS Code Insiders, with issues assigned to Copilot as to a teammate ([GitHub's COO](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/ai-assisted-coding-with-github-s-coo/)).
- He says assistants are starting to blur what a developer is, and expects much more AI outside the editor, in review, CI and operations ([GitHub's COO](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/ai-assisted-coding-with-github-s-coo/)).
- Daigle hopes MCP will let one tool combine context from error trackers, databases and cloud services. Meyer also mentioned MCP ([GitHub's COO](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/ai-assisted-coding-with-github-s-coo/), [Workspace of AI agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/build-a-workspace-of-ai-agents/)).

### Prompting as clear problem description

- Daigle says “it used to be called, like, prompt engineering, and I honestly think it's just describing a problem” ([GitHub's COO](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/ai-assisted-coding-with-github-s-coo/)).
- Meyer offers his RIPE framework (role, instruction, parameters, example) and suggests letting the AI ask questions first ([Workspace of AI agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/build-a-workspace-of-ai-agents/)).
- Soule makes a similar point for edge use: break work into small tasks instead of writing huge prompts ([IBM Granite](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/03/optimizing-for-efficiency-with-ibm-s-granite/)).
