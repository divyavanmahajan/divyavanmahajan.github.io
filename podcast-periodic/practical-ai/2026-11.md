## Overview

November on Practical AI kept returning to what AI means for human work, from a bank moving people up the stack to Chris Benson asking what happens when humans are no longer the most productive option. Other episodes covered how organisations decide what AI should do before they build it, and how federated learning could let enterprises improve agents on private data.

## Main themes

### AI and the value of human work

- Gautham Pallapa of Scotiabank sees coding, documentation and test writing as repeatable, low-value work that agents can take over. He says “you don't actually need people to be coding anymore” ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- Pallapa tells his teams the aim is not to replace people but to remove manual friction and shift them to higher-value work. He still keeps humans in the loop because trust in agents is not high enough ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- Benson argues that human-AI teaming is only a short-term answer: as automation improves, the human role of vision and orchestration shrinks. He raises questions about wages, the right to work and who buys products ([Post-Human World](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/humans-in-a-post-human-world/)).
- Benson also says schools still test for skills the future won't need, and he treats consciousness as ordinary computation, so silicon-based consciousness would not surprise him ([Post-Human World](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/humans-in-a-post-human-world/)).

### Boundaries and value before building

- Don Barger of the International Mission Board says eight people wrote an AI charter almost four years ago, before any code. It rules out AI posing as a human, sole AI decisions about people and handing over soul care ([Midwest AI Summit Part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Barger says “these clear limits are actually freeing me up to be able to do way more than I could do before” ([Midwest AI Summit Part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Mark Lay, CIO at Noblis, weighs each AI task by reward against the risk and consequence of error, and warns of a new technical debt from department-built apps ([Midwest AI Summit Part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Pallapa starts from a definition of value and success, then asks how AI could help. His book Invaluable formalises this as an eight-part value transform ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).

### Private data and agent trajectories

- Lorenzo Sani of Flower Labs explains that federated learning moves training to where data is collected, so raw data never leaves the device or silo ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).
- Sani says agent trajectories hold user prompts, tool calls, MCP servers and RAG results, which makes them private but also the data needed to fix agents' mistakes ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).
- He predicts “a lot of these enterprises looking into these trajectories and try to improve models by themselves” rather than relying on closed providers, and prefers open-weight models for privacy-heavy work ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).

### Small agents aimed at real bottlenecks

- Darren Taillie's two-person analytics team at AeroCore Technologies put a text-to-SQL agent in Microsoft Teams. A daily vision check on work-order photos led to the monthly reconciliation meeting being cancelled ([Midwest AI Summit Part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- At the bank, Pallapa starts with lower-risk uses such as customer service agents, chat assistants, compliance and adviser support. He suggests agent teams that mirror balanced human teams under an orchestrator ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- Sani and his flatmates self-host vision-language models and agents at home, including a vibe-coded costume contest judged by agents ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).
