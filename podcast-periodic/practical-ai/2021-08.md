## Overview

August on Practical AI covered four unrelated corners of applied ML: real-time audio models for musicians, a live-streamed data science competition, an NLP triage pipeline at a Kenyan nonprofit, and a discussion of AI vocabulary. A common thread was that the model is rarely the hardest part; data, workflow and framing matter as much.

## Main themes

### AI as a new musical instrument

- Yotam Mann of Never Before Heard Sounds works on raw audio rather than MIDI and wants models that transform sound in real time, not background tracks for ads ([Never before heard sounds](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/ai-is-creating-never-before-heard-sounds/)).
- Holly+ lets anyone turn an audio file into Holly Herndon's voice, with ownership of creations managed through a DAO ([Never before heard sounds](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/ai-is-creating-never-before-heard-sounds/)).
- Mann trains only on open or fully licensed data and hopes consumers will ask “Who's involved? What are their names? Were they paid for it?” ([Never before heard sounds](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/ai-is-creating-never-before-heard-sounds/)).
- He compares the moment to the electric guitar enabling Jimi Hendrix and expects the AI-as-gimmick phase to fade ([Never before heard sounds](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/ai-is-creating-never-before-heard-sounds/)).

### Competitive data science as community

- Meg Risdal and Nick Wan describe SLICED: contestants get an unseen dataset and two hours, scored on Kaggle performance, visualisation and hidden golden features ([SLICED](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/sliced-will-you-make-the-data-science-cut/)).
- Wan says datasets are kept to tens of thousands of rows so heavy compute gives no edge, and the show replaced the networking lost to virtual conferences ([SLICED](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/sliced-will-you-make-the-data-science-cut/)).
- Risdal calls the risk of embarrassment while live coding “a very strong forcing function to learn.” ([SLICED](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/sliced-will-you-make-the-data-science-cut/)).

### NLP triage where the glue matters most

- Jacaranda Health runs a free SMS service for pregnant mothers across about 700 partner public facilities, now enrolling close to 100,000 a month. Sathy Rajasekharan says a first-in, first-out queue would bury the few urgent messages ([Kenya](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/nlp-to-help-pregnant-mothers-in-kenya/)).
- Jay Patel picked a cloud NLP service, labelled about 13,000 translated questions into 33 intents, and connected SMS, translation, classification and a help desk ticketing tool through APIs ([Kenya](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/nlp-to-help-pregnant-mothers-in-kenya/)).
- Help desk agents correct wrong intents to feed retraining. Consent and explaining model use to government partners remain open problems ([Kenya](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/nlp-to-help-pregnant-mothers-in-kenya/)).

### The words we use for AI

- Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson use AI Now's A New AI Lexicon series to discuss how vocabulary shapes perception. Whitenack says jargon, not the ideas, made data science feel scary when he started ([AI lexicon](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/exploring-a-new-ai-lexicon/)).
- Whitenack tells trainees a model is “just a parameterized function”, while Benson says his own use of the term AI has become aspirational ([AI lexicon](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/exploring-a-new-ai-lexicon/)).
- The hosts question loaded terms such as black box, smart and cognitive, and warn about function creep when a model built for one purpose is reused for another ([AI lexicon](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/08/exploring-a-new-ai-lexicon/)).
