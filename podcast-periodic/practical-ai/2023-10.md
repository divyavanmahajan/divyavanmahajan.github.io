## Overview

October on Practical AI was mostly about running open models yourself: how to pick one, test it, optimise it and serve it, and what managed hosting adds. A panel from All Things Open discussed what AI means for developers' jobs, and Nathaniel Simard made the case for deep learning in Rust.

## Main themes

### From exploration to deployment

- Daniel Whitenack counts around 345,000 models on Hugging Face and advises judging them like GitHub projects, by downloads and likes, after trying hosted demos ([Exploration to deployment](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/generative-models-exploration-to-deployment/)).
- He tests the model card's example in a notebook first. Beyond roughly 7 to 15 billion parameters, models usually need more than a consumer GPU ([Exploration to deployment](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/generative-models-exploration-to-deployment/)).
- Only if hardware is limited does he reach for llama.cpp, GPTQ, GGML, bitsandbytes or QLoRA ([Exploration to deployment](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/generative-models-exploration-to-deployment/)).
- He recommends a REST or gRPC model server separate from application code, using tools such as Baseten's Truss, TGI or vLLM, and notes “each vendor has its own approach” ([Exploration to deployment](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/generative-models-exploration-to-deployment/)).

### Self-hosting and scaling inference

- Tuhin Srivastava says swapping a closed API for Llama 2 or Mistral is much harder than it looks: production needs containerisation, autoscaling, security, versioning and observability ([Self-hosting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/self-hosting-scaling-models/)).
- Teams self-host for cost, data privacy and fine-tuning control, and the users are increasingly engineers rather than data scientists ([Self-hosting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/self-hosting-scaling-models/)).
- Srivastava says Baseten replicated a startup's two-year, four-person serving effort in two days. He adds that “version management is non-existent for the moment” ([Self-hosting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/self-hosting-scaling-models/)).
- Whitenack hosts Prediction Guard models on Baseten, linking the month's two deployment episodes ([Self-hosting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/self-hosting-scaling-models/), [Exploration to deployment](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/generative-models-exploration-to-deployment/)).

### AI and the developer's job

- Jerod Santo says AI code generation works at the line, function and maybe module level. Emily Freeman expects integration to stay with humans for a long time ([Developers panel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/ai-s-impact-on-developers/)).
- On fears of cuts after claimed 40% efficiency gains, Freeman calls them possible but says roles change, as sysadmin roles did. James Q Quick expects companies that cut too deep to get burned when things break ([Developers panel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/ai-s-impact-on-developers/)).
- Quick says AI is in a verification phase where every output must be checked. Freeman invokes the tragedy of the commons and calls for responsible AI standards ([Developers panel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/ai-s-impact-on-developers/)).

### Deep learning beyond Python

- Chris Benson argues AI must become available in languages other than Python to mature ([Burn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/deep-learning-in-rust-with-burn/)).
- Simard says Rust is wrongly labelled low-level and offers fine control over memory and concurrency. Burn is generic over hardware backends, from Torch to WebGPU ([Burn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/deep-learning-in-rust-with-burn/)).
- Most users come to deploy models reliably, including to WebAssembly or devices without an operating system. Community ports include Llama, Stable Diffusion and Whisper ([Burn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/10/deep-learning-in-rust-with-burn/)).
