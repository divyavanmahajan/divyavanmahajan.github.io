## Overview

October on Practical AI was about the foundations under AI applications: good data, simple analytics, durable pipelines and measurable trust. Guests from Hugging Face, MotherDuck, Prefect and NIST each argued for doing the basics well before reaching for anything elaborate. An enterprise builder added the human side, with personas and hands-on training for non-technical staff.

## Main themes

### Data quality comes from collaboration

- Ben Burtenshaw and David Berenstein of Hugging Face's Argilla team say teams jump to fine-tuning or elaborate RAG without a benchmark. They suggest a short list of expected questions as a baseline ([Synthetic datasets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/towards-high-quality-maybe-synthetic-datasets/)).
- Argilla pairs a Python SDK for engineers with a UI for domain experts. Berenstein says “the collaboration is even more important nowadays” now that models are prompted in natural language ([Synthetic datasets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/towards-high-quality-maybe-synthetic-datasets/)).
- Burtenshaw notes LLMs are better at spotting hallucinations than avoiding them, so AI feedback can filter generated data before human review. Re-judging Intel's Orca DPO pairs with Distilabel halved the dataset and produced a better model ([Synthetic datasets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/towards-high-quality-maybe-synthetic-datasets/)).

### Big data is dead, analytics is alive

- Till Döhmen and Adithya Krishnan of MotherDuck argue that many companies do not have big data, and single cloud machines now reach about 24 TB of memory ([Big data is dead](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/big-data-is-dead-analytics-is-alive/)).
- Döhmen calls DuckDB “the SQLite for analytics”; running in-process removes the client-server bottleneck, and Krishnan says many users treat it as a stand-in for data frames ([Big data is dead](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/big-data-is-dead-analytics-is-alive/)).
- Embeddings, full-text and hybrid search arrive through extensions, which Daniel Whitenack sees as a strong base for text-to-SQL and RAG ([Big data is dead](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/big-data-is-dead-analytics-is-alive/)).

### Durable workflows before agents

- Adam Azzam of Prefect says LLM pipelines hit classic failures (flaky APIs, schema drift, broken JSON) plus a new dynamism that ETL tooling never handled ([Workflow orchestration](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/practical-workflow-orchestration/)).
- He recommends deterministic workflows first and agents only where the decision space is unknown at the start. He sees missing durability and resiliency as the biggest reasons agents fail ([Workflow orchestration](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/practical-workflow-orchestration/)).
- ControlFlow, built on Prefect 3, puts an orchestration library behind every action an LLM takes ([Workflow orchestration](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/practical-workflow-orchestration/)).

### Personas and AI literacy

- Mike Lewis of Synthony argues enterprise disappointment usually comes from people who have only tried a chatbot. His first app, a focus-group persona called Andy, produced a publisher team's best idea in seven years ([Possible, doable & scalable](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/understanding-what-s-possible-doable-scalable/)).
- His bootcamp teaches only basic knowledge of tokens and context windows, and he builds proofs of concept on synthetic data within hours to get past privacy concerns ([Possible, doable & scalable](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/understanding-what-s-possible-doable-scalable/)).

### Measuring trust

- Elham Tabassi of NIST says AI systems are not inherently risky; context decides, as face recognition on a phone, at an airport or in law enforcement shows ([Trustworthy AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/the-path-towards-trustworthy-ai/)).
- She recommends starting the AI Risk Management Framework with govern and map, choosing a few subcategories and reassessing regularly because of data and model drift ([Trustworthy AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/the-path-towards-trustworthy-ai/)).
- Tabassi says benchmarks saturate too quickly and calls for better evaluation science, then interoperable global standards ([Trustworthy AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/10/the-path-towards-trustworthy-ai/)).
