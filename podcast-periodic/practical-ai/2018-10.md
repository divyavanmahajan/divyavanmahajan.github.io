## Overview

October brought two heavyweight guests, OpenAI's Wojciech Zaremba and NVIDIA's Bill Dally, who covered robots, safety and the hardware behind deep learning. HireVue's Lindsey Zuloaga took on bias and fairness in hiring. Two news episodes tracked the converging deep learning frameworks and AI's spread into art and assistants.

## Main themes

### Robots, reinforcement learning and safety

- Zaremba says AGI needs compute, new algorithms and data or environments, and argues academic labs mostly focus on algorithms ([OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/openai-reinforcement-learning-robots-safety/)).
- OpenAI trained a 24-degree-of-freedom robot hand in many randomised simulations so it could adapt to the real hand. Solving the task took about three years of virtual experience, and transfer another 97 ([OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/openai-reinforcement-learning-robots-safety/)).
- Zaremba warns that systems optimised for one objective have “a little bit like a profile of psychopath”, and cites misspecification and adversarial attacks such as Microsoft's Tay ([OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/openai-reinforcement-learning-robots-safety/)).
- Dally sees autonomous vehicles as an easier case of robotics and describes NVIDIA's new Seattle robotics lab led by Dieter Fox ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/artificial-intelligence-at-nvidia/)).

### GPUs and the hardware behind deep learning

- Dally argues the algorithms existed since the 1980s and GPUs made them practical. His Stanford language Brook, taken to NVIDIA by Ian Buck, became CUDA ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/artificial-intelligence-at-nvidia/)).
- He estimates CPUs are about 1% efficient, while “GPUs actually spend more than half of their energy doing the payload arithmetic”. NVIDIA adds tensor cores to a programmable GPU rather than building a fixed ASIC like the TPU ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/artificial-intelligence-at-nvidia/)).
- Dally puts training in the cloud and says vehicles need edge inference for latency and connectivity. NVIDIA open-sourced its NVDLA accelerator and uses it in the Xavier chip ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/artificial-intelligence-at-nvidia/)).

### Frameworks converge, practice matures

- The hosts see PyTorch 1.0 pushing into production while TensorFlow 2.0 makes eager execution the default, which Benson links to competition from PyTorch ([PyTorch vs TensorFlow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/pytorch-1-0-vs-tensorflow-2-0/)).
- Both argue companies rarely design new architectures; a custom model usually means training a published one on company data. Whitenack would use Google's Responsible AI Practices as a team checklist ([PyTorch vs TensorFlow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/pytorch-1-0-vs-tensorflow-2-0/)).
- Benson calls China's planned social credit system alarming and argues Western governments should invest more in AI ([PyTorch vs TensorFlow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/pytorch-1-0-vs-tensorflow-2-0/)).

### Bias and fairness in hiring

- Zuloaga says traditional hiring filters on keywords, school or GPA, and that HireVue trains models on objective performance metrics such as sales numbers instead ([Bias in hiring](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/fighting-bias-in-ai-and-in-hiring/)).
- She warns algorithms are good at stereotyping, but unlike humans they can be inspected. HireVue removes features that leak demographics and monitors against the EEOC four-fifths rule ([Bias in hiring](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/fighting-bias-in-ai-and-in-hiring/)).
- Fairness has competing definitions that cannot all be met, so teams should choose, measure and be open about them. IBM AI Fairness 360 came up here and in the month's last news episode ([Bias in hiring](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/fighting-bias-in-ai-and-in-hiring/), [Portraits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/ais-that-look-human-and-create-portraits-of-humans/)).

### Generative art and human-looking AI

- A GAN-made portrait by the collective Obvious headed to auction at Christie's, and Benson expects AI-created art to become very common ([Portraits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/ais-that-look-human-and-create-portraits-of-humans/)).
- Benson covers Magic Leap's human-looking assistant Mica. Whitenack notes that almost-human interfaces can seem creepier than clearly artificial ones ([Portraits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/ais-that-look-human-and-create-portraits-of-humans/)).
