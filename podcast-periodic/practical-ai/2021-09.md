## Overview

September on Practical AI was about the work around the model: deploying it, running it fast, feeding it good labels and deciding where humans fit. Guests from Anaconda, Deci, Label Studio and Outerbounds each covered one part of that pipeline, and the hosts closed the month on balancing human and artificial intelligence.

## Main themes

### Getting models into production

- Peter Wang reads Anaconda's 2021 State of Data Science survey as data work spreading beyond people titled data scientist. Top roadblocks to production were IT security standards, recoding Python and R models, and managing dependencies ([Anaconda + Pyston](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/anaconda-pyston-and-more/)).
- Wang says ops roles rank recoding first and are the least likely to stay with their employer. He presents Anaconda's hiring of the Pyston team, and its role as a governed vendor of signed binaries, as answers ([Anaconda + Pyston](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/anaconda-pyston-and-more/)).
- Ville Tuulos says that with today's libraries and cloud “everything is possible, but I mean, nothing is really easy enough.” Metaflow, started at Netflix in 2017, takes compute, data access and orchestration off data scientists' hands ([Metaflow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/from-notebooks-to-netflix-scale-with-metaflow/)).
- Tuulos calls organisational factors the biggest blocker. Netflix tested ideas in production and accepted that most experiments fail by design ([Metaflow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/from-notebooks-to-netflix-scale-with-metaflow/)).

### Designing for inference speed

- Yonatan Geifman argues latency, cost and size should shape model choice from the start, because inference scales with production data while training happens once ([AutoNAS](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/stellar-inference-speed-via-autonas/)).
- He describes an inference stack of hardware, graph compilers, pruning and quantization, and model design. GPUs favour fewer, wider layers; CPUs favour narrower ones ([AutoNAS](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/stellar-inference-speed-via-autonas/)).
- Deci's AutoNAC searches hundreds of thousands of architectures on the target device. In an MLPerf collaboration with Intel it made ResNet-50 about 3x faster at the same accuracy ([AutoNAS](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/stellar-inference-speed-via-autonas/)).

### Labeling becomes strategic

- Michael Malyuk says ML infrastructure is becoming a commodity, so advantage moves to labeling, which turns raw data into a liquid asset ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/trends-in-data-labeling/)).
- He says model accuracy tracks label quality and expects “quality will dominate over the quantity.” Three labelers can agree and still be wrong, so verification is needed ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/trends-in-data-labeling/)).
- Label Studio is configured with a short markup language and supports models in the loop for iterative retraining ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/trends-in-data-labeling/)).

### Humans and AI as a team

- Using a polarity map on machine translation of COVID information, Chris Benson argues strategy must start from a validated need, not a favoured approach ([Balancing human intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/balancing-human-intelligence-with-ai/)).
- Daniel Whitenack says human plus model often beats either alone, and suggests asking first who the human in the loop is. Benson adds that people trust other humans long before a technology ([Balancing human intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/balancing-human-intelligence-with-ai/)).
- Malyuk makes a related point: pre-trained models will auto-label most data, leaving humans the edge and domain-specific cases ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/trends-in-data-labeling/)).
