## Overview

October on Practical AI was about data in business: getting it out of documents, deciding who owns it, and putting it to work inside established companies. Impira's Ankur Goyal, author John K. Thompson and a panel from Cox Automotive and Aryballe each took one of those angles.

## Main themes

### Asking questions of documents

- Ankur Goyal says document extraction was long possible but rarely used because tools were too hard. OCR templates only fit identical documents, and pre-trained services give no recourse when they misread scans ([DocQuery](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/what-s-up-docquery/)).
- DocQuery, announced on September 1st, came from pasting invoice text into Hugging Face question-answering models. Impira then built a pipeline with Hugging Face for Microsoft's LayoutLM ([DocQuery](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/what-s-up-docquery/)).
- Goyal says question answering gives “a sort of infinite canvas of any fields or any questions that you want”, and that open sourcing it still leaves Impira's data flywheel, UI and integrations hard to copy ([DocQuery](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/what-s-up-docquery/)).
- Next, users want to ask what type a document is and to query tables; Impira is training a model to answer questions across many documents ([DocQuery](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/what-s-up-docquery/)).

### People own their data

- John K. Thompson argues “we own the data. And number two, they should pay for it.” He traces the habit of giving data away free to an old Nielsen deal with grocery stores ([Data for All](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/data-for-all/)).
- He calls GDPR a success and says the Data Act, Data Governance Act and Digital Markets Act set up data pools, unions and exchanges. He expects the EU to be there within 18 months and Australia within about 24 ([Data for All](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/data-for-all/)).
- In his model, a data exchange lets people set prices and usage policies but cannot store or sell data itself. He likens pricing to music royalties, and estimates about $2,000 a year for an average user on three platforms ([Data for All](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/data-for-all/)).
- For analytics teams he expects little change: they already pay for external data and should combine many sources, derived data and synthetic data ([Data for All](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/data-for-all/)).

### AI adoption in established companies

- Yanis Caritu of Aryballe built a team mixing data scientists with physics, chemistry and signal-processing experts, and now spends more time understanding data than on pipelines ([AI adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/ai-adoption-in-large-well-established-companies/)).
- Mary Fischer-Mullins says the pandemic pushed Cox Automotive onto digital platforms faster than planned. She wants AI outputs that do not feel intimidating across skill levels and sees human bias as the biggest challenge ([AI adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/ai-adoption-in-large-well-established-companies/)).
- Caritu argues odour data cannot be labelled after the fact, unlike images, which slows the move to big data in digital olfaction ([AI adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/ai-adoption-in-large-well-established-companies/)).

### Smell as a signal in used-car sales

- Fischer-Mullins says Cox touches three out of four cars on the road. Odour sensing could convey smoke or industrial use when buyers can no longer sniff a car before buying online ([AI adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/ai-adoption-in-large-well-established-companies/)).
- Caritu cites an in-car study that detected malodour with 94% accuracy ([AI adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/10/ai-adoption-in-large-well-established-companies/)).
