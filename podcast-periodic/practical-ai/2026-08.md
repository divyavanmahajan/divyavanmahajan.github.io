## Overview

August was a short month on Practical AI, with three episodes about how organisations actually build with agents. The hosts laid out the vocabulary of models, harnesses and multi-agent systems, Mike Lewis argued that value comes from a few non-technical builders, and Angie Jones explained why agent standards need a neutral home.

## Main themes

### The building blocks of agentic AI

- Daniel Whitenack defines a model as a function that transforms input to output, and separates turn-based AI features from agents that pursue a goal with some autonomy ([Models, Harnesses](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/models-harnesses-and-multi-agent-systems/)).
- Chris Benson describes the harness as something like an operating system for AI that accesses models and orchestrates agents ([Models, Harnesses](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/models-harnesses-and-multi-agent-systems/)).
- Benson expects multi-agent architectures to become pervasive, and notes that “A swarm is usually a fleet, but a fleet is not necessarily a swarm.” ([Models, Harnesses](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/models-harnesses-and-multi-agent-systems/)).
- He says the strongest self-hostable models are now mostly Chinese, while US labs still lead on closed APIs. Whitenack argues firms should build their agent workforce in a vendor-agnostic way ([Models, Harnesses](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/models-harnesses-and-multi-agent-systems/)).

### From AI users to builders

- Mike Lewis of TiER1 Performance uses an L0 to L3 framework, from non-users to builders who make a difference across the enterprise. He does not treat it as a ladder where higher is better ([AI Proficiency](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/ai-proficiency-from-users-to-builders/)).
- Lewis says “threat framing actually does slow down adoption” and that about six in ten resisters are disappointed by quality, a group executives should listen to ([AI Proficiency](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/ai-proficiency-from-users-to-builders/)).
- He focuses on L2 non-technical builders, screened for company DNA rather than technical aptitude, with an L3 nearby to make their tools durable and compliant ([AI Proficiency](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/ai-proficiency-from-users-to-builders/)).

### A neutral home for agent standards

- Angie Jones explains that OpenAI, Anthropic and Block formed the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation at the end of 2025. Its projects are MCP, AGENTS.md, Goose, Agent Gateway and, newly, A2A ([Agentic AI Foundation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/building-the-foundation-for-the-agentic-ai-era/)).
- She argues companies hesitate to build products on a standard owned by one company, and that Anthropic realised MCP should live in a neutral home ([Agentic AI Foundation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/building-the-foundation-for-the-agentic-ai-era/)).
- Jones says members need the standards for their products, so the foundation must move much faster than a traditional one ([Agentic AI Foundation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/building-the-foundation-for-the-agentic-ai-era/)).

### Adoption through champions

- At Block, Jones taught the whole company to use agents, staying realistic about AI's strengths and weaknesses. She recruited 50 engineering champions who built what worked into repos as AGENTS.md files and skills ([Agentic AI Foundation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/building-the-foundation-for-the-agentic-ai-era/)).
- Lewis makes a similar case for a few well-chosen builders over mandates, citing an L2 who built a Claude skill in three hours for a job budgeted at $4,000,000 ([AI Proficiency](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/08/ai-proficiency-from-users-to-builders/)).
