## Overview

January on Practical AI leaned towards access: who gets the data, the skills and the language technology to use AI. Two episodes centred on Africa, one on open speech datasets that reach many under-served languages, and one on keeping deployed models from failing.

## Main themes

### AI in and for Africa

- Hamed Alemohammad and Abba Barde of Radiant Earth argue that satellite data can track the UN's sustainable development goals, but only once it is turned into ML-ready datasets and local practitioners are trained to use it ([Radiant Earth](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/ai-in-africa-radiant-earth/)).
- Alemohammad says African AI practitioners were “not exposed to the remote sensing world”, which led to a bootcamp for about 40 participants with Makerere and GIZ's FAIR Forward, now an online course ([Radiant Earth](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/ai-in-africa-radiant-earth/)).
- Bonaventure Dossou sees AI improving life in Africa through healthcare, education and language access. He works on AI for drug discovery at Mila and hopes to found a Mila-style research institute in Africa ([AI superhero](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/the-world-needs-an-ai-superhero/)).

### Language technology for under-resourced languages

- David Kanter's Multilingual Spoken Words Corpus covers 50 languages spoken by 5 billion people, and is the first keyword-spotting dataset for most of them ([Speech](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/democratizing-ml-for-speech/)).
- Kanter says about half the corpus languages have under 10 hours of data, yet the paper found that data usable for few-shot training and fine-tuning ([Speech](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/democratizing-ml-for-speech/)).
- Dossou builds NLP for sub-Saharan languages through Masakhane. He says a keyboard and translator his team released are already used by musicians, and links language technology to cultural identity ([AI superhero](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/the-world-needs-an-ai-superhero/)).

### Open data, built by machines

- Kanter argues companies often gain more by opening data than keeping it, citing Criteo and hardware members such as Intel and NVIDIA. MLCommons datasets are permissively licensed ([Speech](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/democratizing-ml-for-speech/)).
- People's Speech offers 30,000 hours of conversational speech. Manual labelling was estimated at about $10 million, while machine labelling cost under $20,000 ([Speech](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/democratizing-ml-for-speech/)).
- Radiant MLHub offers labelled satellite training data rather than raw imagery, and Radiant Earth helped convene the open STAC catalogue spec. Barde uses GANs to expand scarce labelled images for crop classification ([Radiant Earth](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/ai-in-africa-radiant-earth/)).
- Alemohammad notes some East African governments had no current cropland map during the locust swarms, which led to the Spot the Crop competition on Zindi ([Radiant Earth](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/ai-in-africa-radiant-earth/)).

### Keeping models from failing

- Yaron Singer separates adversarial input, as with Microsoft's chatbot, from distributional drift, as with Zillow's pricing models after the pandemic. Each needs different algorithms ([AI failures](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/eliminate-ai-failures/)).
- He argues safety should be decoupled from model building, since retraining an image classifier for robustness can cut accuracy from 98% to 37% ([AI failures](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/eliminate-ai-failures/)).
- Robust Intelligence's AI firewall is “a piece of software that wraps around an AI model to protect it from making mistakes”, flagging inputs such as a birth year in an age field. Singer expects it to become standard ([AI failures](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/01/eliminate-ai-failures/)).
