## Overview

October on Practical AI balanced engineering and people. Talks from the Midwest AI Summit and a VulnCheck exploit developer looked at guardrails and what AI can really do in production and security, while NVIDIA made the case for open world models. Two more human episodes covered AI in schools and how people re-author their own story as AI changes their work.

## Main themes

### Guardrails, evals and fit

- Heidi Porter says pilots stall when the LLM is asked to hold data, decide and check its own work. She stresses that “a rule written in a prompt is not deterministically enforced” ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit/)).
- Porter advises reversible actions where possible and approval where not, and warns that averages in evals can hide bad results ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit/)).
- Kevin Solorio argues for building agents with frontline workers rather than for them; those who help build a tool become its champions ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit/)).
- Chris Shinkle and Andrew Dunlap of SEP describe dark factories where agents write and ship all the code, but Dunlap says the goal is fit between a team's constraints and its mode of working ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit/)).

### AI in vulnerability discovery and exploits

- Spencer McIntyre of VulnCheck says models find vulnerabilities well, but “we're missing that gap between the validation and the actual functional exploit that proves attacker utility.” ([Exploit Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/exploit-development-agentic-vulnerability-discovery-and-fake-pocs/)).
- LLMs work well on open source web application logic flaws but struggle where reverse engineering or patch diffing is needed. McIntyre has not seen wide-scale AI exploitation yet but expects that to change within three to six months ([Exploit Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/exploit-development-agentic-vulnerability-discovery-and-fake-pocs/)).
- VulnCheck saw a tipping point in 2026 where more than half of public proof-of-concept exploits looked illegitimate, mostly broken ([Exploit Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/exploit-development-agentic-vulnerability-discovery-and-fake-pocs/)).

### Open models, from robots to security labs

- Ming-Yu Liu of NVIDIA argues physical AI needs open world models that builders can fine-tune for their own sensors and hardware. He says APIs solve problems but do not give insight ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/open-models-and-the-future-of-physical-ai-with-nvidia/)).
- Liu explains that Cosmos 3 fuses world understanding and world simulation, with text, action, audio and video as inputs and outputs ([NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/open-models-and-the-future-of-physical-ai-with-nvidia/)).
- McIntyre says guardrails on American proprietary models have become stricter, pushing many offensive researchers to locally hosted models such as GLM and Qwen ([Exploit Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/exploit-development-agentic-vulnerability-discovery-and-fake-pocs/)).

### People, learning and identity

- Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack argue schools lag behind AI. Whitenack says AI has arrived faster than social media and changes how everyone works ([Education](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/is-education-failing-our-students-in-this-ai-driven-world/)).
- Whitenack cites a 2025 study of nearly 1,000 Turkish maths students where a general chatbot cut later unassisted exam scores by 17%, against larger gains from a structured AI tutor at Harvard ([Education](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/is-education-failing-our-students-in-this-ai-driven-world/)).
- Leadership coach Reed Frerichs frames narrative intelligence as extracting your unique self into your story and evolving it in real time. Whitenack admits pride in his own code slowed AI adoption at Prediction Guard ([Narrative Intelligence](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/10/narrative-intelligence-and-the-human-advantage/)).
