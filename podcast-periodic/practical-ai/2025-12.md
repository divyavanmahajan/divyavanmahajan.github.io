## Overview

December on Practical AI was about AI meeting everyday work. Daniel Whitenack surveyed the models that turn documents into usable data, while two guests looked at people: how AI changes who gets hired, and why companies struggle to use it well.

## Main themes

### Document understanding

- Whitenack argues document processing “is very much not boring” because it shows more architectural variety than LLMs ([Document understanding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/technical-advances-in-document-understanding/)).
- Classic OCR tools such as Tesseract and PaddleOCR run on a laptop CPU but lose layout and need clean scans. Docling, an IBM toolkit and model family, predicts document structure and is paired with OCR ([Document understanding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/technical-advances-in-document-understanding/)).
- Vision language models such as Qwen 2.5 VL reason over images but usually resize inputs; DeepSeek OCR keeps resolution with full-resolution tiles plus a global page view, at the cost of needing GPUs ([Document understanding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/technical-advances-in-document-understanding/)).
- The most common use is preparing documents for RAG, where preserving order and structure matters more than faithful rendering ([Document understanding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/technical-advances-in-document-understanding/)).

### AI and the entry-level job

- Ramin Mohammadi says companies “stop hiring for potential” and now hire for proven capabilities; entry-level postings ask for about three years of experience ([Skills gap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/the-ai-engineer-skills-gap/)).
- He argues today's entry-level AI roles are what used to be mid-level engineering jobs, because MLOps and generative AI automated the routine tasks juniors learned on ([Skills gap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/the-ai-engineer-skills-gap/)).
- Jason Beutler accepts that AI will replace some jobs, but says the clients he works with want people redeployed rather than cut ([SOP agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/beyond-chatbots-agents-that-tackle-your-sops/)).

### Universities fall behind

- Mohammadi says graduates know the theory but have never deployed systems, and one of his courses took years to approve while the tools changed ([Skills gap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/the-ai-engineer-skills-gap/)).
- His survey found 82% of students join hackathons and 60% take extra online courses. Cloud costs make this self-learning inequitable, so he asks providers for credits and wants industry to help academia ([Skills gap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/the-ai-engineer-skills-gap/)).

### Adoption as change management

- Beutler says everyone claims to embed AI in every process but almost nobody does it effectively; most people still treat it like Google ([SOP agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/beyond-chatbots-agents-that-tackle-your-sops/)).
- RoboSource first bolted AI onto each step of its SDLC and became less efficient. After rethinking the workflow from scratch, he says “what used to take us weeks is starting to take us days” ([SOP agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/beyond-chatbots-agents-that-tackle-your-sops/)).
- His Process Coach product runs plain-English SOPs through Teams, Slack, email and text, with agent mode added once people trust it. Without leadership buy-in, he says, the cost of change is too high ([SOP agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/12/beyond-chatbots-agents-that-tackle-your-sops/)).
