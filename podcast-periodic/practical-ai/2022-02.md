## Overview

February on Practical AI covered deep reinforcement learning with Hugging Face's Thomas Simonini, grassroots NLP for African languages with Mozilla's Kathleen Siminyu, and a news round-up on early-2022 milestones. A shared thread was access: open models, free education and community-owned data.

## Main themes

### Deep reinforcement learning, in practice

- Thomas Simonini puts it simply: “deep reinforcement learning is to learn from action”. He says newer models need less and less data to train ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Its biggest weakness is generalisation: his Super Mario agent failed when he changed the background colour, and he says many agents just overfit their environments ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Game studios mostly use hand-built behaviour trees, and deep RL is used mainly for game testing. Simonini is experimenting with NPC dialogue using GPT-3 ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- He advises learning deep learning and CNNs first. His free course needs only Python and goes up to PPO ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).

### Community-built NLP for African languages

- Siminyu says Masakhane grew from a replicable notebook shared at the 2019 Deep Learning Indaba, through distributed leadership, mentorship and room for beginners. She notes that “anybody who is multilingual can participate” ([African languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/ai-in-africa-voice-language-tools/)).
- She is building a Kiswahili dataset on Mozilla's Common Voice covering several regional variants, with deliberate outreach to women because speech recognition performs worse on them ([African languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/ai-in-africa-voice-language-tools/)).
- Siminyu asks researchers to mentor junior native speakers rather than only paying them for data, and wants communities to choose licences that centre their needs ([African languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/ai-in-africa-voice-language-tools/)).

### Many narrow models, not one intelligence

- Chris Benson argues that the month's milestones are narrow pattern learners, and that the real ethical question is society handing more of life to thousands of task-specific models ([One algorithm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/one-algorithm-to-rule-them-all/)).
- Daniel Whitenack explains Meta AI's data2vec, which learns representations for images, speech and text with one self-supervised approach and matches or beats single-modality models such as RoBERTa on some benchmarks ([One algorithm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/one-algorithm-to-rule-them-all/)).
- DeepMind claims AlphaCode codes at the level of an average competitive programmer on Codeforces problems. Benson thinks 95 to 99% of code shares patterns across projects ([One algorithm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/one-algorithm-to-rule-them-all/)).
- The hosts also touched on an autonomous surgical robot and a protein-binding model, asking whether human-plus-machine teams still beat either alone ([One algorithm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/one-algorithm-to-rule-them-all/)).

### Open access to AI

- Simonini says AI “will change everything” but will not solve everything unless it is accessible, and points out that most MOOC learners come from developing countries ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- He calls Hugging Face the GitHub for AI models, where a trained model can be used in two lines of code ([Deep RL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/exploring-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Joyce Nabende says her Luganda researchers get faster help on the open Masakhane Slack than by emailing paper authors ([African languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/02/ai-in-africa-voice-language-tools/)).
