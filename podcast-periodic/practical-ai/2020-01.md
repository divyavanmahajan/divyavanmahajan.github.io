## Overview

January on Practical AI opened with the hosts' review of 2019 and then turned to AI in large institutions. Greg Allen explained how the US Department of Defense adopts AI under ethics principles, Costas Boulis described robotic microfactories, and Tania Allard laid out how to test ML systems. AI ethics was the one item that ran through more than one episode.

## Main themes

### AI ethics and military adoption

- Greg Allen of the Joint Artificial Intelligence Center says the JAIC's job is adopting AI that already works in industry, not research. Problems solvable in zero to five years fit the JAIC; longer research fits DARPA and the service laboratories ([US military](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/how-the-u-s-military-thinks-about-ai/)).
- Allen summarises the Defense Innovation Board's principles, developed over 15 months: responsible, equitable, traceable, reliable and governable. He says “We see AI ethics and AI safety as intimately interconnected” ([US military](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/how-the-u-s-military-thinks-about-ai/)).
- He frames the principles as confidence that technology does what is intended, and lists mission areas from disaster relief and predictive maintenance to cybersecurity and joint warfighting ([US military](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/how-the-u-s-military-thinks-about-ai/)).
- In their year review, Chris Benson notes maturing AI ethics frameworks and predicts tooling for ethical AI in 2020 ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)).

### 2019 in review: transformers, deepfakes and compute cost

- Daniel Whitenack calls 2019 the year of large language models such as BERT and GPT-2. Benson recalls OpenAI's staged GPT-2 release and the debate over withholding the model ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)).
- Whitenack credits Hugging Face's Transformers library for much of the year's momentum, and notes its $15 million funding ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)).
- Benson flags deepfakes ahead of the 2020 US elections, while Whitenack points to detection work and positive GAN uses ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)).
- Whitenack cites a study that training one large model “can emit as much carbon as five cars during their whole lifetime of use”, and expects more distillation and optimisation ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)).

### Automating electronics manufacturing

- Costas Boulis says Bright Machines is trying to “automate automation”, cutting deployment time and building solutions that keep working when conditions change ([Manufacturing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/ai-driven-automation-in-manufacturing/)).
- He describes most factory vision as low-level edge and blob detection that breaks when lighting or alignment shifts, and says standard object detectors are not built for the precision factories need ([Manufacturing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/ai-driven-automation-in-manufacturing/)).
- Boulis calls the digital twin one of the company's biggest assets. With no ImageNet for custom components, it generates data and lets reinforcement learning agents practise without breaking robots ([Manufacturing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/ai-driven-automation-in-manufacturing/)).
- He cites typical manufacturer turnover of 300% and expects repetitive jobs to shift towards monitoring and programming robots ([Manufacturing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/ai-driven-automation-in-manufacturing/)).

### Testing the whole ML system

- Tania Allard defines an ML system as data, pipeline, training and serving infrastructure, and the way users reach the model. All of it needs tests, not just model accuracy ([Testing ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/testing-ml-systems/)).
- Her ML score gives 0 points for an absent test, 1 for manual and 2 for automated in CI; the lowest-scoring area is where to start ([Testing ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/testing-ml-systems/)).
- Allard uses Jupyter for prototyping but moves to software engineering practice once a model stabilises, with Git hooks that clear outputs before commits ([Testing ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/testing-ml-systems/)).
