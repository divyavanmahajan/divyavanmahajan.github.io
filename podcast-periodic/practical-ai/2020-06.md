## Overview

June on Practical AI moved between near-term engineering and longer-range questions. Two guests covered explainable, compact networks and serving models from a distributed database. In two host-only episodes Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack discussed the road to AGI and the different roles people play in an AI project.

## Main themes

### Explainability and trust

- Sheldon Fernandez of Darwin AI argues that “if you don't understand how a decision is being made, you don't understand where it will fail” ([Explainability](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/explaining-ai-explainability/)).
- He describes generative synthesis, which probes an existing network and generates a family of more compact networks with similar accuracy. He contrasts it with AutoML's search across a large space ([Explainability](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/explaining-ai-explainability/)).
- Fernandez gives the example of an autonomous vehicle that turned left more often under a purple sky, because turning was trained in the Nevada desert. Most clients want explanations against data, not individual weights ([Explainability](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/explaining-ai-explainability/)).
- Darwin AI sits on top of TensorFlow and is not SaaS, because enterprises refused to share models and data ([Explainability](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/explaining-ai-explainability/)).

### The long road to AGI

- Benson says deep learning has reached a mature, incremental phase, and research attention is turning to what comes next. Whitenack does not see any danger of apocalyptic AGI scenarios ([Road to AGI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/the-long-road-to-agi/)).
- Benson summarises Yoshua Bengio's case that attention and a computationally defined consciousness are the ingredients of System 2 deep learning ([Road to AGI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/the-long-road-to-agi/)).
- He walks through DARPA's three waves of AI: handcrafted knowledge, statistical learning and contextual adaptation. He calls DARPA “essentially a giant project management office” ([Road to AGI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/the-long-road-to-agi/)).

### Many roles in AI development

- Benson calls it a myth that you need a PhD to work in AI, and compares the field to the early web, when one role split into many ([AI dev roles](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/roles-to-play-in-the-ai-dev-workflow/)).
- He treats AI development as part of agile software development: what ships is a model wrapped in a software component, not a notebook ([AI dev roles](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/roles-to-play-in-the-ai-dev-workflow/)).
- Benson says a neural network should only be chosen if cheaper approaches do worse, and expects most deployment to move to the edge. Whitenack recommends fairness resources such as IBM's Fairness 360 and Deon ([AI dev roles](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/roles-to-play-in-the-ai-dev-workflow/)).

### Operationalising ML in the database

- Nikita Shamgunov of MemSQL argues that models often underperform in production because they run against different data systems than in training, and wants one distributed SQL layer over all data ([MemSQL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/operationalizing-ml-ai-with-memsql/)).
- He says 20 to 30% of MemSQL workloads have an ML or AI angle, including in-transaction fraud detection for major banks with a 40 millisecond decision window ([MemSQL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/operationalizing-ml-ai-with-memsql/)).
- MemSQL plans to make the database aware of stored models so SQL can run data through them, possibly via Apache Arrow ([MemSQL](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/operationalizing-ml-ai-with-memsql/)).
