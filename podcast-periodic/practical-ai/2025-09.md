## Overview

September on Practical AI was about the gap between AI demos and AI in production. Two episodes took on the MIT report's claim that most generative AI pilots fail, and both blamed workflow design and organisation rather than models. The third showed what careful engineering looks like in practice: Thunderbird's private email assistant.

## Main themes

### Why AI pilots fail

- Discussing the MIT report, Daniel Whitenack points to a learning gap in designing AI workflows rather than weak models. Companies need “data integration, data augmentation to these models”, not just a model plus a prompt ([Failed AI pilots](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/cracking-the-code-of-failed-ai-pilots/)).
- Chris Benson says chat interfaces have become a universal hammer, and that many pilots are pushed top down by executives who do not understand the tools ([Failed AI pilots](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/cracking-the-code-of-failed-ai-pilots/)).
- Rajiv Shah of Contextual AI calls the 95% figure partly a hype number. He says adoption depends on workflow fit, training and leadership support ([RAG, now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/we-ve-all-done-rag-now-what/)).
- Benson warns that companies cutting junior developer hiring and betting on seniors with AI are taking risks their executives cannot see ([Failed AI pilots](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/cracking-the-code-of-failed-ai-pilots/)).

### Systems, not models

- Whitenack argues that even a simple SharePoint document workflow needs a document model like Docling, a language model, embeddings, rerankers and safeguards: “What you need is a set of models” ([Failed AI pilots](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/cracking-the-code-of-failed-ai-pilots/)).
- He sees the model layer as commoditised. He reads GPT-5's flat reception, OpenAI's open-weight models and its consulting arm as signs the money is in services ([Failed AI pilots](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/cracking-the-code-of-failed-ai-pilots/)).
- Chris Aquino of Thunderbird found that one large prompt for ranking, highlights and formatting gave poor results. He moved ranking to a local Bayesian classifier and formatting into code, an approach Whitenack endorses ([Thunderbird](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/creating-a-private-ai-assistant-in-thunderbird/)).

### RAG in production and the value of evaluation

- Shah says nearly every company now runs some form of RAG, but a demo that works on 100 documents struggles at a million, with short queries, rerankers and latency trade-offs ([RAG, now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/we-ve-all-done-rag-now-what/)).
- He argues evaluation and error analysis are core data science skills that software developers often need to learn, so data science is not shrinking ([RAG, now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/we-ve-all-done-rag-now-what/)).
- Shah is optimistic that reasoning models making tool calls will improve retrieval, at a latency cost. He also reminds newcomers that not every problem needs an LLM ([RAG, now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/we-ve-all-done-rag-now-what/)).

### Private inference for email

- Aquino says Thunderbird users do not want their emails harvested for marketing or training. The team chose remote inference because local models strain old hardware and laptop batteries ([Thunderbird](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/creating-a-private-ai-assistant-in-thunderbird/)).
- Flower Labs, introduced via the Mozilla Foundation, supplied end-to-end encryption, post-training for summarisation and an eval system. Its engineers told the team “we literally can't help you debug your prompt because it's encrypted now” ([Thunderbird](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/09/creating-a-private-ai-assistant-in-thunderbird/)).
