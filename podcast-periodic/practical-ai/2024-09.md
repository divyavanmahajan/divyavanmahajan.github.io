## Overview

September on Practical AI pushed back against the idea that AI means generative AI. Daniel Whitenack retraced the longer history of machine learning, the hosts weighed scikit-learn's new backing against OpenAI's o1, and a guest showed where graphs improve RAG. A security founder closed the loop with a hard line on treating models as hostile.

## Main themes

### Generative AI is one phase among three

- In a solo episode, Whitenack names a common workshop confusion: “AI equals generative AI”. He walks through statistical ML, transfer learning and generative models, all still in use ([AI is more than GenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/ai-is-more-than-genai/)).
- He estimates 90% or more of a model's processing builds an internal representation, which is why embeddings transfer so well and could be repurposed for semantic search and RAG ([AI is more than GenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/ai-is-more-than-genai/)).
- Most teams are now model consumers rather than trainers. He suggests combining methods, such as a generative model calling a statistical forecasting model as a tool ([AI is more than GenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/ai-is-more-than-genai/)).

### Open data science and OpenAI o1

- Whitenack recommends scikit-learn for learning statistical modelling without GPUs, and reports on seed funding for Probabl, its official operator, with a certification programme planned for Q4 2024 ([AI is more than GenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/ai-is-more-than-genai/), [scikit-learn & o1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/pausing-to-think-about-scikit-learn-openai-o1/)).
- He guesses o1 is a preference-tuned model trained on curated chain-of-thought examples, an evolutionary RLHF step marketed as thinking. Both hosts call their analysis speculation ([scikit-learn & o1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/pausing-to-think-about-scikit-learn-openai-o1/)).
- Chris Benson notes o1 preview is too slow for voice, with an October 2023 knowledge cutoff and no browsing or file upload ([scikit-learn & o1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/pausing-to-think-about-scikit-learn-openai-o1/)).
- Whitenack tested it on density functional theory, his PhD subject, and found the answer “good-ish, but not mind-blowing” ([scikit-learn & o1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/pausing-to-think-about-scikit-learn-openai-o1/)).

### GraphRAG beyond the hype

- Prashanth Rao of Kùzu traces RAG from 2020 papers by Google and Facebook Research to hybrid search, and shows graphs capture explicit relationships that embeddings miss ([GraphRAG](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/graphrag-beyond-the-hype/)).
- In his Marie Curie demo, vector search found only Marie Curie as Pierre Curie's collaborator, while the graph also found Paul Langevin ([GraphRAG](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/graphrag-beyond-the-hype/)).
- He disputes claims that GraphRAG reduces hallucination and names graph construction as the main bottleneck, since LLM triple extraction is hard to reproduce ([GraphRAG](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/graphrag-beyond-the-hype/)).
- His takeaway is that dense vector retrieval and graphs “go hand in hand for the purposes of RAG” ([GraphRAG](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/graphrag-beyond-the-hype/)).

### Security when data becomes code

- Dinis Cruz argues LLMs remove the old separation between code and data, a step backwards for security. He passes validated JSON in and requires strongly typed JSON out ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/cybersecurity-in-the-genai-age/)).
- He calls his approach deterministic GenAI: isolated, read-only, version-locked models fed only the knowledge a request needs, because black-box systems will eventually fail ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/cybersecurity-in-the-genai-age/)).
- Cruz mixes cheap local models with frontier models, and sees AI helping defenders by checking each commit for attack-surface changes ([Cybersecurity](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/09/cybersecurity-in-the-genai-age/)).
