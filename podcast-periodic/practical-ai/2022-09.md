## Overview

September on Practical AI centred on Stable Diffusion, which the hosts covered twice: once for its licence and impact, once for how it works. The other two episodes dealt with the less visible work of making models reliable, from diagnosing trained weights to running data labeling at scale.

## Main themes

### Stable Diffusion and the open release

- Daniel Whitenack credits openness and community for the model's rapid spread, with attention moving away from DALL-E 2, which needs a waitlist and API access ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/), [Stable Diffusion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/stable-diffusion/)).
- He explains the key change: the autoencoder and diffusion model are trained separately, so diffusion runs on compressed images that need about 64 times less memory, which lets the model run on a consumer GPU ([Stable Diffusion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/stable-diffusion/)).
- Citing a team member's tweet, he says training “use 256 A100s in the cloud for 150K hours”, about 600K at market price, within reach of small teams ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/)).
- Chris Benson says the release feels like the open source software world both hosts come from, and expects video, special effects and new creative businesses to follow ([Stable Diffusion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/stable-diffusion/)).

### Licensing and responsibility for model behaviour

- Whitenack argues models fit neither software nor content licences because their behaviour and biases matter. Open RAIL licences, used for BLOOM and Stable Diffusion, add use restrictions such as no medical advice ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/)).
- Benson worries explicit lists will miss unexpected harms; Whitenack notes the BLOOM FAQ calls the list a start. Users must accept the licence on Hugging Face before downloading Stable Diffusion ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/), [Stable Diffusion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/stable-diffusion/)).

### AI reaches art, jobs and government

- Benson cites an AI-generated artwork winning a state fair fine arts competition. He sees AI as a tool like Photoshop but expects some competitions to require hand-made work ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/)).
- On jobs, he cites a claim that call centres could save $80 billion with AI and says “There's really no safe place” across industries ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/)).
- French authorities found tens of thousands of undeclared pools from satellite imagery. Whitenack notes the system first mistook solar panels for pools at a 30% error rate, a case for evaluating behaviour before release ([Licensing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/licensing-automating-creativity/)).

### Evaluating models without test data

- Charles Martin started on the problem training text generators, where there is no test set, and notes production models that break at Thanksgiving and Christmas ([Evaluating models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/evaluating-models-without-test-data/)).
- His open-source WeightWatcher uses statistical physics on weight matrices. Its alpha metric should be around 2 for vision models and 3 to 4 for transformer NLP models; higher values flag under-trained layers ([Evaluating models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/evaluating-models-without-test-data/)).
- Whitenack ran it on an XLM-RoBERTa question-answering model and found 10 under-trained layers ([Evaluating models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/evaluating-models-without-test-data/)).

### Labeling as a managed production process

- Xelex CEO Mark Christensen says commoditised click work stalls specialty projects, so Xelex trains and pays annotators for long-term relationships ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/production-data-labeling-workflows/)).
- He says tooling “is broadly not keeping pace with the growth of the industry”: senior data scientists vet annotator samples and large companies still run projects on spreadsheets ([Data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/09/production-data-labeling-workflows/)).
