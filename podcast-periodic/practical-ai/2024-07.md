## Overview

July on Practical AI was about cutting through hype. Two episodes read Gartner's 2024 AI Hype Cycle and saw generative AI heading for the trough, which the hosts treated as a healthy return to engineering. The rest of the month covered unglamorous but valuable AI in practice, vector databases, a small lab's real-time voice assistant and Stanford's AI Index.

## Main themes

### Reading the Gartner Hype Cycle

- Chris Benson reports that generative AI, after a long stay at the peak, is now heading into the trough of disillusionment while large companies keep spending on it ([Real-time voice assistant](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/the-first-real-time-voice-assistant/)).
- Daniel Whitenack reads the disappointment as a matter of integration, not capability. He says Gartner's composite AI is “almost a term describing data science” ([Real-time voice assistant](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/the-first-real-time-voice-assistant/)).
- With Demetrios Brinkmann, the hosts find odd placements: cloud AI services sit in the trough, AI engineering at the peak, and RAG, multimodal AI, small language models and vector databases are missing ([Gartner Hype Cycle](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/hyperventilating-over-the-gartner-ai-hype-cycle/)).
- Whitenack jokes that “Gartner seems to have fulfilled their mission” because they are discussing the chart at all ([Gartner Hype Cycle](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/hyperventilating-over-the-gartner-ai-hype-cycle/)).

### Broccoli AI and right-sized MLOps

- Brinkmann coins Broccoli AI: unglamorous AI an organisation needs, such as traditional ML for fraud detection ([Gartner Hype Cycle](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/hyperventilating-over-the-gartner-ai-hype-cycle/)).
- Bengsoon Chuah, a lone data scientist in the energy sector, says that kind of AI brought him the most value: “not so sexy, but still really important” ([Broccoli AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/broccoli-ai-at-its-best/)).
- His users labelled sensitive, code-switched safety reports themselves. About 1,800 to 2,000 labels trained a SetFit classifier that reached only around 60-70% F1, yet a daily email of classified reports was enough to win sponsors ([Broccoli AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/broccoli-ai-at-its-best/)).
- An active learning loop lets users correct predictions; Chuah retrains every couple of months on a minimal on-prem stack of MLflow, Prefect, Docker and DuckDB ([Broccoli AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/broccoli-ai-at-its-best/)).

### Vector databases and grounding

- Roie Schwaber-Cohen says the value of vector search lies in embeddings, which place semantically similar content close together, for text and for images via CLIP ([Pinecone](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/vectoring-in-on-pinecone/)).
- He describes RAG as tying user intent to trusted data, with namespaces making multi-tenant RAG a strong use case for Pinecone ([Pinecone](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/vectoring-in-on-pinecone/)).
- Serverless storage now covers 10 million vectors for the earlier cost of about 500,000. He expects LLMs to become orchestrators over vector, graph and relational stores ([Pinecone](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/vectoring-in-on-pinecone/)).

### Small open labs and on-device voice

- Whitenack notes that Kyutai, a non-profit lab of around eight people with roughly a thousand GPUs, released its real-time voice assistant Moshi before OpenAI shipped GPT-4o voice ([Real-time voice assistant](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/the-first-real-time-voice-assistant/)).
- Moshi is sized for a single GPU and can run disconnected, which Benson says suits IP-sensitive enterprises and defence ([Real-time voice assistant](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/the-first-real-time-voice-assistant/)).

### The state of AI in numbers

- Nestor Maslej walks through Stanford's seventh AI Index: training costs of about $80 million for GPT-4 and $190 million for Gemini are pushing frontier research into industry ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/stanford-s-ai-index-report-2024/)).
- About 40 state-level AI bills became US law in 2023 against one federal, and rules are becoming more restrictive. Maslej also finds no consensus on responsible-AI benchmarks ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/07/stanford-s-ai-index-report-2024/)).
