## Overview

August on Practical AI marked the show's 100th episode, with GPT-3 the one topic that came up more than once. Guests covered how fragile NLP models are under small text changes, how Anaconda grew out of Python's packaging problems, and how Hilary Mason plans to use language generation safely for children.

## Main themes

### GPT-3 and where deep learning is heading

- Daniel Whitenack sees GPT-3 as scale, not “a fundamental paradigm shift in terms of like how we're doing modeling necessarily.” He finds OpenAI's API-only access the bigger change ([Practical AI turns 100](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/practical-ai-turns-100/)).
- Chris Benson says weekly breakthroughs have stopped and expects years of commercialisation of existing AI before the next big step ([Practical AI turns 100](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/practical-ai-turns-100/)).
- Whitenack calls a Terminator-style singularity inconceivable in his lifetime. Benson expects high levels of intelligence without consciousness long before any progress on consciousness ([Practical AI turns 100](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/practical-ai-turns-100/)).
- On ethics, Benson names GDPR as the main national-level law and suggests organisations adopt an existing set of AI principles ([Practical AI turns 100](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/practical-ai-turns-100/)).

### Adversarial examples and robust NLP

- Jack Morris explains that a near-invisible pixel change can flip an image classifier, but no change to text is imperceptible. NLP attacks rely on agreed constraints such as synonym swaps or typos ([TextAttack](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/attack-of-the-c-l-o-n-e-s-text/)).
- He separates robustness, meaning no obvious holes in predictions, from safety, meaning resistance to manipulation. The hosts compare finding adversarial examples to unit testing ([TextAttack](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/attack-of-the-c-l-o-n-e-s-text/)).
- TextAttack splits an attack into a transformation, constraints, a goal function and a search method. Morris expects most use in reusable components and data augmentation ([TextAttack](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/attack-of-the-c-l-o-n-e-s-text/)).

### Packaging and foundations for data science

- Peter Wang says Anaconda began because users could not even install SciPy or Matplotlib. Everything in Anaconda is open source ([Anaconda](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/building-the-world-s-most-popular-data-science-platform/)).
- Wang argues Conda makes Docker unnecessary by building portable native binaries per platform. Revenue comes from a governed package server for IT and an ML platform ([Anaconda](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/building-the-world-s-most-popular-data-science-platform/)).
- He describes data needs as a Maslow hierarchy: without basic data management there is no data science and no real AI ([Anaconda](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/building-the-world-s-most-popular-data-science-platform/)).

### Language generation that is safe for kids

- Hilary Mason describes Hidden Door as a creative storytelling aid for kids, built on fine-tuned GPT-2, with the system acting like a dungeon master ([Hidden Door](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/hidden-door-and-so-much-more/)).
- She says it took her “two tries to get something deeply misogynist out of GPT-3”, so raw output needs a constrained domain, classifiers and human review ([Hidden Door](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/hidden-door-and-so-much-more/)).
- Mason argues data products start from a human problem. A slightly more accurate model is no reason to switch if another is more convenient ([Hidden Door](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/hidden-door-and-so-much-more/)).
