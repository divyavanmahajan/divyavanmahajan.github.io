## Overview

June on Practical AI was varied. It finished the federated learning series, looked at AI in the film business, and had two host-only episodes: Daniel Whitenack on raising a seed round for Prediction Guard, and the first debate episode, on autonomous weapons.

## Main themes

### Federated learning as production infrastructure

- Chong Shen Ng of Flower Labs says “the biggest driver is when they realize they can't move their data”, alongside data volumes too large to move ([Federated learning, part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/federated-learning-in-production-part-2/)).
- He puts an admittedly arbitrary 100x on growth since 2021, driven by foundation model training, and says Flower now streams weights for very large models ([Federated learning, part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/federated-learning-in-production-part-2/)).
- Flower's CLI creates templated projects, code moves unchanged from simulation to deployment, and long-running SuperLink and SuperNode services separate administrators from data scientists ([Federated learning, part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/federated-learning-in-production-part-2/)).

### AI in the film business

- Sami Arpa of Largo AI says industry use of AI rose from 2% when Largo started to about 30%, despite two Hollywood strikes ([Film making](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-automated-film-making/)).
- Largo focuses on early development, where about 90% of films are eliminated, offering content analysis, casting and financial forecasts it says are about 80% accurate ([Film making](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-automated-film-making/)).
- Arpa says budget influences financial results most, and Largo also runs simulated focus groups with digital twins of real people that last about a year ([Film making](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-automated-film-making/)).
- He insists the tool only works forward on human-made content and never writes stories for creators ([Film making](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-automated-film-making/)).

### Funding an AI startup

- Whitenack explains fund mechanics and the power law, and argues venture money only fits products aimed at very large markets that must scale fast ([VC funding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/behind-the-scenes-vc-funding-for-ai-startups/)).
- Prediction Guard bootstrapped for its first year or so, then raised a seed round because Whitenack expects AI consolidation within three to five years ([VC funding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/behind-the-scenes-vc-funding-for-ai-startups/)).
- He says many early investors look at AI and see “absolute pure chaos”, so seed companies with traction face the hardest questions on differentiation ([VC funding](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/behind-the-scenes-vc-funding-for-ai-startups/)).
- Prediction Guard, which Whitenack describes as a self-hosted AI system, also came up in the federated learning episode ([Federated learning, part 2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/federated-learning-in-production-part-2/)).

### Autonomy and responsibility

- Arguing by assignment from Kevin Jon Heller's paper, Whitenack says autonomy could reduce harm because human soldiers are biased and error-prone, and that responsibility stays with designers and commanders ([Autonomy debate](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-hot-takes-and-debates-autonomy/)).
- Chris Benson, speaking only for himself, argues that outsourcing lethal decisions removes moral judgement and devalues human life, and points to DoD Directive 3000.09 ([Autonomy debate](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-hot-takes-and-debates-autonomy/)).
- Both warn that a human pulled in late may lack the context to fix things. Whitenack tells product builders to benchmark AI against real human performance ([Autonomy debate](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/06/ai-hot-takes-and-debates-autonomy/)).
