## Overview

May on Practical AI had three guest episodes with little overlap. Linguist Sarah Moeller described active learning for endangered languages, and Hugging Face's Merve Noyan argued that the Hub is the core of open-source machine learning. Absci opened the month with a business case for AI in drug discovery built on in-house lab data.

## Main themes

### Active learning for endangered languages

- Sarah Moeller says of roughly 7,000 languages, 30 to 90% may be endangered by the end of the century, and that documentation stalls on slow manual transcription, morphological analysis and translation ([Endangered languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/active-learning-endangered-languages/)).
- English and Mandarin are morphologically simple, but in Arapaho almost every word equals an English sentence. One model trained on 3,000 annotated words reached only about 30% accuracy ([Endangered languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/active-learning-endangered-languages/)).
- Her aim is to pick about 100 low-confidence predictions whose correction lifts accuracy to 40% or more. She stresses “it's not automated, but it's automatic assistance” to address linguists' fears ([Endangered languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/active-learning-endangered-languages/)).
- Moeller notes that community members want a personal relationship with linguists, and that more annotated data enables tools that can encourage children to speak their languages ([Endangered languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/active-learning-endangered-languages/)).

### The Hugging Face Hub and open-source ML

- Merve Noyan says “the most important thing on Hugging Face is actually Hugging Face Hub”, which hosts libraries beyond Transformers, such as Stanza and Keras models ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/the-ai-community-building-the-future/)).
- New pull requests and discussions on model, dataset and Space repositories bring GitHub-style collaboration, letting users add weights or flag bias. Whitenack links this to a Masakhane finding that some crawled datasets held none of their labelled language ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/the-ai-community-building-the-future/)).
- Noyan built Keras integration that pushes a model with metrics and logs in one line, and is designing automated model cards for scikit-learn to support reproducibility ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/the-ai-community-building-the-future/)).
- From her chatbot work, she would not put a language model in front of end users without filtering, and says the ethics team asks model owners to declare bias ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/the-ai-community-building-the-future/)).

### Data as the bottleneck for AI in biotech

- Absci's Sean McClain and Joshua Meier argue that earlier AI efforts in biotech failed for lack of good lab data, and that a decade of building high-throughput data generation now lets models design drug candidates ([Language of life](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/learning-the-language-of-life/)).
- McClain describes traditional drug discovery as slow and costly with low success rates, predicts much higher rates in future, and says regulators need to be educated now ([Language of life](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/05/learning-the-language-of-life/)).
