## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Pranav Reddy of the venture fund Conviction argue that 2024 turned AI into a market that structurally favours startups. Model competition, open source and falling prices undercut the "GPT wrapper" narrative. In their view, incumbents' advantages in product surface and data are weaker than they look, and the fast pace of change rewards new companies.

## Key points

- **A closer model race.** Reddy notes that a year earlier every OpenAI model led LM Arena by over 100 Elo points. He cites Ramp data showing OpenAI's share of OpenAI-plus-Anthropic spend falling from close to 90% to about 60%.
- **Cheaper, smaller, open.** He says flagship API prices fell roughly 80-85% and Llama 8B beats Mistral 7B by more than ten MMLU points. He also says new modalities are starting to work in biology (Chai-1), voice, code execution and video.
- **Funding is less bubbly than it looks.** By their reading of PitchBook data, a few foundation labs raised $30-40 billion, and spending on everyone else looks rational.
- **Patterns that work.** They point to service automation (Sierra, Decagon, Harvey), new search and companions (Perplexity, Glean, Character), creative tools such as Midjourney, and compute and data infrastructure.
- **Incumbents' limits.** Guo argues that seat-based SaaS pricing creates an innovator's dilemma, and that incumbents rarely saved the reasoning traces startups actually need.
- **Lean companies.** Reddy says many portfolio companies are near $1 million of revenue per employee. Guo argues demand for intelligence is elastic, so cheaper tokens lead to more calls, not lower bills.
