## Thesis

Joel Becker of METR (model evaluation and threat research) tells swyx and Alessio Fanelli that the time horizon trend measures how hard a task is, in human hours, that models can finish with 50% reliability. It does not measure how long agents run. He argues that progress has been surprisingly continuous, that productivity claims are often inflated, and that a capabilities explosion needs the whole R&D loop closed, not just coding.

## Key points

- **What the chart measures.** Becker says the suite of 170 tasks (SWA, Hcast and ReBench) favours automatically gradable, neatly scoped work and leaves out vision-heavy and "messy" real-world tasks. A common misreading treats it as covering all tasks.
- **Opus 4.5 and the trend line.** Becker had backed a seven-month doubling time over the faster four-month one; Opus 4.5 fits the faster line. He says yearly trends tell you more than any single release.
- **Re-running the productivity study.** The earlier RCT found AI slowed experienced developers down. Becker says repeating it is harder now: developers won't accept AI-disallowed tasks, and they now work on several issues at once. He adds that extra side projects inflate perceived speed-ups because they are lower-value work.
- **Threat models and timelines.** METR has deprioritised autonomous replication relative to R&D acceleration. Becker calls himself a relatively long-timelines person at METR, pointing to a long tail of R&D work, such as fixing failed GPUs or calling the water company, that he knows of no benchmark tracking.
- **Compute and slowdown.** A METR paper using OpenAI spending data argues that if algorithmic progress depends on compute, a slowdown in compute growth would also slow time horizon growth.
- **Next research directions.** Becker points to AI Village-style open-ended goals, mining agent transcripts, and checking whether solutions would actually be merged rather than just passing tests. He also says his top Manifold ranking came mostly from one charity market he could move by donating about $5,000.
