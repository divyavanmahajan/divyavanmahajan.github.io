## Thesis

Retool CEO David Hsu tells Alessio Fanelli and swyx that AI coding assistants give only modest gains and chat solves one-off tasks, so the larger opportunity is automating entire business processes inside internal apps. He argues most software is internal, written at non-software companies, where tolerance for imperfect AI output is higher.

## Key points

- **Lean company building.** Hsu skipped his YC Demo Day in 2017 for lack of traction, then raised a Series A at about $1 million revenue with three or four people. He argues startups die from lost motivation, not lost money, and that Retool raised less money at lower valuations in 2021 to protect employees from dilution.
- **Coding assistants.** He estimates Copilot makes coding 10-20% faster, not 10x, because languages and frameworks remain too low level; generated JavaScript still has to be read and debugged.
- **Workflows over agents.** Hsu thinks agents fail because small error rates compound across steps. Retool Workflows has humans lay out the steps while each step can call AI. His example: a top-two clothing manufacturer that generates new patterns with DALL-E from regional sales data, which analysts approve or reject.
- **Build versus partner.** Retool builds where it has a distinct take, such as Workflows, and uses open source elsewhere, choosing pgvector for its vector store because he expects open source tooling to win.
- **State of AI survey.** The hosts cite Retool's survey of about 1,600 people: 52% call AI overrated, 58 report using Stack Overflow less (94% of them due to Copilot and ChatGPT), 27% have AI in production, 66% of those internal, and 80% use OpenAI. GPT-4's NPS was about 45 against roughly 14 for GPT-3.5.
- **Ubiquity.** Retool started sales-led to test product-market fit, but last year changed its North Star from net new ARR to developers building on the platform, aiming for some of the world's 25-30 million developers.
