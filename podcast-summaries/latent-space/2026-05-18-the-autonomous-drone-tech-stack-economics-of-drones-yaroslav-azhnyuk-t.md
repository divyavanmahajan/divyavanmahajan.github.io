## Thesis

Yaroslav Azhnyuk, the Petcube founder who now runs drone-autonomy company The Fourth Law, tells host Brandon Anderson and guest host Noah Smith that FPV drones are the first software-defined weapon platform, and that adding autonomy multiplies their value. He argues mass and cost now decide battles, and that the US and Europe are behind China in autonomy, manufacturing, components and rare earths.

## Key points

- **Scale and lethality.** Azhnyuk says Ukraine made about 4 million FPV drones last year, with a public target of 7 million this year, and that FPV drones cause 70-80% of front-line casualties. He compares a roughly $4,000 155mm artillery shell with a $400 drone.
- **Five levels of autonomy.** His taxonomy runs from terminal guidance through autonomous bombing, target detection and navigation to autonomous take-off and landing. Terminal guidance alone raised one pilot's mission success from 20% to 71% and his kill zone from 3 to 10 km.
- **Fibre versus AI.** Fibre-optic drones resist jamming and radio shadow but add weight and range limits; Azhnyuk says fibre prices rose from about $4 to $32 per kilometre because AI data centres are buying the same cable.
- **Economics of defence.** He questions a $3 million laser system that he says needs about three seconds per drone, when the same money buys around 6,000 drones. Mass, cheap countermeasures (nets, cage armour, interceptor drones) matter more than exquisite systems.
- **China and the West.** Azhnyuk warns China could build billions of drones and deliver them by sea, and says no one should rule out that it is already the leading conventional power. Smith argues wartime urgency could change Western output, as in the Second World War. Azhnyuk wants procurement reform and closer learning from Ukraine, which he calls the "Defense Valley".
- **Ethics.** He argues building weapons is the only moral choice for Ukraine, and that safety thinking must not slow development while adversaries move faster.
