## Thesis

Sarah Catanzaro of Amplify Partners argues that the data stack is not dying but has quietly become part of AI infrastructure, and that the most promising frontier is memory management and continual learning, which she sees as the answer to the churn problem of fast-growing AI apps. She is sceptical of today's funding climate and of RL environments as a standalone business.

## Key points

- **dbt-Fivetran is not the end of the modern data stack.** Catanzaro, whose firm funded dbt, says both companies were beating revenue targets; the merger is a route to IPO now that the bar is closer to $600 million in revenue. Frontier labs such as Thinking Machines adopted dbt within weeks of forming.
- **Data catalogs were built for the wrong users.** Catalog startups (Atlan, Data.world, Metaphor) struggled because Snowflake and dbt features were good enough for humans; she suspects the real opportunity was metadata services for machines and governance. She also plugs Spiral's Vortex file format for efficient GPU data loading.
- **Funding excess.** She regularly sees seed rounds above $100 million with a long-term vision but no six-month plan and seven-day decision windows, often raised to help hiring with large paper equity values. Periodic's wet lab is her example of capital that is genuinely needed.
- **Memory and continual learning.** AI apps grow fast but churn; she wonders how Cursor keeps users from switching to Windsurf or Claude Code. Continual learning makes weights stateful, which she calls a systems problem because inference is stateless today.
- **RL environments are a fad.** Labs pay seven to eight figures for them, but she argues the best environment is the real world, citing Cursor training on real user activity rather than buying app clones.
- **Research-led applications.** She favours companies like Harvey (RAG), Sierra (rule following) and Runway, which solve hard research problems to deliver a better product.
