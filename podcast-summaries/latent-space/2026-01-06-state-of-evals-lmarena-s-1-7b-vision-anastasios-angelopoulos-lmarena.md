## Thesis

Anastasios Angelopoulos, co-founder of LMArena (now branded Arena), argues that evaluation should centre on real users asking their own questions, which keeps the benchmark fresh and resistant to overfitting. He describes spinning the Berkeley project out as a company because only a company could fund the inference and scale needed. Latent Space re-released the conversation after LMArena raised $150m at a $1.7B valuation.

## Key points

- **From Berkeley to company.** Arena began in the LMSYS group at Berkeley and was incubated by Ange at a16z, with grants from Sequoia too. Angelopoulos and co-founder Wei-Lin (transcribed "Weilian Yan") concluded neither an academic project nor a nonprofit could scale it; the company raised $100 million in its earlier round.
- **Scale and spend.** Arena pays for all inference at standard enterprise discounts. Angelopoulos cites about 250 million conversations to date, mid-tens of millions a month, roughly 25% of users working in software, and about half now logged in, with persistent history the main retention driver.
- **How it differs.** He contrasts Arena's organic prompts with Artificial Analysis, which reruns public benchmarks and whose arenas use pre-generated outputs.
- **The Leaderboard Illusion dispute.** Responding to the Cohere-linked paper alleging undisclosed private testing, he says the paper contained factual errors, for example on the open versus closed model sampling ratio, and that pre-release testing under code names (Nano Banana started there) is long-standing and popular.
- **Integrity rules.** Providers cannot pay to be listed or removed; every released model gets a statistically sound score from votes. New occupational and expert categories, Code Arena and video are the roadmap; an API is possible but not a focus.
- **Multimodal value.** Both host and guest say they underrated image generation; Angelopoulos expects multimodal models to be among the most economically valuable, citing marketing and design adoption.
