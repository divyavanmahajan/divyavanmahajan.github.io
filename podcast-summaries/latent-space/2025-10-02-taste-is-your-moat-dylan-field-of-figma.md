## Thesis

Dylan Field argues that when the rate of software creation goes exponential, what wins is brand, point of view, taste and craft. Figma's bet is to lower the floor with Figma Make (prompt to app) while raising the ceiling for designers, and to act as what host Alessio Fanelli calls "the context repository for aesthetics" that coding agents draw from.

## Key points

- **Natural language is a phase.** Field calls the current period the "MS-DOS era of AI" and expects other, more constrained interfaces for exploring latent space to emerge beyond text prompts.
- **Specs, designs and code blur.** Field says the waterfall of PRD, design and code is breaking down; a prototype may replace a PRD. Code Connect maps Figma components one-to-one to code components so the MCP server can hand agents accurate context, though he admits setup is "too much of a pain" today.
- **Beyond the median website.** Asked about AI-generated purple gradients, Field says Figma should help people explore more aesthetic options, not impose one. He hopes for a return to Flash-era experimentation after years of Swiss minimalism.
- **Sceptical of "fast fashion" SaaS.** Field doubts that software becomes disposable. Products like Workday and Rippling encode decades of edge cases that a CIO cannot simply vibe-code away.
- **Feedback and visionary users.** He trains his X feed to surface Figma mentions and recalls an early user test with designer Payam, whose 8-10 page follow-up doc foreshadowed much of the roadmap.
- **Hiring and recruiting.** Field says strong full-stack engineers who like learning do well on Figma's AI products. He took recruiting advice from John Doerr: run your hiring funnel like a sales pipeline.
