## Thesis

Anjney Midha, founder of AMP and teacher of Stanford's Frontier Systems class, argues that the AI build-out wastes compute because the chain from capital to cluster operator is too long to stay aligned. His answer is "output maxing": pool compute across clouds and chips the way electric grids pooled generators, build data centres that visibly benefit their communities, and back teams whose culture survives scarcity.

## Key points

- **Utilisation as a leadership problem.** Midha says node allocation below about 95% should count as an outage, as it did at Google, and best-in-class MFU is 60-70%. Most single-tenant clusters fall short because of compounding misalignment, so he urges iterative bring-ups.
- **AMP as a grid operator.** AMP pools supply from long-standing data centre partners (around 1.3 gigawatts over four years) and demand from labs such as Periodic Labs, guaranteeing base load and allowing spikes. Midha models it on independent system operators like PJM and on the Borg scheduler his co-founders built at Google; he estimates six gigawatts of spike capacity is needed over four years.
- **Net-positive data centres.** Citing General Matter's Scott Nolan, Midha backs adding 50 cents to a $4 GPU-hour and giving it to local communities, and says up to 20% of US data centres this year may face community backlash, while admitting that figure may be overstated.
- **Open standards help new chips.** MatX's Reiner Pope designed around Nvidia's published rack reference architecture so his chips fit existing sites; Midha sees the real bottleneck for chip start-ups as access to upcoming model architectures.
- **Culture over cash.** Midha says Anthropic's coding lead came from four years of preparation under scarcity and a clear priority, while richly funded labs without hardship often fail to ship. He quotes "Culture is not a set of beliefs. It's a set of actions."
- **End-of-life prediction.** He describes his graduate work with Stanford's Nigam Shah on predicting time left for terminal patients, a field he says works technically but is blocked by liability rules, and his next incubation.
