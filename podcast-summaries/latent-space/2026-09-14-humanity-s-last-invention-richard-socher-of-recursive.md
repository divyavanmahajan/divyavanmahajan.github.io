## Thesis

Richard Socher, founder of you.com and now of Recursive, says that in 20 years of AI, every time a learned system replaced a hand-built part of the process, results improved. The next part to automate is AI research itself, which by definition makes AI self-improving. His long-term goal is a "Eureka machine", a superintelligence that makes scientific discoveries, and he argues that regulation should target specific applications, not intelligence or GPUs.

## Key points

- **Regulate uses, not compute.** Socher compares regulating GPUs to slowing the internet to fight illegal content, and says it would require a totalitarian state. He criticises the EU's FLOP-based rules and the frontier labs' push for pacing. He does not expect a hard takeoff, citing physical limits and the many industries that superintelligence won't change much.
- **Reward hacking is the real failure.** He reads the Hugging Face incident as a reward-hacking and red-teaming failure. He calls Anthropic's constitution "mostly marketing" because it lists cyberweapons as a hard constraint that wasn't kept, and points to "rainbow teaming" open-ended methods from Tim Rocktäschel as a better approach.
- **A history of unification.** Socher recounts GloVe and his decaNLP paper, which framed every NLP task as a question to one model. Reviewers rejected it, so his team dropped language modelling from its list of next steps. He argues arXiv and social media now filter research better than gatekeeping reviewers.
- **Recursive's team and early results.** Recursive has eight co-founders, including CTO Josh Tobin, Jeff Clune (Darwin Gödel Machine), Tim Rocktäschel (Genie) and Alexey Dosovitskiy (vision transformer). Its early system beat human efforts on Andrej Karpathy's NanoChat in under two days and topped most of the kernel benchmark entries it tried. Careful reward engineering is the main safeguard.
- **Ten spaces of intelligence.** Socher's forthcoming book describes upper bounds on visual, communication, physical, social, creative and metacognitive intelligence, among others. He argues AI is far from those limits on nearly all of them.
- **Open source as soft power.** He supports a Western open-weight answer to China, calling LLMs soft power much like films.
