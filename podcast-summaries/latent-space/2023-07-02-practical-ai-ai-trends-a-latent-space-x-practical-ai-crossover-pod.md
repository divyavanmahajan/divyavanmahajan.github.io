## Thesis

In this feed-swap crossover, Dan Whitenack, co-host of Practical AI and founder of Prediction Guard, compares notes with swyx and Alessio Fanelli on each show's most popular episodes. Whitenack argues that foundation models have broken data scientists' old train-and-deploy intuition, and that enterprises need a new workflow built on prompting, chaining, structured output, evaluation and, only then, fine-tuning. swyx frames the people doing this work as "AI engineers".

## Key points

- **From MLOps to LLMOps.** Practical AI's most listened-to episode was about Netflix's Metaflow, which Whitenack attributes to interest in the model life cycle. He says ops still matter but now centre on choosing pre-trained models and engineering prompts and chains.
- **Controlling output.** Whitenack built Prediction Guard to let enterprises run models in a compliant way and add structure, validation, self-consistency and factuality checks on top of raw LLM text.
- **Evaluation is unsettled.** Alessio cites the Replit code model episode and Jonathan Frankle's point that benchmarks are mostly multiple choice while production work is open-ended. Whitenack points to model-based evaluators such as Unbabel's COMET; swyx notes that under 1% of Segment Anything's masks were human-generated.
- **Low-resource languages.** Whitenack, formerly at SIL International, highlights grassroots groups such as Masakhane, which build speech and translation tools for African communities that may be oral rather than literate. He worries model performance drops sharply beyond English, Mandarin and a few other languages.
- **Enterprise adoption is shallow.** From his workshops, Whitenack says most enterprises have not integrated LLMs and stop at a single prompt, missing tools like LangChain and LlamaIndex. swyx adds that AI UX, such as GitHub Copilot's inline design, can matter as much as the model.
