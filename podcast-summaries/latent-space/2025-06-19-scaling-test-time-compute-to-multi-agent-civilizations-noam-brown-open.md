## Thesis

Noam Brown, who leads multi-agent research at OpenAI, argues that the reasoning paradigm is still on the trajectory he expected: from o1-preview to o1 to o3, progress has been consistent, and it extends to domains without easily verifiable rewards. He tells developers to expect much of today's scaffolding to be replaced by more capable models, and sees cooperating and competing AIs as a path past the limits of single models.

## Key points

- **Diplomacy and Cicero.** Brown won the World Diplomacy Championship in 2025, crediting what he learned from watching Cicero, the 2022 bot that reached the top 10% of human players on a small 2.7B language model.
- **Verifiability is not required.** Brown points to Deep Research as an existence proof that reasoning models can succeed where there is no easily verifiable metric, as long as people can tell a good report from a mediocre one.
- **System 1 before System 2.** Reasoning only pays off on top of a capable pre-trained model; Brown doubts it would have done much on GPT-2, comparing it to a pigeon thinking hard about chess.
- **Scale washes away scaffolding.** Brown expects harnesses and model routers to disappear as OpenAI moves to a single unified model, and warns developers not to spend six months on things that may be obsolete in six months. Reinforcement fine-tuning is different, because the data stays useful for future models.
- **Origins of reasoning.** In late 2021 Brown told Ilya Sutskever that AGI needed a general reasoning paradigm; he saw conclusive signs of life around October or November 2023, and recalls internal debate over pulling resources from pre-training.
- **Multi-agent and self-play.** The next directions are thinking for hours or days instead of 15 minutes, and multi-agent work. Brown argues self-play converges cleanly only in two-player zero-sum games like Go; outside them, as in Diplomacy or math, picking the objective is hard. He names wall-clock time for serial experiments as the strongest case for long timelines.
