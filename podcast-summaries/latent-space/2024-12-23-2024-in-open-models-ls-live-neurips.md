## Thesis

Luca Soldaini of the Allen Institute for AI (AI2) argues that 2024 was the year open models such as Qwen, DeepSeek and Llama 3 came close to closed frontier models, and the year open training recipes became clear. He warns that the ecosystem is fragile: compute barriers keep rising, open web data is being locked away, and lobbying frames open AI as uniquely dangerous. Sophia Yang of Mistral follows with a recap of Mistral's year of releases.

## Key points

- **OSI definition, with a gap.** Soldaini welcomes the Open Source Initiative's first open source AI definition, which excludes licences like Llama's, but criticises its "sufficiently detailed" data clause as too fuzzy.
- **A compute-rich club.** By his estimate, pre-training research needs 1,000 to 10,000 GPUs and state of the art around 50,000, while post-training can start with eight GPUs.
- **Fully open recipes.** AI2 released OLMoE, Molmo, Tulu 3 and OLMo 2 with data, code, logs and checkpoints, and he points to reuse in both directions, such as OLMo 2 building on DCLM data.
- **Data access is shrinking.** Citing Shayne Longpre's Common Crawl study, he says many sites now block crawling, often through Cloudflare or DNS rather than robots.txt, which favours labs that already have the data.
- **Regulation, his personal view.** Soldaini calls the bio-risk scare a lobbying ploy and says the community "dodged a bullet" on California's SB 1047.
- **Mistral's year.** Yang lists Mistral 3B and 8B, Codestral, Pixtral 12B and Large, Mistral Large 2 and a free Le Chat with canvas, web search and Flux Pro image generation.
