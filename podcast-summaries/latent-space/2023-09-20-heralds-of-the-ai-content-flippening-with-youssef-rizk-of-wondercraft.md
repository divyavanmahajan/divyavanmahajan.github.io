## Thesis

Youssef Rizk, co-founder of Wondercraft, describes his podcast builder as a tool for turning content people already have into other formats, starting with audio. Asked by swyx whether Wondercraft is a thin wrapper around ElevenLabs, Rizk argues that moats come from building the right product for a specific user, and that API providers face the same question. The episode ends with Rizk interviewing swyx about how a human podcaster uses AI tools.

## Key points

- **Product.** Rizk describes Wondercraft as script generation plus ElevenLabs voices, with 28 languages, hosting, show notes, pronunciation fixes and video clips. He announces a Zapier integration that turns a newsletter RSS feed into a draft episode, and AI dubbing checked by professional translators, without lip sync.
- **Validate fast after a pivot.** After his first startup, Moonshot, failed, Rizk shipped a one-page prototype with no authentication on a single EC2 instance. Once it spread on Twitter, a hurried Stripe paywall at $50 a month brought in about $3K in one day.
- **The moat question.** Rizk says general APIs are not built for each use case. An application can pick its text-to-speech engine and add tooling around it, and OpenAI's own moat is mostly that it "paid for the training bill".
- **Daily formats win.** Rizk credits the daily Hacker News Recap for reaching the top thirty tech podcasts on Spotify. swyx notes that daily shows rank higher on download-driven charts.
- **Disclose AI content.** Rizk says AI-generated shows should say so, because being found out later reads as deception, and points to YouTube's new monetisation rules. Wondercraft plans to watermark once there is a standard.
- **A human podcaster's workflow.** swyx describes three Latent Space formats: founder interviews, breaking-news Twitter Spaces (edited in Descript from about two hours to one) and evergreen fundamentals. Show notes come from a 100-line Python script that sends the transcript to Anthropic.
