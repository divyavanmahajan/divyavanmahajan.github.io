## Thesis

Martin Casado and Sarah Wang of a16z tell swyx and Alessio Fanelli that AI model companies blur old lines between venture and growth, and between infrastructure and apps. Casado raises a "bitter lesson" for startups: if a frontier lab can keep raising more capital than everyone building on its API, it can absorb their markets whether or not it reaches AGI. Wang adds that growth only holds while money keeps turning into capability gains that customers pay for.

## Key points

- **Hybrid rounds.** Wang says model companies need growth-scale resources from day one, with compute deals, sometimes with equity for compute, negotiated months into a company's life. Casado argues circular funding differs from the dotcom fibre glut because there are "no dark GPUs".
- **Borrowing against the future.** Casado says labs look gross-margin positive measured against the last model's training cost, but negative counting the next run, which he expects must eventually slow.
- **Custom ASICs per model.** Casado repeats his estimate that a $1 billion training run justifies a custom chip, since saving 20% on inference would cover a roughly $200 million tape-out. Timeline, not money, is now the constraint, and the hosts point to OpenAI's Broadcom deal.
- **Where a16z invests.** Casado calls boring enterprise software underinvested, noting a company growing 5x in a large market is still a great return. The pair mostly pass on robotics because hardware companies end up vertical. Wang says their model bets rest on "N of one" founders such as Ilya and Thinking Machines' Mira and John, and on specialists like ElevenLabs.
- **Talent and noise.** They say Meta's 2025 hiring spree lifted pay across the market, with some L5 engineers offered tens of millions. Wang recounts the Character investment and Noam Shazeer's move back to Google, and both defend Thinking Machines. Casado warns that gossip on X is rarely accurate.
- **Cursor and World Labs.** The guests praise Cursor for building its own models from the app layer down. Casado also codes on SparkJS, an open-source Gaussian-splat renderer for World Labs, and values 3D generation as cutting scene costs by orders of magnitude.
