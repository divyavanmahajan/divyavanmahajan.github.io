## Thesis

Akshay Nathan, who leads core product engineering for OpenAI's productivity team, explains why OpenAI built ChatGPT Work on the same harness as Codex. He argues that once agents get a flexible computer environment they become useful far beyond software development, and that AI is blurring job boundaries, so the product should let users move freely between coding and other work rather than pick an experience by role.

## Key points

- **Origin in internal adoption.** Nathan says non-developers at OpenAI, in strategic finance and marketing, took up Codex and were proud of it. That prompted the "Merge" of Codex into ChatGPT, launched as ChatGPT Work.
- **One harness, two UIs.** The harness is shared; Codex mode exposes Git state and diffs, while Work hides them and applies different sandbox defaults. Plugins are unified across both. The classic ChatGPT chat harness remains, optimized for latency and personality.
- **Artifacts and Sites.** Nathan highlights improved spreadsheets, documents and hosted Sites. He says OpenAI's corporate finance team now shares monthly reports as Sites instead of decks and spreadsheets, and the model-picker slider was prototyped in a Site.
- **Defaults over knobs.** He advises most users to stick with the default model configuration, reserving Ultra and multi-agent setups for complex or parallel tasks and Goal for work with verifiable progress.
- **From developers to everyone.** Nathan describes a sequence: developers first, then general knowledge work, then personal life. He uses ChatGPT Work for meal plans; a colleague moved workout and meal tracking from OpenClaw. The product passed 10 million users and is currently only for paid users.
- **Measuring productivity.** Old proxies such as lines of code or token counts are breaking down. Nathan suggests managers track the number and quality of "at-bats": idea, build, feedback, validation, next idea.
