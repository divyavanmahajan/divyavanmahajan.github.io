## Thesis

This weekend compilation stitches together swyx and Alessio's recent outside appearances. On NLW's AI Breakdown they use the "four wars" framework (data, GPU rich/poor, multimodality, RAG/ops) to argue that compute alone does not build a business and that narrow, vertical agents are what work today. Dylan Patel of SemiAnalysis adds a skeptical view of Groq's economics, and Capital One's Milind Naphade argues that the hard part of AI is the last mile inside regulated industries.

## Key points

- **GPU-rich is not enough.** The hosts read Inflection's move to Microsoft (after raising $1.3 billion, with about 22,000 H100s) and departures from Stability AI as signs of a consolidation wave, not an AI winter.
- **Data and Claude 3.** swyx points to Google's roughly $60 million non-exclusive Reddit data deal; Alessio says Claude 3 Opus beat GPT-4 for his podcast summarisation tooling, raising the bar for GPT-5.
- **Vertical agents.** Alessio notes Auto-GPT went from zero to about 125,000 GitHub stars in six weeks and then plateaued; narrow products such as Brightwave for financial research do better. swyx cites Klarna replacing about 700 support agents.
- **The AI engineer.** At Thursday Nights in AI, swyx lays out three types of AI engineer (AI-enhanced, AI product engineer, non-human coder) and argues long context will not kill RAG because of cost, latency and debuggability.
- **Groq's economics.** Patel estimates Groq's API runs seven to ten times below break-even, using 576 chips with no off-chip memory. He also argues edge inference makes little economic sense compared with the cloud.
- **Applied research at a bank.** Naphade, formerly at IBM Research and CTO of Nvidia's Metropolis, says Capital One built an applied-research job family because finance has a low tolerance for error.
