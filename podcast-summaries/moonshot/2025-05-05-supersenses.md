## Thesis

Astro Teller argues that Google Glass was a prototype and learning platform rather than a failed product: the Explorer programme taught X that Glass was too early for consumers and more useful for workers. Those lessons on timing, knowing who a device is for and making it worth wearing fed into IyO, Jason Rugolo's natural-language audio wearable, which Teller believes is "the right amount too early".

## Key points

- **Prototype every month.** Isabelle Olsson describes a rule of shipping a new internal Glass prototype monthly and wearing it, and sanding down a smartphone camera part to prove a curved custom component could work. Joe Sargent says the Design Kitchen built thousands of prototypes, starting from safety glasses with a compute backpack.
- **The Explorer programme.** About 10,000 people, including hairdressers, firefighters and nurses, received Glass. Ivy Ross recalls use cases such as surgeons teaching through their own view and an Explorer's emotion-reading app for children with autism. Teller admits marketing made it look finished, which led to hype, privacy concerns and social backlash; sales paused in early 2015.
- **Enterprise relaunch.** Ivo Stivoric says a small team went from chip to production with Glass V2 in 11 months, with pre-sales from industrial and medical customers, before Google took it back; Glass Enterprise Edition launched in 2019.
- **Machine-mediated sound.** Rugolo, prompted by Google Brain's 2017 work, concluded that natural-language computing needs an ear-worn device that does not degrade hearing. Outward microphones feed an onboard computer; an early demo separated two people reading aloud at the same time, in real time at about 30 milliseconds.
- **Designing IyO.** Rugolo chose a visible, custom-fit device rather than a hidden one, argues brooches and speakerphones fail on privacy, and plans a "Babel" translation app at launch. A Wi-Fi version was targeted for August or September, with LTE later.
