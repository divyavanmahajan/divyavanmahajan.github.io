## Thesis

Astro Teller frames both moonshots as being about visibility. The Chronicle founders argue that security defenders lose because they throw logs away or cannot search them quickly, and that Google-scale storage and analysis gives defenders an advantage. The Chorus team makes the same case for physical goods: cheap printable sensors that "chirp" their location can expose waste, theft and delays across supply chains.

## Key points

- **The origin.** Mike Wiacek describes a 2015 FBI briefing where the bureau handed over 20 bad domain names that were six months old, while Google's systems already showed what attackers were using that morning. Sergey Brin then suggested making it a moonshot, code-named Lantern.
- **Search, not just storage.** The team compares the industry to the FBI's 1940s paper fingerprint archive. One company they visited needed 17 minutes for a single log query; others deleted data after 90 days to save money. Chronicle later held over an exabyte.
- **Who attacks.** The guests describe three tiers of attacker: notoriety-seekers, organised crime with "org charts" and bonuses, and patient nation states. They say attackers now mostly log in with stolen credentials instead of breaking through a perimeter.
- **Launch and acquisition.** Backstory launched at RSA and drew around 6,000 leads. Google Cloud then acquired Chronicle, and Shapor Naghibzadeh says it is now the core of Google's security operations platform alongside Mandiant and Wiz. Stephen Gillett went on to become CEO of Verily.
- **Chorus.** The Chorus guests cite figures such as 20% of manufacturing spend wasted and nurses losing hours per shift looking for things like infusion pumps. Steven Chen explains the move from a phone-sized GPS tracker to the Seeker, a printable Bluetooth label just over a millimetre thick that is picked up by customers' existing Wi-Fi access points and scanners.
- **End-to-end tracking.** Chorus aims to follow goods across locations, transport modes and changes of ownership, including cold-chain monitoring and automatic inventory counts. One customer's 28-day transit fell below seven days after a trailer was found idle for two weeks.
