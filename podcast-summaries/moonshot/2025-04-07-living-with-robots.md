## Thesis

Astro Teller presents two X projects built on the same bet: robots become useful when they learn through AI instead of being hand-programmed for every task. Hans Peter Brondmo and Catie Cuan of Everyday Robots argue that ageing populations make helper robots a necessity, and that how robots move and sound decides whether people accept them. Wendy Tan White of Intrinsic applies the idea to factories, aiming to make building a robotic application as easy as publishing a website.

## Key points

- **Demographics as the driver.** Brondmo, motivated by his mother's late-stage Parkinson's care, cites falling birth rates and forecasts that China's 1.3 billion people could shrink to 730 million by 2100, with around 40% over 65.
- **Three brains.** Brondmo describes a cloud simulator that ran about 240 million robot instances in 2022, an on-robot "action brain", and a human in the loop who gives quick spoken hints via a language model. Grasping success in the "arm farm" rose from 7% to about 70%, later above 90%; about 180 robots sorted trash and cleaned tables in Google buildings.
- **Choreography and sound.** Cuan argues robots need non-threatening motion, such as adjusting their path early in a hallway. Her "music mode" mapped each joint to an instrument; a study found people rated robots as more likable, intelligent and safe with it on.
- **Jobs and fear.** Brondmo says fear-based narratives shape views of robots. James Manyika cites an International Labour Organization study finding augmentation effects about six times larger than displacement. Everyday Robots merged into DeepMind's robotics work in 2023.
- **Intrinsic and Flowstate.** Tan White says traditional production lines are rigid and costly to reprogram. Flowstate lets users design robot cells visually in simulation, assemble skills like Lego, and share them in a marketplace, positioning Intrinsic as "the Android of robotics". A prototype with IKEA explored robots assembling flat-pack furniture.
