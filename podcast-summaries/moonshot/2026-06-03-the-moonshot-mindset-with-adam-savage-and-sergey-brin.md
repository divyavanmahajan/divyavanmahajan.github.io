## Thesis

In the season two finale, Adam Savage interviews Astro Teller about how X systematises radical innovation. Teller's case is that most moonshot ideas fail, so the organisation must reward people for finding out quickly which ideas are worth pursuing rather than for making their own project survive. In a second segment, Sergey Brin reviews X's projects and argues that AI planning must assume capabilities keep moving.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Teller says founders Larry and Sergey wanted a unit separate from Google to find new problems before the company calcified. Sebastian Thrun co-founded Google X around the early self-driving work, with Teller as his "first mate".
- **Kill ideas on purpose.** Teller says X has started and killed about 2,000 ideas in 16 years, keeps a searchable record of why each died, and reuses people, patents and code from dead projects in new ones. Techno-economics, such as levelised cost of energy, is tested from the first conversation.
- **Train the monkey first.** Teller's monkey-on-a-pedestal story means tackling the riskiest part of a problem first. He told an electric-aircraft team to crash five wings, and he runs "bad idea brainstorms" to lower the pressure on people.
- **AI as electricity.** Teller notes that Google Brain, and with it the transformer and TPUs, came out of X. Still, he tells teams to see how far into a pitch they can get before mentioning AI. X keeps 23 "axioms" about the future, for example that landfill will have positive value by 2040.
- **Brin's retrospective.** Brin calls Glass premature but still believes in the form factor. He says Wing now runs routine deliveries in Australia and Texas and is ramping up in North Carolina, and that Waymo took far more work than people assume. He names Brain, now part of Google DeepMind, as X's most important project.
- **Next moonshots.** Teller would like to see work on education, batteries 10 times denser, and ways to give people purpose, community and mastery apart from pay. Brin suggests self-replicating Von Neumann machines, and the two note that the techno-economics may be the hard part.

## Quotes

> "Radical innovation is mostly about failure and learning. It is not a story of success where some genius just had the right idea." — Astro Teller

> "I think we're going to have to adjust to adjusting." — Sergey Brin
