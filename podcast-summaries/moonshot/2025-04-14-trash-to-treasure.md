## Thesis

Astro Teller presents two X moonshots that treat waste as a lack of information. Emily Ma's Delta project built shared data standards so surplus food can reach food banks before it spoils, and found its biggest impact as open-source software rather than a business. Rey Banatao's circularity team identifies plastics at the molecular level so material headed for landfill can feed chemical recycling.

## Key points

- **Scale of food waste.** Ma says about 40% of US food is never eaten, and that four times as many edible calories are grown worldwide as are consumed. Food waste is the largest source of landfill methane.
- **An air traffic control system for surplus.** Inspired by Dana Yoast, who brokered rejected produce trucks at the Nogales border crossing by phone, Delta standardised how food, location and quality are described, then piloted data sharing with Kroger and Feeding America on Google Cloud.
- **From product to open source.** Without a profitable business model, the project moved to Google's philanthropy arm in 2020 and became Open Product Recovery, a network of nodes now used by parts of Feeding America and the FarmLink project. Mark Mollenkopf of Feeding America says time is the main enemy and sees predictive analytics as the next step.
- **Molecular-level sorting.** Banatao says less than 10% of plastics ever made have been recycled. His system combines infrared spectroscopy, cameras and AI models at millisecond speed to remove additives that would spoil pyrolysis feedstock. Emma Lingle says it can sort black plastics, about 20% of the stream, and berry clamshells at a facility in Salem, Oregon.
- **Results and next targets.** The team delivered a tanker of pyrolysis oil made entirely from landfill-bound plastic and now aims for tens to hundreds of tanker loads. Banatao sees its data informing extended producer responsibility laws, and lists textiles and e-waste as later targets.
