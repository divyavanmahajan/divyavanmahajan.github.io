## Thesis

Sabin Roman, Linear's first engineering manager and a seven-year Uber veteran, tells Gergely Orosz that Linear prefers creativity over process when building product, relying on principles rather than guidebooks. He credits this to being a small startup and to hiring passionate, product-minded engineers whose judgment can replace much of the structure a company like Uber needs.

## Key points

- **No internal email.** Roman says he sent perhaps 20 or 30 emails in three months; Linear runs on Slack for urgent questions and Linear itself for structured, searchable discussion. The company has over 60 people, 25 engineers and, now, two product managers.
- **Goalies and zero bugs.** Two engineers per week triage customer reports and sit in support channels. Under the zero-bug policy the official SLA is a week, the unofficial one a day. Roman says clearing the initial hundreds of bugs was hard, but the steady state is about two issues per person.
- **Hiring for product sense.** Coding and architecture exercises are simplified pieces of real Linear code, followed by a one-week work trial on a greenfield project. Roman's manager interview covers motivation, product and technical experience.
- **Remote trade-offs.** Roman sees more focus and autonomy, and counters weaker team belonging with weekly demos, two or three offsites a year and ad hoc trips. He admits remote is harder for managers but argues it makes the team's lives better.
- **How a project runs.** The project page rework had two engineers, a designer, a customer support person and product input. Roman describes iterative "talk, do, show" cycles behind feature flags, then de-scoping, beta milestones and GA release with no A/B testing, taking about four months.
- **Uber contrast and no levels.** Orosz recalls Uber's Helix rider-app rewrite as an all-hands pressure cooker. Roman says about 40% of his Uber manager job does not apply at Linear, which has no titles; founder Tuomas Artman asks what he has done to make Linear a better product.
