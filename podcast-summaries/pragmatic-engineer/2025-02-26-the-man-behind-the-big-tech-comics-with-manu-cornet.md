## Thesis

Manu Cornet, who spent 14 years as a Google engineer and later worked at Twitter, walks Gergely Orosz through his comics. He argues that many of Google's visible problems, such as confusing product names and frequent shutdowns, are side effects of a bottom-up culture that treated employees well. He also traces how both Google and Twitter moved away from the ideals that first drew him in.

## Key points

- **The org chart comic.** Cornet drew the 2011 org charts of Amazon, Google, Facebook, Apple, Microsoft and Oracle and almost did not publish it because he no longer found it funny. The intended punchline was Oracle's large legal department, but the Microsoft panel drew the most attention.
- **Naming and siloing.** Cornet calls Google's naming confusion the bad consequence of a good culture: engineers start competing efforts and there is little top-down pressure to consolidate. Gergely recalls Uber's integration as Google Wallet became Android Pay and then Google Pay. Cornet says Google is now becoming more siloed, like Apple.
- **Customers versus employees.** On his "guns and roses" comic, Cornet says Google treated employees very well but offered little customer service. Gergely contrasts Amazon's customer focus, citing Steve Yegge's experience at Grab, and notes that Google's SREs take most of the on-call load.
- **20% time and Stadia.** Cornet says Gmail and Google News began as 20% projects, which became less encouraged over time. He agrees with Gergely that this may be linked to shutdowns, and suggests internal testing or labelled experiments like Gmail Labs. His Stadia comic, drawn on launch day, pointed to the risk of the Google graveyard, and a later one blamed network effects.
- **Connecting with users.** Cornet sought out user feedback on Gmail forums and on Twitter, where he offered to help debug a bug Gergely had reported.
- **Twitter under Musk.** Cornet says roughly 80% of staff were eventually let go. He thinks some downsizing was overdue but describes the process as random. He also notes he was in a more secure position than colleagues with visa or health insurance concerns.

## Quotes
