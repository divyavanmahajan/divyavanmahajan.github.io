## Thesis

Elizabeth Stone, Netflix's CTO, argues that Netflix's culture of high talent density, autonomy and being "unusually responsible" is a means to excellent work, not an end in itself. She describes how engineer-led teams built Netflix Live in 18 months, why the company adds only light guardrails as it scales, and why it skips formal performance reviews.

## Key points

- **Scale and pitch to play.** Stone says Netflix captures more than a trillion events a day and runs Open Connect, its own CDN, in 6,000 locations across more than 175 countries. Engineering spans the whole lifecycle from greenlighting a title to playback, which she compares to a CI/CD pipeline "times thousands".
- **Live, learned the hard way.** From a Chris Rock special in March 2023 to Paul vs. Tyson in November 2024, which peaked at 65 million concurrent streams, teams self-organised. Stone describes a makeshift control room, a 40-50 page if-then launch plan, and next-morning memos that fed five weeks of work before two flawless NFL Christmas Day games.
- **Guardrails, not gates.** For live, Netflix introduced service tiers, higher testing bars for Tier 0 and Tier 1 systems, and quiet periods, but Stone says the company keeps dialling these back so other teams are not slowed down.
- **No formal performance reviews.** Netflix relies on continuous candid feedback, an annual 360 as a safety net, compensation reviews tied to "personal top of market", promotion evaluations and the Keeper Test, which Stone says applies to managers too.
- **Pragmatic AI use.** Stone says AI coding tools help most with prototyping, documentation, large migrations and anomaly detection and response, supported by Gen AI champions. She calls it "not a silver bullet".
- **Levels, new grads and open source.** After about 25 years of senior-only hiring, Netflix added levels and now hires early-career engineers while also adding staff and principal roles. Orosz cites a report that about one in five Netflix engineers works on open source; Stone points to nine Emmys for encoding work and 60% lower bitrates for the same or better quality.
