## Thesis

Navdeep Singh, creator of NeetCode, tells Gergely Orosz that data structures and algorithms interviews survive not because they predict job performance but because companies have never had a reliable way to evaluate engineers and the format scales. He argues that the real value of hard preparation is the habit of going deep, communicating a plan and weighing tradeoffs, and that effort and agency become the differentiator when AI can produce code, designs and answers on demand.

## Key points

- **Why interviews are sticky.** Singh says changing the process at large companies means retraining thousands of reluctant interviewers; Meta's AI-assisted coding interviews show how hard that is. He notes Google has largely returned to onsite interviews because remote formats are vulnerable to AI cheating tools.
- **Amazon versus Google.** Singh quit Amazon's Alexa org after two months, describing an intense team where engineers committed code at 3am. At Google his habit of not asking questions read as independence, and he was promoted from L3 to L4 in about a year. Orosz recalls Amazon's 6% unregretted attrition target.
- **Shortcuts as a business decision.** Singh replaced a $3,000-a-month code execution service with his own, built with AI help in two to three days and now costing about $200 a month. It has a memory leak he has chosen not to fix; he runs four instances instead.
- **AI as refactoring help.** NeetCode runs on Angular, Firebase, Google Cloud and TypeScript. Singh says his early code was sloppy and AI now cleans it up, which he sees as validating those earlier shortcuts.
- **Effort over polish.** His $2,500 site redesign contest disappointed him: most entries were AI-generated and their authors could not explain their design choices.
- **Hiring for agency.** Singh says his best recent hire is a student who has not yet graduated and learns any new domain within a week. He defends his video arguing that some people should give up on tech careers, framing it as a warning against cheating through study.
