## Thesis

Addy Osmani, who spent 14 years at Google on Chrome, DevTools, Core Web Vitals and later Gemini and Cloud AI, tells Gergely Orosz that agents have made far more possible, but they also tempt engineers to stop understanding their own systems. He argues that engineers stay valuable by keeping that understanding, by being accountable for what ships, and by building skills beyond engineering.

## Key points

- **From browser to DevTools.** Osmani built a web browser as a teenager in rural Ireland to speed up dial-up browsing. He later created TodoMVC, which led Apple's WebKit team to work with him on the Speedometer benchmark. He says DevTools kept pace with the web, though memory debugging has barely moved forward.
- **Director at Google.** He went from L4 in DevRel to Director (L8), leading an org of around 45-50 people. The biggest change was owning one of Google's top yearly goals and reporting on it every week or two. He also saw directors, VPs and SVPs building weekend projects with AI tools.
- **Cognitive debt and surrender.** Osmani defines cognitive debt as the erosion of your memory and understanding of a problem, and cognitive surrender as accepting the AI's answer as your own. With 20-30 sub-agents in Claude Code or Codex, he no longer reads every trajectory. Instead he reads or asks for a summary of decisions, and has agents log what they learned ("mutual amplification").
- **Loops and software factories.** He describes loop engineering as building the system that prompts, tests and verifies. Without guardrails on blast radius and quality, he calls it "a recipe for disaster". His own app feeds bug reports, analytics and logs into prioritisation.
- **Accountability as the job.** Osmani says an engineer's advantage is taste and judgement. He cites Chromium's OWNERS files, where named people answer for code they did not write.
- **Writing and careers.** He uses deep-research agents and compares his drafts with model drafts. His career advice is that roles are unbundling: learn product, evangelism and go-to-market, and "don't just be an engineer".

## Quotes

> "An agent can tell you if a thing looks correct, if it's matching a spec. It doesn't necessarily mean it can tell you what's good." — Addy Osmani

> "Every time that we've made it easier to create software, we've exponentially created more of it." — Addy Osmani
