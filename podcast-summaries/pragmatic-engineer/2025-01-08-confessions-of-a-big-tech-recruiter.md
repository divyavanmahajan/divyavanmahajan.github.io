## Thesis

Blake Stockman, who recruited for 12 years at Google, Meta, Uber, Flexport, Y Combinator and his own agency before leaving to study law, tells his former Uber colleague Gergely Orosz that recruiting works best as a partnership between recruiter and hiring manager. For candidates, his advice is to be authentic with recruiters but remember that they work for the company, so let the company make the first offer.

## Key points

- **Whose side the recruiter is on.** Stockman says a recruiter's job is to steward the company, so a candidate who names $100,000 against a $110,000-$140,000 range will likely get $110,000. Still, he says he would go out of his way for candidates he believed in.
- **Calibration and talent maps.** At Uber in Amsterdam, Stockman and Orosz met weekly. For the payments team they mapped companies with relevant domain experience and strong engineering, then planned outreach. A recruiting trip to Brazil hired engineers who, Orosz says, are now senior staff engineers.
- **How decisions get made.** Stockman contrasts Google's single, removed hiring pipeline with Uber's recruiters tied to specific teams. He says larger companies add layers of committees, but the hiring manager should own the decision, and only about 20% of hires were unanimous yeses. Orosz recalls Uber teams losing unfilled headcount at year end, which pushed them towards hires made in a hurry.
- **Negotiating.** Stockman says almost every offer has room to negotiate, pay transparency laws in California, New York and Illinois help, and multiple offers are the strongest lever. At Flexport, where offer acceptance started at 20%, he trained recruiters to explain equity, strike price and valuation.
- **Finding startups.** He recommends YC's WorkAtAStartup.com and VC portfolio pages over LinkedIn, which he says charges companies thousands of dollars per posting.
- **LinkedIn and gratitude.** Stockman advises listing what you built, your technologies and your domain, and thanking recruiters, recalling a Brazil hire who told him he had changed his family's life.

## Quotes

> "Any negotiating 101 will tell you not to give salary expectations upfront because that's where people will anchor." — Blake Stockman

> "Recruiting can't do anything on its own, and the manager is very, very limited in what they can do on their own." — Blake Stockman
