## Thesis

Quinn Slack, co-founder and CEO of Sourcegraph, says he regrets many of his 2021 decisions: the company ran with a "peacetime" mindset, started projects that should not have made the cut and hired people drawn by the rocket-ship story rather than a love of code. Today Sourcegraph is more focused, ships many times a day and expects engineers to see how their work creates business value. On AI, Slack argues for automating developer toil in small increments rather than promising full replacement.

## Key points

- **Lessons from the boom.** Slack says revenue grew more than 30x during the pandemic years, and Uber, one of the first customers, signed five years after founding. Hiring Steve Yegge as head of engineering brought a faster, higher-standards culture.
- **Bottom-up automation.** Slack compares fully autonomous agents to concept cars with no steering wheel. He prefers handing AI small tasks such as updating the changelog, and says AI must be checked against ground truth (type checks, tests, deploys), not its own review.
- **Limits of today's tools.** He says AI writes new code better than it understands existing codebases, and predicts more investment in very fast builds so AI can iterate. He sees new grads as more fluent with AI than senior engineers.
- **Job fair, then stable teams.** To pivot quickly to Cody, Sourcegraph had people regroup every month or two around stack-ranked priorities. Slack says it worked as a shock for about six months but hurt ownership and camaraderie, so long-lived teams returned.
- **Zone-based pay.** A couple of months before the recording, Sourcegraph (about 180 people) moved from location-independent to zone-based pay. Slack says the old model created odd incentives and hiring limits. Orosz knows of no company above 200 people that keeps it.
- **A coding CEO.** Slack still commits code daily, wishes he had listened more to co-founder Beyang Liu, and spends his best time with customers and the product.
