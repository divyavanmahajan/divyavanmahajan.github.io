## Thesis

Peter Walker, Head of Insights at Carta, walks Gergely Orosz through Carta data on US venture-backed startups. His argument is that headline VC dollars hide a thinner market: fewer companies raise, teams are smaller, the growth bar is higher, and AI is starting to reduce hiring. Both advise engineers to evaluate a startup the way an investor would.

## Key points

- **Fewer rounds, concentrated money.** Walker says seed and Series A rounds on Carta averaged 7.4 per day in 2025, about half the 2021 level, while a few companies such as OpenAI and xAI raise billions. He adds that VCs who once wanted 100% annual growth now look for 200-300%.
- **Hiring and team size.** Monthly hires at Carta companies fell from 73,000 in January 2022 to 40,000 in 2023 and 32,000 in 2024, with roughly 20,000 expected for January 2025. Series A headcount dropped from about 20-22 to about 15. Walker attributes the early decline to funding and the later one increasingly to AI.
- **ARR per FTE.** Walker calls revenue per employee the metric VCs now ask for earlier. Citing Silicon Valley Bank data, median ARR at Series A rose from about $1.3 million in 2021 to nearly $3 million in 2024, and about $7 million at the 75th percentile.
- **Bridge rounds, down rounds and graduation.** Orosz notes priced bridge rounds that led to a Series A fell from about 33% in 2020 to 8% in 2022. Walker expects 25-30% of seed companies to reach Series A, and says the gap between rounds has stretched from 18-24 months to two and a half to three years.
- **Solo founders.** Solo-founded companies passed a third of 2024 Carta startups, but only about 17% of VC-funded ones. Walker lists VC concerns: key-person risk and doubts about a founder who cannot recruit a co-founder.
- **Equity for engineers.** Walker gives medians of 1.5% for a founding engineer and 0.25% for advisors (5,000 advisors studied), explains 1x liquidation preferences, and suggests asking about revenue, growth, terms and the time since the last raise.
