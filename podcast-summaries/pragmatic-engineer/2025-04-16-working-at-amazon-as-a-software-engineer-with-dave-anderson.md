## Thesis

Dave Anderson, who spent over 12 years at Amazon as an engineering manager and later director, tells Gergely Orosz that Amazon is a rigid behemoth at corporate level but gives individual dev teams near-total control. That autonomy, combined with ownership of oncall, frugality and a written-document culture, produces engineers who adapt well to startups, though it also leaves pockets of badly run teams.

## Key points

- **Levels and promotions.** Anderson describes levels from L4 (entry) to L10 (Distinguished Engineer or VP), with no L9. Promotions require a multi-page narrative and endorsements from people at the next level; he says each step gets roughly exponentially harder, and managers feel pressure to grow headcount to justify promotion.
- **Bar raisers.** Loops usually have five interviewers, one a bar raiser with around 100 or more interviews who can veto but not approve a hire. Debriefs start with everyone reading the notes, and the bar raiser keeps the discussion fair. Anderson says Amazon does not care which language candidates code in.
- **Oncall and outages.** Teams that write code support it in production. Anderson insists a team with frequent pages must be allowed to stop feature work and fix the cause. Orosz recalls an ex-Amazon director at Uber joining a routine top-severity outage call, which reflected Amazon's habit of VPs joining SEV1s.
- **Frugality and URA.** Anderson links frugality partly to thin fulfillment-centre margins, while mocking "frupidity" such as engineers taking departing interns' monitors. He says the 6% to 10% least-effective target mostly bites in organisations of about 50 or more people, and advises anyone receiving negative feedback to transfer teams quickly.
- **Team autonomy.** Teams pick language, process and tooling; one built an important middleware service in Lisp, then had to rewrite it after its two authors left.
- **Early retirement.** Anderson and his wife, both at Amazon, saved about 85% of their income; his Scarlet Ink newsletter now covers more than their spending.
