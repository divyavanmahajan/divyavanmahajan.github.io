## Thesis

Phil Trammell tells Epoch AI researcher Anson Ho that economic theory is mostly useful for showing where intuitions about AI and growth can fail, not for deducing the future. He questions the Jones semi-endogenous growth model, task-based automation models such as GATE, and real GDP as a measure. He still thinks an order-of-magnitude larger economy within five decades is more likely than not.

## Key points

- **Limits of the Jones model.** Trammell argues the model wrongly lets research progress grow without limit as research inputs scale. Parallel work runs into latency between minds, a bottleneck that bigger AI systems could ease, so the model can be wrong in either direction.
- **Task framing.** He notes task-based models emerged right after the O*NET database appeared in 1997. Using an example from OpenAI's Pamela Mishkin (writing an email before a related Zoom call), he argues that automating single tasks can have no effect, so impacts may come later and more suddenly than forecast. Larger integrated AI minds could also produce more growth than these models predict.
- **Better forecasting data.** Trammell prefers extrapolating from real automation, such as METR's task-length study and the Anthropic Economic Index, over interpolating towards an assumed compute threshold, which he calls "totally made up".
- **GDP is slippery.** New goods follow a hump in GDP share, so a Baumol-like effect could keep measured growth low even after full automation. He also argues that chain-weighted real GDP depends on the order in which things happen.
- **Detecting a singularity.** Bringing William Nordhaus's 2021 indicators up to date, he finds them still flat. Network-adjusted capital shares computed from BEA input-output tables, semiconductors included (around 50-50), are flat too.
- **Increasing returns to scale.** Trammell speculates that one Jupiter-sized brain could be worth far more than two halves, which would point to larger gains from trade, possibly a more peaceful world, and more risk tolerance for very long-term investors.
