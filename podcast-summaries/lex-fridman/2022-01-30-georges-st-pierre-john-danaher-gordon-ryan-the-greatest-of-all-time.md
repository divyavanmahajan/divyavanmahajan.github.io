## Thesis

Coach John Danaher argues that in a mature sport the edge comes from finding undervalued skills, such as leg locks or Georges St-Pierre's "shoot boxing", and that confidence is built physically through repeated success in the gym. St-Pierre and Gordon Ryan agree, adding that the right balance of confidence and fear, and continual learning, keep champions at the top.

## Key points

- **Undervalued skills.** Danaher compares sport to fashion: leg locks were long neglected in jiu-jitsu, he pushed them, and Ryan says learning them after repeatedly losing to Eddie Cummings changed his career. St-Pierre says he succeeded by going "left" when everyone went right.
- **Confidence and fear.** St-Pierre says his loss to Matt Hughes came from too little confidence and his loss to Matt Serra from too much. Danaher refuses to use reverse psychology and only praises what works against good opponents in training.
- **Trash talk and emotion.** Ryan says he talks trash because critics attack even the polite St-Pierre, and describes his feud with Andre Galvao. Danaher argues most viewers lack technical understanding and relate to fights through emotion, with Conor McGregor the master of this.
- **Diet and strength.** Danaher says he has never seen diet measurably improve performance in athletes under 30, citing Travis Stevens' Olympic silver on McDonald's and candy. He argues strength gains hit diminishing returns while new skills, like Garry Tonon's guillotine, keep paying off. St-Pierre describes three-to-five-day water fasts four times a year.
- **MMA versus grappling.** Danaher says time to apply technique is the key difference: MMA is unforgiving and stand-up oriented, grappling forgiving and ground oriented. He calls St-Pierre the greatest innovator in MMA takedowns and rates the rear naked choke the highest-percentage submission.
- **GSP vs Khabib and advice.** Danaher calls a prime St-Pierre against Khabib Nurmagomedov a match that could go either way, with St-Pierre ahead standing. For young people, Ryan stresses talent plus enjoyment, St-Pierre a competent and trusted team and investing in yourself, and Danaher focus on one or two skills.
