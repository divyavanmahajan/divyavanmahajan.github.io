## Thesis

Jeremi Suri, a historian at UT Austin and author of The Impossible Presidency, tells Lex Fridman that the presidency has changed beyond recognition since Lincoln, and now concentrates more power in one person than is healthy for a democracy. He argues that great leaders such as Lincoln, Washington and FDR led by listening, storytelling and empathy, and that power should be held for a limited time, as a trust rather than a possession.

## Key points

- **Lincoln as the greatest president.** Suri names Abraham Lincoln, citing Tolstoy's account of peasants in the Caucasus who admired him. He says Lincoln, with two years of schooling, turned politics into an art of opportunity and persuasion.
- **How the office changed.** Suri points to three shifts: presidents can now talk to the public directly, they live under a microscope, and they command military and economic power he calls "inhuman". He argues structure pushed Barack Obama towards using force despite his intentions.
- **Giving up power.** Following historian Garry Wills, Suri says Washington gained authority by surrendering command and leaving after two terms. He contrasts this with Vladimir Putin, who he says has stayed too long and fears what happens after leaving office.
- **FDR's empathy and its limits.** Suri credits Franklin Roosevelt's fireside chats, such as his March 1933 banking explanation, with creating a common mission. He also criticises FDR for not bombing the rail lines to Auschwitz by 1944 and for interning 120,000 Japanese Americans.
- **Kissinger and realpolitik.** Suri, who interviewed Henry Kissinger about 12 or 13 times, describes him as obsessed with power after fleeing Nazi Germany. He credits Kissinger with the opening to China and Middle East diplomacy after the 1973 war, but argues realpolitik becomes self-defeating when power loses its purpose. He says Kissinger is not a war criminal but misused military power.
- **Cold War and communism.** Suri says there has never been communism in the United States, and that Cold War America combined capitalism with high taxes and large public investment.

## Quotes
