## Thesis

Marc Raibert, founder of Boston Dynamics and now Executive Director of the Boston Dynamics AI Institute, splits intelligence into an athletic part and a cognitive part. He argues Boston Dynamics showed what athletic intelligence can be through dynamic, aggressive machines and innovative hardware, but most robots remain cognitively dumb. The institute aims to combine the two, working in "stepping stones to moonshots".

## Key points

- **Dynamic over cautious.** After seeing a slow six-legged robot with tripod stability, Raibert decided to "try the opposite" and built pogo stick robots. He describes 3D hopping balance as three parts: hop height, foot placement and body attitude. He now wants manipulation to move beyond careful static grasping in the same way.
- **Funding and bootstrapping.** Ivan Sutherland gave him about $3,000 for a first model, and DARPA's Craig Fields then sent $250,000. Boston Dynamics started in 1992 as a simulation company, killed a surgical simulator, and had no investors until Google bought it after 20 years.
- **BigDog to Spot.** BigDog put gasoline power and hydraulics on board and was tested on the Guadalcanal Trail at Quantico. LS3, designed to carry 400 pounds, once carried about 1,000. Larry Page's request for a less intimidating 60-pound robot led to Spot, of which about 1,500 are now deployed.
- **Hardware still matters.** Raibert defends hydraulics: a football-sized power unit weighing 5 kg produces 5 kW. He says Atlas's best performances are still mostly model predictive control, and expects learning and traditional control to merge.
- **Team and videos.** His four traits are technical fearlessness, diligence, intrepidness and technical fun. His video rule was no explanation: do something worth showing and show it, including engineers disrupting the robot.
- **AI Institute projects.** "Watch Understand Do" starts with simple bicycle repair; "Inspect Diagnose Fix" builds on Spot's data collection. Raibert says he is not worried about AGI, comparing the risk to cars, which kill about 1.25 million people a year yet remain useful.
