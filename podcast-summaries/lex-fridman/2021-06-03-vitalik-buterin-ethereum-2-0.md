## Thesis

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin tells Lex Fridman that the next stage of Ethereum, now rolled out step by step rather than as a single "Eth 2.0" event, rests on two changes: proof of stake and sharding, combined with layer-2 rollups. He argues that blockchains should scale without making it too hard to run a node, and that crypto's larger purpose is to fund public goods without central control.

## Key points

- **Shiba Inu tokens.** Buterin describes getting half of the SHIB supply sent to his cold wallet. He recovered the key during Covid travel limits, burned most of the tokens and gave the rest to charities, including an India Covid relief fund.
- **Proof of stake.** He argues staking uses far less energy and hardware than mining. It would also cut new ether issuance from about 4.7 million per year to between 500,000 and one million. Attacking it would take as much stake as the rest of the network, and "slashing" automatically burns the coins of validators who provably misbehave.
- **Scaling math.** Sharding uses random committees of validators, plus ZK-SNARKs and data availability sampling, so that no single node checks everything. It starts with 64 shards and leaves room for 1,024. Buterin estimates about 100x from rollups times about 100x from sharding. He expects ZK rollups to beat optimistic rollups in the long run, since they need no week-long challenge period.
- **Block size and forks.** Calling himself a "medium blocker", Buterin says a chain must be cheap to both read and write. He argues hard forks are more honest than soft forks, and calls Craig Wright a scammer.
- **The merge and multiple clients.** He calls early 2022 the realistic target for merging the proof-of-work chain into the beacon chain. He credits having several client implementations with getting Ethereum through the 2016 Shanghai DoS attacks and the April 2021 Prysm bug.
- **Longevity and fear.** Buterin backs anti-aging research and wants aging treated as an engineering problem. Looking back on the 2016 DAO fork, he says fear drove the community to abandon its principles.
