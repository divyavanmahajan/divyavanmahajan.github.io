## Thesis

Alex Gladstein, chief strategy officer at the Human Rights Foundation (HRF) and the Oslo Freedom Forum, argues that money has been left out of human rights discussions. In his view Bitcoin decentralises money the way democracy decentralised politics and the internet decentralised information. He sees it as a sovereign savings account and an unstoppable payments network for people living under dictatorship or high inflation. Lex Fridman is broadly sympathetic but pushes back on the certainty of Bitcoin maximalists and on Gladstein's pessimism about AI.

## Key points

- **Measuring authoritarianism.** Gladstein says about 4.3 billion people in 95 countries live under authoritarian regimes. He offers litmus tests: Natan Sharansky's "town square test", whether a country can hold a gay pride parade, and whether comedians can make money mocking the government.
- **Surveillance versus encryption.** Gladstein calls big data analysis in government hands the biggest threat, citing Chinese surveillance in Xinjiang and exports of surveillance technology under the Belt and Road programme. Fridman counters that user-owned, distributed AI could give people control of their own data. Both agree encryption tools such as Signal help.
- **Money as a rights issue.** Gladstein says only 13 percent of people live in liberal democracies with reserve currencies, and close to 1.3 billion live with double- or triple-digit inflation. He gives examples from Zimbabwe, Sudan, Cuba, Iran and an Afghan CEO who paid female employees in Bitcoin.
- **Why Bitcoin and not others.** He argues the key innovation is a 21 million supply cap that nobody can change, and contrasts this with Ethereum's unclear issuance after its move to proof of stake. He calls almost all other tokens scams.
- **The Trojan horse.** In Gladstein's view, investors, companies such as Tesla and governments buy Bitcoin for "number go up", but in doing so they spread a freedom tool. Exchanges adopt Lightning to cut fees, not for its privacy.
- **Skepticism and accountability.** Fridman warns that cult-like certainty looks like a scam signal. Gladstein urges companies, the IOC and celebrities to pressure regimes, as happened against apartheid South Africa.
