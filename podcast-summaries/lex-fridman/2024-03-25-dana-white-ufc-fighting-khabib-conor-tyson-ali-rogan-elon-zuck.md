## Thesis

UFC president Dana White argues that the UFC succeeded because it fixed what he disliked as a boxing fan: commentators who belittled fighters and promoters who avoided risky matchups. He says his job is to tell the story of each fight and stage it well, while fighters build legacies by taking the hardest opponents. Passion, consistency and team building, not money, are what he credits for the company's survival.

## Key points

- **Lessons from boxing.** White says he muted HBO Boxing to avoid commentators like Larry Merchant, and plays a clip of Mike Tyson's walk-in to show how the broadcast talked fighters down. The UFC instead uses people like Joe Rogan and Daniel Cormier.
- **Buying the UFC.** After a contract dispute with owner Bob Meyrowitz, White told Lorenzo Fertitta the company was in trouble; the Fertittas bought it for $2 million and put in another $44 million. White describes the early years as "the Wild Wild West", full of threats and bad actors.
- **Joe Rogan.** White says Rogan did the first 13 shows for free and shared 3:30 a.m. radio tours with him because fighters were bad at them. Fridman recalls White offering to resign when Rogan faced cancellation.
- **Matchmaking and legends.** White argues upsets make stars, citing Dustin Poirier against Saint-Denis, Sean Strickland over Israel Adesanya, and Conor McGregor against Khabib. He names Jon Jones the greatest of all time because he has never lost in the octagon.
- **Conor, Trump and Elon vs Zuck.** White says McGregor's only fault is lateness and that he never asked for more money to take a replacement opponent. He describes Trump hosting early events at the Trump Taj Mahal, and says he was arranging venues, including in Italy, for a Musk-Zuckerberg fight.
- **Turning points and gambling.** White credits the Forrest Griffin-Stephan Bonnar fight with saving the Spike TV deal, signed on a napkin in an alley. He recounts winning $12 million from Caesars one summer and a $3 million drunken loss that ended his drinking at the table.
