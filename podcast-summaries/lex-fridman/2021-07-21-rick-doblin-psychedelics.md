## Thesis

Rick Doblin, founder of the Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS), tells Lex Fridman that psychedelics do not create experiences but surface what is already within a person, so set, setting and safety matter more than the molecule. His strategy since 1986 has been to use medicine and science as the opening wedge, starting with MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, followed by drug policy reform and eventually licensed legal access.

## Key points

- **Context over chemistry.** Doblin argues that the same drugs that helped Ken Kesey could harm others, and he believes Ted Kaczynski's alleged MKUltra exposure affected him negatively. For him the main challenge is building legal, safe contexts, not patenting new molecules.
- **A non-profit pharma model.** MAPS started after the DEA emergency-scheduled MDMA in 1985. Doblin says it raised about $110 million in donations over 35 years and created a public benefit corporation in 2014. He says Boston Consulting Group modelling suggests around three-quarters of a billion dollars in profit during US data exclusivity, to be reinvested in the mission.
- **Phase 3 results.** Doblin describes the first phase 3 study, published in Nature Medicine: 90 participants with chronic severe PTSD, including people who had previously attempted suicide, across 15 sites. He reports a p-value of 0.0001, a placebo-subtracted effect size of 0.91, no site effect, and better results in the dissociative subtype. He says the two suicide-related events in the study were both in the placebo group.
- **The blinding problem.** Phase 2 showed low doses felt uncomfortable and worked worse than inactive placebo. FDA and EMA accepted full-dose MDMA against inactive placebo, with random assignment and a pool of about 20 independent raters.
- **Timeline and Leary.** Doblin expected FDA approval at the end of 2023 and licensed legalization around 2035. He credits Timothy Leary as an advocate but says Leary played down risks and fudged the Concord Prison data.
- **For-profit concerns.** Doblin criticises Johnson & Johnson's esketamine as a drug-only product without therapy, and argues that drug policy reform will grow the market rather than shrink it.
