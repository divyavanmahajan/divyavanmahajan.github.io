## Thesis

Aaron Smith-Levin, raised in Scientology from age four and now Vice President of the Aftermath Foundation, walks Lex Fridman through its beliefs, practices and leadership. He grants that many members find auditing helpful, but argues that the organisation's demands, information control and enforced "disconnection" from family are what make it harmful. Fridman repeatedly tries to steelman the underlying ideas and compares the drift to other ideologies, including Soviet communism.

## Key points

- **Beliefs by level.** Smith-Levin explains thetans, the reactive mind and engrams from Dianetics (1950), and the confidential OT levels where members learn the Xenu story and audit "body thetans". He says he only learned what the upper levels contain after leaving.
- **Auditing and the E-meter.** He describes auditing as one-on-one talk therapy using an E-meter, where sessions continue until a "floating needle". He calls the claim that the tech works "100% of the time when applied 100% correctly" unfalsifiable.
- **Sea Org.** By his account Sea Org members sign symbolic billion-year contracts, earn about $50 a week, may not have children and work without days off.
- **David Miscavige.** Smith-Levin says Miscavige took power instead of the intended successors, Pat and Annie Broker, believes in Scientology but knows OT levels beyond eight were never written, and has been accused by former staff, including Mike Rinder, of assault. These are his claims.
- **Membership and influence.** From a spreadsheet of 175 to 200 organisations he estimates no more than 35,000 active Scientologists, against a peak of about 100,000. He describes "soft corruption" in Clearwater through lobbyists, attorneys and paid off-duty police work.
- **Family and his channel.** After his mother was expelled he refused to disconnect from her, and his wife's parents were later pushed to cut ties. He posts daily on YouTube so that current members find fair, non-hyperbolic criticism, and says he feels neither hate nor fear.
