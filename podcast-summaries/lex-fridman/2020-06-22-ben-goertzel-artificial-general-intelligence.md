## Thesis

Ben Goertzel, founder of SingularityNET and designer of OpenCog, tells Lex Fridman that AGI is the most important technology on the horizon, but that current deep neural networks will not reach it on their own. He argues for systems in which logic, neural and evolutionary learning share one knowledge representation, and for building AI on open, decentralized infrastructure, so that early AGIs learn compassion instead of serving a few corporations or governments.

## Key points

- **Naming AGI.** Goertzel says the term came out of an email discussion around 2001-2002 while he edited a book provisionally called "Real AI"; he credits Shane Legg or Peter Voss with proposing it. He organized the first AGI workshop in 2006 at a Bethesda Marriott, with about 60 attendees.
- **OpenCog's design.** OpenCog, open-sourced in 2008 and mostly written in C++, centres on the Atomspace, a weighted, labelled, self-modifying hypergraph. Goertzel describes term logic, neural-style links and procedures living in that one graph so that algorithms can help each other out of bottlenecks. He is rebuilding it from scratch as OpenCog 2.0 ("True AGI") with Alexey Potapov's team in St Petersburg.
- **Limits of deep learning.** Goertzel calls GPT-style transformers "very intelligent idiots" that recognize shallow patterns. In his grammar-induction work he uses a transformer only as a sentence-probability oracle that guides a symbolic learner.
- **SingularityNET.** Inspired by Minsky's society of mind, AIs run in containers and use the Ethereum blockchain for identity, reputation and channel setup, while the data itself flows off-chain. About 5,200 AIs ran on it at the time. He compares decentralized AI to Napster and BitTorrent, which shaped later music regulation.
- **Sophia.** Goertzel says Sophia combines a scripted timeline editor, a chatbot with rule-based and neural parts, and sometimes OpenCog. He rejects Yann LeCun's criticism of the robot and says the way it works has been published.
- **Covid, longevity and death.** His teams apply OpenCog reasoning to choose antiviral combinations for covid patients from small, scattered datasets. He calls death simply bad and names joy, growth and choice as his core values.
