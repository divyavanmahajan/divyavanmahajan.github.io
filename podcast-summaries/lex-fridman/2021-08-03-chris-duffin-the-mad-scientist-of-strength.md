## Thesis

Chris Duffin, the only person to squat and deadlift 1,000 pounds for multiple reps, tells Lex Fridman that strength is an engineering problem: control the torso and the foot, manage load increases precisely, and the body releases capacity it was holding back. He links that approach to his hard childhood, his corporate career and his decision to give it up to build Kabuki Strength.

## Key points

- **The 1,000-pound goal.** Duffin describes a five-year plan that ended with a 1,000-pound squat for reps in his gym the March before the interview, two days before the world shut down, at 265 to 285 pounds bodyweight, when others who had done either lift weighed 380 to 440. His 1,002-pound sumo deadlift for almost three reps still stands as a Guinness record.
- **The body's traction control.** He compares the nervous system to a car's traction control: when stability at the torso or foot is poor, it cuts power output. Small changes in neck position, he says, can produce a measurable jump of about 10% in output.
- **Managing load.** Duffin says weekly load spikes of more than 10 to 15% above the longer-term average account for about 80% of injuries, so he raises volume in small steps, sometimes one extra rep a week.
- **Kabuki Strength's equipment.** He says the company works with 29 of the 30 Major League Baseball teams. He presents the Transformer Bar, which changes spinal mechanics, and pressing and trap bars that apply "playground physics" by putting the centre of mass below the centre of rotation.
- **Reinvention and business.** Duffin left an executive career turning around manufacturing companies, sold his homes and took on millions in personal debt to start his businesses. He warns against "hustle porn" and says a business has to be your north star.
- **Steroids and TRT.** Duffin says openly that he has used steroids since age 33 and is now on TRT. He cites data from tested and untested powerlifting federations suggesting about a 10% strength gain on average, and lists health risks he says users need to manage.
