## Thesis

Former congresswoman and Army Lieutenant Colonel Tulsi Gabbard argues that war should be a last resort and that a powerful military industrial complex, spanning both parties, contractors and parts of the media, makes it easier to start wars than to question them. Lex Fridman largely agrees on the need for nuance and diplomacy, while pushing back on whether the problem is confined to one party.

## Key points

- **The cost of war in Iraq.** Gabbard describes checking daily casualty lists for her roughly 3,000-soldier Hawaii National Guard brigade in 2005, and says contractor KBR Halliburton charged about $35 per head per meal while paying Filipino workers around $500 a month.
- **The revolving door.** She points to Pentagon officials, congressional staff and the current Secretary of Defense moving between government and defense contractors, citing Eisenhower's warning.
- **Terrorism and Afghanistan.** Gabbard supported the initial post-9/11 mission against al-Qaeda but says Iraq diverted it, and argues Islamist groups such as al-Shabaab, which she saw on a 2021 deployment, must be fought both militarily and ideologically.
- **Ukraine, Syria and smears.** She calls for a negotiated peace between Russia and Ukraine and says labels such as "Putin puppet", including Hillary Clinton's "grooming" remark, are used to avoid debate. Fridman notes nuance was quickly lost in Ukraine coverage.
- **Nuclear risk and the TikTok bill.** Both worry about normalised talk of tactical nuclear weapons; Fridman cites Annie Jacobsen's account of a president's six-minute decision window. Gabbard argues the TikTok bill lets the executive designate "foreign adversaries" and could reach platforms like X.
- **Leaving the Democrats.** Gabbard recounts resigning as DNC vice chair in 2016 to endorse Bernie Sanders, says the party has become hostile to freedom and dissent, and offers a critique of Biden and a qualified view of Trump. Fridman stresses that both parties have power-hungry politicians. She closes by describing her Hindu faith and the Bhagavad Gita.
