## Thesis

Omar Suleiman, a Palestinian-American imam and president of the Yaqeen Institute, argues that the war in Gaza did not begin on October 7 but grows out of occupation, blockade and settlement expansion that Western media largely ignore. He calls the Israeli campaign a genocide, says the United States is not an honest broker because it funds Israel and shields it from international accountability, and says a ceasefire is the bare minimum, with an end to occupation the real goal.

## Key points

- **Context before October 7.** Suleiman cites the 2021 Human Rights Watch and 2022 Amnesty apartheid reports, the killing of journalist Shireen Abu Akleh, a plan for 13,000 settler units in 2023 and, by his count, over 600 Palestinians killed that year before the attack.
- **Human cost and the media.** He describes Al Jazeera's Wael Al-Dahdouh losing his wife, children, grandchild and cameraman and returning to air, and says US outlets cover the hostages while leaving out the daily Palestinian death toll.
- **Resistance and violence.** Suleiman holds that occupied people have a right to resist under international law, but that killing women, children and non-combatants is terrorism whoever does it. He says nonviolent options, such as the Great Return March and boycotts, were met with snipers or charges of antisemitism.
- **Wadea Al-Fayoume.** He recounts the murder of a 6-year-old boy in Illinois, stabbed 26 times by his landlord, and blames rhetoric such as President Biden repeating a debunked claim about 40 beheaded babies.
- **US politics.** Suleiman says Muslim voters will punish Biden in November, cites polls showing 67% of Americans want a ceasefire, and calls the Republicans no better, noting Trump moved the embassy to Jerusalem.
- **Faith.** He frames Malcolm X and MLK as closer than people assume, explains Al-Aqsa as Islam's first direction of prayer, and says a single human life outweighs the sanctity of any mosque.
