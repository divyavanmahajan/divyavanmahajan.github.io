## Thesis

Alex Garland, writer and director of Ex Machina, Annihilation and the Hulu series Devs, tells Lex Fridman that he sees himself as an interested layperson translating scientists' ideas into stories, not the other way round. He is not anxious about AI itself; his concern is the money and power of the people building technology. He argues the universe is deterministic, free will is an illusion, and science is essentially poetic.

## Key points

- **Dream, not simulation.** Garland says we live in a "dream state" because the brain makes best guesses from sensory input, but he does not think the physical universe is simulated. Psychedelics, in his view, just give an alternate distortion.
- **A truly alien alien.** In Annihilation he tried to avoid human-like motives, imagining an entity with a very slow "clock speed" that reacts to its environment the way an octopus changes colour.
- **Consciousness as projection.** Ex Machina was a Turing test with the machine in plain sight. Garland says Ava is good and HAL is neutral, and that Ava's unobserved smile after her escape was the real answer to the test. He doubts appearance proves consciousness because humans project sentience onto everything.
- **Silicon Valley's veneer.** Garland likens Silicon Valley to 1980s Wall Street, hiding greed behind a cool image, and says wealth can start to feel like evidence of virtue. Fridman, citing Stalin, agrees most people doing harm believe they are doing good.
- **Determinism and Devs.** Devs opens with a quantum computer simulating a nematode. Garland believes there is no free will, but tells how he answered his daughter: meaning still exists because people matter to each other.
- **Space and humility.** Leaving the solar system excites him most, inspired by a trip to Easter Island. He hopes building something like Ava would teach humans humility, and wants to be remembered as part of a conversation, not a maker of proclamations.
