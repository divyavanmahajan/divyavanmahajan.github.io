## Thesis

Chris Urmson, technical director of CMU's DARPA challenge teams, former CTO of Google's self-driving car project and CEO of Aurora, tells Lex Fridman that truly driverless vehicles are a different technology and business from Level 2 driver assistance. He argues that robustness needs lidar, cameras and radar together, that safety must be shown through process and many task-level metrics rather than one number, and that large-scale deployment (tens of thousands of vehicles) is likely within ten years.

## Key points

- **Lessons from DARPA.** Urmson says the main lesson of the Grand Challenges was that it could be done at all. He credits HD maps for the Grand Challenge and multi-beam lidar plus Bayesian estimation for the Urban Challenge, and says Red Whittaker taught him to trust students with real responsibility.
- **Lidar is not a crutch.** Responding to Elon Musk's claim, Urmson grants that humans prove passive vision is enough, but says with about 37,000 US road deaths a year any useful sensor should be used. He expects lidar costs to fall, and says a working $500 sensor beats a $50 one that does not work.
- **Level 2 and over-trust.** He warns that people will over-trust driver assistance, pointing to reports of people sleeping in Teslas. A commuter's 1,800 miles of trouble-free driving says little against roughly 85 million miles between fatalities, and the economics of driver assistance push its technology away from full autonomy.
- **Showing safety.** Urmson told the DMV that disengagement counts are a poor metric. He proposes comparing system and human failure rates per task (traffic lights, unprotected left turns), an aviation-style event pyramid, and evidence reviewed by regulators such as NHTSA.
- **Urban before highway.** Aurora considered trucking first, but Urmson argues freeway failures are rarer and far more severe, so moderate-speed urban driving lets a company learn faster with lower risk.
- **What would speed things up.** He says a perfect five-second model of what is happening around the vehicle would help most, and that his biggest worry is vulnerable road users such as cyclists and pedestrians.
