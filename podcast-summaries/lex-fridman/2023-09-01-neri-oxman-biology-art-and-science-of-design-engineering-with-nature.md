## Thesis

Neri Oxman argues that human-made goods have separated us from nature, and that the goal of design should be to grow rather than build, so that made and grown things become indistinguishable. Her method, developed in the Mediated Matter group at MIT and now at her company OXMAN, is computational templating: physical, environmental and chemical cues that guide organisms, with the aim of giving nature enough "bandwidth" that it can act with its own agency.

## Key points

- **Anthropomass overtook biomass.** Oxman cites Weizmann Institute professor Ron Milo, who identified 2020 as the year human-made mass exceeded biomass; that prompted her "grow everything" approach, which she has called material ecology since 2005.
- **Silk Pavilion.** A six-metre pavilion, later shown at MoMA, was spun by 17,532 silkworms. Her team found that on flat surfaces the worms spin flat patches, and used robots that varied heat and light to guide them into swarm-like collective work.
- **Bees in space.** Her synthetic apiary kept Massachusetts bees in perpetual spring through winter; bees flown on a Blue Origin New Shepherd mission came back reproductive, unlike a 1982 NASA experiment, and gold nanoparticles showed that bees recycle wax.
- **Bacteria, ethics and living materials.** The Vespers death masks used E. coli templated by chemical signals at 20 microns per voxel, producing "hybrid living materials". Oxman turned down transgenic glowing silk, saying she will not genetically modify silkworms but sees a different ethical range for bacteria.
- **OXMAN's three scales.** She describes "large molecule models" to decode a molecular language across kingdoms, starting with a functionalized fragrance; a product grown from CO2 that ends up in soil growing an edible plant; and lab capsules, designed since 2019, that combine environmental control with genetic regulation.
- **Soft control and emergence.** Oxman says empowerment is directional while emergence is multi-directional, and that what we fear from AGI is what we want from synthetic biology: templates that are no longer needed because the system sets its own rules.
