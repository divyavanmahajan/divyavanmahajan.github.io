## Thesis

Diana Walsh Pasulka, a professor of philosophy and religion at UNCW and author of American Cosmic, tells Lex Fridman that beliefs shape reality through their effects even when they are false. She argues that belief in UFOs and non-human intelligence follows the same pattern as the birth of religions, and that it is closely bound up with technology. Fridman is mostly interested in what these myths inspire, whatever their literal truth.

## Key points

- **Beliefs have real effects.** Citing Kant, Pasulka says we can only approximate reality, while beliefs still have real consequences. Her example is limbo, abolished by Pope Benedict in 2007, which she says caused centuries of suffering for parents of unbaptised children.
- **How religions start.** Pasulka describes a building-block model: a person has an intense "contact experience", others believe them, a movement grows, and institutions step in to control the story. She points to Constantine and the Council of Nicaea, and to the Polish nun Faustina, whose devotion the Church first suppressed and John Paul II later promoted.
- **UFOs as religiosity.** She calls the UFO narrative an "X-Files type" religion and treats Roswell as a pilgrimage site where a "hierophany" (contact with the non-human) is believed to have happened.
- **Technology and "downloads".** Pasulka links rocket pioneers Jack Parsons and Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, and a source she calls "Tyler D", to beliefs about receiving information from non-human intelligences. She cites Jacques Vallée, who worked on ARPANET and also studies UFOs, as proof that the two interests are one.
- **Fieldwork and doubt.** She describes being blindfolded and taken to a claimed 1940s crash site in New Mexico, where she says anomalous materials were found. She says secretive "invisibles" in the field believed the Tic Tac videos were real but "spun", possibly for funding.
- **Co-evolution with AI.** Pasulka prefers "technogenesis", the idea that humans co-evolve with technology rather than simply use it. She compares Teilhard de Chardin's noosphere and Kurzweil's singularity to religious language, and supports Neuralink.
