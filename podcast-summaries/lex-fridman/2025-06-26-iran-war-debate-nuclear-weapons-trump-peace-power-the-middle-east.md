## Thesis

Recorded after the Iran-Israel ceasefire, this debate sets Mark Dubowitz (Foundation for Defense of Democracies) against Scott Horton (Libertarian Institute, Antiwar.com). Dubowitz argues that Iran built a nuclear weapons capability through years of deception, and that Trump's limited strike should push it into a "no enrichment, full dismantlement" deal. Horton argues that Iran wanted only a latent deterrent, that demanding zero enrichment is a poison pill, and that attacking Iran makes a bomb more likely. Lex Fridman moderates and repeatedly asks both men to steelman the other side.

## Key points

- **How the war unfolded.** Dubowitz describes five rounds of talks in Oman led by Steve Witkoff, Iran rejecting a US offer, Israel's 12-day campaign, and B-II bombers dropping 12 30,000-pound massive ordnance penetrators on Fordow, a site buried about 80 meters deep.
- **What 60% enrichment means.** Dubowitz says 60% enriched uranium is "99%" of the work towards weapons grade and that Iran holds 15 to 17 bombs' worth. Horton calls that framing meaningless and says Iran enriched to 60% as a bargaining chip after Israeli sabotage at Natanz.
- **Evidence of intent.** Dubowitz cites the AMAD program to build five warheads, the Mossad-seized archive analysed by David Albright, and a half-trillion-dollar programme. Horton cites the 2007 NIE and Tulsi Gabbard's assessment that the supreme leader had not decided to build a weapon, and says he believes Mossad fabricated the archive.
- **Common ground.** Both men say Trump should not have left the JCPOA without first negotiating longer sunsets with Europe, both call the Russiagate accusations against Trump unfounded, and both doubt Iran would launch a nuclear first strike.
- **Proliferation.** Dubowitz expects fewer nuclear states if the strikes work, warning of a cascade in the Middle East and among South Korea, Taiwan and Japan otherwise. Horton fears the lesson of North Korea, Iraq and Libya is to get a bomb fast, and that the NPT could fall apart.
- **Tensions.** The debate turns heated when Horton questions Dubowitz's loyalty as an immigrant; Fridman calls it "deeply disrespectful".
