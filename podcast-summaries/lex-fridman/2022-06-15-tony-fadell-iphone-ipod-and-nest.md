## Thesis

Tony Fadell, co-creator of the iPod, iPhone and Nest thermostat and author of Build, tells Lex Fridman that products succeed when they kill a real pain and deliver an emotional "superpower", and when the team shares the "why" from the start. He argues that first versions depend on opinion-based decisions that leaders must own, and that marketing, product management and leadership style determine whether that story holds together.

## Key points

- **Building the iPod.** As a contractor, Fadell spent six weeks laying out batteries, screens and storage as "Lego blocks" and built weighted styrofoam models. He says Apple in 2001 was a cautious, barely break-even company, and that Steve Jobs committed two quarters of marketing spend to the iPod, which depended on Jeff Robbin's iTunes.
- **Write the press release first.** Fadell says products built from "whats" end up with fictional marketing stories. He compares a product press release to a film treatment that sets the audience, pains and price, and calls product management "the voice of the customer".
- **Opinions versus data.** Fadell says v1 products have no data, and that companies such as Philips stall by turning opinion-based decisions into data-driven ones to protect jobs. Examples: the iPhone's virtual keyboard held as an opinion, while data on carrier networks restored the SIM slot Jobs wanted removed.
- **Jobs's leadership.** Fadell describes Jobs as relentless but challengeable, critiquing work rather than people. Jobs reframed the plastic-versus-glass screen choice: scratches from keys are a design flaw, drops are the user's fault. Fadell says he differs by crediting team ideas openly.
- **Nest and Google.** Fadell says the harder part of Nest was the go-to-market: direct-to-consumer sales, self-installation and retail access in a channel controlled by installers. He says the $3.2 billion Google sale followed four months of talks about leadership and platform funding.
- **Careers and balance.** Fadell advises choosing jobs by what you want to learn and who from, and says burnout after General Magic taught him to write ideas down rather than ruminate.
