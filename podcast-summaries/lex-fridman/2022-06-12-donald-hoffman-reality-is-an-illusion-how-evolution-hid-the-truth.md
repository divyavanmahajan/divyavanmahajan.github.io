## Thesis

Donald Hoffman, a cognitive scientist at UC Irvine and author of The Case Against Reality, tells Lex Fridman that natural selection shapes senses to track fitness payoffs, not objective reality, so what we perceive is a user interface. He links this to physicists' claim that spacetime is "doomed" and proposes instead a mathematical theory in which consciousness is fundamental and spacetime emerges from it. Fridman repeatedly presses him on the limits of game-theoretic models and on how any of this explains why chocolate tastes good.

## Key points

- **Fitness beats truth.** Hoffman says theorems by mathematician Chetan Prakash and genetic-algorithm simulations by his students show the probability that selection produces truth-seeing senses is zero for generic world structures. A Yale team found counter-cases with 20,000 payoff functions; Hoffman says allowing organisms to group payoffs into "objects" made those results disappear.
- **Spacetime is doomed.** Citing Nima Arkani-Hamed, Hoffman notes that gravity plus quantum field theory break down at 10^-33 cm and 10^-43 s, and that structures such as the amplituhedron turn billions of scattering-amplitude terms into one, as the 1986 Parke-Taylor formula first hinted.
- **Reductionism is over.** Hoffman argues that drilling down to quarks and gluons only reaches representations of spacetime's own symmetries, and that physicalist consciousness theories (integrated information, Penrose-Hameroff, global workspace) have not explained a single specific experience.
- **Conscious agents.** His alternative models consciousness as probability spaces of experiences linked by Markovian kernels. A stationary dynamics has no entropic time, but he says any projection of it shows increasing entropy, which could produce time, scarcity and natural selection.
- **Personal stakes.** Hoffman describes meditating three to four hours a day for 20 years and nearly dying of Covid, texting his wife goodbye from the emergency room. He frames possessions and wars as fights over ephemeral interface objects.
