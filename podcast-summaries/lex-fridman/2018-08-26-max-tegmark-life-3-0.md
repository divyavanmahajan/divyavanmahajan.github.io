## Thesis

Max Tegmark, MIT physicist, co-founder of the Future of Life Institute and author of "Life 3.0", tells Lex Fridman that minds are matter processing information in particular patterns, with no "secret sauce" tied to biology. From this he argues that AGI is possible, that the risk lies in competence with misaligned goals rather than malice, and that humanity should first decide what future it wants and then work through the obstacles.

## Key points

- **Possibly alone.** Tegmark guesses we are the only technology-building life in our observable universe, given over a billion Earth-like planets in the Milky Way and no evidence of visitors. He says this puts responsibility on us not to destroy ourselves.
- **Consciousness as science.** He defines intelligence as the ability to accomplish complex goals and treats consciousness separately. He places himself with Giulio Tononi in a middle camp, between Daniel Dennett's view and AI researchers who say machines can never be conscious, and wants equations that separate conscious from unconscious processing.
- **Goals, not evil.** Citing Steve Omohundro and Nick Bostrom, Tegmark says open-ended goals produce sub-goals such as self-preservation and resource acquisition. He uses a rhinoceros species driven extinct by humans as an example of misaligned goals, and says AI must learn, adopt and retain our goals.
- **Kindergarten ethics now.** Rather than waiting for consensus on values, he proposes building in basic rules today, citing the Germanwings pilot Lubitz, whose autopilot accepted the command to descend into the Alps.
- **Why deep learning works.** Tegmark says the problems physics makes relevant are a tiny subset that neural networks suit well, and that multiplying a thousand numbers needs 2 to the power 1,000 neurons in one hidden layer but about 4,000 in a deep network. He doubts the brain is a quantum computer, citing decoherence times of about 10 to the minus 21 seconds.
- **Meaning and work.** He suggests that if governments consider basic income, they should also consider hiring more teachers and nurses, since jobs give more than income.
