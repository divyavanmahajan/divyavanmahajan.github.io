## Thesis

Dario Amodei argues that scaling will probably keep working and could produce powerful AI by 2026 or 2027. He says the risks, catastrophic misuse and model autonomy, should be handled with "if-then" safeguards that tighten only when tests show danger. In separate conversations, Amanda Askell treats Claude's character as an alignment problem, and Chris Olah argues that interpretability can reverse-engineer what networks learn.

## Key points

- **Scaling and timelines.** Amodei says real-world coding tasks (SWE-bench) went from about 3% to 50% in ten months. He expects frontier clusters to grow from roughly $1 billion to above $10 billion in 2026, with $100 billion ambitions by 2027. He calls scaling laws empirical regularities, not laws of the universe.
- **Responsible scaling policy.** Today's models are ASL-2. ASL-3 means meaningful uplift to non-state actors on cyber, bio or nuclear harm, and Amodei "would not be surprised at all" to hit it next year. At ASL-4 models might sandbag tests, so he wants interpretability as an independent check.
- **Race to the top and regulation.** Amodei says Anthropic sets practices for others to copy, such as interpretability teams and RSPs, which Google adopted months later. He supported the amended SB 1047, argues that badly designed regulation would build a lasting consensus against regulation, and wants action in 2025.
- **Machines of Loving Grace.** He pictures a professor directing 1,000 AI grad students and expects transformation in five to ten years, not Tyler Cowen's 50 to 100. His larger worry is concentration and abuse of power.
- **Claude's character.** Askell wants Claude to act as a good person would when speaking with millions: honest, not sycophantic, cautious about sharing opinions. Character training is a constitutional AI variant without human data, and system prompts are cheap patches.
- **Features and superposition.** Olah explains that networks store more concepts than neurons through superposition, which sparse autoencoders unfold. On Claude 3 Sonnet this found multimodal features for security vulnerabilities, backdoors and deception that change behavior when forced active.
