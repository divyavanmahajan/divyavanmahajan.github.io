## Thesis

Robert Playter, CEO of Boston Dynamics and a member of the company since its MIT Leg Lab roots, tells Lex Fridman that the company is moving from a research lab to a product business without dropping its research ambitions. He argues that robots must first be useful and reliable in industry, sold in the thousands, before cheaper consumer or companion robots make sense. Fridman pushes harder on robots as pets and conversational companions.

## Key points

- **Working with the physics.** Playter says Marc Raibert's approach, starting with a pogo stick, was to simplify problems and let a machine's natural dynamics do part of the work. Natural walking took 10 to 15 years, from the PETMAN prototype in 2008 to the newer Atlas in 2022.
- **Faster behaviour development.** Model-predictive control lets Atlas plan a second or two ahead and adjust in the air. Playter says new behaviours that once took six months now take days; four modern Atlas robots are shared by about 40 developers.
- **Spot's business.** About 1,100 Spots are in the field. Playter says profitability needs 1,000 to 1,500 sales a year, mainly for industrial inspection (thermal, acoustic, gauge reading) with customers such as AB InBev, GlobalFoundries and Ontario Power Generation. The office fleet logs 1,500 to 2,000 km of walking a week to find rare failures.
- **Stretch for warehouses.** The box-moving robot began shipping in January to DHL and Maersk, with Gap next and commitments for a couple of hundred units. Playter cites a typical two-year return on investment and says former manual unloaders now operate the robots.
- **Competition and AI.** Playter welcomes Tesla's humanoid effort as validation and notes Hyundai's own manufacturing strength. He sees less risk in learning-based robots than in language models because physical tasks are easy to verify, and doubts language models are conscious.
- **No weapons.** Boston Dynamics wrote a pledge, co-signed by Unitree, Agility and ANYmal, not to put weapons on general-purpose robots, and has a policy director engaging regulators.
