## Thesis

Albert Bourla, chairman and CEO of Pfizer, tells Lex Fridman that the speed of the COVID-19 vaccine came from removing financial caution and setting stretch goals, not from cutting corners in the regulated trial process. Fridman spends much of the conversation on trust: FDA transparency, the industry's reputation, media sponsorship and mandates. Bourla defends regulators and Pfizer's conduct, while Fridman voices scepticism on several points.

## Key points

- **All-in bet.** Bourla says Pfizer usually funds research in stages of around $50 million, but committed about $2.3 billion at once, calling it an easy decision in the context of the pandemic.
- **Speed without skipping steps.** According to Bourla, Pfizer narrowed 20 candidate vaccines to one, ran a phase 3 trial costing almost $1 billion, and recruited through 150 hospitals instead of 30 to finish enrolment in three to four months.
- **Stretch goals.** Bourla says normal yearly capacity was 200 million vaccine doses; he asked for three billion, and asked for the antiviral pill's discovery phase, normally four years, in four months. He says Pfizer runs at about one billion doses per quarter.
- **Trust and reputation.** Fridman criticises the FDA's proposed 75-year timeline for releasing Pfizer data and raises Jordan Peterson's and Joe Rogan's concerns about revolving doors and TV advertising. Bourla says regulators are unbiased, sponsorships are few and vetted, and the 2009 Bextra settlement stemmed from off-label promotion in 2003.
- **Mandates.** Bourla says mandating vaccination for Pfizer staff moved the rate from 90 to 96 percent, but leaves public policy to health officials. Fridman says federal mandates are a bad idea and prefers persuasion.
- **Paxlovid and ivermectin.** Bourla describes the five-day pill course as cutting hospitalisations from ten in a group to one in trials. He says Pfizer's scientists see no reliable evidence for ivermectin; Fridman notes small studies exist.
