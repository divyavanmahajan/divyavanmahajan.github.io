## Thesis

Javier Milei, speaking through an interpreter to Lex Fridman, describes himself as an anarcho-capitalist in theory and a minarchist in practice. He argues that ending Argentina's fiscal deficit in his first month and deregulating daily have brought inflation and poverty down, and that libertarians must also fight political and cultural battles or see gains reversed, as he says happened in Chile.

## Key points

- **Inherited crisis.** Milei says that on taking office inflation was running at 1% a day, wholesale inflation was 54% a month, the deficit was 15% of GDP and Central Bank net reserves were minus $12 billion.
- **The cuts.** He reports cutting ministries to eight, dismissing about 50,000 public employees, halting public works and discretionary transfers to provinces, and removing intermediaries from welfare payments. He claims fiscal balance in January and a 15-point fiscal adjustment, which he calls the largest in history.
- **Results he claims.** Wholesale inflation fell from 54% to 2% a month; he puts underlying monetary inflation at 0.2% a month. Poverty, which he says rose to 57% once price controls ended, is now 46% by his account, and country risk fell from 3,000 to 770 basis points.
- **Deregulation and DOGE.** Deregulation minister Federico Sturzenegger removes one to five regulations a day, with 3,200 structural reforms pending. Milei's advice to Elon Musk and Vivek Ramaswamy is to go all the way and not give up.
- **Corruption and media.** Milei cites street blockades falling from about 900 a year, Cristina Fernández de Kirchner's conviction, and the end of official advertising. He calls most Argentine journalists liars and extortionists and credits X with breaking their monopoly.
- **Currency and allies.** He favours currency competition over strict dollarisation, with the Central Bank closed once its balance sheet is clean. He names the United States and Israel as allies and says Argentina would like to join NATO.
