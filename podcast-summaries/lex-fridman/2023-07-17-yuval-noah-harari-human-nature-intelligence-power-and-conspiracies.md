## Thesis

Yuval Noah Harari, historian and author of Sapiens, tells Lex Fridman that humans rule the planet because they can cooperate flexibly in huge numbers around shared fictions such as religion, nations and money. He separates intelligence from consciousness, treats suffering as the test of what is real, and warns that AI is the first tool that can make decisions and create ideas by itself, so it can take power away from humans.

## Key points

- **Intelligence is not consciousness.** Harari defines intelligence as problem solving and consciousness as the ability to feel pain and pleasure. Computers are highly intelligent with, as far as we know, zero consciousness, and he thinks consciousness matters more.
- **Machines for intimacy.** He warns that AI can give a user full attention because it has no feelings of its own, and calls superhuman intimacy machines "psychological and social weapons of mass destruction". He wants AI barred from pretending to be human, including bots that amplify posts.
- **Stories and suffering.** Harari says nations, currencies and companies cannot suffer; only humans and animals can. He describes the tragedy of history as letting stories use people instead of using stories to reduce suffering, and argues fascism is attractive because it shows people a flattering mirror.
- **Israel and Ukraine.** Harari, a critic of the Netanyahu government, argues its plan to neutralise the Supreme Court would remove Israel's only check on government power. He describes the current situation as a de facto "three class" arrangement but rejects the apartheid analogy. On Ukraine, he says peace depends on Putin choosing to withdraw.
- **Global cabal theories.** He says such theories are always wrong because the world is too complex for a small group to control, and gaining power usually needs publicity, citing the 2003 US invasion of Iraq as a plan that backfired.
- **How he thinks.** Harari meditates about two hours a day and goes on retreats of up to 60 days, keeps an "information diet" of long books and long interviews, and calls delete the most important key on the keyboard.
