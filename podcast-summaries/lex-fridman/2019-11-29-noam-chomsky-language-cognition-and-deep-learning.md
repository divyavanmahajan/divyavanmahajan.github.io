## Thesis

Noam Chomsky tells Lex Fridman that language is above all an internal, genetically endowed system for thought, and that the same biology which gives human cognition its richness also sets limits on what it can understand. From this he argues that deep learning, while useful as engineering, is not science: it fits patterns in corpora and says nothing about how language actually works.

## Key points

- **Internal versus external language.** Chomsky distinguishes the inner system in the brain that fixes sound and meaning from the noises we externalise, and says most use of language is internal thought.
- **Scope and limits.** He calls the belief that human intelligence can answer any question "very strange", citing Newton's discovery of interaction without contact, after which, he says, science abandoned the search for an intelligible world and settled for intelligible theories.
- **Structure dependence.** Using "carefully the guy who fixed the car packed his tools", Chomsky shows that speakers link the adverb to the structurally closest verb, not the linearly closest, even though only linear order is ever heard. He calls this the deepest property of language found so far.
- **Limits of deep learning.** Chomsky says a parser trained on a corpus such as The Wall Street Journal treats random sentences as experiments, which no science does. A system that would learn a language based on linear proximity just as easily is, in his view, a scientific failure.
- **Brain interfaces.** Asked about Neuralink, he says a machine could expand cognition only the way a book does, not beyond native capacities.
- **Nature and institutions.** Chomsky says institutions such as markets arise from historical contingency, not essential human nature, which can accommodate both domination and an "instinct for freedom".
