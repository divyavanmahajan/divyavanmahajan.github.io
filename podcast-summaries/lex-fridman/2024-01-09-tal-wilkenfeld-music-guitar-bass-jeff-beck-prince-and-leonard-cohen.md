## Thesis

Tal Wilkenfeld, a singer-songwriter and bassist, tells Lex Fridman that good music comes from standing "on the edge of the cliff": playing without fear of mistakes and responding in real time. She prefers trust to confidence, treats music as spiritual expression rather than sport, and links her playing to meditation and to how she has learned to handle grief.

## Key points

- **Jeff Beck at Crossroads.** Wilkenfeld recalls Beck handing her the solo on "Cause We've Ended as Lovers" at Eric Clapton's 2007 Crossroads Festival. There had been no bass solo until she auditioned for Beck the day after a hospital night with food poisoning.
- **Trust over confidence.** She argues fear and confidence both act as a brick wall on stage, while trust means knowing you can handle whatever comes. Her band meditates together for at least 20 minutes before shows.
- **Grief and the Comedy Store.** After Prince and Leonard Cohen died in 2016, she put her belongings in storage and found community among comedians. That helped her cope when Jeff Beck died; Green Day's Mike Dirnt then gave her an Olympic White jazz bass she named Jeff.
- **Leonard Cohen's craft.** Cohen advised her to reread a draft lyric in many states of mind so it can mean several things. She says he wrote about 80 verses of "Hallelujah" over 15 to 20 years before narrowing it to four.
- **How she practises.** Limited by her parents to 30 minutes a day, she learned to visualise the fretboard in her head. She practises in short bursts with rests, has never practised more than an hour a day, and recommends playing very slowly to hear each note's tone.
- **Prince and first takes.** Prince asked her to find a drummer for a trio record, Welcome to America, and recorded to tape with no punch-ins. She says take one holds the most "gold" about 80% of the time.
