## Thesis

Adam Frank argues that the question of alien life has moved from speculation to data: exoplanet surveys have pinned down the astronomical terms of the Drake equation, and new telescopes can look for biosignatures and technosignatures in planetary atmospheres. He believes simple life is probably common, but says the real open questions are how long technological civilizations last and how to study life and experience without the reductionist "blind spot" he criticises in science.

## Key points

- **The pessimism line.** Frank says every star hosts planets and about one in five has an Earth-like planet in the habitable zone. With 10 billion trillion such planets, his 2016 paper with Woody Sullivan concludes we are not the first civilization unless the odds per planet are below 10 to the minus 22.
- **No indirect Fermi paradox.** Citing a study by Jason Wright's group, Frank says all SETI searches to date cover a hot tub's worth of an ocean. Simulations by his group show settlement waves cross the galaxy quickly, but finite civilization lifetimes leave large empty regions.
- **Life and planets co-evolve.** Frank argues the Hard Steps model fails because plate tectonics, oxygen and life shaped each other; a stronger form of plate tectonics about a billion years ago boosted primary productivity by a factor of almost 1000.
- **Technosignatures.** Frank leads the first NASA grant for exoplanet technosignatures, building a library that includes chlorofluorocarbons, city lights, solar panel glints and propulsion plumes. He calls JWST's methane detection on K2-18b, 120 light years away, a five sigma result.
- **UFO scepticism.** Frank supports open scientific study of UAPs but says no claimed evidence meets scientific standards; his own bet is that sightings involve state adversaries testing technology. Fridman is more open to pilot testimony.
- **The Blind Spot.** Frank argues that science has forgotten its roots in lived experience, pointing to quantum mechanics, where he favours QBism, and to time as cases where the agent cannot be left out.
