## Thesis

Fiona Hill, who advised the Bush, Obama and Trump administrations on Russia, tells Lex Fridman that America's fractured domestic politics made it vulnerable to Russian influence and signalled to the Kremlin that Washington did not take Ukraine seriously. She describes Putin as a rational actor inside his own historical frame, increasingly isolated and rigid, and urges "strategic empathy" to anticipate his moves. Fridman repeatedly pushes back, arguing that neither Ukrainian resistance nor Putin's miscalculation was obvious in advance.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Hill describes growing up in a declining coal-mining town in County Durham; a grant from the Durham Miners Association, funded partly by solidarity money from Donbas miners, paid for her first Russian studies.
- **Trump and the impeachment.** Hill says Trump often asked valid questions, for example on NATO spending versus Nord Stream 2, but undercut them with insults. She sees a "straight line" from the Ukraine pressure campaign to the war, because the Kremlin concluded the US treated Ukraine as a domestic game. She thinks the impeachment weakened the US, faulting grandstanding on both sides.
- **2016 interference.** Hill argues Russia ran a classic influence operation that exploited existing US divisions; Americans, not Putin, elected Trump, and the Kremlin was happy to take credit.
- **Putin's arc.** Hill credits Putin's first two terms with stabilising the economy, then dates a shift to his 2012 return, with the 2014 annexation of Crimea as the turning point. She thinks COVID isolation narrowed his information sources and that he expected Ukraine to fold quickly, as in Hungary in 1956 or Czechoslovakia in 1968.
- **NATO and nuclear risk.** Hill calls the 2008 open-door pledge to Ukraine and Georgia a strategic blunder, but says Moscow's real objection is Ukraine leaving its orbit and its wish for great-power deals over smaller states' heads. On tactical nuclear use, she says what matters is Putin's own estimate of whether it would force capitulation, and warns of wider proliferation.
- **Endings and hope.** Hill hopes for a ceasefire and negotiated settlement, and compares Ukraine to Finland in the Winter War.
