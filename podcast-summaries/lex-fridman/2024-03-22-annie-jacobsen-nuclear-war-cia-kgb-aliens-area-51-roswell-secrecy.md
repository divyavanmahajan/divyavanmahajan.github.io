## Thesis

Investigative journalist Annie Jacobsen, author of "Nuclear War: A Scenario", argues that deterrence is only a statement of hope: once a launch happens, policies built for speed leave one person a few minutes to choose, and every path ends in catastrophe. Drawing on interviews with former defense secretaries, STRATCOM commanders and FEMA officials, she says the only way out is communication between adversaries. The second half covers her earlier books on Area 51, government ESP research and CIA paramilitary work.

## Key points

- **Six minutes, one person.** Under launch on warning and sole presidential authority, Jacobsen says the US president has about six minutes to order a counterstrike and needs no one's permission. She cites Reagan's memoirs calling this irrational.
- **The numbers.** She puts US deployed warheads at 1,770 and Russia's at 1,674, out of 12,500 worldwide. An ICBM takes 26 minutes 40 seconds from the Soviet Union to the US East Coast; a submarine a couple of hundred miles offshore needs under 10 minutes. The US has 44 interceptors with a roughly 50% success rate.
- **Structural flaws.** Leon Panetta confirmed to her that US ICBMs aimed at North Korea would have to fly over Russia, whose Tundra satellite system can mistake sunlight or clouds for a launch. The 1983 Proud Prophet war game ended in Armageddon in every scenario.
- **Aftermath.** Citing Brian Toon, she describes megafires lofting about 300 billion pounds of soot, nuclear winter, failed agriculture and a death toll she puts at 5 billion.
- **Aliens and Roswell.** Jacobsen says she has no reason to believe aliens have visited Earth and sees UFO stories as CIA strategic deception, starting with U-2 sightings. She names Al O'Donnell for the first time as the source behind her Roswell account in "Area 51".
- **CIA third option.** She explains how Title 50 let the Bin Laden raid run as a CIA mission, and recounts Billy Waugh photographing Imad Mughniyeh from a dumpster in Saudi Arabia.
