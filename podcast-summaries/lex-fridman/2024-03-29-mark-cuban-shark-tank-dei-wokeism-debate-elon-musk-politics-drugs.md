## Thesis

Mark Cuban tells Lex Fridman that good entrepreneurs are curious, agile and able to sell, and that becoming a billionaire also takes luck and timing. He argues that opaque industries such as drug pricing can be disrupted through transparency, which is the premise of Cost Plus Drugs. On DEI and politics, Cuban defends DEI in companies and says social media algorithms, not "woke ideology", drive much of the polarisation, while Fridman pushes back on how hard it can be to criticise DEI programmes.

## Key points

- **Building broadcast.com.** Cuban and Todd Wagner started AudioNet so they could stream Indiana University basketball, grew it into broadcast.com, took it public in July 1998 and sold it to Yahoo for $5.7 billion in stock. Cuban says he then hedged his Yahoo shares with a collar so he was protected when the market fell.
- **Valuation on Shark Tank.** Cuban says a $1 million valuation for a $100,000 stake only makes sense if the business can pay out $100,000 in after-tax cash flow, unless it has a likely exit. He regrets passing on Spikeball, and says he offered to invest in Uber Cab but not at the valuation Travis was asking.
- **Turning round the Mavericks.** After buying the team in 2000 for $285 million, Cuban hired 15 development coaches and told staff they were in the "experience business", not the basketball business.
- **DEI and the clash with Musk.** Cuban says quotas are illegal and calls widespread reverse discrimination a straw man. Fridman points to Google's Gemini image errors and the strong "immune system" of DEI programmes. Cuban answers that Grok has had similar errors and that the market will correct both.
- **Cost Plus Drugs.** Cuban says the company shows its cost, a 15% markup and fees on more than 2,500 drugs. He accuses the big three PBMs of chasing rebates, citing Humira at about $8,000 a month against the biosimilar Yusimry at $594. He says one PBM charged the Mavericks $169,000 for generics that Cost Plus would have sold for $19,000.
- **AI and open source.** Cuban says open-sourcing models is a smart business choice but should not be forced. He rejects Vinod Khosla's Manhattan Project comparison and expects millions of specialised, licensed models.
