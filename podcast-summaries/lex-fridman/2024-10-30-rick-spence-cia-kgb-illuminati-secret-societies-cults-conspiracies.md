## Thesis

Historian Rick Spence, a self-described "heretic", argues that intelligence services, secret societies and cults work through the same mechanisms: compartmentalised secrecy, selective recruitment, oaths, ritual and the exploitation of human ego. He treats many conspiracy theories as stories people want to believe. Even so, he argues the ingredients behind them are often real, and that the practical lesson is to be clear about what you know versus what you merely believe.

## Key points

- **Russian intelligence continuity.** Spence rates the Russian services, from the Tsarist Okhrana through the Cheka and KGB, as the most consistently effective. He says the Okhrana had infiltrated every revolutionary party before 1917 and that its informers went on to staff the early Cheka. He floats, as speculation, that Lenin may have been a witting or unwitting Okhrana asset.
- **MICE recruitment.** He explains the money, ideology, coercion and ego model with examples such as Aldrich Ames and Kim Philby. He ranks ego as the most important motive.
- **Deniability after MKUltra.** Spence describes a 1949 FBI memo on hypnosis and memory implantation. He argues the CIA's lesson from the 1970s scrutiny was to fund such research through private intermediaries for "perfect deniability".
- **Thule Society and the Nazi party.** He argues that Rudolf von Sebottendorff's occult Thule Society, funded by the German army, spawned the German Workers Party. The army then sent Corporal Adolf Hitler in as a trained propagandist, which makes Nazism a "counter-communist" movement from its start.
- **Protocols of the Elders of Zion.** Spence traces the first version to a 1903 St Petersburg newspaper rather than a 1904-05 Okhrana forgery in Paris. He suggests Maurice Joly himself may have written the source text.
- **Manson and the Zodiac.** Following Tom O'Neill's book Chaos, he argues Manson was probably a protected informant. In his view, the Tate-LaBianca killings were copycat murders meant to clear Bobby Beausoleil of the Gary Hinman killing, not a "Helter Skelter" race-war plot.
