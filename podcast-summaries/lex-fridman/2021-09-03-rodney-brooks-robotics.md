## Thesis

Rodney Brooks, former head of MIT CSAIL and co-founder of iRobot, Rethink Robotics and Robust.AI, tells Lex Fridman that people repeatedly over-generalise from impressive demos. He argues that perception, manipulation and deployment in messy environments remain hard, and that progress tends to come when the environment is engineered around the technology. Fridman, more optimistic, pushes back on deep learning, AlphaZero and Tesla Autopilot.

## Key points

- **Computation as a metaphor.** Brooks describes a book in progress, working title "Not Even Wrong", arguing that Turing's definition of computation became the dominant metaphor for brains and AI without clear justification. His example is a drum, which "knows" its stationary points without computing them.
- **Perception is not labelling.** Citing Moravec's paradox, Brooks says evolution spent about 600 million years on perception and movement. Deep learning solved image labelling, not the symbol grounding problem, and colour constancy shows how much context human vision uses.
- **Self-driving cars.** Brooks notes a car drove autonomously on a Munich autobahn in 1987 and says demos have been over-read for 35 years. He predicts the public would not accept autonomous vehicles killing more than about 10 people a year, against roughly 35,000 US road deaths. He expects early deployments in campuses and gated communities, not across San Francisco within 10 years. Fridman says Tesla's data engine and Waymo's driverless rides in Chandler surprised him.
- **iRobot.** Brooks says the company had 14 failed business models before 2002. It built 70,000 Roombas, more than the board had authorised, priced them at $200, and sold out by Christmas. He is proudest of iRobot robots helping at Fukushima in 2011, drawing on about 6,500 robots deployed in Iraq and Afghanistan.
- **Rethink Robotics.** Brooks wanted a $3,000 plastic-geared arm aimed at firms that had never used robots. Switching to metal gearboxes and two arms pushed Baxter to $25,000. Rethink lost $150 million, and foreign-investment (CFIUS) concerns blocked buyers.
- **Advice.** Brooks tells young people that real impact means making many unsafe decisions and sticking with them for a long time.
