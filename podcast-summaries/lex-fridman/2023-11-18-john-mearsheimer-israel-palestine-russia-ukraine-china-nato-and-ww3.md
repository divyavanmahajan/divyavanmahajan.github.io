## Thesis

John Mearsheimer, a University of Chicago international relations scholar, argues that states have no higher authority to turn to, so they maximise relative power to survive, and that this structure explains most great power conflict. He applies that lens to Ukraine, Gaza and Taiwan, often against Western conventional wisdom, while Lex Fridman repeatedly pushes back with the case for leadership, trust and empathy.

## Key points

- **Offensive realism.** Mearsheimer says power rests on population size and wealth, which turn into military strength. He separates his structural realism from Hans Morgenthau's human nature version, and sees Hitler and Napoleon as rare "congenital aggressors" layered on top of structural pressures.
- **Ukraine.** He argues that NATO expansion, especially the April 2008 Bucharest pledge, was the main cause of the war, and claims there is no evidence Putin wanted all of Ukraine, citing a force of at most 190,000 troops. He says the US and Britain told Zelensky to walk away from the March 2022 Istanbul and Naftali Bennett talks, and expects at best a frozen conflict.
- **Nuclear risk.** If Russia were losing, Mearsheimer believes it might use a few nuclear weapons as a "manipulation of risk" signal, a term he credits to Thomas Schelling, with unknown escalation dynamics.
- **Israel and Gaza.** He traces October 7 to the occupation, calls Israel's Gaza campaign a massacre but not a genocide, and believes a two-state solution, though the only workable outcome, is now out of reach. He defends his book The Israel Lobby, written with Steve Walt, as a description of an ordinary interest group.
- **China and Taiwan.** Mearsheimer thinks the Taiwan Strait makes invasion hard and says the US should defend Taiwan to protect its alliances and keep China's navy inside the first island chain. Deterrence does not require winning, he argues, only making Chinese victory costly.
- **US prospects.** He is optimistic about the next century because immigration offsets low birth rates, and he disputes Samuel Huntington's claim that Hispanic Americans are failing to integrate.
