## Thesis

Ye, the artist formerly known as Kanye West, presents himself as "a being with engineering opportunities" and argues that his recent tweet came from years of bad treatment in the music and fashion industries, which he attributes to Jewish business people. Lex Fridman, who says he is Jewish and lost family in the Holocaust, challenges this throughout, calling such group language a dog whistle and urging Ye to name individuals and build alternatives. Near the end, Ye offers an apology to the Jewish people he hurt.

## Key points

- **Engineering over history.** Ye says schools should teach only engineering, not history or other "subjective" subjects. Fridman counters that history, from Nazi Germany to Stalin and Mao, shows how engineering can serve atrocities.
- **The central dispute.** Ye repeats claims about Jewish control of media and record labels and compares abortion to the Holocaust. Fridman rejects both, says the phrasing echoes Joseph Goebbels, and argues that talking about groups breeds hate.
- **Business grievances.** Ye describes a proposed Louis Vuitton group backing deal that he says Bernard Arnault withdrew, and says he spent $5-10 million on Balenciaga while being paid nothing. He proposes comparing the contracts of the top 10 talents in sports, film and music to rewrite industry terms.
- **Hiring and tribe.** Ye says his Stem Player company had about 60 full-time staff but only two Black engineers, and that he now feels responsible for hiring Black engineers together. Fridman says engineers he knows want to be judged as individuals.
- **Trust and family.** Ye talks about his separation from Kim Kardashian and tells Fridman he does not trust him; Fridman says that hurt. Ye says he trusts only God.
- **Platforms and legacy.** Ye plans to build Parler alongside Elon Musk's Twitter and describes housing designs and land in Calabasas and Wyoming. He says he hopes to be forgotten, as good designs become ubiquitous, and closes by calling the tweet a mistake.

## Quotes
