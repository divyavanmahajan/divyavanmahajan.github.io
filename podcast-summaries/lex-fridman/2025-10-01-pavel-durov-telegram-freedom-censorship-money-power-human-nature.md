## Thesis

Pavel Durov tells Lex Fridman that fear and greed are the main enemies of freedom, and that he has built his life and Telegram to be immune to both. He describes his arrest in France and pressure from Russia, Iran and French intelligence. He argues that owning 100% of Telegram and running a small, automated engineering team lets him refuse any request to hand over private messages or censor peaceful political speech.

## Key points

- **Lean engineering.** Durov says Telegram's core engineering team is about 40 people running almost 100,000 servers. Keeping teams small forces automation, and he argues that "humans are attack vectors". Encryption keys are split across jurisdictions, and he says Telegram has never shared a private message.
- **Arrest in France.** Durov was detained in August of the previous year, read about 15 charges and held for almost four days. He says French intelligence later asked him to block conservative channels before the Romanian election, and channels in Moldova. He refused and made the requests public.
- **Russia, Iran and Apple.** When Russia banned Telegram in 2018, Telegram rotated millions of IP addresses through its "Digital Resistance" proxies. Apple blocked updates for weeks and approved one 15 minutes before Durov's deadline to leave the Russian App Store. He estimates about 50 million users in Iran despite the ban there.
- **Money without targeting.** Telegram Premium has over 15 million subscribers and will bring in more than half a billion dollars this year. Ads are based on context rather than personal data, which Durov estimates leaves 80% of value on the table. Mini apps pay a 5% commission, and gifts run on TON.
- **Design and speed.** Durov describes vector stickers of 20-30 kilobytes running at 60 frames per second, a four-colour animated background, and hiring through coding contests rather than from big companies.
- **Discipline and poisoning.** He avoids alcohol, pills and phones and does 300 push-ups and 300 squats each morning. He describes, for the first time, an apparent poisoning in spring 2018 that left him unable to walk for two weeks.
