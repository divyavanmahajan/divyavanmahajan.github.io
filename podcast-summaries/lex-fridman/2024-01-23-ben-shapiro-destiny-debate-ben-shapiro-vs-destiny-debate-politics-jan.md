## Thesis

Ben Shapiro argues for local, limited government and family structure as the root of social problems, and backs Trump on policy despite his rhetoric because he believes the institutional guardrails held on January 6. Destiny (Steven Bonnell) argues that government can fix problems at the margin, that Trump is uniquely divisive, and that his attempt to block the transfer of power should disqualify him from support.

## Key points

- **Schools and families.** Destiny asks for minimum school standards such as air conditioning and free meals; Shapiro, citing Roland Fryer, says two-parent households matter most and LAUSD shows money alone does not lift outcomes. Destiny calls this a "conservative merry-go-round".
- **Trump versus Biden.** Shapiro credits Trump with a better pre-COVID economy and foreign policy and blames Biden for inflation and deficits. Destiny points to the CHIPS Act, IRA and infrastructure bill passed with a 50-50 Senate, and blames Trump's Doha Accords for the Afghanistan exit.
- **Iran and Israel.** Shapiro says Biden's early tilt toward Iran, including delisting the Houthis, encouraged Hamas's October 7 attack and Red Sea shipping strikes; he wants containment. Destiny backs diplomacy with Iran and warns that a civilian toll of 40,000-60,000 in Gaza could reverse Arab normalisation.
- **Ukraine.** Shapiro says Biden outsourced war aims to Zelenskyy when a deal ceding Crimea, Luhansk and Donetsk was likely anyway. Destiny defends the NATO coalition and says early predictions had Kyiv falling in two weeks.
- **January 6.** Destiny lists the Eastman fake-elector plan, pressure on Pence and calls to Raffensperger as a plot against the transfer of power. Shapiro calls Trump's conduct wrong but says neither the legal test for incitement nor insurrection was met, and opposes removing him from ballots under the 14th Amendment.
- **Wokeism and universities.** Shapiro traces DEI "equity" to postmodernism and calls non-STEM higher education a costly IQ-sorting scam. Destiny agrees the ideas went too far but blames conservatives for abandoning academia and opposes separate conservative universities.
