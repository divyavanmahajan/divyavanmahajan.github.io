## Thesis

Anthony Kaldellis, author of "The New Roman Empire", argues that "Byzantium" is a label invented later: its people were Roman citizens of an unbroken Roman state. He calls it a "monarchical republic" in which emperors had no right to the throne and so had to keep their subjects content. Its crises came from outside, he says, while its long stretches of recovery came from within.

## Key points

- **Three swift crises.** Kaldellis names the Arab conquests of the 630s, the Seljuk conquest of Asia Minor in the 1070s and the Fourth Crusade in 1204. The rest of the history, he says, is slow, steady growth.
- **A perpetual referendum.** With about 120 civil wars in a thousand years, all about who held power and none about ideology, emperors watched crowds in the Hippodrome closely. In the 1190s Alexius III dropped a proposed "German tax" when the crowd protested.
- **Citizenship and taxation.** Caracalla's edict of 212 made all free inhabitants citizens, when at most a third had been before, and within a generation the emperors were provincials. Diocletian taxed Italy and censused every taxable asset. Kaldellis names taxation as the single most important factor and says there were no isolated peasants outside the system.
- **Constantinople and Christianity.** Kaldellis estimates the city grew from about 25,000 to 500,000 people in two centuries through migration, starting with roughly 2,500 senators and their households. He sees Constantine's conversion, when Christians were perhaps 10% of the empire, as a personal choice; in his reading the empire absorbed the church rather than the church conquering Rome.
- **Justinian, mixed record.** He credits Justinian with the Corpus Juris Civilis (compiled under Tribonian) and the Hagia Sophia, but blames him for the Nika massacre of more than 30,000 people and for overextension. He doubts claims that the 541 plague killed half the population.
- **The end.** By 1300-1350, after losing Asia Minor, civil wars, Serbian expansion and the Black Death, it was "game over". There were no separatist provinces apart from Bulgaria.
