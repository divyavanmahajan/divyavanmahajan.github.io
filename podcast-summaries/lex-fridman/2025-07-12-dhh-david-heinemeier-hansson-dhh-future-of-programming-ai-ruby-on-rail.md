## Thesis

David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH), creator of Ruby on Rails and CTO of 37signals, argues that much of modern software is needlessly complex and that the remedy is tools and organisations built for human beings: beautiful, succinct languages like Ruby, integrated monoliths, two-person teams with no managers, and owning your own servers. He uses AI every day as a tutor and pair programmer, but he refuses to let it write his code, because he believes the skill is learned through typing.

## Key points

- **AI as pair programmer, not driver.** DHH says that letting Cursor-style tools drive left him "half a percent dumber" while building his Bash-based Omakub setup. He doubts the Anthropic CEO's claim that 90% of code will be AI-written by year end. Lex Fridman counters that vibe coding with careful correction could become a real skill.
- **Ruby and Rails.** He credits Matz's focus on programmer happiness and trust in programmers, defends dynamic typing (Turbo dropped TypeScript), and cites Shopify's roughly 5-million-line monolith serving about one million requests per second on Black Friday.
- **Small over big.** Basecamp and HEY are each about 100,000 lines of Ruby. HEY shipped 40 kilobytes of JavaScript, against roughly 28 megabytes for Gmail. The first Basecamp took 400 billed hours. 37signals' default team is one programmer and one designer.
- **Leaving the cloud.** AWS spend peaked at about $3.2-3.4 million a year. 37signals moved seven applications onto Dell servers in just over six months without hiring anyone, and projects savings of about $10 million over five years.
- **Platform fights.** DHH describes the HEY dispute over Apple's 30% cut and credits Epic, which spent over $100 million in legal fees, with forcing open link-out payments. He criticises Matt Mullenweg's demands on WP Engine as breaking the spirit of open-source licences.
- **Life outside work.** He argues for 40-hour weeks, family as the antidote to loneliness, and flow, which he finds most reliably in endurance racing at Le Mans.
