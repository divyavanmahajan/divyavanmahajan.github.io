## Thesis

Author and anarchist Michael Malice, dressed up with Lex Fridman for a holiday episode, discusses his self-published book "The White Pill: A Tale of Good and Evil", a history of evil in the 20th century centred on the Soviet Union. Malice argues that cynicism is a trap: totalitarian regimes relied on lies, scapegoats and fear, yet the Iron Curtain fell quickly and mostly without bloodshed. Fridman largely agrees, while pushing back on the role of ideology, journalistic integrity and Ukrainian-Russian relations.

## Key points

- **The appeal of socialism.** Malice steelmans socialism as a promise that no one falls through the cracks, and says he could easily have believed in it at the time. He also argues that central planning without a price mechanism, and one state monopoly on news, necessarily bring authoritarianism.
- **Lenin, Trotsky and Stalin.** Malice describes Lenin as a zealot with a pragmatic streak, Trotsky as the strategist undone by his own "the party is always right" logic, and Stalin as the party politician who pushed "socialism in one country".
- **Holodomor and the press.** Malice says the 1932-33 famine in Ukraine was deliberate, citing Anne Applebaum's "Red Famine", and contrasts Gareth Jones and Malcolm Muggeridge with the New York Times' Walter Duranty. Fridman argues the failure of integrity came from the journalists' peer group back home, not just Soviet censors.
- **Terror as a system.** Malice describes the Great Terror as an attack on every bond outside the state: family, ethnicity, foreign contacts. He calls NKVD chief Lavrentiy Beria one of the most evil people who ever lived.
- **Transparency today.** Both praise the release of the Twitter Files and camera phones as checks on power; Fridman stresses this should not be a left-versus-right issue.
- **1989.** Malice gives Gorbachev more credit than anyone for the peaceful collapse, noting that soldiers repeatedly refused to fire on civilians. He also argues self-publishing via Amazon suits established authors, giving about six times the profit.
