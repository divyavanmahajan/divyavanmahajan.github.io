## Thesis

Katherine de Kleer, a professor of planetary science and astronomy at Caltech, tells Lex Fridman that moons are planet-like worlds and that the extreme ones, above all Jupiter's moon Io, test our Earth-centric understanding of planets. She studies them mostly with telescopes and stays skeptical of headline claims about life on Venus or alien origins for 'Oumuamua. Fridman keeps returning to alien life and human exploration.

## Key points

- **Pluto and definitions.** De Kleer explains that Pluto lost planet status because it has not cleared its orbit in the Kuiper belt, the same fate Ceres had in the asteroid belt. She says classification does not affect her interest.
- **Io's tidal volcanism.** Orbital resonances between Io, Europa and Ganymede flex Io by a couple of hundred metres per orbit, melting its mantle. It has hundreds of active volcanoes and very fluid lava, which she sees as a glimpse of early Earth. Telescopes resolve about a dozen volcanoes at a time and allow decades-long monitoring that spacecraft cannot.
- **Where life might be.** She ranks the subsurface oceans of Europa and Enceladus, where water touches rock, and Titan's organic-rich atmosphere as the most plausible habitats. Enceladus's geysers could be sampled by flying through them.
- **Skeptical on claims.** De Kleer does not believe phosphine was detected on Venus, calling it a single weak line. For 'Oumuamua she prefers ice-shard explanations (nitrogen or hydrogen ice sublimating, like a worn bar of soap) over aliens, citing Sagan's standard on extraordinary claims.
- **Asteroids and Mars.** Some asteroids are fragments of planetesimal cores and mantles; Apophis will pass closer than some geosynchronous satellites in 2029. She expects humans to colonize other planets eventually, but not Mars in her lifetime, because self-sustaining resources are the hard problem.
- **Career advice.** She says scientists should enjoy their day-to-day techniques as much as their questions; Fridman links this to David Foster Wallace's idea of being unborable.
