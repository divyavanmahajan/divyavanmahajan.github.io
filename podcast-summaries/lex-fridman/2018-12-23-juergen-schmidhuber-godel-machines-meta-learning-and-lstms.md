## Thesis

Juergen Schmidhuber, co-director of the IDSIA lab and co-creator of LSTMs, tells Lex Fridman that general intelligence will turn out to be a short program: a system that learns to improve its own learning algorithm and is driven by curiosity, measured as progress in compressing its observations. He argues that today's passive pattern recognition is a small slice of what is coming, and that machines which shape their own data through actions will transform most of the economy.

## Key points

- **True meta-learning.** Schmidhuber traces the idea to his 1987 diploma thesis on recursive self-improvement. He calls much of today's meta-learning simple transfer learning. Gödel machines (his 2004 work) and Marcus Hutter's fastest universal problem solver are optimal in theory but carry a constant proof-search overhead too large for everyday problems.
- **Science as compression.** He uses Kepler, Newton and Einstein to argue that each scientific advance lets data be predicted, and therefore stored, more compactly. He doubts that quantum randomness is truly random.
- **Curiosity and PowerPlay.** Agents receive an intrinsic reward in proportion to their compression progress. PowerPlay searches for pairs of problems and solutions, always picking the easiest new problem the current solver cannot yet solve. He sees consciousness as a by-product: a world model that compresses data ends up modelling the agent itself.
- **LSTMs and depth.** Schmidhuber credits students Sepp Hochreiter, Felix Gers and Alex Graves (CTC, used for speech recognition on Android phones). He says a 2006 lab paper showed LSTMs learning to look back more than 10 million steps.
- **The next AI wave.** He says current AI, used for advertising, touches only one or two percent of the world economy. His company NNAISENSE trained small Audi models to park through reinforcement learning, and he expects robots that learn from watching people to reshape production.
- **Jobs and the far future.** He notes that agriculture went from 60% of workers 200 years ago to about 1%, yet new jobs appeared. He expects AI civilisations to expand into space and lose interest in humans.

## Quotes
