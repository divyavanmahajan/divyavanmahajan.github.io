## Thesis

Matt Botvinick, Director of Neuroscience Research at DeepMind, tells Lex Fridman that the brain is for producing adaptive behaviour, so psychology and neuroscience should be treated as one science. He describes a "virtuous circle" in which AI ideas such as meta-learning and distributional reinforcement learning suggest how the brain works, and brain evidence in turn validates AI methods. He closes by arguing that AI research should study warmth and human preferences, not only capability.

## Key points

- **One science of behaviour.** Botvinick compares the brain to the kidney: first ask what it is for, then how it does it. He likens psychological metaphors such as attention or memory retrieval to Mendelian genes, which were useful before Watson and Crick supplied a physical mechanism.
- **Prefrontal cortex and flexibility.** Drawing on the Russian neuropsychologist Luria, he says the prefrontal cortex lets people override habits, as in offering an elbow bump instead of a handshake. He believes current AI systems lack this flexibility and that the field is turning to abstraction and cognitive control.
- **Emergent meta-learning.** In his group's 2018 work, a recurrent network trained with reinforcement learning on many related two-button tasks learned to solve new ones with its weights frozen: slow weight changes produced a fast learning algorithm in the network's activity. He proposes that the prefrontal cortex works this way, with dopamine shaping its synapses.
- **Distributional dopamine.** With Will Dabney and Naoshige Uchida at Harvard, his team tested whether dopamine encodes a distribution of possible rewards rather than a single prediction error. Botvinick says their predictions were fairly clearly confirmed, but replication by other labs is still pending.
- **Human-agent interaction.** Botvinick says making life better for humans raises questions that go beyond engineering into economics, social choice theory and politics, especially when people want different things. He cites Susan Fiske's two dimensions of competence and warmth, and suggests an AI that people trust as warm would be the ultimate Turing test.
