## Thesis

Recorded outdoors at Belcampo's farm in northern California, Anya Fernald, chef and Belcampo co-founder, tells Lex Fridman that how meat is raised shapes its flavour, its nutrition and its environmental impact. She argues that regenerative grazing can sequester carbon and treat animals well, but that scaling it requires human attention that industrial farming has stripped out, which is where she sees a role for technology such as AI.

## Key points

- **Cooking meat is mostly science.** Fernald says two questions decide technique: how much collagenous connective tissue the muscle has (a brisket needs low, slow, moist heat; a rib eye can go hot and fast) and its surface-area-to-volume ratio. Connective tissue matters more than fat content.
- **Regenerative grazing.** She describes grazing that damages grass just enough to trigger regrowth, which draws carbon into the soil. She cites estimates that converting degraded rangeland could sequester 20 to 40 percent of the world's carbon, and says Belcampo's 25,000 acres have been third-party verified as carbon negative.
- **The scaling problem.** Fernald argues regenerative farming depends on skilled people watching the land, and that AI and better data collection could automate many decision points. She wants the tech community involved.
- **Humane standards.** Her criteria are an evolutionary diet, mostly outdoor life, low density, and contact with young and herd. Ruminants should eat grass; chickens and pigs are omnivores and need some grain.
- **Grass-fed labels.** She warns that "grass-fed" is unverified and often refers to an early phase of life; buyers should look for "100 percent grass-fed" or "grass-fed and finished". Belcampo's beef reaches omega-3 to omega-6 ratios near 1:1 versus about 1:20 to 1:30 for conventional beef, she says.
- **Career lessons.** Fernald recalls working as a cheese maker in Sicily for a boss later imprisoned for embezzlement, feeling isolated as a woman running a meat company, and advises young people to grow through discomfort.
