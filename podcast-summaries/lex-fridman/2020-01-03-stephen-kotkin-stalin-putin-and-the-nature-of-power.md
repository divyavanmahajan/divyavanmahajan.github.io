## Thesis

Stephen Kotkin, a Princeton historian writing a three-volume biography of Stalin, tells Lex Fridman that the central question in politics is whether executive power is constrained by institutions. In his view, leaders without checks make more mistakes and cannot correct them. He applies this to Stalin's rise, Putin's Russia and the split between Marxism and social democracy.

## Key points

- **Not everyone craves unconstrained power.** Kotkin points to George Washington, who chose to build institutions instead of taking more power. The only checks on Stalin, he says, were circumstantial, such as distance and the hours in a day.
- **Putin's support and its limits.** Kotkin credits Putin with understanding the people who lost out after communism, and says the Medvedev "castling" briefly won over urban voters, with Crimea later giving a popularity boost. He argues that the lack of real alternatives props up that support, while state officials are disillusioned by corruption and decline.
- **Growth in the first two terms.** Kotkin says growth averaged about 7% a year for a decade and was not mainly due to oil, which averaged $35 and then $70 a barrel. He credits liberalising reforms, Chinese demand reviving Soviet-era industry, and the 1998 rouble crash. He also notes that five to ten million people have left since 1991.
- **Stalin's rise was contingent.** Kotkin calls October 1917 a coup by the Bolsheviks against the rest of the left. Lenin created the post of General Secretary for Stalin and, less than six weeks later, had a major stroke. Stalin was a true believer, he says, but also used lies, slander and violence.
- **Marx and social democracy.** Kotkin says every attempt to abolish markets, private property and parliaments produced tyranny and shortages, unlike social democrats, who regulate and redistribute. He presents this as history, not as his own political position.
- **Power as a drug.** Kotkin says the people he most admires are those who walk away from power. He would ask Putin where Russia will be in 25 or 50 years.
