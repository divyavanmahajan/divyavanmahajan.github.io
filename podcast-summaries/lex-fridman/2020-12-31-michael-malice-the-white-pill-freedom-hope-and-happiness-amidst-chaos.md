## Thesis

Michael Malice, an anarchist author and podcaster, presents the idea behind his forthcoming book The White Pill: against the despair of the "black pill", history shows that good people can defeat evil systems, and that is reason enough to fight. Lex Fridman probes him on Nazi Germany, Stalin, North Korea, anarchism and secession, often pushing for more care and nuance where Malice favours provocation.

## Key points

- **The white pill.** Malice says many "red-pilled" people became cynical, and his book answers them with history: the 1930s, when Western intellectuals excused Stalin, and the 1980s, when the Cold War was won without firing a shot. He recommends Deborah Lipstadt's Beyond Belief and Anne Applebaum's Red Famine.
- **Self-publishing.** Malice calls traditional publishing a slow cartel. He says Dear Reader, his Kickstarter-funded North Korea book, earns about six dollars a copy through Amazon against about one dollar through a publisher, and he will self-publish The White Pill.
- **North Korea.** Drawing on Dear Reader, Malice describes how Kim Jong-il propaganda presents him as the only competent person in the country, for example by claiming he could "shrink time" by multitasking, which makes the state seem impossible without its leader.
- **Anarchism and security.** Arguing against Yaron Brook's objectivist case for a state monopoly on force, Malice points to eBay, insurers settling car accidents, and relations between countries as working examples of order without a single ruler. Fridman questions whether this holds at scale and when violence is involved.
- **Provocation versus care.** Discussing Curtis Yarvin, Fridman says careless talk on race put him off, while Malice says he uses controversial framing on purpose to filter out people who react emotionally.
- **Secession and 2021.** Malice argues that social media drives views to extremes and that peaceful secession, like Brexit, is preferable to forced union. He predicts a chaotic 2021. Fridman, calling himself a romantic, would rather divided ideas compete within one country.
