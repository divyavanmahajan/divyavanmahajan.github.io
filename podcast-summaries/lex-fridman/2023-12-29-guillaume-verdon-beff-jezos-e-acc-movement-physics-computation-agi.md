## Thesis

Guillaume Verdon, the physicist behind the @BasedBeffJezos account and founder of Extropic, tells Lex Fridman that out-of-equilibrium thermodynamics biases the universe towards systems that capture more energy and grow. From that he builds e/acc: keep variance high, keep AI open and competitive, and treat concentrated power, not AI itself, as the main risk. Fridman repeatedly pushes back on whether markets and competition are enough to handle unintended consequences.

## Key points

- **Doxxing.** Verdon says Forbes linked his identity to the account after he passed 50K followers, using voice analysis plus SEC filings, and contacted his investors first. He argues pseudonymity keeps a marketplace of ideas efficient.
- **Market over regulation.** Responding to Fridman's reading of the Bengio-Hinton paper "Managing AI Risk in an Era of Rapid Progress", Verdon calls the one-third safety budget arbitrary, expects third-party auditors to emerge naturally, and warns of a "government backed AI cartel". He wants "separation of AI and state".
- **Fault tolerance.** Drawing on quantum error correction, Verdon argues centralised control is fragile, citing the OpenAI board crisis, and that hierarchical, local control is more robust. He names OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Meta and Mistral as evidence competition is working.
- **No p(doom).** Verdon rejects p(doom) estimates as biased sampling of scary futures in a chaotic system; he ranks a government-corporate AI oligopoly as the darker timeline.
- **Extropic.** After about eight years in quantum computing, Verdon left over noise and timelines; Extropic, with a 14.1 million seed round and CTO Trevor McCourt from TensorFlow Quantum, is building a thermodynamic computer for generative AI.
- **E/acc versus EA.** Verdon describes e/acc as a thin, forkable framework (citing Vitalik Buterin's d/acc) and contrasts its energy-based measure of progress with EA's hedonic loss function, which he says has "spurious minima".
