## Thesis

In Lex Fridman's 300th episode, recorded days before Fridman left for Ukraine and Russia, Joe Rogan argues that difficulty is something to use: brutal exercise, deliberate hardship and refusing to absorb strangers' opinions are what let him get through the January 2022 attempt to cancel him. The two range across trust, intelligence agencies, UFOs, the craft of stand-up and how to question Vladimir Putin.

## Key points

- **Surviving the controversy.** Rogan says he took less than a gram of mushrooms a day, trained hard, kept doing stand-up and read none of the coverage. He says he gained 2 million subscribers in that period and calls the episode a chance to show character under pressure.
- **Politics and guests.** Rogan says he is not a Trump supporter and has turned down having him on, worrying that a podcast can rehabilitate someone's image. Fridman pushes back that an empathetic long-form conversation would be worth having.
- **UFOs and secrecy.** Rogan finds Bob Lazar's story consistent over 30 years, but says he is now sceptical: he suspects government talk of UFOs is cover for advanced drones. Fridman agrees that the DOD and contractors keep technologies such as stealth secret for a long time.
- **Intelligence agencies.** Fridman describes reading about the KGB and asking whether people around him are agents; Rogan says he has suspicions about some guests and assumes a platform his size would be a target.
- **Comedy.** Rogan names Lenny Bruce and Richard Pryor as the greats, says seeing Pryor's concert film at 15 started his interest, and praises Kill Tony because it rewards only being funny. He dismisses the idea that comedy must be socially conscious.
- **Advice and Putin.** Rogan tells young people to find a passion, as martial arts was for him, and tells Fridman to be someone worth being in a relationship with. On Putin, Rogan would ask about democracy and his health; Fridman thinks Putin wants a legacy and believes he is best for Russia.
