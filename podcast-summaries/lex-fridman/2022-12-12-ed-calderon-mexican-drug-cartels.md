## Thesis

Ed Calderon, who worked in counter-narcotics and protection details in Tijuana, tells Lex Fridman that anyone can be corrupted and that Mexico's drug war persists because corruption runs through police, politicians and the military while cartels fill gaps left by the state. He argues that understanding adversaries as humans, not monsters, is what makes defence work, and that the conflict is now a shared US-Mexico problem.

## Key points

- **Corruption and the police career.** Calderon left medical school after the post-9/11 economic slump and joined a force trained by former special forces men, some of whom later formed Los Zetas. He says honest officers were sidelined, vetting centres (C3s) were dropped, and fired officers suspected of cartel links returned with back pay.
- **How cartels take hold.** He estimates 50 to 70 groups, led by the Sinaloa cartel and the fast-growing New Generation cartel, earning from drugs, extortion and protection. Cartels recruit young people with few options and sometimes build roads and deliver aid, acting as the local government.
- **Weapons and escalation.** Calderon cites the ATF's "Fast and Furious" operation, drones dropping explosives, man-portable missiles, US night vision and .50-calibre rifles used against armoured vehicles.
- **Fentanyl, China and politics.** He says New Generation cartel shipments through Pacific ports continued during COVID, which he reads as precursor supply from China not being stopped, and that Chinese money brokers move cartel funds. He describes Mexico's military taking over federal policing.
- **Adversarial thinking.** Calderon teaches improvised weapons, escaping restraints and counter-kidnapping, and says criminals are the best teachers. To train a counter-ambush team, he argues, you make its members good ambushers.
- **PTSD and sobriety.** He recounts losing his brother at 13, a childhood friend killed in a cartel shootout, and his mother just before he resigned. He describes heavy drinking in the US and says he stopped at the start of COVID after his daughter asked him to.
