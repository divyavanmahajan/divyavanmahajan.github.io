## Thesis

Rosalind Picard, MIT Media Lab professor and co-founder of Affectiva and Empatica, tells Lex Fridman that recognizing emotion is as hard as she predicted, but that the more urgent issue is how the technology is used. She argues for consent, user control and targeted regulation, and wants researchers to build AI that extends the abilities of people who are left behind rather than making the rich richer.

## Key points

- **Clippy as a lesson.** Picard says Microsoft's paperclip assistant was smart about context but "emotionally unintelligent": it kept smiling and dancing at angry users, and Bill Gates got a standing ovation when he said it was going away.
- **Surveillance worries.** Picard says use of face-reading technology in places like China made her pull back on some research. She says Affectiva turned away money for reading affect without prior informed consent, and her lab built a way to jam heart rate and respiration signals in video as well as extract them.
- **Regulation she would accept.** Although she generally prefers "carrot" to "stick", Picard wants people to own their data, lie-detector rules extended to emotion recognition in hiring, medical-grade protection for mood predictions, and a firewall between agents that read emotion and agents that sell things.
- **What sensors can read.** A laptop camera can detect heart rate, breathing and heart rate variability from a neutral face, Picard says, but thoughts and nuanced feelings stay private. In studies with New England college students, combined wearable and phone data forecast next-day stress, mood and health; the wearable alone was better than 80% accurate.
- **Epilepsy and Embrace.** An unusual one-wrist skin conductance spike in a child with autism led to seizure detection. Empatica's Embrace is FDA cleared, and Picard links large skin conductance responses to the post-seizure brain flattening that precedes SUDEP. She calls FDA clearance far harder than publishing in Neurology.
- **Limits of AI and science.** Picard doubts AI will match love with good people, prefers AI as a helper, and argues that "scientism" is myopic because history, philosophy and love are also ways of knowing.
