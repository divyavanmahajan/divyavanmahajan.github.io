## Thesis

Chef and entrepreneur Kimbal Musk tells Lex Fridman that food is a gift we give ourselves three times a day, and that sitting down to eat together creates a vulnerability and connection that bars, nightclubs and online arguments do not. He ties this to his life: a violent childhood in South Africa, a gruelling cooking school, a near-paralysing accident in 2010, and years beside his brother Elon at Zip2, Tesla and SpaceX.

## Key points

- **Growing up.** Musk describes seeing a man stabbed on a train at about 16 and watching Elon being beaten at school. He says human life was valued far less there than in America, and that his father mainly taught him what not to do.
- **Cooking and community.** He started cooking at 11 with a roast chicken because his mother was a poor cook. At The Kitchen (Boulder since 2004, plus Denver and Chicago, with Austin planned) he favours community tables and single-conversation dinners that start with simple questions, such as a guest's middle name.
- **Training.** The French Culinary Institute cost as much as Harvard; 25 students started an 18-month programme and six graduated. Master chef Alain Sailhac taught him a 90-second scrambled egg he still makes for his kids each morning.
- **The accident.** In 2010, as an unhappy tech CEO, he ruptured his spine at C6 and C7 on a tubing run in Jackson Hole. A clear inner voice told him to work on kids and food; he resigned from the hospital and now runs the nonprofit Big Green, which works with 150 nonprofits.
- **Zip2.** He says he and Elon were the first people to see vector maps and door-to-door directions on the internet, using Navtech data. Zip2 became a top-100 website but made no money and was sold for cash to the owner of AltaVista.
- **Tesla and SpaceX.** A founding Tesla board member, he backed putting an electric motor in a sports car and says the company was nearly bankrupt two or three times around the Model 3. On Kwajalein, after a failed SpaceX launch, he cooked baked beans for the team.
