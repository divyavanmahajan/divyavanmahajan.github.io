## Thesis

Stephen Schwarzman, CEO and co-founder of Blackstone, tells Lex Fridman that it is easier to succeed by going after large, unique opportunities, because scale attracts talent and leaves room to recover from mistakes. He applies the same logic to philanthropy, explaining his $350 million gift to MIT's new College of Computing as a bid to strengthen US competitiveness in AI and to build an ethics framework so AI does not repeat the unintended consequences of the internet and social media.

## Key points

- **Pattern recognition as seeing the lint.** Schwarzman says he spots opportunities by noticing the one discordant fact that does not fit; two such facts usually point to where things are changing.
- **Solve other people's problems.** He describes preparing for meetings by imagining the other person's biggest unresolved problem, citing a ten-minute conversation with President Bush at the White House in the early 1990s. He credits his middle-class Philadelphia values for staying humble.
- **Philanthropy as starting something new.** Schwarzman says he does not set out to give money away; he builds new organisations around big issues. At MIT the plan was to double computer science faculty and create the first "AI enabled" university, hoping rivals would follow.
- **AI ethics and four drivers.** He wants MIT to convene universities, AI companies, governments and the media, and recalls internet pioneers at the college's launch saying they had made a mistake by not anticipating social media.
- **China and US education.** Schwarzman says a senior Chinese official told him every schoolchild in China will learn computer science, while he guesses five percent or less of US children get such exposure across over 3,000 school districts. He calls for a federal "moonshot" on AI, despite trillion-dollar deficits.
- **Advice for founders.** Starting a business is "a rough ride", he says; founders should not go it alone, pointing to Jack Ma's account of Alibaba nearly dying twice and to the co-founder pairs behind Google, Microsoft and Apple.
