## Thesis

Jason Calacanis, angel investor and host of This Week in Startups and the All-In podcast, tells Lex Fridman that the best returns come from outlandish ideas judged by what happens if they work. He defends capitalism and competition as the engines of progress, says ambitious startups demand sacrifice rather than work-life balance, and argues that loyalty and friendship, not money, are what last.

## Key points

- **Robinhood and WallStreetBets.** Calacanis, an early Robinhood investor, recalls Vlad Tenev pitching him at Antonio's Nut House in Palo Alto with a free trading app for millennials. He says Robinhood paused trading because of a liquidity crunch and collateral demands, not hedge fund pressure, but communicated it poorly. This was the root of his on-air clash with Chamath Palihapitiya, which he says they later settled privately.
- **Capitalism and China.** Calacanis argues competition improves products (he cites chess.com and Tesla pushing carmakers to go electric) and warns that if China "wins capitalism" it would be an existential threat. He also argues that paid COVID challenge trials, 1,000 volunteers at $1 million each, could have ended the pandemic sooner.
- **Leadership and sacrifice.** He compares people who sign up for Elon Musk's or Jeff Bezos's companies to "samurai" and says work-life balance in a startup is a ridiculous concept. He calls Mark Zuckerberg a "marauder" who removes friction to drive growth without thinking through consequences such as automatic group additions.
- **Venture maths.** Calacanis explains that a $300 million fund backing 30 companies needs one or two to return the fund, and that seven of ten startups go to zero. His syndicate put $378,000 into Calm at a $5 million valuation; he says it is now worth $2 billion.
- **Social feeds.** Calacanis blames engagement-ranked feeds for outrage culture. Fridman wants users to control their own feed algorithms.
- **Friendship.** Calacanis recounts Musk telling him in 2008 that Tesla had two weeks of cash, then mailing two $50,000 checks to reserve Model S cars, and receiving reservation number one. He ends by urging listeners to write gratitude letters.
