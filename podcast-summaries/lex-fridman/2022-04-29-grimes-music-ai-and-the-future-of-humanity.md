## Thesis

Grimes, the musician and producer, tells Lex Fridman that humanity is moving from survival of the fittest to "intelligent design": computers have changed our brains enough that she calls us Homo techno, and we now have a responsibility to choose how we evolve. She argues that optimistic, protopian art, a broader idea of profit that counts social good, and lighter bureaucracy are the tools for doing that well. Fridman largely agrees, adding that cynicism is often mistaken for insight.

## Key points

- **Producers are underrated.** Grimes says music production is a new, mostly self-taught art form whose practitioners get little credit, and places herself in the "post Rick Rubin", Skrillex-style school where one person is engineer, producer and artist. She calls art a collective conversation with earlier artists rather than the work of a lone genius.
- **Spotify and middlemen.** Citing Daniel Ek, she says the CD era supported about 20,000 well-paid artists while Spotify lets about a million make a decent living. She thinks the real problem is inefficiency, and describes her manager's app that reads contracts and automates management, publishing and royalties.
- **Protopia over dystopia.** Recommending Iain Banks' "Surface Detail" and crediting the term protopia to protopiafutures, she argues that "life imitates art", so society needs more hopeful visions. Motherhood, she says, made her an optimist and made her want "Totoro"-quality art for children aged one to three.
- **Profit and motherhood.** Grimes argues capitalism does not recognise motherhood as valuable work and floats "social capitalism" or a motherhood DAO; Fridman agrees that profit could include long-term wellbeing.
- **Bureaucracy and technology.** She proposes sunset clauses on laws, pointing to permit delays and rising housing costs in Austin. Fridman criticises politicians who show no curiosity about how AI and recommender systems work.
- **Supply chains and dark ages.** Grimes names supply chain collapse, as in the Bronze Age collapse, as the most likely way society could go wrong. She declines to give opinions on Elon possibly acquiring Twitter, saying she is too close to the situation.
