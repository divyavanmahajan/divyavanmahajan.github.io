## Thesis

Michael Saylor, CEO of MicroStrategy, tells Lex Fridman that economists treat inflation as a single number when it is really the change in price of every product, service and asset, and that this error hides a steady loss of wealth from currency holders. He presents Bitcoin as the first digital property that no person or government controls, and argues that layered networks on top of it can bring "conservation of energy" to cyberspace. Fridman questions him on securities law, conflicts of interest and how practical Bitcoin is in war zones.

## Key points

- **Inflation as a vector.** Saylor says CPI is a synthetic basket that governments can redefine. He points to house prices up 19.2% year over year on the Case-Shiller index against a reported 7.9% CPI. He also cites a house bought for $100,000 in 1930 that Zillow now values at $30.5 million, which he works out as about 6.5% a year.
- **Government policy.** In Saylor's view, policy makers mean well, but every policy has to be paid for and is "inflationary". He says the US dollar has lost 99.7% of its purchasing power.
- **Property versus security.** Saylor argues that Bitcoin is property, like a barrel of oil, because no one controls its supply of 21 million coins. That, he says, is why he can promote Bitcoin but not MicroStrategy (MSTR) stock. Fridman pushes back that securities law is not the same thing as ethics.
- **Layers.** Saylor describes layer one as slow, secure settlement, Lightning as a non-custodial layer two, and exchanges such as Coinbase or Cash App as custodial layer three. He says MicroStrategy's $250 million purchase in August 2020 began institutional adoption.
- **Orange checks and Lightning walls.** Saylor proposes letting Twitter users post a small Bitcoin deposit to get verified. Accounts found to be bots or scammers would lose the deposit.
- **Price and focus.** Saylor says that if Bitcoin is valued as digital property it could reach $5 million a coin (a $100 trillion asset). His advice to young people is to focus, guard their time and "upgrade the world".
