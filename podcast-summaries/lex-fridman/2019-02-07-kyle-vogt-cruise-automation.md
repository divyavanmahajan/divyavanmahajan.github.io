## Thesis

Kyle Vogt, co-founder of Twitch and President and CTO of Cruise Automation, tells Lex Fridman that self-driving cars met his test for a ten-year commitment: hard technology, direct social benefit and a large business. He argues that the remaining work is not one breakthrough but thousands of small improvements to beat human drivers, and that partnering with General Motors frees Cruise from reinventing car manufacturing.

## Key points

- **From BattleBots to autonomy.** Vogt built fighting robots at 13 or 14, then, after a 10-hour drive across western Kansas, decided a computer could follow lane markers. At MIT his undergraduate DARPA Grand Challenge team failed when its only steering motor died. He calls DARPA's prize one of the most effective uses of taxpayer money he has seen.
- **Justin.tv and Twitch.** He left MIT on a one-way ticket during IAP and wrote the video streaming system for Justin.tv, which spread a live feed across three or four cellphone modems, before the company became Twitch.
- **Cruise's original plan.** Founded in October 2013 and bought by GM in 2016 for $1 billion, Cruise first built a retrofit highway autopilot on an Audi S4. Vogt says retrofits failed on liability and validation, and because customers drove 20 to 50 different models rather than three.
- **Detroit meets Silicon Valley.** Vogt describes a culture clash between GM's reward for on-time, on-budget programs and Cruise's experimentation, but calls the three years spent learning to work together one of Cruise's greatest assets.
- **Economics and timelines.** He lists vehicle cost, vehicle lifetime and utilisation as the key fleet variables, with ride sharing and delivery as early markets. He guesses hundreds of thousands of driverless vehicles in less than five years, and says 2019 is the year Cruise aims to move from prototype to production.
- **Startup advice.** Vogt credits passion for the core technology, strong co-founders such as Dan Kan, and persistence, and says Y Combinator's peer pressure was worth repeating.
