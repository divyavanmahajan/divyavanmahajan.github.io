## Thesis

Ryan Schiller, a 21-year-old Yale student, built Librex, an anonymous discussion feed for verified members of Ivy League campuses (now adding MIT and Stanford), because he says campus culture has frozen open discourse. He argues that anonymity is a tool rather than the purpose, and that small, school-based communities with light-touch moderation can support difficult conversations without the failures of earlier apps. Lex Fridman broadly supports the mission and adds his own views on honesty, academia and platform power.

## Key points

- **Origin story.** Schiller says a Yale professor told him he stopped teaching his Middle East specialty after a student reported him. He cites a Gallup figure that 61% of students are afraid to speak their minds because of campus climate.
- **Learning to code from zero.** With no technical background, Schiller bought a Mac, followed a Stanford Swift course on YouTube and leaned on Stack Overflow to ship a minimal app. Fridman praises this ship-first approach over premature optimisation.
- **Launching at Dartmouth.** Schiller slept on a stranger's floor, printed about 2,000 posters and slid them under dorm doors; he says about 90% of the campus signed up overnight.
- **Design choices.** Accounts are tied to .edu emails, one per person, with no usernames, only school names, to keep focus on ideas and avoid doxxing. Users have a "forget me" button, and Schiller says Librex refuses to sell user data, which Fridman reports was one reason Y Combinator rejected it in the final rounds.
- **Moderation and Yik Yak.** Volunteer moderators from each school enforce a core rule of no sweeping statements against core identity groups. Schiller contrasts Librex with Yik Yak, which he says was geographic, short-form and grew from 12 to a thousand schools too fast.
- **Personal history.** Schiller describes myocarditis and pericarditis before college, with a 25 to 35% chance of dying, and says the long recovery made him decide to build something he believed in.
