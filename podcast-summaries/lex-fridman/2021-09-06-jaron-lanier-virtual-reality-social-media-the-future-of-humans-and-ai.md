## Thesis

Jaron Lanier, computer scientist, musician and a founder of virtual reality, tells Lex Fridman that technology should stay subordinate to people: AI is a set of tools, not a creature, and social media's problems come from its economic incentives. His proposed fix is "data dignity", where people are paid for the data they contribute. Fridman is more optimistic that platforms and AI can be designed to put human interests first.

## Key points

- **VR's value is the return.** Lanier says the best moment in VR is taking off the headset and seeing the physical world afresh. He cites the first surgical simulator, built in the 1980s with Dr. Joe Rosen at Stanford, where moving from simulation to a real patient was the valuable part.
- **No AI, only algorithms.** Recalling decades of arguments with his mentor Marvin Minsky, Lanier rejects the "computer as creature" framing. He argues that treating AI as conscious makes people worse engineers, and compares faith in AI to faith in Adam Smith's invisible hand.
- **Engagement feedback is crude.** Clicks and screen time only capture fight-or-flight reactions, Lanier says, so users drift towards paranoia and aggression. He reports that YouTube recommendation experiments with high school students usually reached bizarre, paranoid content after about 17 or 18 hops.
- **Data dignity.** Using the groundskeepers on his Berkeley hill as an example, Lanier contrasts a future where robots replace them and they live on basic income with one where they form a data union and improve the robots. He wants people to pay for social media as they pay for Netflix. Fridman proposes user control of data and personal algorithms; Lanier warns that GDPR-style consent favours technical people.
- **Scepticism about Bitcoin.** Lanier calls Bitcoin a recreation of the gold standard and its proof-of-work security an environmental problem, saying he has not seen it create economic growth. Fridman presents the case that it can resist centralised monetary control.
- **Physics, music and mortality.** Lanier describes "The Autodidactic Universe", a paper with Lee Smolin linking physics theories to machine learning, and says he is less afraid of death because believing in the future requires making room for it.
