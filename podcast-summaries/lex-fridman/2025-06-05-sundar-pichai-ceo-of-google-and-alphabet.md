## Thesis

Sundar Pichai, CEO of Google and Alphabet, says AI will be the most important technology humanity has built, because it can improve itself and speed up creation. He says Google was well placed even while commentators called it the loser of the AI race. His reasons: early bets on TPUs, merging Brain and DeepMind, and one shared model stack that serves Search, Waymo, robotics and Android XR. He is optimistic about the risks because he expects humanity to unite as the danger becomes concrete.

## Key points

- **From Chennai to Google.** Pichai describes a five-year wait for a rotary telephone and water delivered by truck during a drought. He says these step changes taught him how much access to technology can change a life.
- **Scaling and compute.** Pichai sees "a lot of headroom" in pre-training, post-training, test-time compute and tool use. Google is compute-limited: it skips Ultra models because each new Pro reaches the previous Ultra's level and costs less to serve. Monthly Gemini tokens grew 50x in a year, from 9.7 trillion to 480 trillion.
- **AGI timing.** He uses Karpathy's term "artificial jagged intelligence" (AJI) for today's uneven models. He expects dramatic progress and its consequences by 2030, with AGI arriving slightly after.
- **Hard decisions.** Pichai names the merger of Brain and DeepMind as the key move. Jeff Dean returned to research and Demis Hassabis took over. Other decisions were investing in TPUs 10 years ago, building a central AI infrastructure team, and putting more money into Waymo when others doubted it. Waymo has completed 10 million paid rides.
- **Search and the web.** AI Mode runs a fan-out of many searches per query and is a separate tab; features that work move into AI Overviews. Pichai says sending users to the human-created web remains a core design principle, and he sees ads as commercial information.
- **Programming and devices.** About 30% of Google's code uses AI suggestions, but Pichai says the metric that counts is a 10% gain in engineering velocity, and Google still plans to hire more engineers. Fridman demos Google Beam (six cameras, light-field display) and the Android XR glasses.
