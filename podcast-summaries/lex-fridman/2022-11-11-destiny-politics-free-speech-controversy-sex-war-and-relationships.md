## Thesis

Steven Bonnell, known online as Destiny, describes himself as a progressive who is also pro-capitalism, pro-free speech and pro-institutions. He argues that democratic institutions largely reflect what voters want, and that a democracy has to trust its citizens to hear even extreme speech. Lex Fridman agrees with much of this but presses him on his past and present use of slurs, which Fridman says undercuts his goal of better online debate. Destiny's wife, streamer Melina Goransson, joins for the final hour on relationships and streaming.

## Key points

- **Political journey.** Destiny says he grew up Republican, moved through a libertarian and Ron Paul phase, and drifted left after streaming success, partly from seeing how much his son's opportunities came from money rather than merit. He calls a period cleaning carpets, after being fired from a casino diner job, the low point of his life.
- **Ukraine.** Destiny rejects arguments that NATO expansion or the US caused Russia's invasion, and praises Biden for clearly limiting US involvement: no no-fly zone or troops, but money, arms and intelligence.
- **Language and slurs.** Fridman tells Destiny that his casual use of the "r-word" and calling women "bitches" hurts third parties and makes him look ineffective to young fans. Destiny says his views on language have changed a lot over 10 years and that some words may yet go.
- **Institutions and media.** Destiny argues bureaucracy makes specialisation and trust possible, that the media gives people what they choose to consume, and that polling hides how little consensus there is on issues such as healthcare. Fridman worries more about institutions growing and becoming corrupt.
- **Biden and Trump.** Destiny backs Biden on Ukraine, Afghanistan and legislation. Fridman argues Biden's "MAGA Republicans" speech was divisive; Destiny says it targeted election deniers. Destiny says the Twitter ban on Trump was understandable but that permanent bans are wrong.
- **Relationships.** Destiny and Melina describe their open relationship and say they would not recommend it to most people. They criticise the red pill community for stripping romance out of relationships.
