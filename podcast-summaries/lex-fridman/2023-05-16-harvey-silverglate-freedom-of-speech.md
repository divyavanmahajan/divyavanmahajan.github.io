## Thesis

Harvey Silverglate, a civil liberties lawyer and co-founder of FIRE, calls himself a free speech absolutist. He tells Lex Fridman that offensive speech is the price of a free society and is useful because it shows "whom I should not turn my back on". In his view, university administrators who enforce speech codes are the main threat to academic freedom. Fridman pushes back on hate speech at scale, online defamation and Jeffrey Epstein's donations to MIT.

## Key points

- **Narrow exceptions only.** Silverglate accepts the exceptions to the First Amendment for direct threats, fraud, defamation and falsely shouting "fire". He says he would personally drop the defamation exception and answer lies with more speech, as he does with letters to The Boston Globe.
- **Administrative bloat.** Running in 2023 as a write-in candidate for Harvard's Board of Overseers, he says he would push the Harvard Corporation to fire 95% of administrators. He claims this would cut tuition by about 40% and end speech codes and "kangaroo courts".
- **Affirmative action and schools.** Silverglate opposes affirmative action and predicted a 6-3 Supreme Court ruling against it. He puts the root problem in weak elementary and secondary schools and blames public-sector teachers unions, while saying he supports private-sector unions.
- **Donors and naming rights.** He argues universities should not apply morals tests to donors, Epstein included, though he would refuse naming rights to someone "truly evil". He admits this is inconsistent with his absolutism. Fridman says he is sickened that Epstein was welcome at MIT.
- **FBI and federal law.** Silverglate says the FBI's culture, set by J. Edgar Hoover, cannot be reformed and the agency should be abolished. He will not let clients be interviewed unless the interview is taped, because he says Form 302 notes record what agents wished you had said. Interstate-commerce jurisdiction, he argues, lets federal prosecutors charge almost anyone.
- **Dershowitz and Trump.** He defends his friend Alan Dershowitz's representation of Trump as purely professional. He thinks the Alvin Bragg prosecution is improper, though he believes Trump has committed tax crimes.
