## Thesis

Andrew Huberman, a Stanford neuroscientist and host of the Huberman Lab podcast, argues in his third conversation with Lex Fridman that good work and good relationships depend on deliberately getting into, and moving between, the right brain states. Fridman adds his thinking on interviewing controversial figures and on love.

## Key points

- **Routines for getting into state.** Huberman credits Rick Rubin with convincing him to build a fixed routine before his solo episodes: two hours of limited contact, a walk without his phone, about 10 minutes of reciting song lyrics out loud, and panoramic vision to calm down.
- **Difficult conversations.** Fridman says that to reveal something real in someone who has abused power he has to empathize with the capacity for cruelty in himself. He regrets having casually mentioned a possible Vladimir Putin interview before he was ready. Huberman suggests Elizabeth Holmes as a guest.
- **NSDR and focus.** Huberman says non-sleep deep rest (a term he coined, covering practices such as hypnosis and yoga nidra) resets energy with less effort than meditation, and notes that Google CEO Sundar Pichai uses it. For focus he recommends good sleep, narrowing your gaze on a target for 30 to 60 seconds before starting, and 40 Hz binaural beats.
- **Stress is a matter of belief.** Citing Alia Crum's Stanford work, Huberman argues that people taught that adrenaline sharpens them perform better, which is why he does not wear a sleep tracker.
- **Training, cold and heat.** From Andy Galpin, Huberman passes on: avoid cold immersion within four hours of training for strength, muscle growth or endurance, and for strength use a 3-to-5 scheme (three to five exercises, reps, minutes' rest and sessions a week). He cites studies linking sauna use two to three times a week with a 27% lower risk of dying from a cardiovascular event, and four or more times with about 50%.
- **Relationships.** Huberman's top advice is to learn to calm yourself, to do a bit more for the relationship than you think you should, and to understand the four arrows of how each partner feels about the other's feelings. Fridman sees a relationship as shared discovery.
