## Thesis

Devon Larratt, widely seen as one of the best arm wrestlers ever, tells Lex Fridman that winning comes from huge training volume done without failure, technical strength built as "chains" of movement, and a level of commitment where losing should hurt. Preparing for a June match in Dubai against Levan Saginashvili, he describes a life cut down to that one target.

## Key points

- **Volume over failure.** Larratt calls his method "tower building": many easy, specific sessions, such as hours of pulling at clubs, with blood-flow rehab in between and no chasing personal records. He credits Russian arm wrestlers with teaching him "never go to failure", in contrast to North American "no pain no gain".
- **Strength as chains.** Rather than muscles, he describes movements: cupping the wrist towards your centre, pronation (rolling the thumb over) and rising. He says these three lifts should be about 90% of a beginner's work, and that pulling towards yourself matters more than aiming for the pin pad.
- **Styles and pacing.** Specialists win on one move (Matt Mask's top roll, Jerry Cadorette's flop-wrist press) and do well in tournaments; blenders like Larratt do better in super matches. He calls himself a "20-second guy" who wins through endurance.
- **Losses and mindset.** He says Denis Cyplenkov beat him so clearly in a 2018 left-hand match that he is at peace with it. He builds opponents up until he almost fears them, then switches to tearing them down, and uses trash talk as a deliberate weapon.
- **Freedom arm wrestling and beyond.** Larratt argues that arm wrestling across a whole table, without fixed elbow pads, is the sport's future because it needs no equipment. He also describes four weeks of boxing training at Tristar in Montreal before a planned bout with Hafthor Bjornsson.
- **Mortality and robots.** Larratt says immortality is his only wish; Fridman argues finite life gives each moment its richness, and that robots able to connect deeply with humans may also need to be mortal.
