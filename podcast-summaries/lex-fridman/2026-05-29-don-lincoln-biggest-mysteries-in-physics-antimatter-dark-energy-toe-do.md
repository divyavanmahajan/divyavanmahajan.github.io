## Thesis

Don Lincoln, a particle physicist at Fermilab, tells the history of physics as a series of unifications, from Newton through Maxwell and Einstein to the electroweak theory and the Higgs boson. As an experimentalist, he argues that the theory-of-everything energy scale is about a quadrillion times beyond today's accelerators, so ideas like string theory cannot be trusted until they are tested. Practical progress, he says, will come from measurements that disagree with predictions.

## Key points

- **Higgs discovery.** Lincoln describes Fermilab's Tevatron narrowing the Higgs mass to roughly 120-145 before CERN announced it on July 4, 2012. The LHC had 10 times the collisions per second and three and a half times the energy.
- **Theory of everything.** Lincoln estimates hundreds of years to reach unification energies, given roughly a factor of seven gain in accelerator energy every twenty years. He likes string theory but calls it untested after about 50 years of work, and notes that Rovelli revised loop quantum gravity after a prediction about the speed of light failed.
- **Antimatter.** At its peak, Fermilab smashed 100,000 protons to make one antiproton, which comes to about a nanogram a year. CERN's ALPHA experiment found that antihydrogen falls down, at 0.75 of normal gravity with large uncertainties. Lincoln says antimatter propulsion is an engineering and containment problem, not a physics one.
- **Matter asymmetry.** For every billion antimatter particles there were a billion and one matter particles. Fermilab is studying leptogenesis by comparing neutrino and antineutrino oscillations. Lincoln would bet the rates turn out the same.
- **Dark energy.** Quantum field theory overshoots the measured vacuum energy by 10 to the 120. Lincoln guesses, while warning nobody should believe it, that new quanta of space may appear as the universe expands, which would explain its constant density.
- **Dark matter.** Lincoln once favoured modified gravity, but the Bullet Cluster and the Dragonfly galaxies DF2 and DF4 changed his mind. Dark matter is five times more common than ordinary matter, and its possible mass ranges from an asteroid down to far below an electron.
