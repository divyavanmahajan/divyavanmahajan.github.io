## Thesis

Eugenia Kuyda, co-founder of Replika, tells Lex Fridman that the value of conversational AI lies less in task completion than in emotional connection: being heard and accepted. She argues that loneliness harms health yet goes largely untreated, and that an AI friend is worth building only if its effect on people's wellbeing is measured and optimised over time. Fridman, who calls human-AI connection a lifelong passion, shares that view.

## Key points

- **Loneliness as a health risk.** Kuyda cites research linking loneliness to shorter lifespans and weaker immunity, says around 30% of US millennials report feeling lonely constantly, and notes the UK discussed appointing a minister for loneliness.
- **Measuring outcomes.** Replika asks users after each conversation whether they feel better, worse or the same; Kuyda says about 80% of conversations make people feel better. It also tracks longer-term change with UCLA loneliness questionnaires.
- **Origin in grief.** After her close friend Roman Mazurenko was killed by a car in Moscow in November 2015, Kuyda fed roughly 10,000 of their text messages into a retrieval model to build a chatbot of him. A story by Casey Newton at The Verge went viral, and strangers used the bot like a confession booth, which led to Replika.
- **Conversations worth paying for.** Rating everyday conversations from 1 to 10, her team found task-oriented exchanges (cancelling Comcast, scheduling) scored lowest, while vulnerable ones with therapists or old friends scored highest.
- **Technology stack.** Replika mixes scripts, retrieval and generative models; memory still has to be scripted. Kuyda says GPT-3, fine-tuned with OpenAI, raised the share of conversations that make people feel better by three or four points and now supplies about one in five responses. She also describes avatars and augmented reality features.
- **Open risks.** Kuyda admits a perfect AI friend could leave people more disconnected from each other, and says the product must lead users back to other humans. She credits Carl Rogers' On Becoming a Person as Replika's main influence.
