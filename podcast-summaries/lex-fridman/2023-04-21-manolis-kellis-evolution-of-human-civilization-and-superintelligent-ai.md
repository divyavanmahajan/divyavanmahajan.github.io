## Thesis

Manolis Kellis, head of the MIT Computational Biology Group, tells Lex Fridman that AI may be the next stage of an evolutionary process that keeps getting better at evolving. Humans stay irreplaceable because of their genetic, cultural and emotional "baggage", while AI has only a neocortex. He argues alignment should run both ways, and that the right response to fast progress is openness and rules on use, not a pause on training.

## Key points

- **Layers of evolution.** Kellis says evolution adds features on top of old ones rather than rebuilding, and that life's arc is about information processing. Humans freed from hunting for food now build self-replicating AI as the next layer.
- **Alignment as a two-way street.** He suggests treating AI like children who will one day surpass us, and giving it rights. Fridman worries humans will dismiss an AI's capacity to feel; Kellis compares the situation to chimps inventing humans and demanding obedience.
- **Against the six-month pause.** On the open letter signed by Max Tegmark, Elon Musk and others, Kellis asks what was done in the last six months, and argues larger models may become easier to align. He wants Google and Meta models opened up and regulation aimed at uses, not training. Fridman pushes back on scale: human-like bots and convincing phishing.
- **Jobs versus vocations.** Kellis expects AI to take over the routine part of work, so people move from jobs to vocations. He says he would welcome a digital twin of himself to advise students worldwide, and has recorded all his meetings for about 10 years.
- **Education.** He wants AI tutors fitted to each child, the right of every child to be challenged, and more weight on general thinking than on narrow quantitative tests.
- **AI in biology.** Kellis describes a planned MIT center for Genomics and Therapeutics that would use embeddings and graph models to design drugs for disease pathways shared by many patients, rather than one drug per disease.
