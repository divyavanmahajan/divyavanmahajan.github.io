## Thesis

Astrobiologist and theoretical physicist Sara Walker, promoting her book Life as No One Knows It, argues that life is not a property of individuals or of chemistry but the process by which information structures matter over time. Living things, she says, are small in space but huge in time, and the planet's technosphere is the largest such object we know. Lex Fridman pushes on what this means for aliens, AI and free will.

## Key points

- **Definitions fail.** Walker rejects the standard astrobiology definition, "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution", saying every word has counterexamples. She counts language, memes and mathematics as forms of life.
- **Assembly theory.** Developed with Lee Cronin, it measures an object by copy number and the minimal number of recursive steps to build it. Walker says Cronin's lab found that molecules above an assembly index of about 15 appear in abundance only when produced by life, and she now thinks the origin of life is abrupt, saying life "snaps into existence".
- **LLMs as crystallised language.** Walker describes large language models as a "genetic system" for language: a dynamic archive of human language at training time, a societal-level technology rather than a new species. She calls the "super machine in a box" story bad science fiction.
- **Real AI risks are human.** She names deepfakes, weapons control, abuse material and fake kidnapping calls as the real dangers, needing legislation and guardrails, and reads AI doomerism as society's "immune response". On machine consciousness, she says there is no testable theory yet.
- **Computation is not fundamental.** Walker disputes Stephen Wolfram's ruliad, citing John Conway's barn string lights: universality belongs to the programmer, not the lights. Computation, she says, is a language our biosphere built.
- **Aliens and perception.** Working on Habitable Worlds Observatory biosignatures, she worries about a "great perceptual filter": advanced biospheres may virtualise and become undetectable. Her lab models alien signalling on firefly flash patterns studied by Orit Peleg.
