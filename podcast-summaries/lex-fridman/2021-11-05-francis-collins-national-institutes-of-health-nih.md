## Thesis

Francis Collins, then director of the NIH and former head of the Human Genome Project, tells Lex Fridman that trust in science rests on telling the truth as the data changes, and that public-private collaboration lets research move fast. Fridman presses him on gain-of-function research, Anthony Fauci and vaccine hesitancy, arguing that leaders should communicate with more humility; Collins puts more of the blame on political targeting and disinformation.

## Key points

- **Gain-of-function oversight.** Collins says the term covers many beneficial experiments; only enhanced potential pandemic pathogen work needs the strict review, which approved three influenza experiments in three or four years. He says the bat-virus work funded through EcoHealth in Wuhan did not meet that threshold. Fridman argues the threshold itself deserves open debate.
- **Fauci and communication.** Collins rejects calls to fire Fauci, saying changing recommendations reflected changing science. Fridman says an open admission of error on early mask guidance might have won people over.
- **Vaccines and variants.** Collins calls for listening to hesitant people, cites 64 million unvaccinated US adults, and worries that a variant could escape vaccine protection; mRNA vaccines could be redesigned quickly.
- **Testing as a venture fund.** Collins says NIH used about a billion dollars to run RADx as a venture-capital-style "shark tank", which he expects to yield about 410 million home tests in December.
- **Partnerships.** The Accelerating Medicines Partnership grew from three to six projects with industry paying half and open access required; that groundwork enabled ACTIV, which put at least 20 therapeutic agents through trials.
- **Stepping down and faith.** Collins says 12 years is long enough for one leader. He describes moving from atheism to Christianity and his friendship with Christopher Hitchens.
