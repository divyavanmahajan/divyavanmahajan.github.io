## Thesis

Todd Howard, who leads Bethesda Game Studios, tells Lex Fridman that his games are built as simulations: systems that react to the player and keep running out of sight, rather than worlds that exist only for the player. He describes how that approach shapes everything from NPC behaviour to Starfield's planets, and argues that games now have to be architected to be played, and modded, for decades.

## Key points

- **Reactive NPCs and a running world.** Howard says the best characters are written to react to the player, like Skyrim's guards. A single AI manager tracks everyone in the world all the time, updating distant characters at a lower tick rate, and NPCs will pick up dropped weapons and use them.
- **Starfield's planets.** Work began in 2016 after Fallout 4. Howard describes generating realistic landscape tiles offline and wrapping them around planets, with roughly 100 levelled star systems. Daggerfall was a touchstone for wandering procedural space, while quests lead to handcrafted content. Running out of fuel was cut because it was "a fun killer".
- **Lessons from failure.** Redguard won awards but did not sell, and Bethesda nearly went out of business before ZeniMax reformed it. Howard says it taught him not to be conservative; he also cites Fallout 76's bad launch as a problem the team learned from and fixed.
- **Building for decades.** Skyrim, 11 years old, is still Bethesda's most played game, so Howard says The Elder Scrolls VI has to be architected for people playing it for two decades. Mods are supported through the Creation Kit, and Bethesda has hired many modders.
- **Production process.** Howard outlines concepting, a first playable, a vertical slice and full production, with a final six months to a year spent "gluing" features together. Daily bots run through the game to catch crashes and frame-rate drops.
- **Xbox and other projects.** Howard calls Microsoft's $7.5 billion acquisition a good cultural fit and confirms Starfield is Xbox exclusive. He also mentions the Fallout TV series with Jonathan Nolan and an Indiana Jones game being made by MachineGames.
