## Thesis

Investor and podcaster Anthony Pompliano tells Lex Fridman that money is a belief system, and that Bitcoin's transparent, finite supply makes it a "digital sound money" outside the control of governments. He frames it as the base layer of a wider shift to digital assets and automation. Fridman probes the community's tone toward newcomers and asks how ordinary people should approach buying and storing it.

## Key points

- **Army and Iraq.** Pompliano joined the US Army at 17, was deployed to Iraq as a college junior, and says the experience left him and many veterans wary of sending soldiers abroad. He expects future conflict to be mostly cyber and information warfare.
- **Security over speed.** He argues Bitcoin optimises for security and decentralisation, as gold did, with faster payment layers built on top, while platforms such as Ethereum trade some security for features. He calls the tribalism between camps "childish" and says the market will decide.
- **Gold and the price case.** Pompliano says gold is a roughly $10 trillion asset in decline, and that Bitcoin capturing 2x gold's market cap would mean about $1 million per coin. Citing the 2024 supply halving, he predicts $100,000 in 2021 and $1 million by the end of 2026. He says over 95% of his net worth is in Bitcoin.
- **Advice for individuals.** Speaking with a not-financial-advice disclaimer, he recommends dollar-cost averaging a fixed share of each paycheck and explains the trade-off between exchanges (counterparty risk) and hardware or cold storage. He discloses an investment in BlockFi.
- **Automation.** He expects all currencies to go digital, pushing competition to monetary policy, and argues daily or streamed pay could cut the $8 billion in overdraft fees he says the top four banks collected.
- **Community and memes.** Fridman says early critics face off-putting hostility; Pompliano calls the community's meme culture an insurgent tactic against established institutions, and credits Elon Musk's Dogecoin posts with understanding internet culture. He also expects digital art (NFTs) to outgrow traditional art.
