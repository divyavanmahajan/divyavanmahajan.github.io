## Thesis

Pershing Square founder Bill Ackman tells Lex Fridman that investing means finding simple, predictable businesses that are hard to disrupt, buying them at a discount, and fixing their governance. He applies the same lens outside markets: incentives drive behaviour, and he blames Harvard's self-perpetuating board and click-driven journalism for the failures he fought publicly after October 7th.

## Key points

- **Google as an AI mispricing.** Ackman says the poor Bard demo pushed Alphabet to about 15 times earnings, roughly a 7.5% earnings yield against 4% on government debt. He judged Google's data and engineers left it tied with or ahead of Microsoft, while calling AI the biggest disruption risk for any investor.
- **General Growth Properties.** In November 2008 the mall owner's stock fell from $63 to 34 cents. Ackman bought 25% for about $60 million, betting the bankruptcy code would protect shareholders because assets exceeded liabilities; the stock recovered to $31.
- **Canadian Pacific proxy fight.** Pershing Square took 12% of what Ackman calls North America's worst-run railroad, recruited Hunter Harrison as CEO and won 99% of the vote. The stock is up about 10 times since.
- **The Valeant bottom.** Ackman says Valeant cost $4 billion and, with the Carl Icahn-backed squeeze on his Herbalife short, cut the portfolio by more than 30%. A $300 million JP Morgan loan let him fend off Elliott Associates.
- **Harvard and DEI.** Ackman describes the congressional testimony as "an embarrassment to the country", cites Harvard ranking last of 250 on FIRE's free-speech list and criticises the oppressor-oppressed framework he links to DEI. He argues universities need boards with permanently diverse viewpoints.
- **Media and X.** Ackman says Business Insider gave him and Neri Oxman 92 minutes to respond before calling her clerical errors plagiarism, and he plans to pursue a correction or a lawsuit. He credits X with letting him answer critics directly to 1,200,000 people.

## Quotes
