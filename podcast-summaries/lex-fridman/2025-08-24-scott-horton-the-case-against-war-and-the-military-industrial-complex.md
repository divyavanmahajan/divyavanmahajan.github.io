## Thesis

Over more than ten hours, Scott Horton, director of the Libertarian Institute and editorial director of Antiwar.com, argues that US foreign policy since the Cold War has been a chain of interventions sold to the public with false pretexts. In his view these wars served bureaucrats, think tanks, foreign lobbies and arms makers, and created the terrorism they claimed to fight. Lex Fridman largely agrees on the cost of war but pushes back on some points, notably Russia's responsibility for the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

## Key points

- **The cost of the terror wars.** Fridman opens with estimates from Brown University's Cost of War project: 900,000 to 940,000 direct deaths and $8 trillion spent on the post-9/11 wars. Horton adds 37 million people displaced and blames public choice dynamics, calling the military a "self-licking ice cream cone".
- **Blowback and bin Laden.** Horton argues al-Qaeda's stated goal was to bog the US down and "bleed" it to bankruptcy in Afghanistan. He says Delta Force and the CIA were refused reinforcements when bin Laden was cornered in the White Mountains in December 2001.
- **Iraq War II.** Horton blames a small group of neoconservatives. He cites David Wormser's 1996 "clean break" paper and says Ahmed Chalabi sold them on a post-Saddam Iraq friendly to Israel, including an oil pipeline to Haifa.
- **Arms industry and think tanks.** He describes the neoconservative movement as an alliance of the Israel lobby and the military-industrial complex, and names Bruce Jackson of Lockheed, who financed the Committee for the Liberation of Iraq.
- **Iran.** Horton says Iran enriched uranium to 60% as bargaining leverage after Israeli assassinations and sabotage. He says its reply to the US strikes, 14 missiles with advance warning, was symbolic, and that only diplomacy can settle the nuclear question.
- **Ukraine.** Drawing on his book Provoked (477,000 words, about 7,000 citations), Horton argues that NATO expansion broke promises James Baker made on February 9, 1990. Fridman puts chief responsibility for the escalation on Vladimir Putin. Horton agrees Putin escalated the war, but says the US provoked it.
