## Thesis

David Buss, an evolutionary psychologist at UT Austin, tells Lex Fridman that mating is central to what made humans distinct, and that our evolved mating psychology works as a menu of strategies, from long-term pair bonding to mate switching. He argues that the largest psychological sex differences appear in mating and sexuality, while stressing that the distributions overlap and that describing what is does not mean endorsing it.

## Key points

- **What makes human mating unusual.** Buss points to long-term pair bonding, heavy male parental investment and concealed ovulation, which he thinks co-evolved, in contrast with chimpanzees. He adds that humans compete for mates through language, including derogating rivals.
- **Different priorities.** Buss says men on average prioritise physical appearance and youth as cues to fertility, while women weigh more traits, notably resources, status and ambition. He proposes a "mate-switching hypothesis" for female affairs, citing that 70% or more of women who have affairs become attached to the affair partner, versus about 30% of men.
- **Jealousy as a necessary but dangerous emotion.** Buss calls jealousy a feature that guards valued relationships, but notes that male sexual jealousy is the leading cause of spousal violence. In his studies, 85% of women said emotional infidelity upset them more, while men more often chose sexual infidelity.
- **Modern mismatch.** Buss argues that social media images and pornography distort comparisons and expectations, and that dating apps give access to larger pools but encourage decision paralysis. He recommends meeting in person and stress-testing a relationship with travel.
- **Science and controversy.** Buss says he would rather be scientifically than politically correct, and cites falling homicide rates in Sweden and Norway as evidence that environments can suppress evolved tendencies.
- **Love and mortality.** Buss describes romantic love as a commitment device and says caring for his late wife through cancer made death less frightening.
