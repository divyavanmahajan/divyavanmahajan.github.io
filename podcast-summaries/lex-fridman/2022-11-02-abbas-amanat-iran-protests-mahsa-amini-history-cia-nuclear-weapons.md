## Thesis

Abbas Amanat, a Yale historian of modern Iran, tells Lex Fridman that the protests after the death of 22-year-old Mahsa Amini in morality police custody show how far a young, urban, educated generation has drifted from what the Islamic Republic tried to build. He puts the movement in the context of Iran's history since the 1906 Constitutional Revolution, and says he hopes for gradual change rather than another revolution like 1979.

## Key points

- **"Women, life, freedom."** Amanat traces the slogan to Syrian Kurdistan. He reads its three parts as rejecting mandatory hijab and legal discrimination, demanding ordinary personal freedoms, and continuing a century-old call for political liberty. He stresses that the movement has no leader.
- **Repression and propaganda.** He describes trained riot police on motorbikes in every city and cites reported figures of about 12,000 arrests and 230-240 deaths in six weeks. He calls state media's claim that 10,000 people protested fuel prices in Berlin absurd; he puts that crowd at about 100,000.
- **Demographics.** Amanat says the population grew from 35 million in 1979 to 83 million, while the birth rate fell to 1.1%. He cites 56 universities and 86% literacy as forces the regime could not control.
- **1953 and the CIA.** He recounts Mosaddegh's nationalisation of oil and the British- and CIA-backed coup, which he says the US backed out of fear of Soviet influence. Khomeini later used it as rhetoric for the "great Satan".
- **Khomeini and the guardian jurist.** He says the clergy had never before taken over the state. He describes the supreme leader as above elected institutions, and cites the execution of an estimated 6,000-8,000 prisoners at the end of the Iran-Iraq war.
- **Nuclear deal, Israel and Russia.** Amanat links the nuclear push to the Iran-Iraq war, calls the Trump withdrawal from the deal foolish, and criticises both Iranian rhetoric and Israeli policy. Fridman asks him to steelman Israel's security case. Amanat finds Iran's closeness to Putin, including drones for Russia, historically surprising.
