## Thesis

Jeremi Suri, a historian at UT Austin, tells Lex Fridman that his book "Civil War by Other Means" shows how flaws in American institutions caused the Civil War and kept it going after 1865. In his view, Confederate leaders went back to power after the war, so its conflicts carry on in today's fights over elections and democracy. He argues that people who love their country should study its failures and fix its institutions rather than burn them down.

## Key points

- **Causes of the war.** Suri says the Senate, the Electoral College and the Supreme Court gave slaveholders outsized power. He says Lincoln's Republicans ran on free labour and no slavery in new territories, and calls the war one about freedom, especially after the 1863 Emancipation Proclamation.
- **Scale and soldiers.** He puts the Union at about 22 million people against 9 million plus 4 million enslaved in the Confederacy. More than 100,000 former slaves fought for the Union, and Antietam, with more than 22,000 killed, is still the deadliest day in US history.
- **Lee, Grant and Lincoln.** Suri calls Robert E. Lee a skilled general but a villain, because Lee would not help the South accept defeat. He calls Grant a flawed hero whose 1871 anti-Ku Klux Klan law, he says, was later used to prosecute January 6th rioters. He names Lincoln's choice of Andrew Johnson as running mate his biggest failure.
- **Unpunished leaders.** Suri cites Alexander Watkins Terrell, a Confederate general who fled to Mexico and later wrote Texas election laws. He notes that Section 3 of the 14th Amendment bars oath-breaking officials from office but has never really been enforced.
- **Elections.** Suri links the disputed 1876 Tilden-Hayes election to today. He argues that county-level control and the Electoral College make fraud claims easier, and points to Brazil's central vote count as a contrast.
- **Contested politics.** Suri calls January 6th a coup attempt and criticises Trump's response. Fridman pushes back, questioning how much power the rioters had and how politicised the coverage was. Suri describes himself as left of centre on social issues and fiscally conservative.
