## Thesis

Lisa Feldman Barrett, a psychology professor at Northeastern University, tells Lex Fridman that the brain does not react to the world but constantly predicts it from past experience, using sense data only to confirm or correct those predictions. Its main job, she argues, is allostasis: running a "body budget" of glucose, salt and water. From this she derives a view of emotions as concepts the brain constructs on the fly, shaped by culture, and of empathy as costly learning.

## Key points

- **The triune brain is a myth.** Barrett says the idea of an inner lizard brain wrapped in emotional and rational layers, popularised by Carl Sagan's The Dragons of Eden (1977), was already contradicted by molecular genetics, yet still underpins law and economic theory.
- **Prediction over reaction.** Predicting and correcting is metabolically cheaper than constant reacting, she argues; a reflex is a prediction the brain executes without checking. Senses, in her account, evolved to serve movement, likely driven by predation.
- **Emotions as constructed categories.** After meta-analyses and fieldwork with the Hadza in Tanzania, Barrett says no single biomarker identifies an emotion. Like money or countries, emotions are functional categories that people agree on, and infants learn them through words.
- **Men, women and perception.** In 1990s experience-sampling studies, she reports, men and women both believed women were more emotional, but their momentary reports did not differ. Observers read a woman's expressions as her inner state and a man's as a response to his situation.
- **Experiential blindness and empathy.** Barrett argues that people cannot predict what they have no concepts for, citing children of colour being under-prescribed medicine. Learning about people unlike oneself is metabolically expensive, so depleted body budgets reduce empathy.
- **Free will and social bonds.** She says people are not responsible for the internal model they were handed, but are responsible for the experiences they now cultivate. She links loneliness to earlier death from metabolic illness.
