## Thesis

Tim Sweeney, founder and CEO of Epic Games, says Epic has always shipped its tools along with its games, starting with his first game, ZZT. He argues that large multiplayer social games like Fortnite are the real metaverse. Growing it further needs shared economies across games, a new programming model (Verse) and an end to Apple and Google's control over mobile distribution.

## Key points

- **Tools as the business.** ZZT shipped with its level editor. Unreal took three and a half years with about 20 people. Sweeney says engine licences, starting with two from MicroProse worth half a million dollars, kept Epic alive during that time.
- **Rendering progress.** Sweeney estimates CPUs are 100,000 times faster than 30 years ago and GPUs 10 million times. He credits Brian Karis for Nanite and Daniel Wright for Lumen. Realistic humans remain the hardest problem.
- **Fortnite's growth.** About 30 people built Battle Royale in four weeks. Epic grew from 300 employees to thousands and from about $100 million to billions in revenue. On AWS, the backend went from 40,000 to 15 million concurrent users. Creators have earned $400 million through engagement-based revenue sharing.
- **Verse and Unreal Engine 6.** Unreal runs game logic on a single thread, which caps Fortnite matches at about 100 players. Verse, a functional logic language, aims to use transactional memory so ordinary code can scale to millions of players. Sweeney expects UE6 previews in two to three years.
- **App stores.** Sweeney calls Apple's 30% a junk fee. He says Epic's data puts the cost of running Google Play at about 6% of revenue, and cites Google's "Project Hug" payments to publishers. He says Epic spent over a billion dollars on Epic Games Store exclusives, and admits the launcher is clunky.
- **AI and the industry.** He thinks AI video has a consistency problem, and that AI will multiply what artists can do rather than replace them. He expects players to gather in a few big social games, with smaller games succeeding only on lean budgets.
