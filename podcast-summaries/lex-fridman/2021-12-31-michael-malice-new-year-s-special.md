## Thesis

In a loose New Year's Eve conversation, Michael Malice and Lex Fridman agree that goodness, beauty and hope matter, but differ on how to get there. Malice, an anarchist, argues that unchecked power lets elites act without consequence and that only opposing power restrains it. Fridman places more faith in reforming institutions from within, and both argue that friends should be able to disagree sharply without losing affection.

## Key points

- **Truth, goodness and beauty.** Starting from Dostoevsky's "beauty will save the world" and Solzhenitsyn's Nobel speech, Malice says beauty proves perfection is possible, but picks goodness as the most important of the three because it is the easiest default.
- **Epstein and elite power.** Fridman asks how scientists failed to see through Jeffrey Epstein's charisma. Malice points to the shelved ABC story raised by Amy Robach's hot-mic remarks as evidence that the powerful protect each other, and cites a magazine report questioning where Epstein's money came from.
- **Moving to Austin.** Both describe 2021 as the year they moved. Malice says New York's COVID restrictions hollowed out its creative scene; Fridman describes giving up his MIT salary and choosing Austin for the optimism of people such as Elon Musk and Joe Rogan.
- **Writing.** Malice says The Anarchist Handbook briefly topped Amazon's non-fiction list. For The White Pill, a book about hope that began as a take on Camus, he recommends a fixed daily page count and a rough first draft, because editing while writing is like driving forwards and backwards at once.
- **Politics.** Malice defers to Glenn Beck's view that Donald Trump will probably run in 2024 but says the nomination is not certain. He makes his case for a "national divorce" and also steelmans the objections: the dividing lines run between cities and countryside, and smaller bad actors could be emboldened.
- **COVID and the Pfizer interview.** Fridman reflects on criticism that his Pfizer CEO interview was too soft. Malice says he would have asked about the incentive to sell the product to everyone, and suggests questions for Anthony Fauci about AIDS and early mask guidance.
