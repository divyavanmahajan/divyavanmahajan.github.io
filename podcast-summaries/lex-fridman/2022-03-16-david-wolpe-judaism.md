## Thesis

David Wolpe, rabbi of Sinai Temple in Los Angeles, tells Lex Fridman that Judaism gives no single answer about God, but insists on a relationship with a God who cannot be known. He presents the Torah as a human, divinely inspired text read through a conversation lasting thousands of years, and argues that faith, not historical fact, is the basis of the tradition. Fridman also presses him on AI, consciousness and robots.

## Key points

- **Debating atheists.** Wolpe recalls debates with Christopher Hitchens, who convinced him that the claim that religion makes people better is far more complicated than he wished. He calls Sam Harris a deeply spiritual personality who sees religion as intellectually indefensible.
- **Law and tribe.** Wolpe says Judaism and Islam are closer to each other than to Christianity because Moses and Muhammad had to create law in the desert, while Christianity grew up under Roman law. All three share the idea that every person is in the image of God.
- **Scripture and controversy.** Wolpe calls the Torah a human product written over centuries, and said in a Passover sermon that the Exodus did not happen as described. He explains his decision to perform same-sex marriages, which made the front page of the LA Times; he says he should have prepared his congregation more.
- **Online anger.** After a photo of himself unmasked at the Super Bowl drew public attacks, Wolpe answered with a sermon rather than on social media, arguing that instant reactions harden once posted.
- **Free will and AI.** Wolpe argues that a thorough materialist cannot have free will, and warns that believing we can control intelligences greater than ours is hubris. Fridman describes bringing legged robots to life and suggests humans and robots may one day form deep bonds; Wolpe resists the idea.
- **Israel and advice.** Wolpe calls himself a strong supporter of Israel who feels for the plight of Palestinians, and hopes economics will override ideology as he says it did with the Abraham Accords. To young people he advises reading books and building an internal sense of what matters.

## Quotes
