## Thesis

Jared Kushner, former senior advisor to President Trump, recorded one session with Lex Fridman on October 5 and a second on October 9, two days after the Hamas attack on Israel. He argues that Israel must eliminate or severely degrade Hamas, that Iran's renewed revenue funded the attack, and that peace comes from strength plus a credible economic future, not from settling historical grievances. He presents trust and first-principles thinking as his diplomatic method.

## Key points

- **Hamas and Iran.** Kushner calls Gazans hostages of Hamas and says Iran fights through proxies. He claims Iranian oil sales fell from about 2.6 million barrels a day to about 100,000 under Trump sanctions, and criticises the Biden administration for lax enforcement and the $6 billion released in a hostage exchange.
- **An economic plan for Gaza.** His Peace to Prosperity plan was 181 pages and about $27 billion for 5 million Palestinians. Bahrain conference attendees put Gaza's rebuild at $7-8 billion; Kushner says governance, not Israel, blocks investment.
- **Separating the issues.** After Sultan Qaboos of Oman said Palestinians carry "the burden of the Muslim world," Kushner split Al-Aqsa access from the territorial dispute. That opened normalisation deals with the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, Kosovo and Morocco, marked by the first El Al flight to the UAE.
- **Negotiation method.** He describes building trust, agreeing on the outcome first, closing off a leader's "escape routes" and offering a "golden bridge." He cites USMCA, covering $1.3 trillion in annual trade, and the First Step Act, which passed the Senate with 87 votes; he says recidivism among program participants is 12% versus 47% overall.
- **Leaders and rivals.** Kushner describes Mohammed bin Salman's habit of setting a hundred goals and accepting failure on half. He claims the Ukraine war would "100%" have been avoided under Trump, and calls Trump's tariffs on China "the biggest hand of poker."
- **Personal setbacks.** He says his father's imprisonment and the Russia investigation, which cost him over 20 hours of testimony, taught him to focus only on what he can control.
