## Thesis

Matt Garman, who joined AWS as an intern in 2005 and became CEO in May, argues that cloud computing is still early: about 85% of workloads still run on premises. He sees generative AI as a tailwind for that migration. In his view inference will become one more standard building block, offered through a platform with many models (Bedrock), not owned by one model provider.

## Key points

- **Building blocks, not paradigm shifts.** Garman says AWS won early by offering familiar primitives (a Linux server in 30 seconds, S3 storage) instead of making developers change how they build.
- **Solve the hardest customers first.** Banks like Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan once said their internal workloads would never move, so AWS spent a decade working through their compliance lists. Garman calls the US intelligence agency contract, made public when IBM sued, a major stamp of credibility.
- **Three bets on generative AI.** Customers will care about security. There will be many models, big and small. A company's own data is its real IP and must not leak into a model. Garman says Titan embeddings is the most popular embeddings model in Bedrock and calls Anthropic's Claude models the best-performing. AWS launched Meta's Llama 3.1 on its release day.
- **More than the model.** Garman expects models to matter less over time compared with knowledge bases for grounding, guardrails (such as stopping a bank chatbot from giving financial advice), agentic workflows and distillation. Partners such as Scale AI and LangChain fill gaps.
- **Capacity stays tight.** Fabs, memory and data centres take years to build, so Garman expects constraints for a while. He positions Trainium and Inferentia as diversification alongside Nvidia, which he says runs its own training clusters on AWS.
- **Advice to startups.** Garman's first startup raised $27 million and ran out of money in about 18 months. He tells AI founders to plan for monetisation and not assume more funding will come. He argues most enterprises will use applications, not build their own models or manage GPUs.
