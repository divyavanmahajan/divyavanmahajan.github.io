## Thesis

Dan Hendrycks, director of the Center for AI Safety, argues that labs can do little beyond basic safeguards because competition with China forces them to race. The larger risks are geopolitical, so he proposes a deterrence regime modelled on nuclear strategy, plus chip non-proliferation, instead of voluntary slowdowns. He also explains why exam-style evals are close to saturation.

## Key points

- **Limited role for labs.** Hendrycks says companies can add simple anti-terrorism refusals, for example on virology, and expose expert capabilities only to known customers ("talk to sales"). He sees no large trade-off between safety and benefits there.
- **Alignment is a subset of safety.** US and Chinese AIs fully obedient to their principals would still feed a strategic competition with high risk tolerance and rushed military integration.
- **Against pauses and pure racing.** A voluntary pause only weakens the side that complies. He criticises Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness plan to race to superintelligence: it ignores espionage (he says 30%+ of staff at some top labs are Chinese nationals) and how China would respond to a trillion-dollar cluster visible from space.
- **Mutually assured AI malfunction (MAIM).** In the paper with Eric Schmidt and Alexandr Wang, states deter each other from bids for an AI super weapon by threatening sabotage, such as cyberattacks on data centres; he notes espionage could be as easy as a zero-day on Slack.
- **Concrete asks.** A CIA cell spying on foreign AI programmes, Cyber Command options to disable destabilising projects, and tracking AI chips like fissile material through licensing and end-use checks. He says BIS enforcement did not check where the 10% of Nvidia chips shipped to Singapore went, and that DeepSeek shows you can restrict a superpower's intent but not its capabilities.
- **Evals.** After MMLU and MATH, Humanity's Last Exam collects hard closed-ended questions from professors; near-ceiling scores would mean a superhuman STEM solver, while agent tasks remain "near the floor".
