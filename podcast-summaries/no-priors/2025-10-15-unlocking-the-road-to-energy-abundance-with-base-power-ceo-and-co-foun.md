## Thesis

Zach Dell, co-founder and CEO of Base Power, tells Elad Gil that the next five decades of energy will be defined by solar and storage, as the last five were by coal and then natural gas. He argues that cheap generation is not the bottleneck: the cost of moving electrons is rising, and batteries placed in homes, run by software, can shift power through time and compete with poles and wires.

## Key points

- **What Base does.** Base designs, manufactures, installs, owns and operates home batteries, and sells power directly to homeowners in Texas. It also sells the technology to regulated utilities. Dell says customers save 10-20% a month, and he describes the fleet as the world's largest distributed power plant.
- **Latent grid capacity.** Dell puts US peak demand around 700 GW against average demand near 300 GW. He argues that batteries and software can time-shift electricity to use the gap, though more generation will still be needed.
- **Make versus move.** In Dell's view, solar has pushed down the cost of making electricity over 20 years. The cost of moving it keeps rising because the infrastructure is ageing and regulated utilities earn a set return on capex. He doubts nuclear can compete with solar and storage on cost unless it becomes much cheaper and faster to build.
- **Regulation.** Dell wants faster permitting, shorter interconnection queues and price signals that pay users for flexible consumption. He notes that Texas deregulated after California's attempt ended with Enron, and that electricity is the last major industry still regulated this way.
- **Team and capital.** Co-founder Justin led manufacturing at SpaceX and Anduril. Early leaders came from Starlink, Tesla (Model 3 batteries, Powerwall) and SpaceX procurement, and the company now has 250 people. Base has raised $1.3 billion in about 18 months and is building its first factory in Austin. Dell attributes 90% of cost reduction to technology and 10% to cheaper capital.
