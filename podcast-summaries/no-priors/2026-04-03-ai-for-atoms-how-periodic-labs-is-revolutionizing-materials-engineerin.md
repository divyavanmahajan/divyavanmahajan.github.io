## Thesis

Liam Fedus, a ChatGPT co-creator, former VP of post-training at OpenAI and now co-founder of Periodic Labs, tells Elad Gil that science requires experiments, not just hard thinking. AI will not speed up the physical world, he argues, until models are connected to experiments in closed loops. Periodic is building that loop for materials and chemistry, with the aim of bringing scaling-law thinking to the physical sciences.

## Key points

- **Background.** Fedus did dark matter research as a physics student, then worked on sparsity and mixture-of-experts architectures at Google Brain from 2016-2017. At OpenAI he helped turn GPT-4 into a product. He recalls that John Schulman pushed for a general chatbot rather than ideas such as a meeting-notes bot.
- **Why now.** ChatGPT-era models were too weak for this work, Fedus says. Reasoning, test-time inference, more reliable tool use and coding agents made physical-world connection possible. Periodic spends no effort on coding models and relies on Codex and Claude Code.
- **Data is the bottleneck.** Periodic builds on the tens of trillions of tokens in open-source models, which gives it a strong prior and sample efficiency. But literature is noisy: one material property's reported values spanned many orders of magnitude. Experimental data grounds the models, and each result drives the next experiment.
- **Architecture.** Language models act as an orchestration layer. They read literature and lab data and call specialized, symmetry-aware neural nets for atomic systems as tools and reward functions. Generalization works well across quantum-mechanical systems but not, for example, into fluid dynamics.
- **Business and capital.** Periodic is its own "customer zero" and positions itself as an intelligence layer and system of record for advanced manufacturing, with possible biotech-style discovery upside. Compute, not lab infrastructure, is the main cost.
- **Self-improvement is domain-bound.** Fedus calls intelligence "spiky", not a scalar. Recursive self-improvement is happening "now-ish" in software engineering because tests are cheap to verify; AI research is a slower loop. Science needs its own closed loops. Robotics, such as a dexterous humanoid, would be an accelerator but is not required.
