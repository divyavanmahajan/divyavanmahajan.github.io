## Thesis

Rick Caruso, the real estate developer who finished second in the 2022 Los Angeles mayoral race, tells Elad Gil that climate played a part in the January 2025 fires, but that unmanaged brush, empty reservoirs and an underfunded fire department made them far worse. He argues the rebuild should be run like a business project: many tasks in parallel, with infrastructure upgraded rather than replaced like for like.

## Key points

- **Preventable damage.** Caruso says the Palisades fire was fed by 40 years of unmanaged brush and that the largest reservoir had been drained for repairs, three weeks after a nearby Malibu fire. He calls it bad planning and "border negligence", saying the fire could have been substantially mitigated.
- **How Palisades Village survived.** His development used no combustible materials (even the "wood" is concrete), and a rapid-response team brought fire retardant, backup water and a private firefighting crew, using a playbook from the Rosewood Miramar fire. It also saved eight adjacent homes.
- **Hydrants.** He rejects the broken-pipes explanation: his team reported empty hydrants because the gravity-fed reservoirs were not kept full.
- **Crime and budgets.** Drawing on his time as police commission president, when he brought in Bill Bratton, Caruso calls for proactive policing and more officers. He says billions spent on homelessness have not reduced numbers, and that the busiest fire station in the US, near MacArthur Park, is driven by overdoses.
- **Rebuild plan.** He wants the area split into quadrants with several incentivised contractors, power lines put underground and water systems upgraded during the cleanup. He suggests a one-year target (three to four months for hazard cleanup) and welcomes Governor Newsom's suspension of Coastal Commission permitting.
- **State finances.** Gil suggests a Norway-style sovereign wealth fund funded by Hollywood and tech; Caruso likes the idea and lists the power grid, water, schools and internet access as priorities.
