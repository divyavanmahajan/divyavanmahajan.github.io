## Thesis

Jared Kushner argues that his time in government taught him to read the governance and macro direction of whole countries, which he now uses at Affinity Partners to find mispriced markets. With Elad Gil, he presents BrainCo as an AI implementation company that matches large institutions holding hard problems and good data with engineers who can solve them, and he makes the case for private-sector tours of duty in government.

## Key points

- **Countries as mispriced assets.** Kushner says the roughly 200 countries are far less efficient than public markets; he cites Affinity's investment in Albania under Prime Minister Rama and expects Mexico to benefit from supply chains moving away from Asia.
- **BrainCo's origin and model.** Kushner, Gil, Eric Woo and Luis started BrainCo in January 2024 after failing to find good AI providers for their portfolio. Gil describes a common platform plus reusable apps sold to the world's largest institutions; the company is now over 40 people.
- **Construction permitting case.** Kushner describes a government permit process of over 40 steps that took 3 to 4 months; BrainCo now reaches over 90% accuracy on 35 steps, and the client plans to cut more than 10 steps from the process.
- **Customer fit.** Kushner looks for a complex problem, the right data, and a command-and-control structure that can push past internal resistance; Gil adds that the main obstacles are people and process, not technology.
- **Energy and policy.** Kushner says the US has about 1,300 gigawatts of energy but about 2,000 gigawatts waiting for permits, and backs the administration's AI plan of building energy fast and not overregulating early.
- **Abraham Accords and Phoenix.** Kushner recounts deals with the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, Morocco and Kosovo, and says Affinity's stake in Israel's Phoenix Holdings, bought at about $37 a share, has returned almost nine times in just over a year.
