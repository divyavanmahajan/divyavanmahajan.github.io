## Thesis

Lukas Biewald, CEO of Weights & Biases, argues that ML practitioners need simple tools built for researchers rather than DevOps engineers, and that customers will act rationally, which today means using GPT. He describes LLMs as his company's first real existential threat and says he has moved the whole company onto LLM workflow tooling, while noting that very few companies actually run LLMs in production yet.

## Key points

- **Training data matters most.** At Yahoo, Biewald saw the same ranking model succeed or fail by country depending on how seriously each team collected training data. That led to Figure Eight (originally Dolores Labs, then CrowdFlower), which he says went through an eight-year lull until autonomous vehicles revived growth, before Scale overtook it in self-driving.
- **Tools for the people doing ML.** Biewald jokes that he doesn't understand git or Docker. His point is that researchers need simpler tools for experiment tracking, data versioning and model registries, because research code always ends up in production.
- **LLMs as an existential threat.** Tasks like sentiment analysis can now be done by prompting an LLM. His board saw nothing in the metrics, but he rallied the company behind its Prompts product and estimates that about 95% of people use GPT for these tasks.
- **Few LLMs in production.** Biewald says production LLM deployments are hard to find and enterprise adoption is slow. Gil links this to enterprise planning cycles of around six months, with companies like Notion and Zapier moving first.
- **Where the customers are.** Biewald is most excited about pharma, where companies are hiring hundreds of ML staff, and also points to games companies, agtech such as John Deere's weed-targeting sprayers, and fintech.
- **Closed source and customer focus.** Biewald says staying closed source gives Weights & Biases usage data that open-source competitors lack. His advice to founders is to care more than they think about building something people want, and to use insecurity to push early customers for honest feedback.
