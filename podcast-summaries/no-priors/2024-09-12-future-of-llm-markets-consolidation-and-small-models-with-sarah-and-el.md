## Thesis

Elad Gil argues that frontier language models are consolidating because only hyperscalers and sovereigns can fund the next rounds. Sarah Guo counters that the market has become more competitive, with falling prices, open-source players and a shift to product fights. Both see room for startups in specialised models, smaller models, risk-taking on data and output, and new chip architectures.

## Key points

- **Capital moats.** Gil notes teams from companies such as Inflection, Character and Adept joining larger firms, and says the venture industry is too small to fund labs raising billions; Apple and Samsung may each need a model partner.
- **Price collapse.** Gil says API costs dropped roughly 200x in 18 to 24 months, which pushes new entrants towards specialised models, post-training or vertical applications. Guo expects providers to add lock-in features such as prompt caching, JSON output and fine-tuning.
- **History rhymes.** Gil compares the market to social networks, where Friendster and MySpace gave way to Facebook, then Instagram, Twitter, Snapchat and TikTok. Guo points to small teams at Runway, Pika, Ideogram and Luma Labs competing in image and video.
- **Smaller models.** Gil says performance per model size keeps rising, citing the 20 or 30 watt brain as a hint of what is possible; Guo expects real-time image, video and audio applications as a result.
- **How much risk to take.** Gil recounts Google's copyright fights over snippets, which ended with robots.txt, fair use and content deals, and contrasts Napster. He separates legal, regulatory and reputational risk, citing Grok's looser output policy; Guo asks whether limits belong on generation or distribution.
- **AMD's systems play.** Guo frames AMD's Silo deal (about $600 million) and the ZT acquisition (roughly 1 to 2 billion dollars for a thousand systems designers) as attempts to sell full systems like Nvidia.
