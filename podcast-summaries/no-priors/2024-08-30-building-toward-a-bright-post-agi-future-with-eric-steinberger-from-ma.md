## Thesis

Eric Steinberger, co-founder and CEO of Magic, works backwards from AGI. A system that can do everything reduces to a system that can build that system, and that means one that writes code and runs experiments. He wants to use each generation of model to do alignment and safety research on the next. He believes the transition to AGI will end either very well or very badly, with little in between.

## Key points

- **From reinforcement learning to language models.** Steinberger decided at 14 to work on AI, later did RL research with Noam Brown, and says he had no background in language models when Magic started.
- **Long context over retrieval.** Magic announced a 5-million-token context window about a year earlier. Steinberger argues that in-context learning is what makes Transformers great. Retrieval only shows the model a subset of the data, so a model that sees everything could learn retrieval if retrieval were optimal. He cites Richard Sutton's bitter lesson.
- **Trading training compute against inference compute.** He frames performance as a function of both and expects spending to be spiky, with a few users paying a lot and billions paying little. That makes test-time compute strictly useful. He admits he underestimated how much compute AGI needs, and Magic is announcing one of the largest clusters to be built.
- **A high product bar.** Magic chose not to ship code completion because the next generation would replace it. Steinberger says the market unlocks at a step-function moment when developers trust the system to write most of their code without draining reviews.
- **Hiring overlooked talent.** Without a famous founder story, Magic recruited people the big labs overlooked, such as an Amazon Alexa AI engineer and a kernel engineer who wants to be the best outside Nvidia. Steinberger says Magic does not poach staff from other labs to get their IP.
- **Life after AGI.** He expects all computer work to be automated and handmade goods (he points to Etsy) and games to grow. Competitive people may lose meaning. Elad Gil recommends Ryan Avent's 2016 book "The Wealth of Humans".
