## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil replay their favourite 2025 moments in a 19-minute compilation. The common thread is founders and scientists leaning into new AI capabilities at the right moment, from legal work and enterprise search to clinical notes. Alongside that are guests' warnings about job displacement and the geopolitics of superintelligence.

## Key points

- **Harvey's origin.** Winston Weinberg recalls testing GPT-3 with chain-of-thought prompts on about 100 landlord-tenant questions from Reddit's r/legaladvice. Three attorneys said 86 of 100 answers could be sent unedited, which got the founders a meeting with OpenAI's leadership.
- **Spatial intelligence.** Fei-Fei Li of World Labs describes reconstructing a 3D world from light as one of evolution's hardest problems. She argues that making 3D creation easy and editable would open a new world for people.
- **Displacement.** Mercor's Brendan Foody expects fast, painful displacement in roles such as customer support and recruiting, and a populist backlash. He thinks automation in the physical world will move more slowly.
- **Deterrence.** Dan Hendrycks of the Center for AI Safety compares the situation to nuclear strategy. He suggests states may deter rivals' superintelligence bids with pre-emptive cyber attacks on data centers.
- **Reasoning and tools.** OpenAI's Brandon McKinzie and Eric Mitchell say tool use, such as cropping images or writing code, gives o3 a much steeper test-time scaling slope. Isa Fulford describes being surprised at how well deep research training on browsing tasks worked.
- **Builders.** Noubar Afeyan (Flagship Pioneering) wants entrepreneurship to be a profession, not a game of shots on goal. Arvind Jain says SaaS APIs made Glean possible: Rubrik had 300 SaaS systems, and one customer now holds over 1 billion documents. Abridge's Shiv Rao shares a doctor's note about being able to come home for family dinner.
