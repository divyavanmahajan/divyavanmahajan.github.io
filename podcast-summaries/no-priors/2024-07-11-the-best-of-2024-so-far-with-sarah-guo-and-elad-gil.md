## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil replay clips from six 2024 conversations. A common thread runs through them: the guests expect AI to augment professionals rather than replace them in the near term. They also stress that how teams build, test and measure AI systems will decide how quickly people trust and adopt them.

## Key points

- **Fintech (Emily Glassberg Sands, Stripe).** She sees merchant identity as a large open problem: knowing who a business is and what it sells, for credit, risk and card-network rules. She also expects LLMs to write payment integrations that today need specialist engineers.
- **Design (Dylan Field, Figma).** Field argues that design depends on context that is hard to fit into a prompt, such as culture, emotion and user research. He expects augmentation of designers and more, better software, and says engineers may spend more time on design.
- **Hardware velocity (Brett Adcock, Figure).** Adcock describes iterative build-and-test cycles plus formal conceptual, preliminary and critical design reviews, a practice he brought from Archer. He says hardware is more methodical than software because timelines are an order of magnitude longer.
- **Sora (OpenAI team).** The team highlights the artist short "Air Head" from shy kids and a "bling zoo" sample made in under an hour of prompting. They expect new media formats and say video training teaches physical detail that matters for robotics.
- **Engineers (Scott Wu, Cognition).** Wu says fundamentals such as logic and how computers work stay valuable, even if "the hottest new programming language is English".
- **Evals (Alexandr Wang, Scale).** Wang says academic benchmarks are often in models' training data. Scale's held-out GSM1K maths test showed some models performing well below their reported scores, and he calls for public leaderboards and expert evaluation.
