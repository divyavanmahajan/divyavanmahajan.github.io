## Thesis

Jesse Zhang, co-founder and CEO of Decagon, argues that AI customer service agents substitute for mundane human labor inside existing enterprise systems, and that over time they become a unified "concierge" through which consumers deal with a brand. In conversation with Elad Gil he links Decagon's fast move up-market to top-down AI mandates from boards, and expects consumer agents to soon be talking directly to company agents.

## Key points

- **Enterprise pull.** Zhang says Decagon started with digital natives such as Rippling and Notion, then was pulled up-market to banks, airlines and telcos, where enterprises moved faster than expected because AI adoption is now driven by the C-suite and board.
- **Measured impact.** According to Zhang, customers first measure efficiency; case studies show contact-center costs cut by 60-70%, with customer satisfaction tracked as an equal or higher priority.
- **Product approach.** Zhang contrasts Decagon with classic SaaS such as Salesforce: the agent integrates with the existing CRM and telephony stack, and non-technical business users build, iterate and analyze it, while engineers own the API connections.
- **Pricing per conversation.** Zhang argues seat or per-minute pricing makes no sense for agents; customers buy an allotment of conversations and burn it down. Gil notes this shifts the addressable market from seats to the salaries of the people doing the work.
- **Scaling the company.** Approaching 200 people, with offices in New York and one being set up in Europe, Zhang says Decagon is shifting from greedy short-term deal closing to longer-term org design and core product work, citing Ramp and Databricks as models. He also advises would-be founders to join companies that already have the commercials figured out.
- **Labs and defensibility.** Zhang expects AI labs to push into applications, starting with consumer use cases and coding, but says enterprise needs a thick software layer: observability, insights and simulation testing for QA.
