## Thesis

Roblox founder and CEO Dave Baszucki tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that AI mainly speeds up a vision Roblox has held for 20 years: a photorealistic, physics-backed "holodeck" where thousands of people share a space. He sees the hard problem as synchronising the state of 10,000 players, not just rendering. Roblox's recorded play data is meant to train NPCs that go beyond LLMs.

## Key points

- **Two poles.** Baszucki places products between high-fidelity communication with real people and solo "real-time dreaming" where everyone else is an NPC. He expects 4D simulation to become a superset of video calls, with sound that fades with distance for meetings of a thousand people.
- **History as vector data.** Roblox aims to store everything that happens on the platform, about 13 billion hours a month, as vector data rather than video. That would allow replays from any camera angle for safety reviews or family moments, and it gives Roblox training data that others struggle to find.
- **NPCs in three stages.** First, NPCs that can play any Roblox game. Second, opt-in, privacy-compliant "virtual doppelgangers" that copy a user's gestures and speech. Third, agentic doppelgangers sent out to act for you. Gil compares the shift to end-to-end models in self-driving.
- **World models as one piece.** Baszucki asks whether memory will live in video latents or in a new 3D latent space. He expects a multi-part stack: a synchronisation engine, dedicated NPC models, 3D and 2D upsampling, and possibly world models.
- **Assets and creators.** Cheaper assets haven't changed gaming, he says, because consumer expectations rise just as fast. Roblox is adding dynamic level of detail and prompt-based assets generated on demand. Top creators now earn tens of millions a year and update their games weekly.
- **Long view, weekly shipping.** Roblox's interim goal is 10% of global gaming content, about 300 million daily users, or roughly 3x today. Since acquiring Imbellus, Roblox has used its own Roblox-built assessments to hire graduates and ignores which university they attended.
