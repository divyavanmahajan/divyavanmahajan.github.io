## Thesis

Cristobal Valenzuela, co-founder and CEO of Runway, describes the company as an applied research lab that turns neural networks into creative tools. He argues that a model is not a product: the work lies in deployment, user interaction and unit economics, and Runway's edge comes from putting researchers and artists at the same table. In his view, AI in art is a new medium in the same way that the paint tube was, with the artist still at the centre.

## Key points

- **From model store to toolkit.** Valenzuela says Runway started as a thin accessibility layer over research models: an app store that at one point hosted about 400 models. It now offers around 35 "magic tools" for editing video, audio and images.
- **Owning the stack.** He argues that relying on third parties makes it hard to react when a breakthrough happens elsewhere, so Runway built its own research team. He says about half the team has an arts background, and until two weeks before the recording there was no dedicated product person.
- **Building Green Screen.** Users were exporting video frames to run image segmentation models on them. Instead of building full automation, Runway trained a human-in-the-loop model on simulated user clicks. The first version ran at four frames per second but was still much better than the alternatives.
- **80% is enough in creative work.** Valenzuela contrasts creative tools with autonomous vehicles: a tool that gets a filmmaker 80% of the way, with the rest finished in Nuke or Flame, still saves days. He says directors can now try three ideas instead of one.
- **Product-market fit signals.** An ad agency dismissed early, blurry GAN outputs; he says the mistake is judging one moment instead of the rate of progress. Users making tutorials without any marketing team was his signal of traction.
- **Paint tubes and AI art.** Valenzuela compares portable paint tubes, which enabled plein-air painting and impressionism, to today's diffusion wave. This follows a first wave of GAN art from 2015 to 2022. He expects the next movement to come from creative-coding communities.
