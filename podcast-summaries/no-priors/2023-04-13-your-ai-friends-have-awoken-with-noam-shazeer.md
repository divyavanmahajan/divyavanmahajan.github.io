## Thesis

Noam Shazeer, co-inventor of the Transformer and CEO of Character.AI, argues that language modelling is "AI complete" and has not hit a scaling wall. He describes Character as both AGI-first and product-first. Users invent their own personas, and the product gets better as the model gets smarter.

## Key points

- **Why transformers won.** Shazeer says deep learning took off because it suits modern hardware. Transformers swapped sequential RNNs for attention that processes a whole sequence in parallel on GPUs and TPUs. He calls next-word prediction the best problem because web data is effectively unlimited.
- **No tap-out yet.** Shazeer expects factors-of-two gains from algorithms, chips and quantization, plus 10x to 1,000x more spending. He is not worried about data, since billions of people talking, often to AI, will generate more.
- **From Meena to LaMDA.** Shazeer credits co-founder Daniel de Freitas, who built Meena as a 20% project using borrowed TPU quota. It was later renamed LaMDA. Shazeer says Google held it back over the risk of a product that "can say anything", so the two left to move faster as a startup.
- **Many personas, not one.** Shazeer argues that a single assistant persona like Siri or Alexa ends up boring. Character lets users create characters for role-play, games, fan relationships and emotional support. He says users who send a message are active about two hours a day on average.
- **Business and safety.** Shazeer says the team of 22 (21 engineers) plans a premium subscription for capabilities that cost more to serve. Characters are labelled as fiction, and filters block self-harm content and porn.
- **Motivation.** Shazeer says his real goal is to speed up technology such as medicine through AI. He says de Freitas hires for something between "burning desire and childhood dream."
