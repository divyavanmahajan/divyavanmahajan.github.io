## Thesis

Percy Liang, Stanford professor and director of the Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM), argues that academia should focus on what it does best. Now that companies with data and compute can scale past most technical barriers, he says that means understanding how foundation models work, studying their social impact, exploring alternative architectures and demanding transparency. He also presents Together, a decentralized cloud, as a way to make compute more accessible.

## Key points

- **The GPT-3 moment.** Liang says GPT-3's in-context learning was a paradigm shift because it began to "dissolve" the idea of a task. That led CRFM to coin the term "foundation models" in 2021. The centre now involves more than 30 faculty across 10 departments.
- **Academia's role.** He says academic research used to focus on making models work at all, and should now focus on rigorous understanding of training data, architectures and objectives, and on economic, legal and disinformation impacts. He also argues AI should aim for reliable, superhuman performance rather than mimicking humans.
- **Beyond the transformer.** Liang hopes the transformer will be replaced within a decade. He points to Chris Ré's group, whose architectures without attention are competitive at smaller scale. He says academia can train models of about 1 billion parameters, but promising ideas still have to be tested at scale.
- **HELM.** The Holistic Evaluation of Language Models compared 30 models across 42 scenarios and seven metrics, including robustness, calibration, fairness, toxicity and efficiency. It is now refreshed every two weeks, and Liang flags jailbreaking of models that take outside inputs as a growing risk.
- **Together and smaller models.** Together aims to pool idle, weakly connected compute, which Liang says can be 100 times slower to interconnect than data centres. It is also releasing OpenChatKit, built on EleutherAI's NeoX model. He cites BioMedLM, trained with Mosaic on PubMed articles, as a case for smaller domain models.
- **Transparency norms.** Liang argues that "aligned" models raise the question of whose values they reflect, and compares disclosure to nutrition labels.
