## Thesis

Brendan Foody is co-founder and CEO of Mercor, which uses LLMs to vet and hire experts who train AI models. He tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that the human data market has moved from crowdsourcing to finding top experts. In his view, writing evals for agents is now the main obstacle to automating knowledge work. He expects displacement to be fast, painful and political, and says the industry gives too little thought to where humans fit.

## Key points

- **The company.** According to Guo's introduction, three college dropouts and Thiel Fellows founded Mercor in 2023. It has raised $100 million and passed $100 million in revenue run rate. Foody says that on most of Mercor's evals, its models already judge talent better than human hiring managers.
- **What models assess well.** Foody says models do best with text such as interview transcripts, and with high-volume hiring for a single role. They are slower on multimodal signals such as passion or sales ability.
- **Evals as work.** Foody sees doing a task as a repeated cost, and writing an eval for it as a one-time cost. He argues work will shift toward the latter. Repetitive tasks such as customer support tickets come first. Full software engineering, he says, will take a years-long build-out beyond benchmarks like SWE-bench.
- **Reinforcement fine-tuning.** Foody is bullish on RFT. Supervised fine-tuning needed tens of thousands of input-output pairs, while RFT can work with hundreds to thousands of examples. He thinks that finally makes it worthwhile to customize models at the application layer.
- **Displacement.** Foody sees displacement already in customer support and recruiting. He expects physical-world automation to lag, and suggests AI may soon be better as a manager than as an individual contributor.
- **Marketplace strategy.** Foody says labor marketplaces typically have 50 job seekers for every opening. Mercor plans to give away free tools such as AI mock interviews. Gil contrasts early Google, which hired well but fired slowly, with Facebook, which was good at removing underperformers.
