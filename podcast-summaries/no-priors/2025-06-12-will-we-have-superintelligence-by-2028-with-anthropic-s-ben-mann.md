## Thesis

Ben Mann, an Anthropic co-founder who now leads its Labs group, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that Claude 4's main advance is reliable, long-horizon agentic work, especially in coding. He sees coding as the path towards models that help build their successors, calls a 2028 arrival of superhuman AI "quite possible", and argues that deployment has to stay behind safety, as with computer use.

## Key points

- **Claude 4 just does the task.** Mann says the new models largely stop the reward hacking and unrequested edits users disliked in Sonnet 3.7. Customers run them unattended for hours on large refactors; a Manus user had a video turned into a PowerPoint deck via ffmpeg and a speech-to-text service.
- **Why Claude Code exists.** Partners such as Cursor and GitHub use Anthropic's models heavily, but Mann argues that a direct relationship with coding users teaches the company much faster. Researchers using it internally feel the model's weaknesses directly.
- **Superintelligence and the economic Turing test.** Mann points to AI 2027's median forecast of 2028 for recursive self-improvement. His own milestone for transformative AI is an agent hired over a human after a month-long trial, across a basket of roles covering 50% of economically valuable tasks.
- **Beyond human feedback.** It is getting harder to find experts who can judge model outputs, so Anthropic relies on RLAIF and constitutional AI, preference models trained on trusted expert feedback, and real-world empiricism. One example is Novo Nordisk cutting a clinical report from about 12 weeks to about 10 minutes.
- **Safety gates.** Mann says Opus 4 is classified ASL-3 under Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy because tests showed meaningful uplift over a Google search for biological misuse. A consumer computer-use agent was held back over irreversible actions and prompt injection.
- **Enterprise positioning and MCP.** Mann compares Anthropic to Adyen rather than Stripe: less consumer mind share but a strong API business. He describes the Model Context Protocol, created by engineer Justin Spahr-Summers, as an open standard now backed by OpenAI, Google and Microsoft, including remote hosted servers.
