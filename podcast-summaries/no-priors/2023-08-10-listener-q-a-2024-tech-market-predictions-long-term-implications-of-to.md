## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil answer listener questions. They argue that the GPU supply crunch is structural and will push the industry toward alternative chips and more efficient training. Enterprise AI adoption has barely started. Agent builders should begin with one narrow task, and the private tech market still has a reckoning ahead for companies that raised in 2021.

## Key points

- **The GPU crunch is a physical problem.** Guo points to a small set of suppliers led by Nvidia, a dependence on foundries such as TSMC, and hard yield problems. She says most large clouds are sold out through April of next year.
- **Second-order effects of scarce compute.** Gil expects GPU clouds and aggregators, former crypto-mining hardware being rented for AI, and demand for alternative silicon such as Cerebras, which signed a $100 million deal in the UAE for nine supercomputers. Guo adds that efficiency research, including FrugalGPT-style routing, distillation and better data selection, is undervalued.
- **Enterprise adoption is still ahead.** Gil says only AI-native companies and early adopters like Notion and Zapier have shipped so far. Large enterprises need planning and prototyping cycles, so large-scale deployments are probably one to two years out.
- **Agents should start narrow.** Guo describes agents as model-driven planners that act autonomously and often write executable code, citing Inflection, Minion and Harvey. Gil argues, using Facebook's college-by-college rollout as the example, that a focused first use case beats a general "make you happy" assistant. He adds infrastructure tooling as a third approach, comparing Stripe with Facebook's auth platform.
- **The 2024 market splits in thirds.** Gil predicts that of non-AI unicorns from 2021, a third will go under, a third have already reached their peak valuation, and a third will grow past it. He expects easier hiring, pressure on commercial real estate and knock-on effects for venture funds.
- **Cash burn matters more than valuation.** Guo advises founders to stop anchoring on old valuations. Gil says valuations can always be reset, but burning $50-100 million to reach $5-10 million of revenue is the real problem.
