## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil argue that AI is in a short period of relative calm: language model capabilities are converging, the infrastructure and application layers are taking shape, and standards like MCP are speeding up integration. Macro turbulence matters little to software startups. The open opportunities are in specialized models for domains where the training data does not exist yet.

## Key points

- **Image generation keeps surprising.** Gil traces "wow" moments from a GAN artwork sold at Sotheby's through early Midjourney and Stable Diffusion to the current anime-style wave. Guo says users assumed image generation was solved and are now seeing much more controllability, for example HeyGen's natural-language voice and emotion controls.
- **Macro is a shrug.** With the Nasdaq down 8%, Gil recalls Sequoia's 2008 "Rest in Peace, Good Times" presentation and says pure software startups are barely affected. He sees tariffs as justified in specific cases such as Chinese cars in Europe. Guo calls for a broader industrial policy.
- **Model convergence.** Gil cites artificialanalysis.ai benchmarks showing a tight cluster of frontier models. He notes that x.ai reached a roughly state-of-the-art model in about 9 months, and that Google's latest Gemini release shows it is still competitive.
- **Specialized models.** Gil sees under-funded opportunities in physics, materials, robotics and health. Guo says the key question is the data collection engine: physical-world data has to be generated. She also points to state space models (Cartesia), formal methods with Lean, and agent RL environments.
- **A two-by-two.** Gil sorts models by speed and cost against reasoning quality. Fast niche models suit vertical tasks, while slow general models serve high-value work, often behind an orchestration layer.
- **MCP and talent.** Guo describes Anthropic's Model Context Protocol, from Ben Mann's group, as an open, two-way standard for connecting models to data and tools, and notes that OpenAI said it will support it. Both want talent in hardware-software co-design, evals and agent orchestration.
