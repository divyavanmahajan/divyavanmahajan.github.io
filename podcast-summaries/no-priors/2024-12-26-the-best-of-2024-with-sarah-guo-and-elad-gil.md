## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil replay seven clips from their 2024 interviews. Together the guests describe AI progress as a stack: data-centre-scale compute at the bottom, models that may shrink or become world models, and products such as company agents and new interfaces on top. Several guests stress that the last stretch, whether self-driving reliability or general intelligence, is slower and harder than early demos suggest.

## Key points

- **The data centre is the computer.** Jensen Huang says Nvidia builds and tests entire data centres in every configuration, then disaggregates and sells the parts so that CUDA runs across GCP, AWS, Azure and OCI.
- **Exocortex and small models.** Andrej Karpathy asks whether "not your weights, not your brain" applies to AI, argues that open models must keep progressing as a fallback, and expects a small "cognitive core", perhaps around a billion parameters, made possible by distillation.
- **Company agents.** Bret Taylor compares branded AI agents to having a website in 1995. Sierra powers agents for Sonos and SiriusXM ("Harmony"), and he expects agents to cover everything a company does within three or four years.
- **Video as world model.** The Sora team says the model learned 3D structure and everyday physics, such as a bite leaving a mark on a hamburger, purely from video, and that simply predicting data scales best.
- **The long tail of nines.** Dmitri Dolgov says Waymo's 2009 goal of ten 100-mile routes without intervention took about 18 months, but removing the driver depends on the long tail of reliability. Waymo completes over 100,000 fully autonomous rides per week.
- **Interfaces and AGI.** Dylan Field expects new interfaces to add to existing UI rather than replace it, and is bullish on intelligent cameras. Alexandr Wang compares the path to AGI to curing cancer: many separate problems, each needing its own data flywheel, with little transfer between modalities.
