## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil argue that the model market turned out less monopolistic than expected: several GPT-4-level models, some open source, are likely by the end of 2024. Gil adds that the frontier may still end up an oligopoly, with the clouds quietly capturing much of the value. Both hosts see the first wave of AI applications as prosumer, much like the early internet and mobile waves.

## Key points

- **Cheaper capability.** Guo points to Mistral and Databricks' DBRX, and to Databricks' "Mosaic's law" claim that a model of a given capability costs a quarter as much each year.
- **Microsoft and Inflection.** Guo reads the deal as Microsoft being a true believer that wants its own AI product and research leadership, and partly as a hedge on its reliance on OpenAI.
- **Hyperscalers fund the market.** Gil notes that VCs put in hundreds of millions but big tech and Nvidia put in billions. He cites roughly 5% Azure growth from AI on about $25 billion of quarterly revenue.
- **White space and memetic waves.** Gil says a dozen teams pile into the same idea while obvious areas sit empty, which has led him to incubate companies. He compares it to the many photo apps that came before Instagram. Guo names time series, finance and accounting data, robotics and biotech as under-served or promising domains.
- **Video and agents.** Guo, an investor in HeyGen, says commercially useful video needs controllability, not just raw generation. Gil describes Devin's plan/shell/code/chat tabs as a new agent UI where humans watch and redirect the agent.
- **Prosumer first.** Guo argues that prosumer tools grow faster than enterprise sales cycles allow, noting that $1 billion of Canva's $1.7 billion ARR is prosumer.
