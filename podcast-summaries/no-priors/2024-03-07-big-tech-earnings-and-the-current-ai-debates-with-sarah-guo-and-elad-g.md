## Thesis

Sarah Guo and Elad Gil argue that, for now, most of the value from AI is going to incumbents: hyperscalers, Nvidia and companies like Meta that spend heavily on compute and see it in their earnings. Enterprise adoption is still early, but the hosts expect it to spread use case by use case once early adopters prove the savings. Competing with Nvidia is attractive but hard.

## Key points

- **Model news.** Gil points to Gemini 1.5's million-token context window and Mistral reaching near GPT-4 level in under a year, plus its Azure deal with Microsoft. Guo argues longer context opens new trade-offs with retrieval rather than killing RAG.
- **Google woke up.** Gil, the ex-Googler, says 1.5 made him more bullish on Google, which he once called a sleeping giant. Guo says Google has the research, data and distribution; the open question is focus.
- **Agents get narrower.** The hosts see agent teams succeeding by targeting specific domains such as code, RPA and customer support, with feedback loops and paid human feedback data, instead of agents that try to do anything.
- **Capex is paying off.** Guo calls Meta's earnings beat, after guiding $30-37 billion of 2024 capex, a one-day answer to David Cahn's "$200 billion question", with $197 billion of market cap added in one session. Gil estimates AI added about 5% to Azure revenue, or $5-6 billion annualized.
- **Enterprise substitution.** Gil cites Klarna's assistant: 2.3 million chats in four weeks, two-thirds of inquiries, resolution in 2 minutes versus 11, the work of 700 agents. His team puts US software spend at about half a trillion dollars against $3.5-5 trillion of payroll for services AI could affect.
- **Second source for Nvidia.** Gil lists Nvidia's moat as chip performance, CUDA and Mellanox interconnect, with AMD, Groq, Cerebras and Etched as challengers. Guo adds TSMC capacity and yield, and asks whether Transformer-specific chips are a safe bet given interest in state space models.
