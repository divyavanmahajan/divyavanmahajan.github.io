## Thesis

Datadog co-founder and CEO Olivier Pomel argues that if AI makes engineers ten times more productive, they will also understand much less of the code they ship. In his view, that moves value from writing software to observing, securing and fixing it. He believes LLMs open new doors for operations, but only when they are paired with real program state and precise traditional models, because operational workflows have little tolerance for error.

## Key points

- **Origin story.** Pomel says Datadog did not start as monitoring. It started as a way to get dev and ops teams onto the same platform. The name came from a feared production Oracle database, "Data Dog 17". He says being based in New York kept the company efficient and close to non-tech customers.
- **A new stack to observe.** Pomel says customers prototype with frontier APIs such as OpenAI while planning which functions to move to self-hosted open-source models. He thinks LLMs make LLM ops viable as a product category because the users are now every developer, not a handful of data scientists.
- **Precision matters.** Pomel says classic statistical methods such as Watchdog's anomaly detection are not going away. He notes that being wrong even 2% of the time in operational workflows becomes painful very quickly. Pasting a stack trace into GPT is usually wrong, he says, but adding the program state gives very precise answers.
- **Platform and M&A.** About half of Datadog's team works on the unified platform. Acquired companies, Sqreen among them, are re-platformed in the first year, and each deal has a plan to ship something together within three months.
- **Security as a different bet.** With more than 5,000 security customers, Pomel argues that the industry sells plenty of tools but does not produce better outcomes. Datadog relies on wide deployment and developer adoption rather than top-down sales to CISOs.
- **Automation is still early.** Pomel says LLMs have "ignition" and may reach production "liftoff" within about a year. It is not yet clear whether a further breakthrough is needed, since even Google-scale operations are only lightly automated.
