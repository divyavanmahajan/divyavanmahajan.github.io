## Thesis

Bob McGrew, former chief research officer at OpenAI, early Palantir executive and now CEO of Arda, argues that the hard-takeoff story is wrong: researchers are not the bottleneck, GPUs and physical experiments are. Superintelligence therefore has to reach into the physical world, and he is building software that lets US factories reconfigure automated lines as quickly as they retrain human workers.

## Key points

- **Why recursive self-improvement stalls.** McGrew says that in 2024 OpenAI was limited by GPUs, not by how many researchers it could hire. Automating research might lift GPU use from about 35% to 90% of optimal, a big one-time gain like the jump to reasoning models, but not a runaway loop.
- **Why America does not build what it designs.** The reason is labour cost. About 75% of the parts in each new iPhone change every year, and automation is bespoke, so those parts are assembled by hand. Arda's goal is to redesign and reconfigure an automated line in seven days.
- **Closed-world robotics.** Assembly lines are a closed world. McGrew thinks one or two arms and a camera are enough there and humanoids are not needed. Half the work in traditional automation is calibration, which perception, manipulation and RL can reduce.
- **Work with Anduril.** One manufacturing engineer cut a virtual build of an actuator from a few days to 90 minutes. Arda is now deployed in all of Anduril's product divisions, from drones and missiles to the Fury aircraft. McGrew calls the product "Cursor for manufacturing engineers": frontier models act as orchestrators of motion-planning and CAD tools.
- **Forward deployed engineering.** McGrew says this is a product strategy and not consulting. You find a sponsor and a power user, and you productise what FDEs learn, or you get no leverage.
- **The long view.** McGrew expects many robot forms, factories that build factories, and self-replicating machines spreading through space. His answer to the Fermi paradox is that such a wave travels near light speed, so we would only see it shortly before it arrives.
