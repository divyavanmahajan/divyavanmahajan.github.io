## Thesis

Replit CEO Amjad Masad argues that AI-assisted coding is still primitive and that the next gains will come from the infrastructure around models: giving them context, tools and the ability to act inside a development environment. He says beginners get the most value today, while professional programmers will see larger multiples as agents mature. In his view, Replit's advantage is its end-to-end platform from first line of code to production, not a data moat.

## Key points

- **Productivity today.** Masad estimates from Replit's usage research that AI gives 30 to 50 percent more productivity on coding itself, but says debugging, deployment and turning specs into code are still untouched. He cites a user who built a quarter-million ARR startup in a few months after not knowing how to code earlier in the year.
- **Agents in 6 to 18 months.** Holding current model capabilities constant, Masad expects basic agentic experiences, such as asking an AI to build a login page, within 6 to 18 months. Replit is building a service inside the container that lets the AI install packages, read and write files and clone repos.
- **Why train its own model.** Commercial APIs were slow and expensive and were moving away from completion models, so Replit trained a 3 billion parameter model for autocomplete. Masad says 1 billion "felt kind of too dumb", and that adding Replit data gave a 50 percent improvement over the open-sourced base model.
- **Llama and open source.** Masad says he pitched Mark Zuckerberg on treating open LLMs like the Open Compute Project, a commoditize-your-complement strategy. Gil notes that most open source waves had corporate sponsors, such as IBM for Linux.
- **Bounties and money as a primitive.** Replit bounties pay programmers in countries such as India, Thailand and Serbia, with MVPs available for 50 or 100 dollars. Masad wants a native currency in the platform and imagines automated bounty-hunter agents. He calls Bitcoin a long-term "TCP of money".
