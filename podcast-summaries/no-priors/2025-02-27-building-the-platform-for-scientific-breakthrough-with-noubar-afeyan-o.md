## Thesis

Noubar Afeyan, co-founder and CEO of Flagship Pioneering, tells Sarah Guo that entrepreneurship can be a profession run in parallel, and that breakthroughs are emergent rather than designed. Flagship's job, he says, is to build an environment for variation, selection and iteration, to take on uncertainty that others will not, and to build every company as a platform.

## Key points

- **Startups as a profession.** Afeyan started his first company in 1987 at 24. He then set up a "company creation company", first called NewcoGen and later renamed Flagship, because he thought the startup is humanity's most value-creating invention and should not be left to chance.
- **Risk versus uncertainty.** Afeyan separates risk, where adjacent innovations can be given probabilities, from uncertainty further out, where they cannot. Everyone crowds into the adjacencies, which brings commoditisation. Moderna, Flagship's 18th company, began when nobody was working on mRNA drugs.
- **Choosing sectors.** Joule engineered bacteria to turn CO2 into diesel between 2008 and 2012, but carbon fell from $50 to $5 a ton and diesel stayed a commodity. Afeyan's lesson: some sectors cannot pay a premium for leaps.
- **AI in discovery.** Flagship has 550 people and files 600-700 patents a year. Generate Biomedicines came out of a project to design proteins from function and sequence data alone, and now has more than 15 computationally designed antibody programs. Afeyan describes autonomous hypothesis-experiment loops and multi-agent systems in consumer products and mental health.
- **Clinical bottlenecks.** Afeyan calls cancer staging a "super macro approximation". He wants molecular bio-staging, with thousands of stages rather than four, to choose trial subgroups, allow smaller first approvals and treat Operation Warp Speed, with its guaranteed purchase prices, as the model.
- **Platforms over single assets.** Every Flagship company is a platform. Afeyan sees a "mass extinction" of single-asset biotechs, made worse by cheaper Chinese assets. In his 2025 letter he describes "polyintelligence", a triangle of human, machine and nature's intelligence.
