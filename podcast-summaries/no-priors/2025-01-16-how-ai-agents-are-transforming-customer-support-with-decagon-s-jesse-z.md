## Thesis

Jesse Zhang, co-founder and CEO of Decagon, tells Elad Gil that customer service is the "golden use case" for AI agents. He says the companies that win will be ones that make the agent transparent, not a black box. He expects near-term agent adoption only where two conditions hold: the agent can be rolled out gradually, and its ROI is easy to measure.

## Key points

- **Results are measurable.** Gil cites Klarna's figures: 2.3 million chats in four weeks, satisfaction on par with humans and resolution in 2 minutes instead of 11. Zhang says Decagon customers judge it on the share of conversations the agent handles and on customer satisfaction or NPS. Bilt Rewards stopped growing its support team within about a month and, by its published case study, saved around 65 agents of headcount.
- **The value is in software, not models.** Zhang says everyone has access to the same models. Decagon's edge is an orchestration layer, built with evals and shaped around each customer's business logic. It adds tooling that shows what data and steps the agent used, and it uses an LLM to read every conversation and spot gaps in the knowledge base.
- **Instruction following matters more than reasoning.** Zhang says recent progress in models like o1 and Sonnet has mostly been in maths and coding. Support agents mainly need to follow an SOP or workflow exactly.
- **Voice is next, and latency is the problem.** Zhang says voice-to-voice models are fast, while a speech-to-text pipeline allows data lookups and multiple model calls. Fillers like "give me a sec" hide the delay. He names ElevenLabs, OpenAI and Cartesia.
- **Many agent markets will be slow.** In security, agents must be close to perfect from day one. In tasks like text-to-SQL, the agent turns into a copilot whose value is hard to price. Zhang is bearish on most agent use cases in the near term.
- **Maths olympiad networks.** Zhang says contest alumni who once went into academia or quant trading now found startups, and they angel-invest in each other's companies.
