## Thesis

Sridhar Ramaswamy, 18 months into his tenure as Snowflake CEO, tells Sarah Guo that Snowflake's place in AI is the data platform layer, not models or raw cloud. He argues that a narrow, trustworthy agent over enterprise data, built through fast iteration and partnerships, is more durable than a general "one agent to rule them all" approach.

## Key points

- **Reorganising for speed.** Ramaswamy says the hypergrowth-era Snowflake had seven to ten team layers between an engineer and the customer. He reorganised product engineering into areas such as AI and core warehousing, and set up pods that pair product with go-to-market. His rule: "speed wins".
- **No foundation model.** Snowflake built a credible model early last year but stopped, judging it lacked the capital to compete with OpenAI or Anthropic. It focused instead on search and text-to-SQL over data already in Snowflake, which he says holds data for about half of qualifying Fortune 2000 companies.
- **Snowflake Intelligence.** Ramaswamy calls it an opinionated agentic platform for every employee, not just SQL users. It grew out of Raven, an internal sales data assistant that went through three earlier versions, and was tested with Cisco, Fanatics and the USA bobsled team. It is sold on consumption, and every launch or model change needs an eval.
- **Living next to giants.** Ramaswamy describes OpenAI and Anthropic as empires that have not yet met their limits, and warns that a set of prompts on top of a model is a weak position. Guo adds that defensibility is built, not strategised.
- **Partnerships.** He describes a better relationship with Microsoft than 18 months ago, similar terms with AWS and GCP, and a bidirectional data share with SAP.
- **ROI and search.** His easiest ROI cases are coding agents, customer support and wider data access. He advises spending small amounts at a time, expects ads to reinvent themselves in chat, and argues LLMs should keep using search as a tool, just as they should call Python rather than do maths themselves.
