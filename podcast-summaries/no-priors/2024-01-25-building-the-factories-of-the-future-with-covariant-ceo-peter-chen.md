## Thesis

Peter Chen, co-founder and CEO of Covariant and a former OpenAI research scientist, tells Sarah Guo that the internet holds no dataset for training a robotics foundation model. In his view, the only way to collect that data is to deploy robots that customers already pay for, following what he calls the Tesla approach, and to improve the model step by step. Covariant's core bet is that real robot data, not simulation or any particular architecture, will decide who leads in robotics.

## Key points

- **From research to company.** Chen says he and his co-founders left OpenAI in 2017 believing that foundation models were the future of AI. They concluded that only a company running production robot fleets could collect enough data for robotics.
- **Dumb versus smart robots.** Chen contrasts a pre-programmed Tesla factory robot that moves car bodies with an e-commerce warehouse robot that has to handle hundreds of thousands of unique items. Covariant's customers often see more than 100% annual staff turnover.
- **Put wall.** Covariant's typical use case sorts mixed orders to individual customers, which Chen calls a "physical router." He says grasping needs the most AI, but AI also improves identification, for example by locating barcodes.
- **Scale and deployment.** Covariant has about 200 people, with customers split roughly half Europe and half North America. Its robots run in more than 10 countries and all feed one shared model.
- **Grounding and scaling.** Chen says internet image-text data lacks sub-millimetre precision and action-outcome pairs, such as how hard to press a tomato. Because dense data coverage in a narrow domain is enough, Covariant does not depend on emergent capabilities from scaling. He treats simulation as augmentation, since contact dynamics and 100,000-SKU warehouses are hard to model.
- **ChatGPT moment and safety.** Chen says robotics needs ChatGPT-like generality plus high reliability, because a 70% success rate is not acceptable. He expects it to arrive first in industrial settings, with one person supervising 10 to 30 robots. Industrial safety rules, such as cages and certified controllers, already constrain Covariant's systems.
