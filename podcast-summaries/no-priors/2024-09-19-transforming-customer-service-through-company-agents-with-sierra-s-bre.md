## Thesis

Bret Taylor, co-founder of Sierra and chair of OpenAI's board, argues that customer-facing "company agents" can be built with today's technology, because the processes and systems behind them are well defined. He expects every company to need a branded AI agent, much as every company needed a website in 1995. In his view, durable AI businesses will sit on top of foundation models the way SaaS sat on top of cloud infrastructure, and will charge for outcomes rather than tokens.

## Key points

- **Three kinds of agent.** Taylor separates personal agents (the hardest, probably owned by Apple, Google, OpenAI), persona agents that do one job (Harvey for law, coding agents) and company agents. He sees company agents as ready to build now. Sierra customers include Sonos and SiriusXM, whose agent is called Harmony.
- **Cost per contact.** A call-centre contact costs about $13 today; Taylor says AI brings it well below a dollar, so companies may hold far more conversations with customers.
- **Beyond RAG.** Answering questions from a knowledge base is "a great demo", he says, but real service means taking action across 10+ systems of record under business and compliance rules. Software moves from rules engines to "goals and guardrails", enforced partly by supervisor models checking other models.
- **AI rhymes with cloud.** Taylor expects a few capital-heavy pre-training labs, a layer of tool vendors (he names Scale AI), and solution companies. A startup whose value shrinks with each model release is not a real solution.
- **Outcome-based pricing.** Sierra charges for the job done, which Taylor compares to the shift from impression ads to cost-per-click. A host cites Klarna's support automation, recalled as cutting about 700 roles while raising satisfaction.
- **What's next.** Taylor names back-office analyst work as the next target and wonders whether voice interfaces will finally loosen the smartphone's grip on how people use computers.
