## Thesis

Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince argues that the web's value model, built on search traffic, is breaking as people consume AI-generated derivatives instead of original pages. If creators can no longer earn from sales, ads or even recognition, they stop creating, which starves both the web and the AI companies. His fix is to create scarcity by blocking AI crawlers by default, force Google onto the same terms as everyone else, and then let a market price content.

## Key points

- **Traffic is collapsing.** Prince says that compared with 10 years ago it is 10 times harder for content to get a click from Google; with OpenAI it is 750 times harder and with Anthropic 30,000 times harder.
- **Content independence day.** On July 1st Cloudflare began blocking AI training crawlers by default for all customers, paid or free, and is working with the IETF on standards for crawlers to declare what they do. Prince says 80% of major AI companies are Cloudflare customers and that they agree content should be paid for, provided the playing field is level.
- **Google is the sticking point.** Prince wants Google to separate search indexing from uses such as AI overviews and Gemini. He calls flat deals, such as $20 million for all of a publisher's content, naive and argues for revenue shares that grow with AI companies.
- **Pay for the holes in the cheese.** He describes AI models as Swiss cheese and argues creators should be rewarded for filling gaps rather than for cortisol-driven clicks, citing Spotify, which pays out over $10 billion a year and surfaces unmet search demand to musicians.
- **Efficiency over brute force.** Prince recalls launching edge GPU inference with Nvidia in 2020 to no demand, then relaunching four years later. He expects pruning and compression to put current ChatGPT-class models on phones.
- **Agent rails.** He expects agent protocols like MCP to pass through Cloudflare, and sees cryptographically signed agent identity and possibly micropayments across its 15 trillion daily requests.
