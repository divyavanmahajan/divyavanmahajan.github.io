## Thesis

Josh Goldman, cofounder and president of KoBold Metals, argues that the scarce resource in mining is not metal in the ground but information about where ore deposits are. KoBold focuses on exploration, where he says a few million dollars can return 100 to 1,000 times. It aims to beat an industry whose success rate has fallen roughly tenfold in 30 years by combining AI with geologists' judgement.

## Key points

- **Data spread everywhere.** Goldman says much geoscience data is public but split across tens of thousands of repositories. Examples range from satellite and airborne surveys to almost century-old hand-painted Zambian maps on linen, which serve as ground truth for training models.
- **A full stack.** KoBold's technology has three parts: its own sensors (a hyperspectral imaging system built in under a year that captures 600 colours), a unified data system that uses LLMs for unstructured reports, and dozens of predictive models. These are retrained daily as field teams collect samples where the models are most uncertain.
- **Mingomba.** KoBold's Zambian copper deposit has a core grade above 5%, against about 0.6% for operating mines. Goldman explains that higher grade means less rock to move, lower capital intensity and a smaller footprint.
- **Industry economics.** By Goldman's account, $1 billion of exploration yielded about eight quality discoveries 30 years ago and less than one today. KoBold targets $50 to 100 million per discovery across more than 60 projects on four continents.
- **Epistemology as practice.** KoBold has an internal "epistemology of exploration" document and a chief philosopher, Michael Strevens. Teams must make falsifiable predictions and keep several alternative hypotheses in play, which Goldman sees as a guard against confirmation bias.
- **Scarcity claims.** Goldman says rare earths are not that rare. Their prominence, he says, comes from China's concentration of processing capacity. Demand from batteries and data centres means the world must mine more copper in the next 25 years than in all of history.
