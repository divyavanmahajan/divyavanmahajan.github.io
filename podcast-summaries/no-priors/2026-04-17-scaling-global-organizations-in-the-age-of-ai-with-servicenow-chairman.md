## Thesis

ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott tells Sarah Guo that the "SaaS apocalypse" theory misreads how enterprises work. Language models can advise, he argues, but a workflow platform carries the data, context and accountability needed to close a case, and rebuilding one with generated code would cost about ten times more. He also expects agents to take over much of the support work that used to require new hires.

## Key points

- **Customer first, from the deli on.** McDermott bought a deli at 16 for $5,500 in notes ($7,000 with interest), stocked it on consignment and served 500 customers a day. His lesson: the customer alone decides whether you win or lose.
- **"AI thinks, workflow acts."** Using a compensation case as his example, he says a model can list the steps, but closing the case means routing data through HR, finance, legal and compliance. ServiceNow's white paper on the "blueprint for agentic business" makes this distinction.
- **The cost of rebuilding.** Add the cost of new code, engineers and GPUs plus tokens, and ServiceNow's maths puts a simple app at 10x the cost when replicated with a language model. Non-deterministic mistakes are another problem. He thinks single-department vendors are the ones most at risk.
- **Control tower strategy.** ServiceNow wants to sit across hyperscalers, models and roughly 800 systems of record. He cites more than 85 billion workflows in flight, 7 trillion transactions, and the Moveworks, Veza and Armis acquisitions (security and OT), which he says were integrated in 20 days.
- **Workforce effects.** He says 90% of ServiceNow's customer service cases are handled by agents, that net new headcount will drop sharply, and that 2.2 billion agents will enter the workforce in the next couple of years. He links today's cuts partly to over-hiring over Zoom in 2020-2024.
- **Adoption.** Only 11% of the Brazilian companies he met had moved past experiments. The US leads, and financial services moves fastest. Customers now want prescriptive advice and go-lives in under 30 days.
