## Thesis

OpenAI president and co-founder Greg Brockman argues that the resolution of almost 400 open maths problems settles whether AI can produce new knowledge, and that the same methods should transfer to biology wherever enough data exists. He says the "physics" of cybersecurity are also changing: defenders have a limited window to use frontier models to fix decades of insecure code, while humans stay in the driver's seat.

## Key points

- **Maths as a milestone.** Brockman says OpenAI published almost 400 important open problems now resolved, and that experts reviewing them report surprising, readable insights rather than brute-force proofs like the four colour theorem. He links explaining such results to debate and iterated amplification, oversight techniques OpenAI has discussed since 2017.
- **Data is the bottleneck in biology.** Brockman frames capability as compute, data and algorithms. Biology is data-bound, so wet-lab and simulation work will matter. Some bottlenecks are mundane: AI could shorten the year spent writing clinical trial filings. The hosts note a drug takes about 15 years and $1.5 billion to develop, most of it in clinical trials.
- **Regulation should follow proof.** If AI changes what is possible in drug discovery, Brockman says, regulation should adapt, as it would for self-driving cars that are 10 or 100 times safer than human drivers. He notes trials already move between countries such as Australia and the US.
- **Dots and no UI.** Brockman describes Dots, an always-on proactive agent that reads his inbox, Slack and calendar and leaves him a morning to-do list. He expects consumer and work assistants to converge, separated mainly by compute cost, and cites ChatGPT's 1.2 billion consumers. Generative UI will build bespoke interfaces on demand.
- **The defender's window.** Brockman points to AI-driven bank hacks in South Korea and to OpenAI's Daybreak programme and $1 billion commitment for defenders. Internally, OpenAI put about 25% of production engineers on securing its systems with its best model until it found no more issues. He expects formal verification and secure-by-default AI code to follow.
