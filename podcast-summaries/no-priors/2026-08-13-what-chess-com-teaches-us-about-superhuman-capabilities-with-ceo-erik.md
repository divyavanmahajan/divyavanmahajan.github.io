## Thesis

Erik Allebest, co-founder and CEO of Chess.com, tells Sarah Guo that chess is the longest-running test of what happens to a human activity once machines become superhuman at it, and the result is more players, not fewer. He argues that people want to compete with other humans, and that AI tools have made the game more exciting and faster to learn. Guo draws the wider conclusion that human skill will stay relevant alongside superhuman AI.

## Key points

- **Bootstrapped from a domain name.** Allebest and a friend bought the chess.com domain at a bankruptcy auction in 2005 for $56,000, after most venture capitalists told him the idea was uninvestable. They launched in 2007, charged for memberships within about 18 months, and grew on revenue alone.
- **Scale today.** Chess.com has about 10 million daily active users, 40-50 million monthly users, over 250 million registered members, a little over $200 million in revenue expected this year, and about 650 fully remote staff. The goal is a billion players.
- **Waves of growth.** COVID and The Queen's Gambit lifted usage from about a million daily users. A 2023 wave driven by short-form video, the Mittens bot and the cheating scandal set a higher baseline. Allebest says only in 2024 did they believe it would be a big business.
- **Private equity without new capital.** General Atlantic and later CVC bought secondary shares, so the company remains bootstrapped. Allebest says both pushed operational discipline rather than following the private-equity stereotype.
- **Engines changed chess twice.** Stockfish made top play dull for a while, he says. Then neural-net engines such as Leela Chess Zero played aggressively and pushed the game forward. Still, he argues that expertise comes from repetition, such as daily puzzles and reviewing mistakes, and there are no shortcuts.
- **AI inside the company.** Chess.com uses AI for support automation, data analysis and agentic development. CTO Josh built an internal knowledge layer called GNS. It is prototyping an AI coach that reviews a week of games. It also launched Gambit, a poker site where players compete for ratings.
