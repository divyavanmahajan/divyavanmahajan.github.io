## Thesis

Jacob Helberg, US Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs, argues that the AI supply chain is far more than chips. He says the US must reduce its dependence on China for thousands of inputs. Pax Silica, a 14-country economic security coalition, is his vehicle. Unlike China's Belt and Road, it puts private companies in the driver's seat, on platforms that can eventually run without government support.

## Key points

- **A forward-deployed industrial base.** The Philippines is granting the US 4,000 acres, about a third of the size of Manhattan. In phase one the State Department holds it as diplomatic property, governed like an embassy. Phase two gives two years to negotiate investor protections and tax rules for a multi-decade framework. The robotics supply chain is an early target.
- **Beyond chips.** Helberg lists precision reducers, servo motors, rare earth magnets and actuators as inputs where US concentration risk is very high.
- **Lessons from Belt and Road.** After studying 25 years of the initiative, Helberg describes waste, "roads to nowhere", cost overruns that can reach 10x and debt that converts to Chinese equity. The US approach is product-centric, built on joint ventures that share risk and upside.
- **Reindustrialization through automation.** The US accounts for 20-30% of global consumption with 4% of the world's population but produces far less. With unemployment at 4%, new production must be highly automated, as Singapore shows. Fabs stay in the US, while other countries become regional hubs.
- **Minerals and pricing.** Rare earths are not that rare; refining capacity outside China is. Helberg cites a February 4 critical minerals summit with more than 55 countries. On the supply side the government is investing capital; on the demand side it is negotiating prices. He asks venture investors to help spot strong operators and to back innovations such as rare-earth-free magnets.
- **Durability and the underdog.** Helberg says tax reform is hard to undo but declines to discuss electoral politics. He calls America a "global underdog" that performs best under pressure, and gives the COVID vaccine as his example.
