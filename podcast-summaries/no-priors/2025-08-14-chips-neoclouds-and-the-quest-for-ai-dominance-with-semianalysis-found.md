## Thesis

SemiAnalysis's Dylan Patel tells Sarah Guo that Nvidia is a three-headed dragon, strong in chip engineering, networking and software, so specialised challengers rarely win once all the penalties add up. He expects most of the roughly 200 neoclouds to consolidate or fail, and sees AI infrastructure held back by many bottlenecks at once rather than a single one. Guo argues that infrastructure, not open-source model optimisation, will be the lasting differentiator for inference providers.

## Key points

- **OpenAI's open model.** Patel expects the first best-in-class American open-weights model since Llama 3.1 405B; it is reasoning-focused, good at code, and ships with custom kernels so inference is optimised from day one.
- **Neocloud shake-out.** Patel says SemiAnalysis tracks about 200 neoclouds. Some, like CoreWeave, are sold out on multi-year contracts, while others burn cash on low utilisation; the paths are to go very big (Crusoe), move into software, accept real-estate returns, or go bankrupt.
- **Why challengers struggle.** Patel argues a new chip pays a penalty of 20-30% for each lag on process node, memory and networking, so a unique design must be far more than two to four times faster. First-wave firms such as Cerebras, Groq, SambaNova and Graphcore bet on on-chip memory, then models grew too big to fit.
- **Best second choices.** Patel expects AMD GPUs, Amazon's Trainium or Google's TPUs to become alternatives before any chip startup does, citing Amazon's rack integration problems as a sign of how hard hardware supply chains are.
- **Bottlenecks everywhere.** Patel lists CoWoS packaging, HBM, transformers, power generation, turbine backlogs and electricians; Meta is using tent structures for GPUs, and he credits xAI with building Colossus faster than anyone.
- **Export policy.** Patel supports selling the highest-value layer (services, then tokens, then infrastructure), but says China cannot be cut off entirely because it can retaliate with rare earth minerals.
