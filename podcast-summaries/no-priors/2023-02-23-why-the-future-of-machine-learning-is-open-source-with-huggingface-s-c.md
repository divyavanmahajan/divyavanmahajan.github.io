## Thesis

Clem Delangue, co-founder and CEO of Hugging Face, argues that open source is the main reason machine learning moves from research paper to production in weeks rather than decades. He expects open and proprietary approaches to keep trading the lead task by task, and he sees a future of many specialised models rather than one company or model that rules them all.

## Key points

- **From AI tamagotchi to open source.** Delangue says Hugging Face spent almost three years building a chatbot "AI friend" with billions of messages exchanged, then shifted after its open-source Transformers work took off and the team raised a Series A on that traction.
- **Exploration budget.** He advises founders to keep 30-40% of company effort on exploration. Spaces, Hugging Face's ML demo product, started as one employee's experiment and passed 50,000 demos in a year.
- **Community is everyone's job.** Hugging Face hired no community managers or PR staff; every team member, including researchers, engages with the community, and everyone can tweet from the company account.
- **Open vs proprietary.** Delangue says proprietary models lead in text generation (ChatGPT) while open source leads in areas such as classification and image generation (Stable Diffusion). He points to 250,000 models from almost 15,000 companies on the hub as evidence that specialised models are cheaper, faster and often more accurate, citing Bloomberg's preference for small, low-latency models.
- **Infrastructure and data consent.** He wants clearer infrastructure costs (criticising "cloud money laundering"), more decentralised and online training, and opt-out datasets such as BigCode's. BigScience's BLOOM involved about 1,000 researchers from 200 organisations.
- **Business model.** Delangue describes a freemium model: about 15,000 companies use the platform and 3,000 pay, mostly for security, compliance and compute such as GPU Spaces and inference endpoints. He says GitHub moved into compute too late, and Hugging Face aims to become the gate to compute earlier.
