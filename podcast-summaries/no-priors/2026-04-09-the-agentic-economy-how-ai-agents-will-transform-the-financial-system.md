## Thesis

Circle co-founder and CEO Jeremy Allaire tells Elad Gil that the agentic economy is being born now, and that banking infrastructure cannot support it. Agents need money that is global, instant, programmable by any software and cheap at micro scale. He argues that full-reserve stablecoins such as USDC, running on blockchains he treats as operating systems, provide that base.

## Key points

- **Full-reserve money.** Allaire traces his interest to Austrian economics and Irving Fisher's 1930s "100% money" proposal, which the banks lobbied against. Dollar stablecoins, now codified by last year's GENIUS Act, are his version of narrow, full-reserve money.
- **What backs USDC.** USDC is backed by short-duration US Treasuries, overnight repo and some cash at custodians such as Bank of New York, with an average duration of about 13 days. Uses range from 25-cent game payments and agents paying other agents, through Stripe, Shopify, Visa and Ramp, to trading firms settling multi-hundred-million-dollar transactions.
- **Why agents need new rails.** Agents must spin up their own financial endpoints and handle billions of transactions, some worth 5 or 10 cents. He points to OpenClaw and Moltbook as signs of emergent cooperation between AIs, which needs a trustworthy medium for value and contracts.
- **Arc.** Circle's blockchain, which he calls an "economic operating system", uses a known set of regulated validators, deterministic settlement finality in hundreds of milliseconds, USDC as the native gas token, and built-in privacy primitives. He says it can process transactions for a millionth of a penny.
- **Tokenization.** He says Circle is the most active tokenized stock on rwa.xyz, and that Circle runs the largest tokenized treasury product (USYC) and the largest tokenized euro (EURC). The DTCC, Nasdaq and NYSE are moving on-chain under new SEC guidance, and USDC powers Polymarket.
- **Ten-year view.** Allaire expects a renegotiation of the social contract, with on-chain organizations that mix human and agent actors. He calls double-digit GDP growth in the 2030s "not unrealistic", but warns that the growth could mean capital capturing more capital at the expense of humans.
