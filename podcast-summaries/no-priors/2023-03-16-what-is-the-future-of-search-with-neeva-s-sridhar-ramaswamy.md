## Thesis

Sridhar Ramaswamy, former head of Google Ads and co-founder of Neeva, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that users have always wanted answers rather than links, and that large language models make single cited answers possible for most queries for the first time. He sees distribution and the publisher ecosystem, not technology, as the biggest open questions.

## Key points

- **Lessons from private search.** Ramaswamy says Neeva's first three years, built around an ad-free private engine, taught harsh lessons: US consumers cared less about privacy than expected, and Neeva did much better in Europe.
- **Answers beat links.** He notes Google's featured snippets never covered more than about 5-7% of queries, whereas LLM summaries let Neeva write one authoritative answer for 50-70% of queries over its own index of roughly 4 billion pages.
- **Retrieval augmented generation.** Neeva treats the search engine as a tool the model uses, and Ramaswamy expects chatbots and search to blur further, with models calling calculators or APIs.
- **Cost of serving.** He estimates a large-model call at about 5 cents, against US revenue of roughly $40-50 per thousand queries, but says fine-tuned 5-10 billion parameter models handle most tasks; he adds that Microsoft could rationally offer Apple a 100% revenue share to win traffic.
- **Ads versus subscriptions.** Ramaswamy says ads carry "elements of self-destruction", calls Amazon's ad-heavy search experience a joke, and argues answer products are harder to monetise with ads.
- **Publishers and what comes next.** He expects large sites such as Reddit and Quora to restrict LLM use of their content, small ad-funded blogs to struggle, and action-oriented models to be the next big but still nascent area.
