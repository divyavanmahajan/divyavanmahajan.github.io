## Thesis

In a hosts-only episode, Sarah Guo and Elad Gil review the main constraints on AI progress: how much capability fits on a device, who can afford frontier-scale compute, how far context windows will grow, and where the energy will come from. Both see energy and permitting as the point where scaling stops being only a software problem.

## Key points

- **Music generation.** Gil sees Suno and Udio as the latest step in a sequence of content waves: text with Jasper, images with Midjourney and Stable Diffusion, chat, and video with Pika. Guo asks how many people actually want to create music, since most users of media platforms consume rather than create.
- **Small and local models.** Guo reads Apple's small open models on Hugging Face as a sign that Apple will offer interfaces for running models locally. Gil notes that platforms usually absorb the most valuable apps, as Microsoft did with Office, with rare exceptions such as Veeva, which started on top of Salesforce. He also asks how much reasoning and knowledge a 3B or 7B model can hold.
- **Meta and scale.** Guo says Meta's results show that training past "optimal" points still improves performance for a firm with clusters of up to 350,000 GPUs. She estimates hyperscaler AI compute spending at almost $200 billion this year and compares it with oil majors at $80 billion a year and broadband at about $100 billion.
- **Long context.** Gil, an investor in Magic, says Magic launched a 5 million token window before Gemini 1.5's million. He expects 10 million plus to become common and points to biology models, where longer context improves protein folding results.
- **Energy.** Guo notes that nobody has yet built a 500 megawatt to gigawatt data centre, and that training needs co-located GPUs. Gil says nuclear supplies 17-18% of US power against about 70% in France, and cites Microsoft's nuclear engineering job ads and its $1.5 billion investment in G42 in Abu Dhabi.
