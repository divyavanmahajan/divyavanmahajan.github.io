## Thesis

Notion co-founder and CEO Ivan Zhao argues that retrieval over embeddings is an underrated AI category. With RAG, a team gets "perfect memory" and no longer has to organise its knowledge. He also argues that language models want data in one place, which favours bundled tools like Notion over fragmented SaaS.

## Key points

- **Lego bricks.** Zhao says Notion spent five-plus years building primitives such as a text editor, a relational database, permissions and comments. AI then acted like a new engine for those bricks, which let Notion ship AI writing, AI database features and Q&A quickly.
- **GPT-4 as the turning point.** Zhao admits he "slept on" GPT-3. An early preview of GPT-4 convinced him the model could reason, and the company bet on AI. He describes knowledge work as paper-pushing that language models can now partly do.
- **Q&A over search.** Notion Q&A replaces keyword recall with semantic retrieval. Zhao estimates internal Slack questions cost 10 minutes to write and 20 to answer, which Q&A can cut to seconds. He predicts the organising sidebar may become unnecessary.
- **Team shape.** Notion had few ML staff. Zhao describes AI work as probabilistic software engineering and says it needs both patient ML people and fast-learning young AI engineers. Notion uses high-end OpenAI and Anthropic models.
- **Bundling cycles.** Quoting Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Zhao says business moves between bundling and unbundling. One host adds that companies such as HubSpot and Rippling now accept products that are only 80% as good in exchange for cross-sell. Zhao says bundling also saves enterprise customers money on project management and issue tracking tools.
- **Design.** Zhao runs design centrally, "more Apple like less Amazon like", and says about 80% of Notion's designers can code.
