## Thesis

Illia Polosukhin, a co-author of the Transformers paper and co-founder of NEAR, argues that AI and web3 fit together. Blockchain accounts let AI agents pay people and coordinate work, and cryptographic signing plus on-chain identity give society a way to handle misinformation at the scale AI makes possible. In his view the real alignment problem is human, not AI.

## Key points

- **From AI to blockchain.** Polosukhin says NEAR started in 2018 to teach machines to code. Paying crowdsourced student developers in China and Eastern Europe proved hard, so the team built a blockchain to solve its own payments problem.
- **AI-run organizations.** He describes agents with blockchain accounts acting as a "CEO" for a mission such as cancer research: funding labs, tracking delivery and replacing middle management. He expects DAOs, starting with Discord onboarding bots, to adopt this first.
- **Human alignment and provenance.** Citing the Byzantine fault tolerance problem, he calls for camera secure enclaves that sign images, signed content with an SSL-style "green lock", and named accounts like root.near with scoped keys for agents. He expects the next US election and voice-clone fraud to test these defenses.
- **Inference over training.** Polosukhin says ex-Ethereum mining GPUs are not the A100s and H100s AI teams want, and decentralized training cannot match 800-gigabit links between GPUs. Private, scalable decentralized inference using MPC or zero-knowledge proofs is more realistic.
- **Labeling markets and SaaS.** He argues a web3 marketplace with escrow, buy-ins and honeypot questions beats Mechanical Turk on fairness and quality. He predicts SaaS apps will become user-owned data with generated, task-specific interfaces.
- **Architecture lock-in.** He expects Transformers to stay, adding the ability to "think" before outputting a token. One of the hosts compares the GPU-Transformer lock-in to Wintel.
