## Thesis

Recorded on 9 January 2025, while the Los Angeles fires were still burning, this conversation has Bill Clerico (WePay founder, now running the wildfire-focused fund Convective Capital) argue that climate change makes fire seasons worse. He blames the past decade's surge in damage mostly on problems people created: dense forests, an ageing electrical grid, housing in the wildland-urban interface, and regulated insurance pricing. Elad Gil and Sarah Guo keep pushing on how much of this comes down to regulation and policy.

## Key points

- **A century of suppression.** Clerico says the US Forest Service's "10:00 a.m. policy", in place since the 1930s, has left forests about three times denser. Florida does 2 to 3 million acres of prescribed burning a year, while California set a goal of 100,000 acres and probably missed it.
- **Red tape.** Clerico spent three years getting permission to treat his 170 acres of timber, including six northern spotted owl surveys. Gil cites 4.7 to 7.5 years of environmental review for prescribed-burn projects. Clerico backs the bipartisan Fix Our Forests Act.
- **Utilities and rates.** Utilities cause 11% of ignitions but 50% of the damage, Clerico says. He argues the utilities would happily spend the CapEx and the real pushback comes from rate regulators. Undergrounding costs $3-4 million per mile.
- **Insurance exodus.** According to Clerico, insurers couldn't price in reinsurance costs, which have doubled or tripled, and had to use historical rather than forward-looking models, so they pulled out of the market. The commissioner relaxed both rules only after the market failed.
- **Water and access.** The Palisades water tanks held enough water, Clerico says, but the system lost pressure as burning houses opened their plumbing. Abandoned cars also kept engines out of the area. He wants the homes on a community's edge hardened, with defensible space, a cleared "zone zero" and water sources.
- **Technology ahead.** Clerico expects pre-positioned drone swarms to handle initial attack and structure defence within 5-10 years. He also warns that wildfire is an under-discussed national security risk.
