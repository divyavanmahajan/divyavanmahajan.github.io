## Thesis

Elias Torres, a fifth-time founder who sold Drift for more than $1B, tells Sarah Guo that B2B companies have never worked out how to look after tens of thousands of customers personally. His new company, Agency, is betting that LLMs can make that possible. He also argues that today's database-centred software makes people work for it, and that the next generation should act for users without being asked.

## Key points

- **From exit to OpenAI consulting.** Torres says the Drift sale felt like a failure because he had wanted a $3B or public company. After ChatGPT launched, he took on OpenAI's overflow customer work, building LLM apps for clients such as the NBA, Ticketmaster, Red Bull and Klaviyo.
- **Customers, not a CS category.** Agency grew out of helping Klaviyo scale customer success across hundreds of thousands of customers. Torres says he cares about putting the customer in charge, the way he can simply text his barber, not about "CS" as a market category.
- **$1B revenue, under 100 people.** At Drift he had about 250 people in the customer organisation and says that did not solve the problem. He argues Paul Graham's "do things that don't scale" no longer applies when AI is available.
- **Contract-to-hire for everyone.** Torres says nobody joins Agency without first working for him as a contractor for one or two weeks and delivering world-class work. For now he is mostly hiring engineers.
- **Change management and trust are the hard parts.** He says LLMs solve only a small part of the problem. Customer success managers question every word of an AI-drafted email to a $1M customer, even though their own emails are no better.
- **Software should be invisible.** Torres says he would tell CEOs to throw out their Salesforce instances. He describes software that, for example, reads a flight in his calendar and arranges coffee meetings without being asked, and he expects old database "wrappers" to die unless they adapt.
