## Thesis

Jacob Helberg, Under Secretary of State designate for economic growth, energy and the environment, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that AI is primarily a productivity technology that gives workers "superpowers" rather than replacing them. Combined with reshored supply chains and much more nuclear energy, he argues, it can shift the US from a consumption- and services-driven economy to a high-investment, industrial one.

## Key points

- **Brittle supply chains.** Helberg cites 90% reliance on critical minerals refined in China and semiconductors made in Taiwan, and calls China a systemic rival whose global influence flows from being the world's factory floor.
- **A template for rare earths.** He says China drove Western refiners out by flooding the market after its Made in China 2025 plan; the DoD's $750 million deal with MP Materials, with an anchor buyer and a price floor, is his blueprint for a response.
- **The capex shift.** Helberg says capex is over 2% of GDP and likely to double, with AI infrastructure adding about a full point of GDP growth, while US electricity demand is rising for the first time since 2008.
- **Superintelligence century.** He predicts a "second great divergence" in which early AI adopters leapfrog others, the cheap-labour advantage of developing economies collapses, Europe keeps declining (he blames the AI Act) and the Middle East rises.
- **Nuclear and foreign capital.** Helberg calls nuclear the best path to doubling electricity output, points to France getting 75% of its energy from nuclear, and wants CFIUS to signal openness to investment from trusted partners, including in the Middle East.
- **DeepSeek and open source.** He claims DeepSeek understated its compute and distilled ChatGPT, and calls Meta's open-source efforts important for promoting the American stack abroad.
