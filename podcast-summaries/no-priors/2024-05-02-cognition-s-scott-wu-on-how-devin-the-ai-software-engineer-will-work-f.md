## Thesis

Scott Wu, co-founder and CEO of Cognition, presents Devin as an AI software engineer that carries out tasks end to end while a human watches and steers. He argues that this will lead to more engineers, not fewer. In his view, engineers will spend their time deciding what to build and how the solution should work, while agents like Devin handle the implementation.

## Key points

- **Competitive programming roots.** Wu started programming at about 9 and competed in the International Olympiad in Informatics. He says contest problem solving, which means questioning assumptions you take for granted, has a lot in common with building a startup. Much of Cognition's founding team, which started around November, came from that community.
- **What Devin does.** Devin writes and edits code, uses the shell and browser, reads documentation, and tests, debugs and deploys. Wu says the UI is meant to feel like looking over another engineer's shoulder, so users can give short feedback several times rather than giving an agent one shot at the task.
- **Strengths and weaknesses.** Wu says Devin set up a database and Kubernetes after the team had been stuck for about an hour. He also cites end-to-end data analysis as a strong use case. Its weakness is deciding what to do: it needs a precise specification.
- **The case for more engineers.** Wu estimates that engineers spend 20% of their time on problem solving and 80% on writing code, and says they could do 5x more. He notes there are 30 million software engineers, about 0.4% of people, while demand for software keeps growing.
- **What will drive agents forward.** Wu says better base-model reasoning, planning, tool use and inference speed all matter. He sees them as competing "rising tides."
- **Hiring.** According to Wu, almost everyone on the team was a founder or had considered starting a company. He looks for high ownership and a focus on outcomes over politics.
