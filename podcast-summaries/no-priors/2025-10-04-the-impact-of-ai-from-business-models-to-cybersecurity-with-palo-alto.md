## Thesis

Nikesh Arora, CEO of Palo Alto Networks and former chief business officer of Google, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that agents, not chatbots, are the real disruption, because they bypass the user interfaces many apps and ad models depend on. In security, he argues that AI has cut the time from intrusion to data theft to minutes. Defenders therefore need consolidated data and platforms rather than dozens of point tools.

## Key points

- **Search and agents.** Arora thinks Google can move search to answering intent, but the business model must shift from leads to completed transactions. He sees thin front ends to transaction systems, such as travel booking, as most exposed to agents.
- **AI as a service.** Enterprises will not accept inaccurate agentic actions yet. Arora tells his teams not to build generic AI tools such as accounts payable automation but to rent them. Durable AI companies will own systems of record, not wrappers.
- **Attack timelines collapse.** When Arora joined seven years ago, the typical time to breach and exfiltrate data was three to four days. The fastest he has now seen is 23 minutes, while average response still takes days. He says 89% of attacks involve credential theft.
- **Identity and anomalies.** Instead of trusting a login, Palo Alto wants just-in-time rights and anomaly detection, even spotting typing patterns. It is buying an identity company for this. One CISO friend of the hosts runs 118 identity tools.
- **Platforms and M&A.** Arora notes that sales, marketing and support take 50-65% of costs below a billion dollars of revenue, so the leverage is in selling more to trusted customers. Palo Alto has bought 27 companies, including Protect AI, and treats acquisitions as distributed R&D. It has grouped 44 Magic Quadrant mentions into three platforms.
- **Jobs and leadership.** Arora expects 80-90% of customer support to disappear within two to five years as products improve, with smaller cuts in administration and few in sales. He meets 50 employees every two weeks and enlarged his staff meeting from 8 to 25 people after hearing Jensen Huang's approach.
