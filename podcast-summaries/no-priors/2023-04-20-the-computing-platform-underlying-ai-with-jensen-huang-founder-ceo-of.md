## Thesis

Jensen Huang argues that NVIDIA exists to solve problems that general-purpose CPUs can't. He says sticking with that mission and keeping one compatible architecture (CUDA) across every chip is what put the company at the centre of deep learning. In his view, AI is a platform shift in which human language becomes the programming language, and NVIDIA's role is to go as far up the stack as developers need and no further.

## Key points

- **Walking the line.** Huang describes the core tension of an accelerator: become too general-purpose and you're just a CPU, stay too narrow and R&D budgets can't keep pace. NVIDIA made every chip CUDA-compatible for years with few customers, and he says gross margins "started out poor and it got worse."
- **The 2012 moment.** Huang says Andrew Ng contacted chief scientist Bill Dally about training on a few GPUs instead of thousands of CPU servers, while Geoff Hinton's and Yann LeCun's labs were exploring the same idea. He calls ImageNet the "big bang."
- **Not a model company.** Huang encourages developers to use OpenAI, Microsoft or Google models. He says NVIDIA would build foundation models only for niches it cares about, such as 3D graphics, robotics or climate.
- **Hopper.** Huang names FP8 numerical formats (versus 64-bit in scientific computing) and a dedicated transformer engine as the H100's main architectural ideas.
- **Bespoke org design.** Huang argues companies shouldn't copy the US military's hierarchy. He has about 40 direct reports with no one-on-ones, one instruction set, and two modes of work: a refined engineering organisation and agile skunkworks.
- **Long bets and advice.** Huang predicts a robotics foundation model in roughly five years, and describes Earth-2 for climate and Clara for healthcare. He calls ignorance an entrepreneur's superpower and recalls missing a quarter by $2 billion during crypto.
