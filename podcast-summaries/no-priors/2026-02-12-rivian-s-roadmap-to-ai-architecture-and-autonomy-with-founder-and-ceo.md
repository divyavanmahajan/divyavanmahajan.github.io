## Thesis

Rivian founder and CEO RJ Scaringe tells Sarah Guo that self-driving has moved from rules-based systems to end-to-end neural networks, and that most earlier work is now throwaway. He argues that carmakers without a software-defined architecture and high levels of autonomy will shrink, and that very few companies outside China have the data, compute and vehicle fleet to compete. He also says US EV adoption is held back by a lack of choice, which Rivian's R2 is meant to address.

## Key points

- **Clean-sheet reset.** Scaringe says Rivian knew at the R1 launch in late 2021 that its rules-based "1.0" stack, built around a third-party front camera, was wrong. The Gen 2 platform, launched in mid-2024, shared no code and no perception or compute hardware with it.
- **The ingredients.** He lists full control of the perception platform, event triggering on the vehicle, a robust data upload path and large GPU training. He puts the number of capable companies at "more than one less than five", and agrees with Guo that Tesla and Waymo are among them.
- **In-house chip for cost.** Cameras, radar and lidar are now cheap, Scaringe says; onboard inference is the expensive part. Rivian built its own chip so that every car can run high levels of autonomy.
- **Software-defined architecture.** Traditional cars have 100-150 ECUs running software written by suppliers' suppliers, which makes coordinated updates hard. Rivian's zonal design uses one to three computers and ships an over-the-air update about once a month. The approach underpins a $5.8 billion licensing deal with Volkswagen Group.
- **Sensors as a data machine.** With no "internet of driving data", Rivian aims for more perception than Tesla and less than Waymo. Putting lidar on every R2 turns the fleet into a training platform. Drive modes ("mild, medium, and spicy") hint at future preference-based driving styles.
- **Choice drives adoption.** US EV adoption is about 8%, and Model 3 and Model Y hold roughly half the EV market. Scaringe says rivals copied the Model Y instead of offering something different. R2 starts at $45,000; the R1 averages around $90,000.
