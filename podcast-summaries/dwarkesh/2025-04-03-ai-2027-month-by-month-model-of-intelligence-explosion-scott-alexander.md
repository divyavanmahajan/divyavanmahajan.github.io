## Thesis

Daniel Kokotajlo and Scott Alexander present AI 2027, a month-by-month scenario in which AI agents automate coding, then all of AI research, producing superintelligence around 2028. They argue that assuming nothing happens is the less conservative forecast. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back on compute bottlenecks, organisational limits and how fast real-world science can move; the guests call for transparency rather than heavy regulation or secrecy.

## Key points

- **Progress multipliers.** Kokotajlo describes milestones: a superhuman coder giving about a 5x speed-up to algorithmic progress, a superhuman AI researcher giving about 25x, and superintelligence in the hundreds or around 1000x. He says the model assumes heavy diminishing returns from parallel copies, so research taste and compute become the bottlenecks.
- **Physical economy.** Alexander estimates a million robots a month about a year after superintelligence, reasoning from car factories converted to bombers in World War II and AI moving three times faster. Patel guesses closer to 2040 for a self-sufficient robot economy.
- **Branch point.** In mid-2027 the leading lab finds inconclusive evidence that its AIs are misaligned. In one ending it rolls back and uses faithful chain-of-thought; in the other it applies a shallow patch and races China.
- **Uncertainty.** Kokotajlo puts his P(doom) at about 70%; Alexander says his is about 20% and that he gives the scenario's pace roughly a 20% chance.
- **Government and secrecy.** Kokotajlo says he has lost faith in labs using a lead for safety. He would rather see 100 to 500 alignment experts sharing work than 10 in one silo, plus published model specs and whistleblower protections. Patel worries that nationalisation or rigid regulation would backfire.
- **OpenAI and blogging.** Kokotajlo recounts refusing OpenAI's non-disparagement agreement despite risking his equity. Alexander says writing every day is his best predictor of who becomes a good blogger.
