## Thesis

Dario Amodei tells Dwarkesh Patel that his 2017 "Big Blob of Compute" hypothesis still holds, now for RL as well as pre-training, and that the world is close to the end of the exponential. He puts 90% odds on a "country of geniuses in a data center" within ten years and has a hunch it comes in one to three. He argues that diffusion into the economy will be much faster than past technologies but not instant, which shapes how Anthropic buys compute and thinks about regulation.

## Key points

- **Scaling and generalisation.** Amodei says RL now shows the same log-linear gains seen in pre-training, and that broad RL environments aim at generalisation, as the move from GPT-1 to GPT-2 did, not at teaching each skill.
- **Code first.** He separates 90% of code written by models (which he says happened at Anthropic) from end-to-end software engineering, which he expects in one to two years. He estimates coding models give a 15-20% total factor speed-up today, up from about 5% six months earlier. Patel pushes back with the METR study showing a 20% slowdown.
- **Revenue and diffusion.** Anthropic's revenue went from zero to $100 million in 2023, $1 billion in 2024 and $9-10 billion in 2025. Amodei says enterprise procurement, security and change management explain why adoption is fast but not infinite.
- **Why not buy more compute.** If Anthropic bought $1 trillion a year of compute for 2027 and growth came in at 5x instead of 10x, Amodei says it would go bankrupt. He estimates industry build-out at 10-15 gigawatts this year, rising about 3x a year.
- **Lab profitability.** In his toy model each model is profitable at gross margins above 50%; companies lose money because they spend far more training the next one. He expects three or four players, like cloud, but with more differentiated products.
- **Policy and China.** Amodei opposed the 10-year federal moratorium on state AI laws, favours federal transparency standards, wants faster FDA approval for AI-discovered drugs and supports chip export controls so democracies hold the stronger hand.
