## Thesis

Shane Legg, founder and Chief AGI Scientist at Google DeepMind, tells Dwarkesh Patel that large language models changed something fundamental and that he sees no big walls on the way to AGI, only research on gaps such as episodic memory, search and factuality. He argues that alignment should come from capable systems that reason explicitly about ethics, with humans checking that reasoning.

## Key points

- **Measuring AGI.** Legg defines AGI as a machine that can do the cognitive things people typically do. The test is a broad suite of tasks plus an adversarial phase: if people can no longer find tasks where the machine falls below human performance, it is AGI for practical purposes.
- **Missing pieces need architecture.** Current models have a fluid context window (like working memory) and slowly trained weights (like cortex), but nothing like the hippocampus for rapid episodic learning. Legg expects fixes to be architectural, not scale alone.
- **Search for creativity.** Citing AlphaGo's Move 37, he argues true creativity needs search through possibilities; today's models mostly mimic and blend their training data.
- **Alignment via System 2.** Sampling a model is like Kahneman's System 1, and RLHF or Constitutional AI only nudges that distribution. Legg wants systems with a good world model, robust reasoning and a deep grasp of ethics, with principles set by society and humans auditing the reasoning. He says this is his own view, not a DeepMind position, and points to Geoffrey Irving's Deliberative Dialogue work.
- **DeepMind's net impact.** He admits DeepMind has accelerated capabilities but says the counterfactual is hard to judge, noting that its first safety hire, around 2013, would only work part-time.
- **Timelines and next landmark.** His forecast dates to around 2001 and Ray Kurzweil's argument about exponential growth in compute and data. He expects models to become more factual and multimodal, with full multimodality as the next landmark.
