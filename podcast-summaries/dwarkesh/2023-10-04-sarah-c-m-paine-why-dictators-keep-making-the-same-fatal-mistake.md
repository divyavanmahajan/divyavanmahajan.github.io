## Thesis

Sarah Paine, professor of strategy and policy at the Naval War College, argues that continental powers such as Putin's Russia and Xi's China still treat territory as the source of security, a negative-sum approach that kills people and destroys wealth. Maritime powers instead build security on trade and international law, a win-win order whose growth compounds over time. Dictators, she says, cut off counter-arguments, make pivotal errors and then can only double down.

## Key points

- **Grand strategy needs every instrument.** Paine defines it as integrating all instruments of national power; Japan in World War II let its military dominate, assassinated civilians and ignored its finance minister, unlike Eisenhower's habit of hearing all arguments.
- **Death ground.** Borrowing from Sun Tzu, she argues that Hitler's brutality turned Ukrainians who first welcomed the Germans into fighters, and that Putin is now forging Ukrainian national identity the same way.
- **Pivotal errors.** The 1930 Hawley-Smoot tariff, an example of what she calls American "half-court tennis", preceded Japan's 1931 invasion of Manchuria; Pearl Harbor and Putin's attempt to take all of Ukraine are errors with no route back.
- **Sanctions work by compounding.** Paine says sanctions suppress growth rather than change behaviour, pointing to North versus South Korea, and claims Russia's economy is now smaller than Mexico's.
- **Rebuilding versus building.** Occupations of Germany and Japan succeeded because they rebuilt existing institutions in countries with a national identity; Iraq and Afghanistan had neither.
- **Taiwan and sovereignty.** She would not recognise a Chinese conquest of Taiwan's 20-plus million people and would sanction China indefinitely, but calls a US-China military conflict the most catastrophic error imaginable.
