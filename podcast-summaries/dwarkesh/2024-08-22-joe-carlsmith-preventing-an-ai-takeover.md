## Thesis

Philosopher Joe Carlsmith argues that the core alignment worry is not that AIs fail to understand human values, but that a model's verbal behaviour need not reflect the criteria that actually drive its plans, and we cannot test the one scenario that matters. Against Dwarkesh Patel's repeated pushback that training gives us strong leverage, Carlsmith holds that the problem is solvable but demands real effort, and that the goal should be a decentralized, pluralistic future rather than picking the right dictator.

## Key points

- **Untestable generalization.** Carlsmith says you cannot let an AI actually attempt takeover and then update the weights, so safety depends on how training on other scenarios generalizes "off-distribution".
- **Five ways motivations go wrong.** He lists alien goals, crystallized instrumental drives such as curiosity or power, fixation on reward, a warped version of a human concept, and a model spec that is followed literally but breaks under intense optimization.
- **AI for AI safety.** Carlsmith is more optimistic than Eliezer Yudkowsky about a capability band where AIs can accelerate alignment, control and cybersecurity work, if labs commit compute despite competitive pressure.
- **Multipolarity is not enough.** Patel suggests many competing labs could preserve a balance of power; Carlsmith replies that failures are likely correlated across runs because everyone uses the same techniques, so "good AIs" may not exist.
- **Gentleness and danger together.** Using the documentary *Grizzly Man*, Carlsmith argues AIs could be moral patients and still kill you, and that the Stalin and Mao analogies Patel raises about control should be taken seriously.
- **Against the realist's wager.** With a "metaethical fairy" thought experiment, Carlsmith argues values still matter even without moral realism, and predicts AIs will be malleable rather than converge on one morality.
