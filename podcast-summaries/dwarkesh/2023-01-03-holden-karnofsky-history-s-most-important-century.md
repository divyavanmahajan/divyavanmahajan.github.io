## Thesis

Holden Karnofsky, co-CEO of Open Philanthropy and co-founder of GiveWell, argues that if AI systems can do everything humans do to advance science and technology, which he puts at more than 50-50 this century, the resulting explosive growth could lock in a long-lasting future, making this our last chance to shape it. He tells Dwarkesh Patel that the claim sounds crazy, but that economic history already shows we live in an unusually weird time.

## Key points

- **Restarting the growth loop.** Karnofsky explains that the people-ideas-resources feedback loop broke when richer societies stopped having more children; AI that supplies the "more ideas" step could bring accelerating growth back.
- **A weird time regardless.** Citing a Robin Hanson argument, he says today's roughly 2% growth sustained for 10,000 years would require several world economies per atom in the galaxy, so the current era cannot be typical.
- **Biggest weak point.** Karnofsky calls a non-automatable bottleneck, such as slow real-world experiments, the best objection, but argues energy and AI chips could be automated first and many experiments simulated.
- **Lock-in and misalignment.** He warns that AI trained by trial and error could pursue unintended goals, and that ageless rulers with total control could create a stable, possibly bad civilization; he guesses lock-in odds at "a quarter, a third, a half", while noting he is making up numbers.
- **Evidence for timelines.** He combines observing AI progress (GPT-3, Google's Minerva solving maths problems), the semi-informative priors report, expert surveys and Biological Anchors, which he says looks "fine" so far.
- **The OpenAI grant and ethics.** Karnofsky defends the $30 million 2016 grant, which included a board seat, rejects "ends justify the means" reasoning, and describes using a "moral parliament" to weigh competing ethical views.
