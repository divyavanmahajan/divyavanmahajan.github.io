## Thesis

Adam Marblestone, CEO of Convergent Research, argues that machine learning has neglected the role of loss functions: evolution likely packed a detailed curriculum of bespoke reward signals into the brain, while the cortex runs a fairly generic learning algorithm. Drawing on Steve Byrnes's split between a Learning Subsystem and a Steering Subsystem, he suggests this answers Ilya Sutskever's question of how the genome encodes abstract desires, and says neuroscience needs large-scale data collection to test it.

## Key points

- **Learning vs steering.** Marblestone describes innate circuits in the hypothalamus, brainstem and superior colliculus (for example a spider flinch reflex), with parts of the cortex and amygdala learning to predict them. That lets innate rewards generalise to learned concepts such as the word "spider".
- **Small genome, compact rewards.** Dwarkesh notes the human genome is about 3 gigabytes; Marblestone cites single-cell atlas work showing far more diverse, bespoke cell types in steering regions than in cortex, consistent with reward "code" taking up the genomic space.
- **Omnidirectional inference.** He speculates the cortex can predict any subset of its inputs from any other subset, unlike next-token LLMs, and says this is close to Yann LeCun's energy-based view.
- **RL without value functions.** Following Ilya, he calls LLM reinforcement learning conceptually crude, contrasting it with dopamine reward-prediction errors, model-free learning in the basal ganglia and model-based planning in cortex.
- **Connectome economics.** E11 Bio aims to cut a mouse connectome from several billion dollars to low tens of millions; he puts a concerted push at hundreds of millions to low billions, citing the roughly million-fold drop in genome sequencing costs. His own AGI timeline is about 10 years.
- **Automating math.** As a Lean board member, he expects reinforcement learning on verifiable proofs (Harmonic, AlphaProof) to automate the mechanical parts of math and enable formally verified software, though conjecturing remains open.
