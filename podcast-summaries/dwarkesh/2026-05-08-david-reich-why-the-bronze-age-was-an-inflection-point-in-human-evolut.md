## Thesis

Harvard geneticist David Reich tells Dwarkesh Patel that a new study led by his colleague Ali Akbari overturns the consensus that directional natural selection has been quiet in humans for hundreds of thousands of years. Using ancient genomes from about 16,000 people in Europe and the Middle East over 18,000 years, they find selection has been pervasive and intensified sharply between roughly 5,000 and 2,000 years ago, as the Bronze Age pushed people into denser, more agricultural lives.

## Key points

- **Scale and method.** The study adds data from about 10,000 new individuals, a roughly 14-fold increase. Akbari's method predicts genotypes from relatedness and asks whether a consistent selection push explains the data better. Reich says 98% of allele-frequency change is drift and migration, yet at least 479 positions are 99% likely to be under selection and thousands more are probable.
- **Validation.** Positions with a selection statistic above about five showed a roughly five-fold enrichment for variants linked to traits in the UK Biobank, independent evidence that the signals are real. Reich says the team spent years trying to make the result go away.
- **What changed.** Immune and metabolic traits are most enriched. A TYK2 tuberculosis-risk variant rose and then fell sharply in the last 3,000 years, and lighter skin pigmentation was selected most strongly 4,000 to 2,000 years ago. Reich links lower genetic risk of body fat to the "thrifty gene" hypothesis.
- **Cognition.** The genetic predictor of educational attainment and test performance rose by about one standard deviation over 10,000 years, mostly in the Bronze Age, with no detectable selection in the last 2,000 years. He cautions the predictor correlates with traits such as age at first birth and may reflect something like deferred gratification; the same variants predict schooling in China.
- **Time, not population size.** Once populations reach about a million, every possible mutation recurs, so time span limits evolution. Reich finds it striking that farming arose independently only after climate stabilised 12,000 years ago.
- **A heretical Neanderthal model.** Reich speculates Neanderthals may be genetically swamped modern humans from a Middle Stone Age expansion 200,000 to 300,000 years ago, comparing current models to Ptolemaic epicycles, while saying it is probably wrong.
