## Thesis

Naval War College historian Sarah Paine tells the story of Russia, the United States and China trying to work their influence on India and Pakistan, and argues that before intervening you must map who is whose primary adversary. Russia and India shared a China problem, and Pakistan and China shared an India problem; the US shared neither, so whatever it gave one side alienated the other. In the Q&A, Dwarkesh Patel presses her on which ally the US should have picked and on lessons for a second Cold War with China.

## Key points

- **Pivotal decisions.** Paine starts with Mao's conquest of Tibet and the road network built there between 1950 and 1957, which removed the buffer with India. She adds Eisenhower's "pactomania": arming Pakistan through the Baghdad Pact, which she says poisoned US relations with India for the rest of the Cold War.
- **1962 and its costs.** China seized the Aksai Chin Plateau during the Cuban Missile Crisis. Paine argues it gained territory but made India a permanent enemy: India doubled its army to 750,000 and created 10 mountain divisions. Pakistan's 1963 cession of territory to China was, she guesses, for help with nuclear weapons.
- **Cutthroat billiards.** The 1969 Sino-Soviet border clash made Russia and China primary adversaries of each other, which handed the US a swing position. She contrasts the WWII Allies, held together by one common threat, with the Axis, whose enemies never lined up.
- **Instruments of power.** The 1960 Indus Waters Treaty (the US paid half of a billion dollars) and 20% of the US wheat crop sent during the 1967 Bihar famine bought no lasting gratitude. Sending the carrier Enterprise into the Bay of Bengal in 1971 enraged India, and funding insurgencies produces frozen conflicts such as Kashmir.
- **Q&A.** Paine says Pakistan's location mattered before satellite imagery, but India and the US now share China as their main adversary. She expects the Xi-Putin partnership to end over Siberia and Lake Baikal, and recommends checking a history book's bibliography for local-language sources.
