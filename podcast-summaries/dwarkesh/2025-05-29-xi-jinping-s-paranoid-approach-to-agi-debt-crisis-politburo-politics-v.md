## Thesis

Victor Shih, who directs the 21st Century China Center at UC San Diego, argues that the Chinese Communist Party listens to experts but almost always ranks staying in power above technical considerations. In his view this explains Zero-COVID, a state-directed financial system that wastes capital, and an approach to AI that builds brakes alongside the technology. With power centralised in Xi Jinping, he expects a disorderly succession and slower growth than most forecasts.

## Key points

- **Centralised decisions.** Shih says fiscal autonomy has fallen since the 1994 tax centralisation, and that pushing decisions into Leading Small Groups chaired by Xi means nobody of equal rank debates him. Xi meets on policy roughly 270 days a year.
- **Ding Xuexiang and AI.** The Standing Committee member who runs the Central Science and Technology Commission and the cybersecurity commission office would oversee any large AI effort. Shih reads his Davos speech, which called for building brakes alongside AI investment, as revealing, and expects staff inside DeepSeek who can pull the plug.
- **DeepSeek as a policy tracker.** Shih finds DeepSeek the most useful model for his research and believes High-Flyer trained it to surface government policy documents, which drive Chinese stock prices.
- **Local debt.** He estimates local government debt at 120-140% of GDP, putting total government debt near 200%. The roughly 10 trillion renminbi refinancing only swapped expensive debt for cheaper debt. A 1% deposit rate and a $20,000 annual limit on moving money abroad push savings into state banks.
- **Corruption and overbuilding.** Shih links investment to promotion through kickbacks rather than GDP targets. He says state-picked projects fail far more often than they succeed, and that BYD, Xiaomi and CATL started with mainly private money.
- **Taiwan, succession, growth.** Xi has not taken big gambles in 12 years, and Ukraine probably raised his bar for acting. With no succession plan, a power vacuum could set off capital flight, since $3 trillion in reserves is under 5% of money supply. Shih expects China to be 1-1.2x the size of the US economy by 2040.
