## Thesis

Recorded the day after his TIME article calling for a halt to large AI training runs, Eliezer Yudkowsky argues that humanity is on course to build minds smarter than itself without any reliable way to check their goals, and that this most likely ends in extinction. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back with a series of reasons for optimism: training on human text, gradual scaling, human-level AI helping with alignment, and scepticism of first-principles arguments. Yudkowsky rejects each, saying that his pessimism comes from spreading uncertainty over the right space of outcomes, not from a detailed theory.

## Key points

- **Why call for a halt now.** Yudkowsky says he had heard that people outside tech were open to stopping, and that waiting for GPT-5 risks the technology becoming entrenched while algorithms keep improving. His preferred exit is human intelligence enhancement, which he calls a "Hail Mary pass".
- **LLMs as actresses, not humans.** Patel suggests that training on human text gives models something like human psychology. Yudkowsky counters that next-token prediction trains a system to switch masks across everyone on the internet, which he says is a different thing from being any one of them.
- **Evolution as a warning.** Yudkowsky argues that humans drift further from inclusive genetic fitness as they get smarter and gain options, pointing to ice cream and condoms. Patel replies that most people still want and have children.
- **The broken verifier.** Patel argues that checking a solution is usually easier than producing one. Yudkowsky says alignment is the exception: a proposal can pass every test while the system is weak and fail once it is capable, and people already cannot settle his disagreements with Paul Christiano.
- **Opacity and interpretability.** Yudkowsky says stacking more layers made systems less legible than the AI of 2001, and proposes $100 billion in interpretability prizes, while noting researchers still study models smaller than GPT-2.
- **Predictions.** Asked for a track record, Yudkowsky cites his bet with Christiano on an AI winning IMO gold by 2025 (his 16% against Christiano's 8%) and argues the endpoint is easier to predict than the path.
