## Thesis

Discussing his book *Talent*, Tyler Cowen tells Dwarkesh Patel that finding talent will remain an art rather than a science: context is what is scarce, and intuition matters more than credentials or genomics. On the long term he is gloomier, arguing that accumulated risks such as nuclear war make a permanent setback for civilisation likely, even though he is optimistic about any given year.

## Key points

- **Reading candidates.** Cowen says he is suspicious of humble applicants, sees a crisply dressed 20-year-old as too conformist, and notes that writing is a form of thinking, citing writing cultures at Amazon and Stripe.
- **Talent clusters.** He finds it disappointing how geographically clustered talent is, with London and New York acting as durable sinks, while the origins of talent, such as Renaissance Florence, are poorly explained by theory.
- **Education and context.** Cowen agrees with Bryan Caplan that students forget classroom facts, but argues they learn context: how the world works, aspirations, and how to deal with different personality types.
- **Emergent Ventures.** He describes a bimodal applicant pool, says small grants are underrated, and says the programme has no staff and a low cost of exit, so it should not go stale.
- **Existential risk.** Cowen thinks existential risk matters more than most people think, but says effective altruists are not epistemically modest enough about AGI and that real work looks like growth, science and safer nuclear weapons.
- **Stagnation and intellectuals.** He attributes slowing research productivity mainly to exhausted low-hanging fruit rather than bureaucracy, and says he stays relevant by avoiding single big bets, unlike several Intellectual Dark Web figures he expects to decline.
