## Thesis

Lars Doucet, author of *Land is a Big Deal* and founder of the mass-appraisal startup Valuebase, argues that land is an underrated force in the economy: because location cannot be produced, rising productivity and population are soaked up as land rent. He says a tax on the unimproved value of land, replacing taxes on buildings, income and sales, is both pro-worker and pro-business. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back with critiques on speculation, competition, banking and property rights.

## Key points

- **Virtual housing crises.** Doucet says games such as Ultima Online, which still has a housing shortage about 25 years on, and 2022 crypto land sales showed land speculation re-emerging even where land could be created without limit.
- **Ricardo's law of rent.** Doucet argues tenants compete one-sidedly for scarce locations, so landowners can charge up to the best free alternative; he says frontier expansion, cars and Zoom only delayed this until the gains were priced in.
- **Speculation critiques.** Responding to Gochenour and Caplan's search-theoretic critique, Doucet says even the most extreme Georgists he knows seek about 85% capture, and cites Norway's oil severance tax as evidence that discovery still happens when rents are taxed.
- **Revenue and transition.** Doucet estimates $44 trillion of US land value and about $4 trillion a year in potential land rents. He proposes starting with revenue-neutral split-rate property taxes, citing Gordon Tullock's transitional gains trap as a reason to protect people who bought in good faith.
- **Assessment is the bottleneck.** Doucet says only 15% of assessment offices use multiple regression and that landlords tend to be under-assessed; Valuebase, in a round led by Sam Altman, aims to modernise mass appraisal with open models and data.
- **Beyond land.** The pair discuss app-store chart positions, domain names, patents, copyright terms, outer space and the "Disney World question" of a private city that internalises its own externalities.
