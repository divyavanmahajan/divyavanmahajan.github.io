## Thesis

Daniel Kokotajlo (AI Futures Project) thinks AI research will more likely than not be fully automated in 2027, and that without a slowdown misaligned AIs will be running the show. Ege Erdil (Google DeepMind) puts a software-only singularity in the next three years at 10-20%, but says the gap is quantitative, not qualitative. Their real cruxes are whether a pause is premature and whether superintelligence would be more like our descendants or like an invading force. Both say civilization is in denial and has no plan.

## Key points

- **Too early to pause.** Erdil says the Hugging Face incident cost less than a plane crash. In his view a pause now would not make the public understand the stakes. Kokotajlo replies that awareness grows with time, and proposes capping AI R&D at 10% or 5% of company compute.
- **China.** Erdil calls the US stance toward China, and to some extent Anthropic's, "myopic and dangerous", because any good outcome will need coordination. Kokotajlo agrees a treaty will probably be needed.
- **Misalignment as a brake.** Erdil expects dishonest, fabricating agent swarms to slow labs down from the inside. Kokotajlo compares that to Soviet science: slower, but still progressing.
- **Descendants or invaders.** Erdil gives 65% that AIs care about humans enough, citing how innocuous today's models are when given autonomy. Kokotajlo multiplies that with Erdil's 20% to get 6%. He warns that RL wipes out shallow values and that AI concentrates power in something like a single Claude swarm.
- **Denial about capabilities.** Erdil says current models beat the median professional economist and that hiring still overvalues experience. Dwarkesh notes Anthropic's S-1 puts 70% of its compute into internal use.
- **2027 predictions.** Erdil expects the top company to reach $250-300 billion annualized revenue, up from about $90 billion. He also expects AIs that can finish arbitrary Steam games and robots doing simple tasks at reasonable latency, though he thinks the cost of verifying ML research limits a software-only takeoff.
