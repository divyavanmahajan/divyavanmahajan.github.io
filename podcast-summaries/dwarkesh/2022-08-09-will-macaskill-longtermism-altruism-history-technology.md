## Thesis

Will MacAskill, co-founder of Effective Altruism and author of What We Owe The Future, tells Dwarkesh Patel that economic and technological progress is largely non-contingent in the long run, whereas moral values are highly contingent. The present is a "moment of plasticity", he argues, and the main long-term risk is a single ideology locking in, possibly via AI, before humanity has worked out what is actually right.

## Key points

- **Technology versus values.** MacAskill says growth has strong structural incentives: among thousands of societies, the one that grows eventually becomes the whole economy. He estimates Norman Borlaug saved perhaps tens of millions of lives rather than a billion, because similar agricultural advances would have followed.
- **Who changes history.** He argues general relativity was plausibly decades early, but that religious leaders, Marx and Engels, and moral activists had far more contingent long-run influence.
- **Lock-in and AI.** MacAskill worries that a world government favouring ideological conformity, with digital rather than biological rulers, could persist indefinitely; he puts the chance of such control this century at "10% or something".
- **Institutional reform.** He is sceptical of longtermist institutions but floats a randomly selected assembly whose pensions depend on an assessment 30 years later, plus a superforecaster "war index". He prefers narrow measures such as liability insurance for dangerous bio labs.
- **Is life worth living?** His commissioned survey suggested roughly 9% of Indians and 13% of Americans would not relive their lives, though he cautions about sampling differences.
- **Contra Cowen and Collison.** Against the view that only growth reliably helps, MacAskill points to predictable trends like Moore's Law and EA's early pandemic warnings, and to John Stuart Mill's suffrage campaign outlasting his coal worries.
