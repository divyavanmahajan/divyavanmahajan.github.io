## Thesis

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella argues that AI should be judged by whether it lifts economic growth to Industrial Revolution levels, not by claims of reaching AGI. He expects hyperscale compute to be a durable winner, models to face open-source competition, and adoption to be limited by change management and legal liability rather than capability. Dwarkesh Patel challenges him on capex, AGI and labour.

## Key points

- **No winner-take-all.** Nadella says enterprise buyers always want multiple suppliers, so hyperscale and models will not be winner-take-all; open source will check closed models. Consumer products like ChatGPT can still gain network effects.
- **Growth is the benchmark.** He notes the developed world grows around 2%, near zero after inflation, and wants 10% growth as the marker. Asked about Microsoft's $13 billion in yearly AI revenue, he says supply and demand must map, and expects to lease cheaper capacity in '27 and '28 as overbuilding pushes prices down.
- **Lean for knowledge work.** Nadella compares AI adoption to the shift from faxed forecasts to emailed spreadsheets: workflows must change. He already uses about ten Copilot agents and expects an "agent manager" interface, with SaaS business logic moving into an agentic tier.
- **Majorana 1.** He describes Microsoft's topological qubit as a "transistor moment" for quantum computing, aiming at a chip with a million physical qubits and thousands of logical ones, with a fault-tolerant machine possibly in '27 to '29.
- **Muse and gaming data.** Microsoft's world and human action model generates consistent gameplay from controller input; Nadella compares gaming data to what YouTube is for Google.
- **Legal limits on AI.** Nadella argues deployment requires a human indemnifying the AI, and that courts will act before any takeoff. He says labour's share, about 60% of the economy, must be revalued for democracies to stay stable.
