## Thesis

Ilya Sutskever, co-founder of Safe Superintelligence (SSI), tells Dwarkesh Patel that AI has moved from an "age of research" (2012-2020) through an "age of scaling" (2020-2025) and is now back to research, just with much bigger computers. In his view, the central unsolved problem is that models generalise dramatically worse than people. He also says SSI's approach rests on unproven ideas about how to fix this.

## Key points

- **Evals vs. real-world impact.** Sutskever explains the gap between strong benchmark scores and weak economic impact with two factors. RL environments are often inspired by evals, and models generalise poorly. He compares models to a student who practised 10,000 hours of competitive programming, against one who practised 100 hours but has the "it" factor.
- **Emotions as value functions.** Citing a patient who lost emotional processing and could no longer make decisions, he suggests human value functions are shaped by emotions hard-coded by evolution. He expects value functions to make RL more efficient, but not to change things fundamentally.
- **Compute for research.** Sutskever says there are now "more companies than ideas". He notes that AlexNet used two GPUs and the transformer at most 64. SSI has raised $3 billion, and he argues that its research compute is comparable to rivals', since much of their spending goes to inference and products.
- **Superintelligence as a learner.** He reframes AGI as something like a "superintelligent 15-year-old" that learns on the job after deployment. He now favours incremental release and predicts labs will become "much more paranoid" about safety and will collaborate on it.
- **Alignment and timelines.** He favours AI "robustly aligned to care about sentient life" and suggests capping the power of the strongest systems. He gives 5 to 20 years for human-like learners. He says his former co-founder and CEO left for Meta after Sutskever turned down Meta's acquisition offer, made while SSI was raising at a $32 billion valuation.
- **Research taste.** Sutskever looks for "beauty, simplicity, elegance" and correct inspiration from the brain, which sustains a top-down belief when experiments disagree.
