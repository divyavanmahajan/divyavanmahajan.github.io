## Thesis

In a 2022 interview recorded in the Bahamas, Sam Bankman-Fried, then CEO of FTX, tells Dwarkesh Patel that the world has many inefficiencies and the scarce ingredient is people willing to start things and do messy grunt work. He describes how his Future Fund tries to give differently from other donors, and argues that blockchain settlement and automated risk management will carry over into traditional finance.

## Key points

- **Founders are the bottleneck.** Bankman-Fried says that for both altruistic and profit-minded projects, the missing piece is usually the right person to lead, and that communities should accept that new ventures may fail.
- **Future Fund design.** He says the fund had given away about $100 million so far that year and uses re-grantors (10 to 100 experts with their own pots) to make many small grants, such as $25,000 to start an organisation, while auditing random samples for adverse selection.
- **Political giving.** On his $12 million for Carrick Flynn in the May 2022 Oregon race, chosen for Flynn's focus on pandemic preparedness, he says a single loss allows only a limited update.
- **Grunt work and focus.** Bankman-Fried recalls senior Alameda staff spending hours in bank branches instructing wires, and uses pitcher fatigue to argue that context makes one full-time employee more effective than two part-timers.
- **Scaling and hiring.** He says experience correlates little with how much mentorship a hire needs, and that exchanges which grew headcount fast became a mess with diffuse responsibility.
- **Crypto and tradfi.** He expects stablecoins, blockchain settlement and tokenisation to spread, and defends his CFTC proposal for an automated clearinghouse, saying it would contain risk such as the LME nickel episode.
