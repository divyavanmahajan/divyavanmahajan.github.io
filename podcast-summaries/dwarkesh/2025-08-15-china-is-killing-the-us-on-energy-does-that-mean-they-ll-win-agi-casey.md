## Thesis

Casey Handmer, founder and CEO of Terraform Industries, argues that China's lead in solar manufacturing does not decide the AI race, because chips rather than power are the real bottleneck. Since hyperscalers barely notice the cost of electricity, he expects them to hit the limits of gas turbines and the grid, and then turn to large off-grid solar and battery sites. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back on timing, permitting and why the people spending real money are choosing gas today.

## Key points

- **China does not win by default.** Handmer says US solar manufacturing is about five years behind China's and could scale in two years or less. A 200% tariff on Chinese panels would hardly matter next to the cost of GPUs.
- **Why gas now.** Gas is quick to tap, but turbines are sold out until about 2030. Handmer puts the Brayton-cycle hardware alone at $35 a megawatt hour. In his estimate, around 10 cents of electricity produces $1,000 of value for a user.
- **Solar learning rate.** Handmer cites a 43% Wright's Law coefficient, with production doubling about every 2-2.5 years. He predicts that by 2027 most new data centres breaking ground will run mostly on solar.
- **The numbers.** For 1 megawatt at four nines of uptime he estimates about 10 acres of solar and six Tesla Megapacks, so 5 gigawatts needs roughly 50,000 acres. He compares that with Hanford's 100,000 acres and notes the land is about 0.1% of a $250 billion GPU bill.
- **Regulation and batteries.** Handmer says NEPA reviews treat solar like a chemical plant and calls for a categorical exemption. He describes batteries as time-shifting power, which reduces how far electricity has to travel over the grid.
- **Beyond GDP.** Patel, crediting Gwern and James Bradbury, suggests energy use may track AI's value better than GDP. Handmer imagines thin silicon solar sails in space, each carrying one mind.
