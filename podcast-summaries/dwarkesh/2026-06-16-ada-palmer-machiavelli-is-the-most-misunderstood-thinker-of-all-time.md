## Thesis

Historian and novelist Ada Palmer argues that "Machiavellian" describes a character later readers invented, not the man. The real Machiavelli wrote in a Renaissance Italy where cut threads of legitimacy and unpredictable, warlike popes made every regime unstable, and he analysed how to keep power, not gain it, for the sake of Florence. She calls him one of the most patriotic, selfless figures she has studied.

## Key points

- **Why Italy was so unstable.** Once a long-standing government is overthrown, more overthrows follow; by the time of *The Prince* most Italian cities had lost continuity. Successive popes normalised overthrowing cities for their sons, and elected popes often came from the faction opposed to their predecessor.
- **At Cesare Borgia's side.** As Florence's envoy, Machiavelli's job was to buy time by swearing loyalty to the conqueror, even breaking a 300-year alliance with Bologna. He watched the massacre at Senigallia. He judged Borgia by the probable outcome before fortune struck: father and son falling ill together. Borgia's neutral justice made his tyranny popular with ordinary people.
- **Means matter.** Breaking your word works for a feared ruler like Borgia but ruined Savonarola, whose power rested on seeming infallible. Palmer says Machiavelli was also the first European to see competing political parties as potentially stabilising, citing Siena.
- **Patronage as glue.** Romans rioted demanding more nepotism when Paul III appointed a competent general instead of his son. Most capital sentences ended in fines because patrons intervened; Giordano Bruno was burned when he lost his patron.
- **Art as diplomacy and religion as utility.** Florence spent on art and gifts because culture was cheaper than armies. Like Thomas Paine later, Machiavelli judged religion by its effects on citizens.
- **The Prince as job application.** Exiled to the countryside after torture, Machiavelli refused richer foreign posts and wrote *The Prince* only for Florence's rulers. Palmer adds that the Inquisition's pre-publication licences became the first copyright, after printers pirated his works with typos.
