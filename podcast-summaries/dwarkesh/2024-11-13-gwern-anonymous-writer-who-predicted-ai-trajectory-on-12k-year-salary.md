## Thesis

Gwern Branwen, the pseudonymous writer behind gwern.net, tells Dwarkesh Patel that intelligence is not a master algorithm but search over a huge number of special-case solutions, so more compute means more intelligence. That view, built up from years of watching deep learning results, let him see LLM scaling coming. With AGI plausibly a few years away, he now spends his time on work he enjoys and on writing down preferences and judgments that future models cannot infer.

## Key points

- **Seeing scaling early.** Gwern says he was sceptical of the Moravec, Kurzweil and Shane Legg compute argument until AlexNet and DanNet. People missed results like the 2017 Baidu scaling-laws paper and BigGAN scaling to 300 million images. The few-shot chart in the GPT-3 paper convinced him.
- **Compute first.** He argues papers hide how ideas really arise: trial and error and serendipity need compute. ResNets were published in 1988 but only mattered in 2015.
- **Timelines.** From 2005 to 2010 he placed AGI well past 2050; after AlexNet his estimate moved closer by about 2 years per year. He treats Anthropic's 2028 timeline as a personal planning point.
- **Writing for the models.** He argues that writing is a vote on future LLMs, citing Kevin Roose being treated badly by chatbots after the Sydney episode. He prefers recording preferences, since "the AI cannot eat ice cream for you".
- **Automation and agency.** He expects firms to automate bottom-up, leaving a human CEO for long-term vision. Current models get agency only as a byproduct of internet data, he says, because almost no one trains agents with proper RL.
- **Living on little.** His Patreon brings in about $900-$1000 a month, with early Bitcoin savings covering the rest. He would move to San Francisco for $50-100K a year. He also describes being hearing impaired since birth and learning to write by editing Wikipedia.
