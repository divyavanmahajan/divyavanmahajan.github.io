## Thesis

Grant Sanderson, who runs the 3Blue1Brown channel, argues that good explanations are useful but secondary. What makes people learn is motivation from teachers, friends and personal projects. He applies the same skepticism to AI: an AI that wins gold at the International Math Olympiad would be impressive, he says, but more like the chess and Go milestones than a step to AGI.

## Key points

- **IMO gold is not AGI.** Sanderson says he doesn't know what people mean by AGI and sees progress as continuous. He expects math to lend itself to synthetic data, such as proofs in Lean, trained the way AlphaGo learned through self-play.
- **Misallocated math talent.** He thinks math academia, finance and computer science/data science probably attract too much talent. He floats the idea of NSF grants requiring 10% of a researcher's time to go to collaboration with another department. Patel cites Lars Doucet, who applies land-valuation algorithms for cities.
- **Analogies drive research.** Sanderson says the scarce input for mathematicians is the number of analogies they can draw between fields, which is why math has become collaborative and travel-heavy. He links the recent arrival of information theory and chaos theory to new needs and tools: Bell Labs and computer simulations.
- **Teachers shape trajectories.** He plans to teach high school math for some years to keep his empathy for learners. He tells of a substitute teacher who told him that "music people just aren't math people", and he cites the book Failure to Disrupt on how education technology mostly helps students who are already motivated.
- **Summer of Math Exposition.** The contest began in 2021 after an internship call drew 2,500 applicants. The first year had 1,200 submissions. Sanderson says prizes matter little: the total is $15,000, including $500 for each of 20 honourable mentions. Peer review created co-watching, and over a hundred videos passed 10,000 views in two weeks.
- **Self-teaching.** He tells self-learners to do the calculations, with a notebook and pencil treated as part of reading.
