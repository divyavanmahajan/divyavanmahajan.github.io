## Thesis

Satya Nadella, interviewed by Dwarkesh Patel and SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel after a tour of the Fairwater 2 datacenter, argues that AI will not end in a winner-take-all model. With multiple frontier models and an open-source check, he says, value spreads across infrastructure, models and application scaffolding, so Microsoft builds a fungible fleet that serves many models across generations and geographies. The hosts push back that continual learning or bare-metal deals for labs could shift the margins elsewhere.

## Key points

- **Fairwater scale.** Scott Guthrie says Microsoft aims to 10x training capacity every 18 to 24 months; the building's network optics nearly match all of Azure two and a half years ago, and sites link over a petabit network and an AI WAN to Wisconsin.
- **Coding competition.** Dwarkesh cites Claude Code, Cursor and Copilot near $1 billion each; Dylan puts the category at a $5-6 billion run rate with Microsoft below 25% share. Nadella notes Copilot grew from 20 to 26 million subscribers and pitches Agent HQ as "cable TV" for third-party agents on GitHub.
- **Winner's curse for labs.** Nadella argues a model company can be one copy away from commoditization, while whoever holds the data and scaffolding can fine-tune open checkpoints. He describes Excel Agent as a model in Office's middle tier, not a UI wrapper, and sees Microsoft becoming infrastructure for agents.
- **MAI and OpenAI.** Microsoft keeps OpenAI model access for seven more years, plus its system and chip IP except consumer hardware. MAI's first text model was trained on about 15,000 H100s, and an omni-model comes next.
- **The leasing pause.** Dylan says forecast 2028 capacity fell from 12-13 GW to about 9.5 GW while Oracle grows. Nadella says he refused to be a hoster for one model company, avoided over-building one Nvidia generation, and wants spread across geographies for data residency.
- **Trust as the edge.** Nadella frames Microsoft as capital- and knowledge-intensive, citing 5x-40x software throughput gains, and says US tech wins abroad through trust, sovereign clouds in France and Germany, and respect for resilience.
