## Thesis

Adam Brown, who leads Blueshift at Google DeepMind and taught general relativity at Stanford, gives Dwarkesh Patel a blackboard lecture on the core idea of Einstein's theory. He argues that Einstein's key move was to take seriously a coincidence in Newtonian physics, the equality of inertial and gravitational mass, and conclude that gravity is an inertial force caused by curved spacetime. From there Brown derives the behaviour of black holes and closes with how far AI could get in physics by thinking alone.

## Key points

- **The clue.** Brown notes that the equality of inertial and gravitational mass was known to one part in 1,000 in Newton's time and is now confirmed to one part in 10^15. Inertial forces like centrifugal force always scale with inertial mass, so Einstein asked whether gravity is one too.
- **Straight lines in curved spacetime.** Using a swinging water bucket and a flight map from San Francisco to London over Greenland, Brown explains that a falling piece of chalk follows a straight line in curved spacetime, while someone sitting in a chair does not.
- **Black holes as power plants.** Lowering a brick towards a mass extracts energy: about 7x10^-10 of its rest mass at Earth's surface, close to the 1.5x10^-10 from hydrogen-oxygen rocket fuel. Fission and fusion reach roughly 10^-3 to 10^-2, but lowering a brick to a black hole's event horizon extracts essentially all of it.
- **Falling in.** A distant observer sees an infalling person slow, redden and fade without ever crossing the horizon. The person notices nothing special; for a large enough black hole they could live a whole life inside before reaching the singularity.
- **Evidence.** Brown lists stars orbiting Sagittarius A*, LIGO's 2015 detection of two roughly 30-solar-mass black holes merging about 1.6 billion light-years away, and the Event Horizon Telescope. He recounts failed eclipse expeditions before Eddington's 1919 confirmation.
- **AI and theory.** Brown thinks many parallel AI "Einsteins" could explore the few options open to theorists, but most fields need experiments. He expects LLMs to explain proofs as well as produce them, citing the AI-produced disproof of the Erdős unit distance conjecture.
