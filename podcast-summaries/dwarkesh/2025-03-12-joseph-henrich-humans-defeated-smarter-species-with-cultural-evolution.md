## Thesis

Harvard evolutionary biologist Joseph Henrich argues that humans did not win because of raw individual brainpower but because large, interconnected populations accumulate and transmit know-how that no single person understands. The size and connectedness of this "collective brain" explains which groups expanded, and, he argues, why the Industrial Revolution happened in Europe after the Church broke up intensive kinship. Dwarkesh Patel presses him on what this means for societies of AIs.

## Key points

- **Smarter species lost.** Henrich notes Neanderthals had larger brains, yet the expanding population from the Near East won, likely through a package of technology and social organisation. He cites lost European explorers who starved where foragers thrived, and multi-step cassava processing that nobody could explain causally.
- **Brains are shrinking.** Henrich says human brain size has declined over the last 10,000 years as societies specialised and spread knowledge across people, similar to ants whose brains shrink when they develop occupational castes. He treats IQ as a culturally shaped set of abilities, citing the Flynn effect.
- **The WEIRD thesis.** The Church banned polygyny and cousin marriage (out to sixth cousins) and freed inheritance, producing monogamous nuclear families. Henrich links this to guilds, urbanisation, universities and mobility; places with bishoprics produced more creative people and, today, more patents.
- **Diversity drives innovation.** Henrich's team finds county cultural diversity drove US patenting from roughly 1850 to 1940, and that children patent in the same narrow domain as a same-sex parent nine times more often.
- **AI and the collective brain.** Patel suggests AIs could copy themselves and share knowledge at scale. Henrich worries about too much homogeneity and fidelity, since serendipity, copying errors and shocks inject new information. He agrees with Patel that a single superintelligence matters less than networks of minds.
- **Genius in context.** Henrich explains Einstein's 1905 work partly through his Olympia Academy reading group and his patent office work on train-clock synchronisation devices.
