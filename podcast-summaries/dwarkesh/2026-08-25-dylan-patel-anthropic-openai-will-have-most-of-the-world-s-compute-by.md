## Thesis

Dylan Patel of SemiAnalysis argues that Anthropic and OpenAI now earn so much more revenue per megawatt than anyone else that they can outbid the market, and could control most of the world's usable FLOPs by the end of 2028. He and Dwarkesh Patel then explore the side effects: AI capex funded by debt could raise interest rates, hit non-AI equities and push indebted countries towards default. The forces they discuss all point towards centralisation, and neither can see an obvious counterweight.

## Key points

- **Lab economics flipped.** Dylan says Anthropic turned profitable in Q2 and earns up to $50 million per megawatt, against a base compute cost of about $10-15 million. Both labs went from roughly 2 GW at the start of the year to above 5 GW, and he expects them to take 40-50% of new compute next year.
- **Compute prices rise.** Anyone can turn a profit at $10-15 million per megawatt by serving open models, so the labs must pay $25-50 million to keep taking share. Dylan cites SpaceX selling capacity to Anthropic, and calls Meta and SpaceX the only plausible number three because they build compute before they have customers.
- **Brakes on progress.** Dylan says regulation slows the US labs more than Chinese open models, citing OpenAI withholding Astra and pausing training for two weeks, Anthropic withholding its next model, and data centre bans and moratoriums in New York and Texas.
- **Less inference, more R&D.** His non-consensus view: labs will give a shrinking share of compute to revenue-earning inference, because internal research is worth more. Today's split is roughly 50% research, 10% development and 40% inference.
- **China.** Under 10% of new AI watts are deployed in China, which Dylan expects to reach 30 GW or less by 2028, though Chinese labs stay close to the frontier with 100-200 MW each.
- **Debt and interest rates.** SemiAnalysis models about $11 trillion of capex from 2024 to 2029, with more than $5 trillion financed by debt. Dwarkesh draws a parallel with the Volcker shock and argues that countries such as Pakistan or Nigeria could default.
