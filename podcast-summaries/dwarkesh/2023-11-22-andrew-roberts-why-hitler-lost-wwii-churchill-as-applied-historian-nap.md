## Thesis

Andrew Roberts, historian and co-author with General David Petraeus of "Conflict: The Evolution of Warfare from 1945 to Ukraine", tells Dwarkesh Patel that wars are usually won by strategic leadership rather than by the side with more men, cities or weapons. He applies that lens from post-1945 conflicts to Ukraine and Taiwan, and argues that Hitler lost because he kept putting Nazi ideology ahead of German strategic interest.

## Key points

- **Four tenets of leadership.** Roberts says the winning side gets the big idea right, communicates it, implements it, then keeps refining it. His example: the Guomindang held the major cities, four or five times the men and captured Japanese weapons, yet lost the Chinese Civil War.
- **Nuclear umbrella.** He attributes the limited nature of about 140 post-1945 wars to the bomb, and credits Truman, with Clement Attlee, for refusing MacArthur's push to use it in Korea and then sacking him.
- **Ukraine and Taiwan.** Roberts says Biden has not set out a big idea for Ukraine and calls US arms supply piecemeal, despite $44 billion in aid. He holds that deterrence still works on Xi Jinping, helped by strategic ambiguity and Taiwan's share of high-end semiconductors.
- **Hitler's ideological blunders.** Hitler declared war on the US and invaded Russia because of racial beliefs, Roberts argues. He grants American output: 98,000 warplanes in 1944 against 40,000 each for Germany and Russia, and four of every five German battlefield deaths on the Eastern Front.
- **Future of war.** Roberts expects defence to need more than Clausewitz's three-to-one ratio in cities like Gaza, and machine-on-machine drone warfare with humans "on the loop". He sees tech firms such as Starlink and Palantir as mostly positive for the West.
- **Napoleon and Churchill.** Roberts accepts Patel's comparison of Napoleon to a startup founder and says such a figure today would build a Silicon Valley company. He credits his own output of 20 books to starting at 4am and a daily half-hour nap.
