## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Alexander Wissner-Gross, joined by Viome founder Naveen Jain in place of Dave Blundin, argue that AI is moving from an age of scaling back to an age of research, while competition between frontier labs turns into a sprint. They debate what this means for jobs, GDP measurement, the US debt and energy demand. They disagree on several points, especially whether jobs disappear and how fast new energy sources can arrive.

## Key points

- **Beyond scaling.** Discussing Ilya Sutskever's interview, Wissner-Gross agrees naive scaling will not reach mature superintelligence but says other scaling laws, such as "action scaling" for agents, remain. Jain thinks scaling has maybe a year left.
- **Values in models.** Wissner-Gross describes Anthropic's roughly 14,000-token "soul document" for Claude Opus 4.5 as treating the model as a moral patient or even a person. Jain argues each country will want models encoding its own values.
- **Lab race.** On OpenAI's reported "code red" over Gemini's rise, Jain says Google's chips and proprietary data could let it win. Wissner-Gross says OpenAI is holding back strong unreleased models.
- **Jobs and GDP.** Diamandis cites a McKinsey claim that AI can already automate 57% of US work and an MIT estimate of 11.7% of the workforce. Ismail argues demonetisation will shrink measured GDP. Jain expects people to do more rather than lose jobs. Wissner-Gross expects the Jevons paradox to raise demand.
- **Debt and growth.** Responding to an Elon Musk clip saying AI and robotics are the only fix for US debt, Ismail blames fiat money and points to Bitcoin. Wissner-Gross thinks AI-driven hypergrowth could help. Jain says growth only works with a balanced budget.
- **Energy and robots.** The hosts discuss Microsoft's Fairwater site, xAI's solar farm in Memphis and Helion's fusion plans for Microsoft. Diamandis cites China installing 295,000 industrial robots last year, nine times the US figure.
