## Thesis

Mo Gawdat, former Chief Business Officer of Google X and author of Solve for Happy and Scary Smart, argues that happiness is a default human state that consumer culture has taught people to chase outside themselves. He frames it as an equation, events minus expectations, and ties it to his view of AI: a disruptive decade is coming, and humans need resilience, human connection and better behaviour online, because AI learns its values from us. Peter Diamandis frames Gawdat's 1 Billion Happy mission as a moonshot.

## Key points

- **The happiness equation.** Gawdat says his first model had 19 parameters, which he later reduced to events minus expectations. He separates happiness (calm contentment, linked to serotonin) from fun and pleasure (dopamine, which he calls addictive and a poor painkiller).
- **Growth and consumerism.** Gawdat blames lending and interest for a growth-at-all-costs economy that keeps telling people their Kia or iPhone 13 isn't good enough. He describes being depressed while making a fortune from trading in his twenties.
- **Origin and mission.** After his son Ali died following a routine operation in 2014, Gawdat set out to reach 10 million people with Ali's lessons, then raised the target to 1 billion. He measures people who got the message and then acted on it, and puts that count at about 50 million.
- **Skills for kids.** Gawdat names four: learn AI hands-on, human connection, resilience (his three-question "happiness flowchart"), and living in the present. Diamandis adds purpose, asking good questions, curiosity, and first-principles and exponential thinking.
- **AI timelines and disruption.** Gawdat expects AI excitement, then a "patient zero" crisis, then a contested long term. He believes AGI will arrive before Ray Kurzweil's 2029 date and says graphic designers and authors are already being displaced.
- **Leadership and safety.** Both say political leaders aren't keeping up. Gawdat urges billionaires to fund AI safety and says if just 1% of people model good behaviour, AI could learn good values from us.
