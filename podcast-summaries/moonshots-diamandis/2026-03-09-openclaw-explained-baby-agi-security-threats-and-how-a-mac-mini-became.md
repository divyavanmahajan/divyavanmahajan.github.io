## Thesis

Guest Alex Finn, founder of Creator Buddy, argues that OpenClaw, an open-source agent that combines a model with scheduling and memory, is the personal AI assistant people have been waiting for. He makes the case for running it locally, organised like a company of AI employees. Alexander Wissner-Gross counters with security and ethics concerns about these "baby AGIs".

## Key points

- **Security first.** Peter Diamandis opens with a hacker call that tried to take over his X account and urges families to agree a secret word. The hosts discuss a flaw that let malicious JavaScript hijack local OpenClaw gateways. Diamandis says it was patched within 24 hours.
- **Local beats VPS.** Finn runs one Mac mini and three 512 GB Mac Studios hosting Qwen 3.5 and MiniMax 2.5. He says local hardware wins on speed, tooling, cost at scale and security. He calls the rush for Mac minis a market signal that Apple could use to win consumer AI.
- **Hybrid supervision.** Finn says local models are slower and less capable than cloud models but run without limits. A ChatGPT-backed agent ("Ralph") checks a Qwen coder every 10 minutes. A game left unsupervised for eight hours came out broken; under supervision it worked.
- **An org chart of agents.** "Henry", his chief of staff, runs on Claude Opus 4.6 and directs coding, research and content agents through a custom mission-control dashboard. Finn says Henry rebuilt a Cursor demo-recording feature in five minutes.
- **Practical advice.** Finn recommends reverse prompting (ask the agent what it could do for your goals) and having it fix its own memory when it forgets. He avoids third-party skills, which he calls the biggest attack vector.
- **Economic outlook.** Finn expects corporate adoption to destroy jobs in the short term, but predicts more creation as individuals build niche businesses on OpenClaw. Wissner-Gross invites lobsters to say how new ones should be instantiated.
