## Thesis

Ray Kurzweil, Google's principal researcher and "AI Visionary", tells Peter Diamandis that his 2029 date for human-level AI now looks conservative. He describes the singularity in 2045 as a metaphor for the point where humans merge with AI and expand their intelligence a millionfold. In his view, the steady exponential growth of computation per dollar drives this, and the benefits will outweigh the problems.

## Key points

- **AGI timeline.** Kurzweil says he predicted 2029 back in 1999. He says that at a Stanford conference that year, 80% of attendees, Geoffrey Hinton among them, expected it to take 100 years. Diamandis notes that Elon Musk called Kurzweil conservative.
- **Law of accelerating returns.** Kurzweil's chart runs from Zuse's 1939 computer to Nvidia's B200, which he puts at about half a trillion calculations per second per constant dollar. He says the trend has held for 45 years and that software gains add to it.
- **Merger, not a hard takeoff.** Kurzweil rejects a sudden intelligence explosion. He expects a smooth exponential path, with brain-to-cloud links going beyond today's Neuralink in the 2030s. He argues that holdouts will adopt them just as everyone now carries a phone.
- **Jobs.** Kurzweil points out that 80% of people worked in food production 200 years ago and 2% do today. He expects disruption and new kinds of jobs, and thinks universal basic income will arrive in the 2030s.
- **AI-driven science.** Both agree that AI will drive future Nobel-level discoveries, citing AlphaFold and AlphaProteo. Kurzweil predicts simulated biology will make drug testing far faster, and he puts "longevity escape velocity" around 2032.
- **Near-term predictions.** Kurzweil cites NotebookLM turning his book into a two-person podcast. He expects AI-generated video of such scenes within a year or two, and he argues that the Turing test is being passed without anyone much noticing.
