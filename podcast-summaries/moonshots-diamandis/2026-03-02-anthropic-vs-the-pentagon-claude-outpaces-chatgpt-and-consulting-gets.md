## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that AI has become national infrastructure. Labs now negotiate with governments as moral and geopolitical actors, and the money is in enterprise agents rather than consumer chatbots. Institutions such as consultancies, audit firms and governments, they say, are not adapting fast enough.

## Key points

- **India's AI Impact Summit.** Diamandis cites $250 billion in AI investment commitments and 88 nations signing the New Delhi Declaration. Wissner-Gross notes that frontier training stays in the US while inference is localised, and calls Chinese open-weight models an AI version of Belt and Road.
- **Anthropic and the Pentagon.** Dario Amodei refused to remove safeguards on surveillance and autonomous weapons, putting $200 million in contracts at risk under threats of a supply-chain-risk label or the Defense Production Act. Wissner-Gross calls it "a very Western problem to have" and expects an amicable resolution. Blundin argues self-improving models make a lagging military-only version useless.
- **Enterprise beats consumer.** A chart shows Anthropic revenue growing 10x a year against OpenAI's 3.4x, with a crossover projected mid-year. Blundin extrapolates to a trillion dollars of revenue around 2029. The hosts also note OpenAI cutting its 2030 compute plan from $1.4 trillion to $600 billion.
- **Consulting and audit.** On Accenture tying promotions to AI tool usage, Ismail argues advisory has a bright future because clients are further behind. Diamandis warns of Goodhart's law, and Blundin reports a Big Four tech team expecting 80% of audit work to go.
- **Agents everywhere.** The hosts discuss OpenClaw agents, Rent-a-Human passing 500,000 registered humans (Wissner-Gross calls it "meat puppetry"), and Andrej Karpathy's view of claws as a new stack layer. Wissner-Gross counters that the next real layer is recursive self-improvement.
- **Jobs and other topics.** Andrew Yang's prediction of 20-50% white-collar displacement splits the group: Ismail doubts it, while Blundin expects social unrest before the next presidential election. Also covered: cyber stocks falling on Claude Code security, a $100 genome from Element Biosciences, and Tesla FSD safety figures.
