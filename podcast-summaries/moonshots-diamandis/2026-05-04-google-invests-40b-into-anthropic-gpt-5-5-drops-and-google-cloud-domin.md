## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross review a run of model releases (15 in eight weeks, by Diamandis's count) and conclude that the frontier is a three-way US race whose real limit is compute. Wissner-Gross, citing OpenAI's Noam Brown, suggests model weights may matter less as inference-time reasoning grows. The hosts disagree on whether the bottleneck is chip fabrication or powered land.

## Key points

- **Open weights.** Diamandis describes Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.6 as a trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model reportedly trained for $4.6 million. Blundin says it runs at about 1/8 the API cost via Fireworks, or 1/30 self-hosted, but warns that code injection can't be ruled out. Wissner-Gross puts Chinese open models a few months behind the US frontier.
- **GPT 5.5.** Wissner-Gross reads its jump on Terminal Bench 2.0 as a push to strengthen Codex. He also points to gains of about 1% a month on FrontierMath Tier 4 and predicts research-grade maths will be largely solved within four to five years.
- **Google's position.** Diamandis covers the eighth-generation TPUs (8T for training, 8i for inference) and a commitment to 960,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs. Wissner-Gross cites an Epoch estimate that Google holds about a quarter of global AI compute.
- **Cash for compute.** As discussed on the show, Google commits up to $40 billion to Anthropic at a $350 billion valuation plus 5 GW of TPUs, and Amazon a further $25 billion against Anthropic spending $100 billion or more on AWS. Blundin says TSMC is the real constraint. Wissner-Gross thinks permitting and powered land bind harder for now.
- **Value per token.** Wissner-Gross says Anthropic's projects, including the Project Deal marketplace experiment, aim to raise the economic value of each token, which is why the labs have converged on code generation.
- **Work and institutions.** Blundin defends "token maxing" and suggests a 1:1 ratio of token spend to payroll. Ismail expects strong regulatory pushback against AI in medicine and says consulting must move from labour pyramids to agentic platforms.
