## Thesis

Palmer Luckey, founder of Oculus and Anduril, tells Peter Diamandis that Anduril was built on the assumption that AI and autonomy would work, while legacy defence firms planned as if they would not. He argues that speed, latency and software that scales across many platforms matter more for the military than superintelligence, and that China is a distinct threat which hard-power deterrence has to address.

## Key points

- **UAPs and aliens.** Luckey says he has seen non-public material that is "very hard to explain" but no conclusive evidence such as recovered craft. He suspects an unknown natural phenomenon, and rejects the idea that microelectronics came from alien wreckage.
- **Lattice over superintelligence.** Diamandis cites a reported 135 IQ for OpenAI's o3. Luckey says he runs Anduril more conservatively than the optimistic forecasts. Its core product, Lattice, is named after Laplace's demon, and he describes its goal as predicting what an enemy will do, not only seeing what it is doing.
- **China, not only rogue actors.** Luckey disagrees with Diamandis's framing of "US and China versus the rogue actor". He calls nation states mostly rational, but says China has territorial ambitions beyond Taiwan, has about 300 times US shipbuilding capacity, and requires ferries to be built to military specification. Some Anduril teams work under a "China 27" mandate.
- **Design for surge production.** Anduril avoids vertical integration so that parts can be made by any machine shop and production can scale 10x or 100x in wartime. Luckey expects warfare in 2030 to look mostly like today, with new systems only 1-2% of the fight. He names subterranean warfare as the next major domain.
- **Autonomous weapons ethics.** Luckey argues for case-by-case review rather than blanket bans, pointing to landmines and decades-old autonomous defences, as long as a human stays accountable. He describes the Pulsar L electronic warfare system downing 25 autonomous drones in a test.
- **Beyond defence.** Topics include the wildfire XPRIZE, early Bitcoin mining, his view of Mark Zuckerberg, and his proposal that billionaires make "impact pledges" instead of giving pledges.
