## Thesis

Keith Ferrazzi, author of "Never Eat Alone" and CEO of Ferrazzi Greenlight, argues that every leader should write a book, even if it is never published, because it forces you to define your brand. Peter Diamandis agrees. Both say the money comes from speaking rather than book sales, and that a number one New York Times ranking changed little for either of them.

## Key points

- **Start small.** Ferrazzi's method: write 10 sentences on what you want to teach, discuss them with friends, expand them to 10 sections, then 10 paragraphs. He calls his process "radically agile", with chapters rewritten many times. Diamandis writes for an hour most mornings.
- **Writing partners.** Both credit co-writers (Tahl Raz for Ferrazzi, Steven Kotler for Diamandis). Ferrazzi always puts the co-writer on the cover.
- **Agents and advances.** Diamandis says John Brockman auctioned the "Abundance" proposal, which Simon & Schuster won for $240,000, paid in thirds. Ferrazzi says his agent accepted about a quarter of a million less so he could publish with Harvard Business School, which he says boosts international speaking.
- **Self-publishing.** Diamandis self-published "Exponential Organizations 2.0" with Salim Ismail through Kary Oberbrunner's Igniting Souls. He values the speed, while traditional publishers take about a year after delivery.
- **Bestseller lists.** Diamandis's team estimates 5,000-10,000 first-week sales to reach the New York Times list and 50,000-100,000 for number one. Ferrazzi notes that the list is editorial and flags bulk purchases. Ferrazzi raised $2 million with University of Phoenix for one launch, and Diamandis recruited 200 community members to each sell 100 copies of "Bold".
- **ChatGPT.** Ferrazzi paused his book on high-performing teams to rework it after prompting ChatGPT for how Freud or Jung would see executive teams. Diamandis describes a GPT-4 model trained on the EXO book and his "Peter bot".
