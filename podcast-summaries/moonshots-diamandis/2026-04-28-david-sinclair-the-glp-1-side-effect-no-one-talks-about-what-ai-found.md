## Thesis

In an audience Q&A recorded at the 2026 Abundance360 Summit, Peter Diamandis and Harvard geneticist David Sinclair take questions on longevity. Sinclair's main technology claim is that AI now does real discovery work in his lab, from screening molecules to proposing new ways of measuring biological age. Diamandis adds that mindset and environment matter alongside the coming therapeutics.

## Key points

- **AI as a lab scientist.** Sinclair says his lab works with AI companies and academics on AI-driven drug design, screening billions and eventually trillions of molecules for ones that might reverse ageing. Image models classify cells as old or young, looking at millions of cells in about 10 minutes.
- **A discovery the team had not thought of.** Sinclair says a collaboration with a group he calls Cadence, fed with transcriptomic data, proposed a new way to read biological age, and a paper has been submitted. He rejects the claim that AI can only repeat what it was taught.
- **GLP-1s as a market opener.** Sinclair says GLP-1 drugs have "paved the way socially" for longevity medicines, which he expects to be much bigger. He also flags a rare reported vision-loss risk and says his group's ER-100 therapy will be tested on that condition.
- **What investors should check.** Asked for longevity KPIs, Sinclair says the team drives about 50% of his investment decisions, followed by cash on hand, valuation, runway and distance from the clinic. Capital shortage is described as the top cause of biotech failure.
- **Genetics and monitoring.** Sinclair cites a recent paper suggesting lifespan may be closer to 50/50 genetic, and says doctors should use genome sequencing and continuous monitoring more. Diamandis argues lifestyle governs the first 70-80 years and genetics the later decades.
- **Animal-model results.** Sinclair reports early animal results for OSK/ER-100 in ALS-related motor neuron regrowth and macular degeneration, stressing that these are animal data, with Life Biosciences pursuing clinical tests.
