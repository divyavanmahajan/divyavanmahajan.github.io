## Thesis

Ray Kurzweil tells Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates that his timelines still hold: human-level AGI by 2029 and a singularity by 2045, which he defines as humans becoming a thousand times more intelligent by merging with AI. He argues that the public argument has moved from whether this will happen to whether it will be good for humanity, and he remains optimistic.

## Key points

- **A strict AGI definition.** Kurzweil defines AGI as matching expert humans across thousands of fields and combining insights between them, which he calls stricter than the Turing test. He expects disagreement to fade over a three-to-four-year band ending in 2029, and notes that Elon Musk says 2026.
- **Merging, not competing.** Kurzweil says people will not be able to tell whether an idea came from their biological or computational intelligence. He repeats his claim that 2025-2035 will bring as much change as the previous century.
- **Consciousness and avatars.** Kurzweil says there is no scientific test for consciousness but expects society to accept AIs that act conscious within a few years. Wissner-Gross disagrees, pointing to emerging benchmarks for model self-awareness. Kurzweil says he is building an avatar of himself from his 11 books and articles to take interview requests this year.
- **Longevity timelines.** Kurzweil predicts longevity escape velocity around 2032, driven by AI simulation of biology rather than nanotechnology. He calls cryonics a fallback and says he has signed up for it.
- **Work and income.** Kurzweil expects abundance without conventional jobs and stands by his TED prediction of UBI-like support in the 2030s. Diamandis cites a figure that 79% of corporate employees find no meaning in their work.
- **Compute, energy, robots.** Kurzweil expects "computronium" by 2045, with one litre outthinking 10 billion people. He suggests slower, massively parallel circuits and reversible computing to cut energy use, says he is not enthusiastic about nuclear, and expects practical robotics in 2026-2027.
