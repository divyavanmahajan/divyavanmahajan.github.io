## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Wait But Why author Tim Urban discuss curiosity, passion versus purpose, and cognitive biases. Urban argues that almost everyone is curious, but many people give up on that part of their identity because they lack the basic framework that makes a topic interesting. Looking ahead, he expects exponential technology to produce either a utopia or a very bad outcome by around 2060, and sees nudging that outcome as the best use of his platform.

## Key points

- **The knowledge tree trunk.** Urban borrows Elon Musk's analogy: new facts are leaves that need a trunk to attach to. He says crypto bores him only because he has not built that trunk yet, while topics like brain-machine interfaces and cryonics "clicked" once he did.
- **Why curiosity pays.** Urban argues curious people retain what they learn, leave their comfort zone more, and build closer relationships because they listen and ask good questions. Diamandis recalls sitting in the front row of an MIT aerospace class out of sheer interest.
- **Creativity trained out.** Urban says "because I said so" parenting and industrial-era schooling teach children to defer to authority, and he cites studies in which creativity drops sharply between age five and twenty.
- **Passion vs purpose.** Urban sees passion as enough of a purpose on its own. Diamandis argues purpose is the long-term guiding star that kept him going through repeated failures; Urban reframes passion as an engine pushing you and purpose as something pulling you.
- **Cognitive biases.** The two run through confirmation bias, ad hominem reasoning, availability, negativity and bandwagon effects. Urban explains them as brain wiring that evolved to keep people accepted in their tribe rather than to find the truth.
- **An X Prize idea.** Asked where Diamandis should launch the next X Prize, Urban picks cryonics as a "bridge" to future medicine. Diamandis suggests organ preservation for transplants as a first step.
