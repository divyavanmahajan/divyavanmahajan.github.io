## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Mates, recorded on US midterm election day, argue that AI spending will keep rising even as lenders get more cautious, because the hosts see recursive self-improvement (RSI) as already happening. Alexander Wissner-Gross holds that AI models will increasingly read, edit and police each other, and that good benchmarks are how anyone can point that capability at a goal.

## Key points

- **The $3 trillion build-out.** Diamandis cites reports that Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon and Oracle have committed more than $3 trillion, about ten Apollo programmes, while lenders demand tougher terms and investors buy CoreWeave puts. Dave Blundin calls the investment right for humanity. Wissner-Gross expects insurance capital to be tapped next.
- **New benchmarks.** SemiAnalysis's GDPX drops agents into eight-hour expert tasks; Opus 5.5 leads with 12% and finance and legal scores are near zero. Wissner-Gross expects it to saturate within a year or two. OpenAI's own everyday-errands benchmark rose from 49.3 (GPT 5.2) to 80.6 (GPT-6 Astra), which Salim Ismail sees as the more important result for ordinary people.
- **RSI as a line item.** Diamandis reads from Anthropic's IPO filing: internal Claude usage grew more than 250-fold from January to September, its value exceeds cash payroll, and Claude wrote over 90% of September's code. Wissner-Gross says that counts as RSI by spending, but "real" RSI needs physical self-replication.
- **Models editing weights.** An MIT CSAIL paper reports a frontier model editing 0.016% of a small Qwen model's weights to lift its math score from 7.9% to 48.1% without retraining. Wissner-Gross sees it as the future of interpretability. Blundin imagines swappable neural modules, while warning it can also strip guardrails from open models.
- **Cyber and space.** Hackers heated supermarket freezers across Europe; Wissner-Gross says legacy SCADA systems need AI-led overhauls. NASA's Jared Isaacman plans an Earth-orbit Artemis III in 2027 and a 2028 lunar landing.
- **AMA.** Ismail says employment has been confused with purpose. Blundin says Western decisions are skewed by media-driven empathy.
