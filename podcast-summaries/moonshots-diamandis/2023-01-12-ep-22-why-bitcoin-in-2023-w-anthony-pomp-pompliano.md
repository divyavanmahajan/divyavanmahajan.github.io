## Thesis

Anthony Pompliano argues that Bitcoin is a hard digital asset with a fixed supply that protects savings against a depreciating dollar. In his view it must first become a store of value, and he expects payment use to grow first in countries with weak currencies. Peter Diamandis presents Bitcoin as a driver of abundance and an example of his "6Ds" of exponential technology.

## Key points

- **Store of value first.** Pompliano says money moves from store of value to medium of exchange to unit of account. He cites Alex Gladstein's idea of "financial privilege": Americans need a new payment currency less than people in places like Zimbabwe.
- **Ownership is spreading.** Pompliano says that after a 75% price drop, 67% of bitcoin had not moved, while small wallets kept reaching all-time highs. He reads this as conviction and decentralisation.
- **Price scenarios.** Pompliano, stressing that this is not investment advice, puts a global store of value at about $1 million per coin, or twice the roughly $10 trillion gold market. He says it would be worth "millions" as a medium of exchange, and that its price would stop mattering if it became the unit of account.
- **How to hold it.** For ordinary savers he suggests 1-5%, bought through dollar-cost averaging and held for 5-20 years. He notes that the US wash-sale rule did not apply to crypto and tells listeners to check with an accountant. Diamandis says he used this to book a $40K loss.
- **Catalysts and risks.** Pompliano lists the halving, government bans (which he says increased adoption in Nigeria and Pakistan) and central bank digital currencies. He warns that CBDCs could allow surveillance and personalised monetary policy. He says adoption has run from individuals to companies such as MicroStrategy, Block and Tesla, with countries such as El Salvador next.
- **Mindset and custody.** He describes the Bitcoin mindset as self-reliance. He says sceptics like Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger see Bitcoin as outside their circle of competence. His advice is "not your keys not your coins": test a hardware wallet such as Ledger with about $5, and understand the lending risk behind exchanges that pay interest.
