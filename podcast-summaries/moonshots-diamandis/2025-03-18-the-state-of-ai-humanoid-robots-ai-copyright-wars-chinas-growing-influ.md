## Thesis

In this "WTF Just Happened in Tech" episode, recorded just after the 2025 Abundance Summit, Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail review the week's AI news and the summit's talks. Their shared view is that AI is moving faster than education, copyright and regulation can adapt. Institutions that resist, they argue, will be forced to change, and global coordination is preferable to an AI race against China.

## Key points

- **Humanoid robots.** Diamandis reports that Figure's new factory can build 12,000 robots a year and is scaling to 100,000, with Brett Adcock expecting volume prices below $20,000. Ismail says he is revising his earlier scepticism because robots share what they learn.
- **China and copyright.** The hosts discuss Sam Altman calling DeepSeek state-controlled and OpenAI and Google asking to train on copyrighted content. Ismail argues copyright terms have been stretched far beyond their original intent, but says content owners still need compensation. Diamandis points to Bill Gross's ProRata AI, which splits revenue by source attribution; he discloses that his venture fund is an investor.
- **Collaboration over rivalry.** Diamandis cites Eric Schmidt's view that rogue actors, not China, are the main threat. Ismail proposes a Bretton Woods-style global convening on AI.
- **AI in schools.** Diamandis contrasts Estonia and Beijing, which are rolling out AI in schools, with US teens at the summit who were all told not to use AI. Ismail expects teachers' unions to resist and then be forced to adapt.
- **Summit highlights.** The hosts mention Ilya Sutskever's superintelligence startup, reportedly raising $2 billion at a $30 billion valuation; Ismail asks for clearer definitions of AGI and superintelligence. They also discuss Max Hodak's biohybrid brain interface, Dave Blundin's data that MIT leads in AI unicorns, Mo Gawdat's warning of a dystopian transition period, and Google's Wing drone delivery and Starline.
