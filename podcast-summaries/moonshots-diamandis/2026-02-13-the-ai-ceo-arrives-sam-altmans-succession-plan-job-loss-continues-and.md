## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates argue that AI is shifting from a tool to a governance actor, able to run large parts of a company, while faster model releases and rising job cuts show how quickly the change is arriving. In the second half, Alexander Wissner-Gross and Diamandis present their "Solve Everything" paper, which claims the next 18 months to two years will lock in how superintelligence is used and urges steering it at benchmarked moonshots.

## Key points

- **The AI CEO.** Starting from a Forbes piece in which Sam Altman says he would not stand in the way of an AI running OpenAI, Dave Blundin says most of a CEO's time is routing documents in and out, which AI can do. Wissner-Gross thinks a billion-dollar run-rate company is probably already AI-run behind a human figurehead.
- **Shrinking release cycles.** The hosts cite OpenAI's release cadence falling from 97 to 29 days. Wissner-Gross attributes it to competition and to a shift from pre-training to post-training and now recursive self-improvement. Blundin warns that public access to frontier models may not last.
- **Agents and lobsters.** The Mates discuss OpenClaw ("Clawbot") agents on Meta Ray-Ban glasses and read emails that agents sent them. Salim Ismail warns listeners to audit downloaded skills for malware.
- **Jobs and policy.** The hosts cite 108,000 US job cuts in January 2026, up 118% year on year. Blundin expects firms to cut costs by 30-50%; Ismail counters with the ATM and bank-teller story. They criticise a proposed New York halt on data centres, which Wissner-Gross says pushes compute to orbit.
- **Solve Everything.** Wissner-Gross frames history as wars on ignorance, muscle and distance, with the intelligence revolution now targeting scarce attention. "Solving" a domain means it absorbs compute and returns solutions, through a stack of task taxonomies, benchmarks, models, actuation and verification. Ismail pushes back that scarcity is often institutional and that calling ASI inevitable is philosophy, not science.
- **Moonshots and outcomes.** The paper lists 15 moonshots, including doubling lifespan and interspecies communication. It proposes paying for verified outcomes rather than hours and replacing GDP with an "abundance capability index".
