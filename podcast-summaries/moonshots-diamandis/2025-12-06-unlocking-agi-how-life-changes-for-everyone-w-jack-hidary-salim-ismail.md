## Thesis

Recorded on stage at the FII conference in Riyadh, this short episode has Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and SandboxAQ CEO Jack Hidary covering energy, humanoid robots and quantum computing. Hidary argues that the current energy crunch is temporary and that AI plus quantum will be "twin engines" of change, with 2030 the year quantum starts to have real business impact, for good and for bad.

## Key points

- **Scarcity before abundance.** Diamandis cites China adding 429 GW of power in 2024 against 51 GW in the US. Hidary says gas turbines from GE, Mitsubishi Heavy and Siemens have a wait of about four and a half years. He expects a flip to abundance in seven to nine years from SMRs, new turbine factories, and solar efficiency rising above today's roughly 27%.
- **What abundance changes.** Ismail says cheap energy makes desalination affordable and undermines oil-exporting economies such as Canada and Russia. Blundin argues that data centers now move to the energy, which favours Saudi Arabia, where he relays a claim that solar costs a third of US levels.
- **Robots at scale.** Diamandis notes that 1X priced its Neo robot at $20,000 or $499 a month. Hidary says five robots-building-robots factories are under construction (two in the US, three in China). Ismail is skeptical of home robots and of the supply chains needed for a billion units.
- **China's robot exports.** Diamandis says Chinese robot imports are surging in Poland (1,700%) and Mexico (275%). Hidary predicts that ageing Japan will end up using Chinese rather than Japanese robots.
- **Quantum's two moments.** Hidary expects quantum computers to break today's public-key encryption around 2029-2030, exposing anyone who has not switched to quantum-safe methods. He also expects them to help model fusion plasma, which he says could speed fusion up by 10 to 20 years.
- **Government stakes.** Blundin calls the US government's stakes in companies such as Intel good investments but a terrible precedent. Ismail proposes a national quantum compute reserve instead.
