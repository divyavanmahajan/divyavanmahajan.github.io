## Thesis

In an audience Q&A hosted by producer Nick, David Sinclair argues that longevity medicine is moving slowly not for lack of science but because drug development is expensive and regulators are built around safety, with aging not yet classed as a medical condition. Peter Diamandis adds that faster pathways and large incentive prizes could speed things up, and both see adding healthy decades, not immortality, as the realistic goal.

## Key points

- **Pipeline timing.** Sinclair says Metro Biotech, a spin-out from his lab, has NAD-booster candidates in phase two with positive data, possibly two to three years from market, and that eye-reprogramming work in non-human primates could reach humans as soon as the following year.
- **Why drugs are slow.** Sinclair notes companies can spend $400-500 million and still fail; Diamandis argues the FDA weighs deaths from approving a harmful drug far more heavily than lives lost by not approving one, citing early gene-therapy setbacks as the reason for caution.
- **Aging as an indication.** Sinclair reports the FDA was open to approving a drug that slows aging, but because aging is not a medical condition, investment is held back; he wants more public and political pressure on the issue.
- **Right to try and repurposing.** Diamandis proposes an "accredited patient" model; Sinclair prefers certification of doctors over open online access. Diamandis cites David Fajgenbaum's Every Cure effort to screen 3,000 approved drugs against diseases without treatments.
- **Kicking the can.** Sinclair sees 150 years as reachable but not immortality; Diamandis points to AI, robotics and Ray Kurzweil's early-2040s singularity estimate. Both are lukewarm on cryonics, calling it a niche for few people.
- **Age-reversal XPRIZE.** Diamandis describes a planned, majority-funded $101 million prize, with Sinclair and George Church as scientific co-chairs, for a therapy under a year that reverses biological age by 20 years or more. Sinclair repeats an earlier estimate of $86 trillion in long-run US value from one extra healthy year.
