## Thesis

Ben Horowitz of Andreessen Horowitz tells Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates that AI progress is predictable but societal change will be slower than viral essays suggest. He argues the real danger is not failing to pause AI but slowing it enough in the US to fall behind China, and that AI and crypto will grow together as computer and network. Alexander Wissner-Gross holds that recursive self-improvement is already under way.

## Key points

- **Viral doom essay.** Horowitz calls Matt Shumer's essay on imminent AI disruption too aggressive about the pace of change. Dave Blundin says boards now expect AI to make staff three times more productive, which without growth means a two-thirds headcount cut.
- **xAI departures.** The hosts discuss co-founders leaving xAI after its merger with SpaceX. Wissner-Gross suggests a natural reorganisation rather than ITAR concerns. Horowitz says the Chinese government is cracking down on US academia's use of Chinese open-source models.
- **Pausing and classifying AI.** Horowitz recounts a meeting with the previous administration where an official said math could be restricted, as nuclear physics was in the 1940s. He argues classification did not stop the Soviets getting the bomb.
- **Capital versus labour.** On a slide showing wages up 3% and profits up 43% since 2019, Horowitz says Anthropic's reported $14 billion revenue is under 1% of the market. He rejects the idea that graduates have a two-year window, saying people with initiative can direct armies of agents. Founders building a new foundation model, he says, must be able to raise $2 billion before a product.
- **Agents, crypto and Apple.** Wissner-Gross notes an agent that paid for a child bot's server and API access with Bitcoin Lightning. Horowitz calls crypto internet-native money for AI and says a16z funded an AI bank. Both argue Apple's unified-memory Mac minis give it an opening to host OpenClaw agents, which Horowitz calls perhaps the best product strategy idea.
- **Moon before Mars.** After Horowitz leaves, Diamandis discusses Elon Musk's shift towards lunar mass drivers launching AI satellites, tracing the idea to Gerard K. O'Neill.
