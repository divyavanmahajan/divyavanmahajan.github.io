## Thesis

Recorded live at Moonshots Live 2026, X co-founder Astro Teller tells Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates that taking moonshots is easy if you do not care about return on investment. The hard part, he argues, is systematising radical innovation so it is efficient, which depends on a protected culture of humility, intellectual honesty and early killing of ideas rather than on Alphabet's money.

## Key points

- **What counts as a moonshot.** Teller defines it as a huge, nameable problem, a science-fiction-sounding product that would solve it, and a breakthrough technology that gives at least a small chance of building it. Teams need audacity and humility in equal measure.
- **The funnel.** X gives code names to 100-200 ideas a year; Teller puts the total at about 2,000 over 16 years, with roughly 35-50 graduates. Google Brain, started at X with Andrew Ng and Jeff Dean, is his example of a "seed crystal" that led to TPUs and the Transformer.
- **The million-or-billion test.** Teller asks executives to pick a guaranteed $1 million or a 1-in-100 chance of $1 billion. Almost all choose the billion, but few say their CEO or board would back it, so he argues choice-B work must sit on the edge of the company. Diamandis frames this as a separate unit reporting to the CEO.
- **Moonshots are getting cheaper.** Teller says X's cost per graduate has fallen by about a factor of three in 16 years, partly due to AI. One of the Mates questions whether that just tracks economic growth; Teller says that confuses cost with value.
- **Unsolved targets.** Clean water at about a penny a litre (X's attempts reached 10 cents), time- and location-shifting energy, and education. On new materials, he says one flake of a room-temperature superconductor is only "the first 5%" of a business.
- **AI as an implementation detail.** Asked by Dave Blundin about AI versus salary costs, Teller says salaries still dominate for the water team, and expects humans to stay in the loop for a decade or two. Salim Ismail offers the methodology of his 50,000-person community as a way to copy moonshot factories.
