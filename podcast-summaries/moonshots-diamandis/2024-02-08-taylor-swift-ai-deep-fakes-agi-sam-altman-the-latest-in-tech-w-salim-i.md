## Thesis

In the first of their monthly news episodes, Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail agree with Sam Altman that AGI will come sooner than most people expect, change less than feared at first, and then change more over time. Ismail argues that the bigger risk is that institutions, from schools to marriage to law, cannot update fast enough. Both hosts see deepfakes as an arms race with a dangerous gap before defences catch up.

## Key points

- **Altman and normalisation.** The hosts play an Altman clip predicting a two-week freakout when AGI arrives. Ismail agrees and cites Louis C.K.'s joke about in-flight Wi-Fi to show how quickly novelty becomes normal. He also says no one has a clear definition of intelligence.
- **Shrinking timelines.** Diamandis notes that Kurzweil predicted human-level AI by 2029 back in 1999. He shows an ARK Invest chart in which forecasts fell from 50 years away in 2020 to 8 years in 2023, and which points to 2027 if the trend holds.
- **AI as leveller.** Diamandis cites a Microsoft future-of-work report finding that AI helps novices far more than experts. Ismail says board members who don't use ChatGPT are falling short. Diamandis cites average OpenAI researcher pay of $865,000.
- **Education flips to demand.** Ismail argues that universities are job-skill schools. He says students should start from the problem they want to solve, and that GitHub ratings now matter more than degrees in Silicon Valley.
- **Prediction effects.** Ismail tells of a Singularity University alumnus who traced a 50% drop in car-wash revenue in Buenos Aires to better weather forecasts.
- **Fixing deepfakes.** After the Taylor Swift deepfakes, Diamandis proposes four responses: harsh criminal penalties, white-hat entrepreneurs (including an XPRIZE idea of "AI for truth"), blockchain metadata for authentication, and a social default of disbelief. Ismail adds teaching critical thinking.
