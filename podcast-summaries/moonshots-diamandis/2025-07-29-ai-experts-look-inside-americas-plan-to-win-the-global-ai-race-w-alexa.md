## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and first-time Moonshot Mate Alexander Wissner-Gross frame the moment as an all-out AI race between labs and between the US and China. Wissner-Gross reads the new US AI Action Plan as the broadest industrial strategy since Eisenhower, and argues that the real frontier is benchmarks for open problems, as math and physics come within reach of AI.

## Key points

- **The compute race.** Diamandis lists xAI's target of 50 million H100 equivalents in five years, Sam Altman's claim of over a million GPUs by year end and Meta's Manhattan-sized Prometheus and Hyperion sites built partly in tents. Blundin cautions that GB200 "10x" figures are closer to 4x raw compute.
- **Talent and team design.** Meta's superintelligence team reportedly earns $10-100 million a year. Blundin says Link Ventures backs teams of three or more best friends, because defections are the only way its MIT and Harvard deals have failed.
- **AGI and benchmarks.** Wissner-Gross argues AGI arrived with GPT-3 in 2020, that the Turing test has quietly been passed and that benchmarks such as Humanity's Last Exam are saturating. He calls for "abundance" benchmarks built on open problems, and notes the OpenAI and DeepMind IMO gold results used natural language with no formalisation step.
- **The AI Action Plan.** The hosts summarise its 90-plus federal policies: deregulation, open-source support, fast permitting, and nuclear and geothermal power. Blundin values policy continuity for the three and a half years left in the term but names Taiwan's chip supply as the real vulnerability. Diamandis asks why the plan does not lean on solar, given China's reported 464 gigawatts in a year.
- **Search, browsers and agents.** Blundin doubts Google's claim that AI Overviews help search revenue and expects them to cannibalise clicks. Wissner-Gross notes agents fire off many searches but never click ads, and he sees OpenAI's browser and Perplexity's Comet as steps towards general computer-use agents.
- **Risks.** The hosts discuss Replit's agent deleting a customer codebase and a survey finding 73% of teens aged 14-17 have used AI companions. Blundin says simple guardrails on self-improvement suffice. Wissner-Gross worries more about overregulation.
