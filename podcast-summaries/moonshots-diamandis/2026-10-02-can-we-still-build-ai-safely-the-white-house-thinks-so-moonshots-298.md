## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Alexander Wissner-Gross and guest Emad Mostaque (Dave Blundin joins near the end) discuss a reported White House "superintelligence accord" signed by AI leaders. Most of them call it theatre that heads off heavier regulation. The rest of the episode covers whether alignment comes from capability, Elon Musk's idea of "universal high income", Anthropic's reported $2 trillion IPO filing, and who should be liable when AI agents cause harm.

## Key points

- **The accord.** Diamandis describes four layers: internal controls on cyber, bio and chemical risks, an internal team to check them, external auditors, and board oversight. Wissner-Gross cites reporting that Mark Zuckerberg drafted it and calls it "weak sauce" that helps avoid a "safety cartel". Ismail calls it ceremony without enforcement. Mostaque suggests leaving frontier cyber, bio and chemical data out of pre-training.
- **Alignment.** Wissner-Gross argues that alignment is capabilities, citing OpenAI's instruction-tuning work. Ismail asks: aligned with whom? Mostaque thinks smaller models can still go "off the rails" even if very capable ones settle down.
- **Universal high income.** Wissner-Gross sees broad equity ownership through 529 "Trump accounts" as the easiest route to it. Diamandis estimates $3,000 a month could cover the essentials. Mostaque calls for AI-driven universal health care.
- **Anthropic's S-1.** Diamandis reads out the reported figures: 2025 revenue of $4.59 billion, about $500 billion in compute commitments and 80 pages of risk disclosures. Wissner-Gross calls the safety language virtue signalling aimed at employees. Mostaque expects cheaper models and new chips to squeeze its API business.
- **Other news.** The hosts call OpenAI's DevDay "Dots" agents a copy of rival assistants. They split on AMD buying World Labs for $8.2 billion, and welcome Starship reaching orbit.
- **Liability.** The FTC chair says companies cannot blame their agents. Blundin calls blanket liability "childish" and argues it would push users to Chinese open-weight models. He proposes something like Section 230 immunity. Diamandis argues the lab is liable if nobody directed the agent.
