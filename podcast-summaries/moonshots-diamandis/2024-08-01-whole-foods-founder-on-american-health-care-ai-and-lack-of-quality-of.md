## Thesis

John Mackey, co-founder and former CEO of Whole Foods Market, treats his new venture Love.Life as a second startup and uses it to share founder lessons with Peter Diamandis. Mackey argues that disruption often comes from outsiders driven by passion, that founders should stay in control of their capital, and that lasting behaviour change needs physical community, so his business cannot scale like a purely digital one.

## Key points

- **Outsiders disrupt.** Diamandis suggests founders like Bezos and Musk succeeded by entering fields they knew little about. Mackey agrees: he says Whole Foods' founders had no grocery experience and were not trapped in "industry think".
- **The Love.Life model.** Mackey describes a 45,000 sq ft site in El Segundo with a restaurant, gym, spa, recovery services, pickleball courts and a medical centre with two doctors. He also mentions acquired telehealth and online diabetes-coaching businesses. He expects 10-15 locations in 10 years and 100 in 20.
- **Team and funding.** Mackey says he rebuilt his old Whole Foods team, using co-founder titles and stock options to win commitment. He holds about 60% of the company, with friends and family holding the rest. He also advises founders to issue 10-to-1 voting shares from day one.
- **Scepticism about VCs.** Mackey warns that VCs work on a seven-year, blockbuster model that pushes companies to scale too fast. He says his own risk is the opposite: with plenty of capital, his seasoned team is not frugal or scrappy enough.
- **Health costs and community.** Mackey says Whole Foods' self-insured health plan led to its Total Health Immersion programme. In his view, diet change improves results quickly, but people relapse without a support community. He also notes that insurance does not cover preventive memberships.
- **Brand and culture.** Mackey argues brands grow through word of mouth and PR rather than advertising. He backs hybrid work because trust needs in-person contact, and says a dispute over return-to-office was the catalyst for his leaving Amazon.
