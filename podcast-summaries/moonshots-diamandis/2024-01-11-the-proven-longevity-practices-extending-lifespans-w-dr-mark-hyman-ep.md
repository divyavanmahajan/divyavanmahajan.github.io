## Thesis

Functional medicine physician Mark Hyman argues that ageing has been neglected as a process in its own right and that it should be treated like other manageable conditions. He believes existing lifestyle measures could take most people into their 90s, while longer gains depend on regenerative technologies that are not yet proven. Peter Diamandis frames the conversation around the investment, diagnostics and therapy ventures he sees building up around longevity.

## Key points

- **Limits of lifespan.** Hyman thinks living to 120-150 is a much harder problem that may need regenerative therapies; he calls epigenetic reprogramming with Yamanaka factors promising but still unproven in humans.
- **Money and tools.** Hyman says billions in private capital, cheaper tools and AI are speeding up the field. Diamandis mentions his $101 million Healthspan XPRIZE and says his fund, Bold Capital, is now about two-thirds biotech and health tech and is looking for companies tackling sarcopenia.
- **The food system.** Hyman blames ultra-processed food (he counts over 600,000 products) for much of the rise in chronic disease. He cites a Kevin Hall trial in which participants ate 500 more calories a day on ultra-processed diets, and links the shift to 1970s farm subsidies.
- **Caution on GLP-1 drugs.** Hyman accepts that Ozempic helps some patients but is wary of wide use, pointing to muscle loss, weight regain after stopping and reported gastrointestinal risks.
- **Experimental therapies.** The two discuss plasma exchange, stem cells and natural killer cells. Diamandis says Fountain Life has installed therapeutic plasma exchange and is setting up an investigational protocol for placental-derived stem cells. Hyman calls for more clinical-trial funding.
- **Diagnostics at scale.** Hyman describes Function Health, which he co-founded: more than 110 biomarkers for under $500. He says 20,000 people have used it, and he reports high rates of undetected risk markers in that health-focused group.
