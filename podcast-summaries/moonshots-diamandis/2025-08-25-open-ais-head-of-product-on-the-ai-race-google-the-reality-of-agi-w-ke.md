## Thesis

Recorded at OpenAI's San Francisco headquarters shortly after the GPT-5 launch, Peter Diamandis and Dave Blundin talk with chief product officer Kevin Weil. Weil argues that OpenAI's strategy is iterative deployment: ship early, learn from users, and push capabilities from paid tiers down to free ones. He says compute is the binding constraint, that nobody fully knows what the next model will be good at, and that every pre-AI product will be reinvented.

## Key points

- **GPT-5 and feedback.** Weil describes GPT-5 as OpenAI's smartest and best coding model, with extra work on health questions. He says feedback from about 700 million users showed the personality was "a little bit wooden", which the team fixed quickly.
- **Compute is maxed out.** Weil says OpenAI uses the same settings as external users and is "completely maxed out", which is why it is part of Project Stargate (more than $500 billion of infrastructure). Blundin argues GPUs will not commoditise.
- **Free over paywall.** Weil says features such as deep research move from Pro to Plus to free, the reverse of most companies. He cites prices roughly 100x lower than at GPT-4's launch and a new discounted plan for India.
- **Advice to builders.** Weil tells startups to build where models can only just do the task, so the next model helps rather than obviates them. He predicts AI coding could grow developers from 30 million to billions.
- **AGI and benchmarks.** Weil says most benchmarks are saturated and OpenAI now tests economically valuable tasks such as financial models. He sees test-time compute as a second scaling axis and says OpenAI is using AI for its own chip design.
- **Army reserve role.** Weil describes joining the US Army Reserve as a lieutenant colonel alongside executives from Palantir and Meta to bring tech expertise closer to the DoD.
