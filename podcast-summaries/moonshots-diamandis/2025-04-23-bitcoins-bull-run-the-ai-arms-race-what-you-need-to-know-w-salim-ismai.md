## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail argue that the AI race has two separate leaderboards. Google's Gemini 2.5 leads on capability benchmarks, while OpenAI leads on revenue, which they credit to a first-mover "user interface moment". They also see signs of froth in AI venture funding, discuss the AI 2027 scenarios, and argue that Bitcoin is an asymmetric long-term bet.

## Key points

- **China's tech focus.** Diamandis compares robot and AI ads at Beijing airport with fashion ads at JFK. Ismail says China's population decline leaves it little choice but to automate.
- **Model IQ and benchmarks.** Diamandis shows a chart that puts OpenAI's o3 at an IQ of about 133 and Gemini 2.5 at about 127. On most metrics, including Humanity's Last Exam, Gemini 2.5 leads. Ismail argues that IQ is only one dimension of intelligence.
- **OpenAI's revenue lead.** Diamandis cites about $2.5 billion in OpenAI revenue at the end of 2024, roughly five times Gemini's. He compares ChatGPT to Mosaic as a usability breakthrough. Ismail says incumbents struggle to change interfaces, using a Yahoo Mail send-button story as an example.
- **Values and alignment.** Discussing Anthropic's study of Claude's values (practical, epistemic, social, protective, personal), Ismail suggests training AIs on the US Constitution and UN human-rights documents. Diamandis asks which documents China or Russia would use.
- **Funding froth.** Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab raised $2 billion at a $10 billion seed valuation. Ismail warns that companies that raise heavily at the peak of a boom tend to get bloated. Diamandis says he looks for early revenue.
- **AI 2027 and Bitcoin.** Diamandis summarises the paper's "race" scenario, which ends in an AI-made bioweapon, and its "slowdown" scenario, which ends in abundance. Ismail frames the choice as Star Trek versus Mad Max. With Bitcoin back above $90,000, both hosts say they are long-term holders.
