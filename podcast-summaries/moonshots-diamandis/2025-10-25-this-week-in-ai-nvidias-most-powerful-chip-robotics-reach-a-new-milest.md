## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Emad Mostaque and Eric Pulier recorded this week's news roundup at XPRIZE Visioneering 2025 in Malibu. Their common argument is that hardware, robotics and quantum computing are moving faster than institutions can adapt. Mostaque expects human cognitive labour to lose its economic value within about three years. Diamandis says the answer is a positive vision of the future, and argues that popular media currently fails to offer one.

## Key points

- **AGI definitions.** Diamandis cites Aleksander Madry's view that AGI could arrive by 2026. Mostaque contrasts this with Andrej Karpathy's 10-year estimate and notes that 2029, Ray Kurzweil's date, falls between the two. Ismail says the term has no agreed definition or test.
- **Chips and supply chains.** On Nvidia's first US-made Blackwell wafer, Pulier calls the announcement largely political posturing, since advanced packaging still happens in Taiwan. Mostaque says the new GB-class systems allow training on 500,000 to a million chips instead of 10,000 to 30,000, and expects 100x-200x algorithmic efficiency gains.
- **Persuasion and companionship.** The hosts discuss AI sycophancy and a survey that says one in five high schoolers have had a romantic relationship with an AI. Mostaque says models already score around the 99th percentile on persuasion tests.
- **Robots.** The panel covers Figure 03's real-time speech, Unitree (H1 at about $20,000, H2 at $90,000, R1 at $6,000, with a reported IPO valuation of $7 billion) and Amazon's robot fleet, which has grown 66-fold in a decade. Ismail remains sceptical that home robots can handle the many edge cases.
- **Work and medicine.** Diamandis cites a Stanford/Harvard study: doctors alone were 74% accurate in diagnosis, 76% with GPT-4, and about 92% when GPT-4 worked without them. Ismail points out that Sweden, South Korea and Germany combine high robot density with low unemployment.
- **Money and quantum.** Pulier argues that the dollar is being debased and that central banks are buying gold. Ismail cites Jeff Booth's claim that each $1 of global GDP growth has come with $4 of new debt. The panel also covers Google's verifiable quantum advantage: an algorithm 13,000 times faster than the Frontier supercomputer.
