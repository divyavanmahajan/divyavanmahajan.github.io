## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross, Salim Ismail and Blitzy CEO Brian Elliott (a show sponsor) read the week's news as a single story: AI demand outruns compute supply, and that scarcity shapes everything from Google's earnings to OpenAI's strategy and government oversight. Their main split is over who should gatekeep frontier models, governments or the labs themselves.

## Key points

- **Google's quarter.** Peter cites Alphabet revenue of $109.9 billion, up 22%, and Google Cloud at $20 billion, up 63%. Dave argues search volume has been flat since 2017 and AI-driven ad targeting explains the revenue growth.
- **Pre-release vetting.** On a proposed White House review of models, Wissner-Gross links the shift to Anthropic's Mythos and its cyber security capabilities. Elliott says government must preview models but not hold veto rights. Wissner-Gross worries more about labs self-censoring.
- **Compute is the new normal.** After a Demis Hassabis clip saying even Google lacks spare compute, Dave argues most companies wrongly assume surplus compute and will struggle in two to three years. Wissner-Gross expects compute to go to the tokens with the highest dollar value.
- **OpenAI's position.** The hosts discuss OpenAI ending Azure exclusivity, its reported missed targets and a CFO suggestion of a 2027 IPO. Wissner-Gross calls the consumer-first bet an error and wonders whether the leaks are meant to lower expectations. Elliott claims GPT-5.5 matches Mythos.
- **Private equity as channel.** On OpenAI's $10 billion venture with TPG, Brookfield and Advent and Anthropic's $1.5 billion venture with Blackstone, Goldman Sachs and Hellman, Ismail says AI will enter firms top-down through owners. Wissner-Gross notes a skeptic could see circular sales.
- **Where data centers go.** The hosts cover an ocean data center startup backed by Peter Thiel, StarCloud's orbital plans and a claim that 67% of planned US data centers are rural. Elliott says local concern centres on electricity bills.
