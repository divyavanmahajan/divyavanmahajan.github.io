## Thesis

Gaby Toledano, who has led people functions at Tesla, Electronic Arts, Microsoft and Oracle, argues that founders underestimate how hard the people side of a company is and bring in a senior head of people far too late. She says that person should be a consigliere to the CEO, on a par with the CFO and general counsel. Peter Diamandis steers the conversation through culture, hiring, firing, layoffs, Glassdoor and remote work.

## Key points

- **Culture starts with stories.** Toledano says culture comes from the mission and from founder behaviour. Early storytelling, used in recruiting and onboarding, sets it, and so do the myths, such as Elon Musk sleeping on the factory floor.
- **Hiring at Tesla.** At 40,000 to 50,000 employees, Toledano says she met Musk weekly about director-and-above candidates found by hundreds of in-house recruiters. Her own hiring took two days. She prefers in-house recruiting, says the CEO rather than a recruiter should make the offer, and recommends behavioural questions such as asking what a named former boss would say about the candidate.
- **Interview mechanics.** Toledano favours 30-minute one-on-one interviews after thorough screening, with the interviewers meeting in person afterwards. She warns that recruiting through referrals reduces diversity. Diamandis raises Amazon-style bar raisers and Laszlo Bock's finding at Google that the first 30 seconds are highly predictive.
- **Firing and layoffs.** Toledano says firing should be quick when the problem is clear, but done kindly. She sees performance improvement plans as legal documentation and prefers real-time feedback. She says layoffs should go through managers, never email, and cost cuts should not be presented as performance cuts.
- **Pay fairness and legal risk.** Toledano says most lawsuits start with complaints that nobody heard. She would fix pay gaps quickly and use sign-on bonuses rather than salary outliers. She also warns that misclassifying hourly jobs can lead to class actions during crunch periods.
- **Remote work.** Toledano prefers in-person work for loyalty and for younger employees, but expects hybrid work to last. Diamandis points to the access to global talent that remote hiring gives.
