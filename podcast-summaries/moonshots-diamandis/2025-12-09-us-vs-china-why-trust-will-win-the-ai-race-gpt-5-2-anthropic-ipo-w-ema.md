## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, his Moonshot Mates and guest Emad Mostaque describe AI as a continuous, multi-front race in which frontier labs leapfrog each other almost weekly and demand for compute keeps outrunning falling token prices. They argue that US-China decoupling will produce two competing tech stacks, and the hosts suggest that trust, more than raw capability, may decide which stack other nations adopt.

## Key points

- **NeurIPS and the research gap.** Alexander Wissner-Gross reports more than 29,000 registrants and 146 accepted papers from Alibaba. His read is that US frontier labs have "gone dark" while Chinese labs keep publishing and releasing open-weight models, a classic "commoditize your complement" strategy.
- **Leapfrogging and code red.** The hosts discuss a leaked, unverified chart for GPT-5.2 and OpenAI's code red. Dave Blundin argues the cost of intelligence will not approach zero, because fleets of parallel agents and many iterations per task push total demand the other way.
- **AI IPOs.** On reports that Anthropic could IPO in 2026 at a valuation near $300 billion, Wissner-Gross says public listings would let retail investors share in the gains of superintelligence. Salim Ismail notes that this is only about 10x projected revenue, compared with Palantir at 111x.
- **China's chip push.** Mostaque says Cambricon plans to triple output to 500,000 accelerators in 2026 and that a Moore Threads IPO was 4,000 times oversubscribed. Wissner-Gross expects a "Cambrian explosion" of Chinese architectures after decoupling.
- **Europe and kids' capital.** Blundin says the EU's AI gigafactory plan comes too late. On Michael and Susan Dell's $6.25 billion Invest America pledge, Wissner-Gross calls it the start of "universal basic equity".
- **Space and robots.** The hosts discuss a possible SpaceX IPO, orbital data centers (Wissner-Gross calls it a race to build Dyson swarms) and a planned US robotics executive order. One participant questions whether humanoids with very high joint torque should walk public streets.
