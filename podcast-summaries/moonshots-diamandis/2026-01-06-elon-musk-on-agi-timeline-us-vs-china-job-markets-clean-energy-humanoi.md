## Thesis

At the Austin Gigafactory in December 2025, Peter Diamandis presses Elon Musk for reasons to be optimistic. Musk says AI and robots will eventually saturate human demand for goods and services. His worry is the next three to seven years, which he expects to bring radical change, social unrest and great prosperity all at once. He sees electricity, not chips, as the near-term limit on AI.

## Key points

- **Solar and batteries.** Musk calls Earth-based fusion pointless when the sun is available. He credits China with about 1,500 GW a year of solar production capacity. He argues that batteries alone could double US energy throughput, since peak demand is about 1.1 TW while average use is about 0.5 TW.
- **Compute in orbit.** Musk sees a path to 100 GW a year of solar-powered AI satellites, built on a million tons a year to orbit with a fully reusable Starship at roughly $1 million of propellant per flight. Later, satellites could be built on the Moon.
- **Jobs and universal high income.** Musk estimates AI could already do half or more of white-collar jobs, held back mainly by inertia. He expects prices to fall as output grows faster than the money supply, and says saving for retirement will stop mattering.
- **AGI timeline.** Musk predicts AGI in 2026 and AI smarter than all humans combined by 2030. He sees two orders of magnitude of algorithmic gains still available. His safety formula is truth, curiosity and beauty.
- **Power is the bottleneck.** Musk says Colossus 2 in Memphis will be the first gigawatt training cluster, running on gas turbines with Megapacks to smooth out power swings. He thinks TSMC is right to worry that chips will outpace the power available to run them. He also warns of a "chip wall" without a Tesla fab, and expects China to lead in AI compute.
- **Robots and medicine.** Musk claims Optimus will outperform the best human surgeons within about three years (four to five with a margin). He points to three exponentials multiplying each other: AI software, AI chips and robot dexterity.
