## Thesis

In a bonus roundtable, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross react to a week with Grok 4.5, GPT-5.6 and Meta's Muse Spark. Wissner-Gross argues the frontier is no longer a two-lab duopoly. The hosts disagree on whether consumer distribution or access to compute will matter most as models converge and leapfrog each other.

## Key points

- **Four labs at the frontier.** Citing the Artificial Analysis cost-versus-score chart, Wissner-Gross says Meta and SpaceX AI have joined Anthropic and OpenAI, with Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro reportedly delayed. He notes OpenAI said it used its top GPT-5.6 model to post-train a smaller one.
- **Distribution versus compute.** Diamandis argues distribution is the moat, pointing to Meta's roughly 3.5 billion daily users. Blundin says he would rather own chip fabs or launch capacity, since demand outruns supply. Wissner-Gross sees distribution mainly as a way to justify data-centre capex to capital markets.
- **Product quality and voice.** Wissner-Gross calls OpenAI's new ChatGPT Work app a copy of Claude's mode toggles. The hosts praise GPT Live's full-duplex voice and real-time translation, and one Mate ties it to Jony Ive's hardware work.
- **Apple sues OpenAI.** Diamandis summarises Apple's 41-page trade-secret complaint naming hardware chief Tang Tan. Ismail reads it as Apple stalling. Blundin notes that suits like this are hard to win in Northern California.
- **Space and robots.** The hosts discuss SpaceX's filing for 100,000 V3 Starlink satellites, China's first orbital booster landing and a Falcon 9 booster flying its 36th mission. They also cover 1X's 25-degree-of-freedom tendon-driven Neo hand, with a stated goal of 10,000 units in 2026.
- **Rules and synthetic actors.** The Mates see Illinois' SB 315 as a low bar favourable to the labs. Ismail calls the EU driver eye-tracking mandate legislation theatre. On the AI actor Tilly Norwood, one Mate suggests offering her SAG membership.
