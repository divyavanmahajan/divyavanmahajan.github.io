## Thesis

Peter Diamandis opens with a chart showing that experts keep underestimating exponential growth, and the Mates apply that lens to robots, fusion and AI. They argue that falling hardware costs, a first licensed fusion plant and government control over frontier models all point to a faster, more regulated transition. Starcloud CEO Philip Johnston then argues that compute will gradually move into orbit.

## Key points

- **Robots at commodity prices.** Diamandis cites Morgan Stanley raising its forecast to 50,000 Chinese robots, with 500,000 by 2030, and Unitree's R1 selling for $4,900. Wissner-Gross argues that robots assembling robots would make physical labour "too cheap to meter". Blundin sees hardware becoming the long-term investment theme.
- **Fusion arrives.** Helion has cleared Washington state approvals for its Orion plant, which is meant to supply Microsoft with 50 MW from 2028. Wissner-Gross says steady gains in the fusion "triple product" made this predictable decades ago.
- **Fable 5 and Sonnet 5.** Anthropic's Fable 5 has been offline for 15 days after the US government pulled it. Wissner-Gross suggests the pause cost Chinese labs about a month of distillation, and Ismail tells founders not to depend on a single model. Blundin calls Sonnet 5 a stopgap with "mediocre capability at a high price point". Wissner-Gross finds its cost-performance against Opus 4.8 puzzling.
- **Starcloud's roadmap.** Johnston says Starcloud 1 trained nanoGPT and ran Gemma on an H100 in orbit. Starcloud 2 launches in January, and the planned Starcloud 3 is a 200 kW, three-ton craft, about 50 of which fit on one Starship. He puts break-even launch cost at around $500 per kilo and says the company's radiator has a tenth of the ISS radiator's mass per watt.
- **Answering the energy objection.** Responding to Masayoshi Son, Johnston argues that energy plus infrastructure is at least 30% of cost, and that terrestrial permitting takes 5 to 10 years. Launch capacity is his main bottleneck.
- **Other stories.** Orlando police drones, Switzerland lifting its nuclear ban, the Vesuvius Challenge, monthly Grok pre-training runs and Rocket Lab buying Iridium.
