## Thesis

Josh Tetrick, CEO of GOOD Meat (part of Eat Just), argues that meat can be made from cells in a production facility instead of by slaughtering animals, and that the main remaining barrier is the economics of scale, not the science. Peter Diamandis frames it as a moonshot that digitizes food production, and he presses Tetrick on cost, regulation and storytelling as a founder skill.

## Key points

- **The case against conventional meat.** Tetrick says about a third of the planet's land goes to feeding farm animals. He also claims livestock emit more greenhouse gas than all transport combined, and that dense farming encourages zoonotic disease.
- **How it works.** According to Tetrick, cells from a cell bank are given a feed of vitamins, minerals and amino acids and grow in a stainless steel bioreactor for about three weeks. The result is extruded into nuggets, strips or patties.
- **Path to cost parity.** Tetrick puts conventional meat at about $4 per pound. He sees three levers: moving from roughly 3,500-litre vessels to more than 100,000 litres, cutting feed from above $1 to about 20 cents a litre, and raising cell density. He expects parity within 10 years.
- **Regulation.** He describes a roughly two-and-a-half-year review by the Singapore Food Authority, approved in 2020. In the US, the FDA covers the process up to the vessel and the USDA covers everything after it.
- **Scale today.** Tetrick says the company is still tiny: two pounds of chicken went to José Andrés's restaurant, and the facility tops out at 100,000 pounds a year. He thinks large incumbents such as Tyson will adopt the technology once it is proven, and names ADM as a feed partner and investor.
- **Storytelling.** Both men argue that concrete personal stories persuade better than big numbers. Tetrick names conference rooms after customer stories, such as a Singapore hawker who put cultivated chicken curry rice on his menu.
