## Thesis

Ben Lamm, CEO of Colossal and co-founder with George Church, argues that de-extinction is a systems problem, not just a biology problem. He says a software entrepreneur without a science background can lead it with the right team and investors. Peter Diamandis, an advisor whose fund Bold Capital is an investor, presents Colossal as a case study of how much one CEO can change the outcome of a moonshot.

## Key points

- **From $100,000 to $225 million.** Lamm says Church's mammoth work had about $100,000 over roughly a decade. Colossal, started in 2021, has since raised $225 million at a post-money valuation of around $1.5 billion. It reports 112 staff, about 90% of them scientists.
- **Editing, not cloning.** Lamm explains that Colossal is not cloning. It builds a mammoth reference genome from 54 genomes, compares it with the Asian elephant (99.6% similar), and edits in the genes behind key traits. He says roughly 20 of about 60-65 target genes have been edited and that the first calves are targeted for early 2028.
- **Three public species.** Colossal has announced the mammoth, the thylacine (13.5-day gestation, well-preserved genome) and the dodo. Lamm says primordial germ cell culture is the rate-limiting step for the dodo, and he rules out dinosaurs because no usable DNA survives.
- **Business model.** Lamm describes two revenue lines. The first is option value from technology spin-outs such as Form Bio, which he says raised $30 million and has a Google partnership. The second is education and partnerships, including a docuseries and large government conservation contracts.
- **Artificial wombs.** Lamm says a team of about 25 people is working on ex utero development, which he considers the bigger long-term moonshot.
- **AI and ethics.** Lamm says Colossal uses ChatGPT and other LLMs for literature review, and internal models to pick editing tools with fewer off-target effects. On ethics, he points to bioethicist Alta Charo joining the team and to working with conservation groups.
