## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin, Emad Mostaque and Brian Elliott argue that AI spending is backed by real revenue and is not a repeat of the dot-com bubble. Their recurring theme is "abundance everywhere except compute": demand for chips and power is outrunning supply. Mostaque goes further, predicting that humans will soon add negative value in cognitive work.

## Key points

- **College value is falling.** Diamandis cites polling showing the share of Americans who rate college "very important" dropped from 75% in 2010 to 35%. Elliott says a degree has mostly been a credential, and Mostaque suggests university endowments should fund GPU clusters.
- **Compute scarcity.** The hosts discuss Nvidia's planned $100 billion investment in OpenAI for 10 gigawatts of compute. Blundin points out that this equals half of annual US venture funding. He advises founders to reserve compute early and use distillation to cut costs.
- **Data center race.** The panel covers xAI's Colossus 2, Elon Musk's claim that xAI will be first to 10 gigawatts and beyond, and Mark Zuckerberg's pledge of $600 billion for US data centers by 2028. Mostaque argues Google holds an edge because of its TPUs and full-stack integration.
- **Not a bubble.** Blundin compares Cisco in 2000, when its price outran its earnings, with Nvidia, whose price moves with forward earnings. Mostaque says every GPU OpenAI uses will be booked out. Elliott argues that the lag between capex and earnings is now almost zero.
- **Work and jobs.** The hosts are sceptical of CEO predictions of a three-day week. Elliott says firms will pick between full-time humans and no humans at all. Blundin suspects executives are now downplaying job losses, and Diamandis cites a study where GPT-4 alone out-diagnosed physicians.
- **Domain expertise and Blitzy.** Elliott argues mid-career experts can build enterprise systems from their process knowledge. He positions Blitzy as bringing vibe-coding speed to enterprise-scale software.
