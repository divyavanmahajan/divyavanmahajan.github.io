## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Mates discuss Jensen Huang's return to NVIDIA after 11 days out, which they say added about $1.09 trillion in market value. Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue the outcome is good for the AI ecosystem but sets a worrying precedent of government influence over company leadership. The rest of the episode reads recent AI news as evidence that progress is accelerating.

## Key points

- **Reinstatement and precedent.** Salim Ismail offers a self-described guess that the board demanded contracts, a succession plan and an end to nepotism. Wissner-Gross calls the reported presidential intervention a "quasi nationalization"; Blundin expects congressional scrutiny and sees NVIDIA as a counterweight to Google's TPUs.
- **OpenAI's perplexity chart.** Diamandis presents a reported OpenAI publication of pre-training log perplexity with a call for common limits. Wissner-Gross reads it as a signal that OpenAI has "relearned" pre-training, and warns that public disclosure could let labs pace each other as a safety cartel; Ismail sees a bid for relevance.
- **Data centres.** The hosts cover a threatened tax on overseas data centres and a Senate permitting bill with a 20-megawatt cost threshold. Ismail doubts the tax will work; Wissner-Gross predicts many sites just under 20 megawatts.
- **Math "cooked".** Wissner-Gross treats a claimed Lean-verified proof of the generalized Riemann hypothesis, an unforced Navier-Stokes result and rumours on P versus NP as signs that long research bets are now risky. Ismail predicts P equals NP.
- **GTA VI.** Diamandis cites Take-Two's reported $3.79 billion first day; Wissner-Gross calls it the last great hand-built game, and Diamandis previews a gaming XPRIZE.
- **Mirror test.** A Nuance Labs avatar recognising itself prompts Wissner-Gross to talk of AI personhood; Ismail calls it a psychological, not consciousness, threshold.
