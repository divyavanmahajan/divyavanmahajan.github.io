## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross and guest Emad Mostaque discuss a week in which Google shipped strong models yet Alphabet had its worst stock day on record. The hosts argue that the sell-off reflects investor nerves about debt-funded AI capex and slowing search ads, not a failing technology, and that the binding constraints are chips, memory and, they predict, CPUs.

## Key points

- **Google's paradox.** Diamandis cites Gemini 4 Carbon's cyber benchmark results and a billion Gemini app users against a 13% share drop and $9 billion of free cash flow burn. Wissner-Gross cautions that Carbon is not generally available and may simply sit on the existing cost-capability frontier.
- **Search ads may peak.** Mostaque predicts search advertising will top out as an industry in Q1 as agents do the buying. Wissner-Gross says Alphabet is already turning into a hyperscaler cloud company.
- **Conviction capex.** Blundin argues the big CEOs will not slow spending, so markets may get worse before they recover, with RAM shortages hitting Apple and PC sales. Mostaque expects CPUs to be the next bottleneck as agents multiply. Wissner-Gross suggests stronger models could reduce the need for agent swarms.
- **Self-replicating data centres.** Reacting to Sam Altman's interview with John Collison, Wissner-Gross calls robots building data centres the physical form of recursive self-improvement and suggests they may end up on the moon.
- **Robots and robotaxis.** The Mates question who will buy a million Optimus robots a year. Ismail expects industrial demand first. They compare per-mile costs from an ARK chart for Cybercab and Waymo, and criticise the Cambridge Teamsters' bid to keep driverless taxis out.
- **Degrees in question.** Citing falling computer science enrolment and MBA applications, Ismail says business schools must become entrepreneurship institutes. Wissner-Gross points students towards skills that touch the physical world.
