## Thesis

Jack Hidary, CEO of SandboxAQ, tells Peter Diamandis that AI and quantum physics do the same basic thing: they compress a lot of data about the world into a model that can make predictions. Language models are limited to recombining their training text. Hidary argues that most of the world, including drugs, materials and batteries, is described by numbers. He says "large quantitative models", trained on data generated from quantum equations, can explore that space today on GPUs without waiting for quantum computers.

## Key points

- **Background.** Diamandis notes that Hidary founded Alphabet's quantum group in 2016 and spun out SandboxAQ in March 2022, with Eric Schmidt as chairman and a $500 million round.
- **Limits of LLMs.** Hidary says there is "no equation" for English, so language models are probabilistic engines that mix and match their corpus. Drug candidates or new battery chemistries are outside that data set.
- **Equations as data generators.** SandboxAQ runs the quantum equations to generate training data and limits the calculation to the valence electrons at the molecule's "business end", which keeps the problem tractable on GPUs. Hidary dates the first breakthrough to three years earlier.
- **Drug economics.** Hidary cites 8-10 years of preclinical work, 90% clinical failure and roughly $3 billion per drug. He describes a possible future of one tenth the cost, half the time and 50-60% trial success, which would make more targeted drugs viable.
- **Software, not biotech.** He frames SandboxAQ as a horizontal platform, comparing it to Oracle, that serves pharma, Dow Chemical, battery developers and US Army lightweight-materials work. Its code is designed to run later on a hybrid CPU-GPU-QPU cloud.
- **Quantum hardware timeline.** Hidary estimates that breaking RSA needs about 5,000 logical qubits, or 5 million physical qubits at a 1,000:1 ratio, compared with a few hundred today. He expects modular blocks of 1,000-5,000 physical qubits by 2029 and treats 2031-2032 as the critical period. He also calls neutral atoms the fastest-scaling modality.
