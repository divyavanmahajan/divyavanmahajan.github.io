## Thesis

Recorded on stage at Abundance360 2024, inventor Dean Kamen tells Peter Diamandis that the science of cells, tissues and organs has outrun the ability to manufacture them. He describes his approach as bringing engineering discipline, not biology expertise, to the problem. He applies the same logic to drug delivery and to education, where he says the US faces a culture problem rather than an education problem.

## Key points

- **iBot and the FDA.** Kamen recounts how the FDA first refused to review his stair-climbing wheelchair. He says that after more than 10 million field hours without a system failure it moved from class III to class II, and that CMS added coverage in January after 16 years.
- **ARMI.** The Advanced Regenerative Manufacturing Institute, funded by the Department of Defense, started with $80 million. Kamen says it has just received a further $100 million grant, has 200 member organisations, and plans to use the recipient's own iPSCs so each organ carries the patient's DNA.
- **Regulation first.** Kamen hired Richard McFarland, a former FDA policy director at CBER, as ARMI's first employee, plus standards staff from NIST, because he expected approval to be the biggest uncertainty.
- **Products in the pipeline.** Kamen mentions a retinal implant for macular degeneration, bioreactors he says make billions of islet cells per day for JDRF-funded researchers, pediatric-size hearts, and an organ life-support "pod" that received FDA breakthrough status. The Manchester mill yard has been branded "Regen Valley".
- **Daisy patch.** A roughly 20-part device with semiconductor-made microneedles delivers vaccine into the skin. Kamen claims it needs about a tenth of the dose and no trained clinician, and says several pharma companies are planning clinical trials.
- **FIRST and FIRST Global.** Kamen says FIRST has grown from 23 teams to events in 182 cities, and that FIRST Global drew 191 teams. He describes a sub-$100 robot kit that New Hampshire is putting in every classroom.
