## Thesis

Salim Ismail says that when he and his team studied 200 of the fastest-growing unicorns, the one trait they all shared was a massive transformative purpose (MTP). Peter Diamandis frames the MTP as the canvas and emotional fuel for a founder's work. Moonshots are the concrete, ten-year, 10x objectives painted on that canvas. Both argue that no one should start a company without one.

## Key points

- **MTP as filter.** Diamandis paraphrases Martine Rothblatt: the most successful entrepreneurs say no to almost everything, and the MTP decides what to refuse. Ismail adds that a clear MTP lets junior managers make decisions on their own and helps with hiring and retention of purpose-driven younger staff.
- **Canvas and moonshots.** Diamandis's first MTP, opening up space privately, led to the International Space University and the Ansari XPRIZE. He now states his MTP as inspiring entrepreneurs to create an abundant future. Ismail's is "transform civilization", shaped by his father's remark that the world has been materialized but not civilized.
- **Lost purpose.** Ismail cites BlackBerry as lacking an MTP and Kodak as forgetting George Eastman's purpose of preserving memories, which is why it did not exploit its own digital camera.
- **Focus.** Diamandis tells how Elon Musk "burned the ships" twice: he dropped a working Falcon 1 for Falcon 9, and plans to stop Falcon 9 production once Starship works.
- **Business value.** Ismail says Unilever had every brand adopt a purpose after hearing him speak, and that CMO Keith Weed later showed the five most profitable brands were the ones that adopted it most. He stresses that an MTP should be a declarative call to action, like Google's.
- **10x, not 10%.** Using Astro Teller's definition, Diamandis argues that aiming for 10x forces a clean-sheet rethink instead of a "smartness competition". Ismail describes a Tesla trip from Miami to Toronto where self-driving rose from 35% to 80% of the time within 18 months.
