## Thesis

Bill Barhydt, founder and CEO of the crypto wallet Abra, tells Peter Diamandis that Bitcoin's drop from about $68,000 to below $50,000 is short-term noise driven by market liquidity, not a break in its long-term exponential trend. He argues that Bitcoin's guaranteed scarcity makes it a store of value against an expanding dollar supply. He also expects US politics to soften towards crypto and sees DeFi merging with AI agents within a few years. Diamandis discloses that he holds his Bitcoin on Abra.

## Key points

- **The Japan carry trade.** Barhydt blames the sell-off on the unwinding of leveraged yen carry trades after the Bank of Japan raised rates by 25 basis points, and points to the 15-year log chart as the right way to look at price.
- **Election-year politics.** Barhydt describes speeches at the Nashville Bitcoin conference by RFK Jr., Vivek Ramaswamy and Donald Trump, and Senator Lummis's bill to add Bitcoin to US reserves. He estimates 50 to 70 million US crypto holders and argues the volume of SEC Wells notices against crypto firms shows the industry is being singled out; these are his views.
- **Allocation by age.** Barhydt suggests about 10% of wealth for older holders and a higher share for younger ones, and says he thinks of his net worth in Bitcoin rather than dollars. Both note this is not financial advice.
- **Borrowing instead of selling.** For retirement income, Barhydt describes "lifestyle loans": posting Bitcoin as collateral in DeFi contracts at a low loan-to-value ratio and receiving stablecoins, through an SEC-registered adviser so clients keep title. He expects Bitcoin yields of roughly 4 to 6% over the next cycle.
- **DeFi as the new banking stack.** He says DeFi is rebuilding banking services in open software with "no off switch" on Ethereum and Solana, running 24/7 rather than bank hours.
- **AI meets DAOs.** Barhydt predicts billions of DAOs acting as autonomous agents, such as a robotaxi that owns itself, pays in crypto and returns profits to token holders.
