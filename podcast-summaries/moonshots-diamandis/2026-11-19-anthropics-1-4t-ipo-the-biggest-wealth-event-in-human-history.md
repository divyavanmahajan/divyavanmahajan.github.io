## Thesis

In a live roundtable, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross treat Anthropic's IPO as the first public price for a frontier AI lab. Their main argument is that this new liquid wealth, held mostly in San Francisco, will be put back into AI startups and speed things up further. They also discuss the Nvidia leadership crisis, Starship's first ship catch, and the US move to restrict chips and Chinese AI models.

## Key points

- **The IPO numbers.** Diamandis cites 825 million shares at $70-78, about $64 billion raised at a $1.4 trillion valuation, and an average employee stake of $72 million. Blundin says the liquidity amounts to about a decade of the whole venture industry. Wissner-Gross says retail investors should have been able to buy in much earlier. Ismail notes the company is only about five years old.
- **Nvidia after Jensen Huang.** The Mates disagree on whether Huang returns. Ismail blames a missing succession plan. Wissner-Gross expects activist investors to step in. Blundin predicts AI will erode the CUDA moat.
- **Outages and legacy systems.** Diamandis lists the Outlook outage, an FAA ground stop, bank disruptions and the Optimizely script attack. Wissner-Gross sees no coordinated plot, just ageing infrastructure meeting AI-discovered vulnerabilities. Blundin warns that AI attacks now run with no human in the loop, while defence still depends on people.
- **Starship's tower catch.** Diamandis estimates fully reusable launches could cost $27-53 per kilogram. Wissner-Gross compares this to packet switching: missions made of many interchangeable flights.
- **Chips and China.** The Mates cover the Pax Silica declaration, licence rules for chips going to five gateway countries, and a proposal to make companies disclose when they use Chinese AI models. Diamandis says he would rather see a label than a ban. Blundin argues only hardware can be controlled, not software.
- **SpaceX AI build speed and seasteading.** Citing SemiAnalysis, Diamandis says SpaceX AI builds data centres two to three times faster than rivals. Wissner-Gross sees ocean data centres, connected by Starlink, as the use case that could finally make Palmer Luckey's Slab-style seasteads viable.
