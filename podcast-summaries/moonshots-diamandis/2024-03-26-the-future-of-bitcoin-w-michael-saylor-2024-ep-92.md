## Thesis

Recorded live at the 2024 Abundance360 Summit, Peter Diamandis talks with his MIT classmate Michael Saylor, executive chairman of MicroStrategy. Saylor argues that Bitcoin should be understood as digital property and a store of value, not a currency. In his view, cash and sovereign debt lose value to inflation, so companies, charities and families should hold Bitcoin as their treasury asset.

## Key points

- **The MicroStrategy decision.** Saylor says COVID left the company with $500 million earning 0% interest and facing either a fast death or a slow death. He describes educating the board with videos and one-on-one meetings, then a Dutch auction tender at $140 a share alongside the first $250 million Bitcoin purchase in August 2020.
- **Property, not currency.** Saylor argues that most of the world's wealth wants a store of value, while only a small share wants a medium of exchange. Calling Bitcoin property, he says, makes most common objections irrelevant. He compares it with buildings, sports teams and art, which he says can be taxed or seized more easily.
- **Accounting as the barrier.** He names indefinite intangible accounting as the main reason other public companies have not followed, saying it distorts both the balance sheet and the P&L.
- **Adoption timeline.** Saylor dates mainstream institutional adoption to the January 2024 spot ETF approvals and predicts a roughly ten-year "gold rush" to 2034, by when he says 99% of Bitcoin will have been mined. He expects the April halving, which cuts daily miner supply from about 900 coins, to be the most consequential so far.
- **Layers and AI.** He describes the base chain as a settlement network and Lightning as a layer two for millions of transactions. He argues AI agents cannot get credit cards and will need Bitcoin to transact.
- **Other crypto and custody.** Saylor declines to back other tokens, calling them full of regulatory and technical uncertainty. He says the right custody choice (ETF, institutional or self-custody) depends on a holder's age, skills and country.
