## Thesis

Recorded live at Moonshots Live, Anduril founder Palmer Luckey tells the Moonshot Mates that he is an AI optimist who worries far more about people misusing moderately capable AI than about AI itself. He argues that autonomy in weapons is not new, that defense will stay a field of highly specialized machines, and that founders should expect to delegate the technical work they love.

## Key points

- **Wildfire XPRIZE and a new prize.** Anduril won $2.2 million in the wildfire XPRIZE; Luckey says its autonomous firefighting system dates to 2017-2019 and stresses rejecting false fires and matching assets to fires. He teases a $10 million prize he is funding on communicating with wild animals.
- **Autonomy moves forward.** Answering Dave Blundin, Luckey says weapons need on-board autonomy mainly because central command links can be jammed, hacked or targeted, not because of latency. He cites the Aegis, CIWS and SeaRAM systems and Vietnam-era radar-seeking missiles, and argues traps and mines made autonomous weapons ancient.
- **No LLMs in the missile.** Luckey says Anduril has built its own models since 2017, and that foundation models are non-deterministic and non-auditable. Defense needs models for raw radar and sonar data.
- **No humanoids.** Asked by Alexander Wissner-Gross about convergence, Luckey says defense rewards specialization and overwhelming edges. Anduril skips humanoids, small attack quadcopters and space launch because others do them well; he would rather use humanoids to operate legacy systems such as radar sites.
- **Selling to the Pentagon.** Responding to Salim Ismail, he says about 80% of major defense programs went to five companies, and Anduril wins by funding its own products and delivering on schedule.
- **VR and founders.** Luckey praises Meta's new VR glasses for off-boarding compute and batteries, and says AI changes little of a founder's job: legal, interpersonal and final decisions stay with people.
