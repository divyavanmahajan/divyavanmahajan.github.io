## Thesis

Recorded the day after OpenAI released GPT-5.2, this roundtable argues that frontier capability is now well ahead of how companies use it. Alexander Wissner-Gross reads the GDPval result as evidence that knowledge work can be automated. Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail say most large companies are stuck, and Ismail predicts a historic corporate collapse in 2026.

## Key points

- **Three knobs for a rushed release.** Wissner-Gross says a lab under "code red" pressure can only add compute, loosen safety or post-train on chosen benchmarks. He notes ARC-AGI-2 rising from 17.6% to 52.9%, GDPval from 38.8% to 70.9%, and a claimed 390x efficiency gain on ARC-AGI. On FrontierMath Tier 4, he counts Gemini 3 Pro (about 19%) beating GPT-5.2 (14.6%) as a win for Google.
- **Why adoption stalls.** Blundin, who chairs about a dozen companies, says firms fail on edge cases such as legacy Java code or customer emails locked in Outlook. His advice is to rebuild from scratch in Python.
- **Paralysed incumbents.** Ismail says that of roughly 20 large companies OpenExO works with, maybe three are doing half of the right thing. He recommends building a new AI-native stack at the edge. Blundin suggests partnering with startups rather than trying to hire scarce talent.
- **Jobs and reskilling.** Diamandis cites 1.1 million layoffs in 2025, the most since 2020. Blundin expects AI to be capable of replacing 80-90% of jobs, with a slow start and then panic by late 2026. Diamandis predicts demand for reskilling consultancies.
- **Open weights and sovereignty.** Blundin runs Kimi K2 but worries about vulnerable code from Chinese models. Wissner-Gross says Silicon Valley mostly fine-tunes Alibaba's Qwen, and that Mistral's Devstral 2 may be too late. He expects demand for sovereign AI stacks.
- **Infrastructure.** The hosts back the federal executive order preempting state AI laws. They praise Boom's 42 MW gas turbine for data centers, which has a reported $1.25 billion backlog. They also discuss China building nuclear power at a cited $2 per watt against $15 in the US, and Google's plan to test orbital data centers in 2027.
