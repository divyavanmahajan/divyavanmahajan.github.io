## Thesis

This audience Q&A, recorded at Peter Diamandis's Abundance360 summit in March 2023, pairs two approaches to health. Regenerative medicine researcher Doris Taylor explains how her team plans to build personalised replacement hearts from a patient's own cells. Functional medicine physician Mark Hyman argues that the bigger lever is prevention: in his view the food system causes most chronic disease and cost, and fixing it needs regulation and industry change.

## Key points

- **The "ghost heart".** Taylor explains that a pig heart is stripped of every cell, leaving an extracellular matrix scaffold of proteins and sugars that guides human stem cells as they grow onto it.
- **A manufacturing road map.** Taylor says the rate-limiting step for ten years was growing billions of heart cells, which iPS cells changed. Her team builds the vasculature first, seeds cells, then trains the heart electrically and against blood pressure. An audience member cites her estimate of about $300 million and five years to accelerate the work.
- **Telling the truth about costs.** Asked how a healthier society can stay profitable for those who profit from sick care, Taylor says the first step is being honest that transplants are not a cure-all and are costly, with an audience member citing her figure of $32,000 a month for drugs.
- **Food as a national emergency.** Hyman claims poor food drives over 80% of the $4.1 trillion US healthcare bill. He says he started a nonprofit to push "food is medicine" policy in Washington.
- **Personalised diagnostics.** Hyman argues that responses to diet vary widely between people, so care should rest on individual testing such as lipid fractionation rather than single markers.
- **Influence of big food.** Hyman says the food industry shapes professional bodies and research funding, and calls for changes to labelling, marketing to children and school lunches.
