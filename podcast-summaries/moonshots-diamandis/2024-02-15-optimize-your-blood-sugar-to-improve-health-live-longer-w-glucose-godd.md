## Thesis

Jessie Inchauspé, the biochemist and author known as the Glucose Goddess, says her goal is to free people from the pharma and food industries by teaching them how food affects them. She and Peter Diamandis argue that front-of-pack marketing hides how much sugar and starch processed food contains. Her answer is a set of low-cost habits ("hacks") rather than strict diets. Diamandis frames this as behaviour change that should sit at the level of public health.

## Key points

- **Origin in a tech pilot.** Inchauspé says her interest started at 23andMe, where the company asked staff to volunteer for an in-house pilot of continuous glucose monitors (CGMs). She noticed that on days when her glucose was steadier, her mood was better. She now wears one only about twice a year, saying the absolute readings are not very accurate.
- **Label versus ingredients.** The two read the backs of products on air: Frosted Flakes, Pop-Tarts, fruit roll-ups and dried apple chips. Inchauspé says the front label is unregulated marketing. She argues that cartoon characters on children's cereal should not be allowed.
- **Habits over diets.** Inchauspé's main hacks are a savoury breakfast, eating vegetables first and carbs last, a tablespoon of vinegar in water before a meal, about 10 minutes of movement after eating, and adding protein, fat or fiber to carbs. She cites studies saying that eating a meal in this order cuts the glucose spike by 75%.
- **Diamandis's experience.** Diamandis describes a 22-day no-sugar challenge run through a WhatsApp support group and says he stopped needing his blood-pressure medication. He targets about 150 g of protein a day and takes his Zoom calls on an exercise bike.
- **Calories are not enough.** Inchauspé argues that calorie counts hide what is in food. She criticises the diet industry's weight-loss teas and shakes.
- **Her own product.** Inchauspé presents Anti-Spike, a plant-based supplement built around white mulberry leaf. She claims it cuts the glucose and insulin spikes of a meal by 40% and frames it as an extra tool, not a magic pill.
