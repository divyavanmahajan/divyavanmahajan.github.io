## Thesis

In an audience Q&A at the 2025 Abundance Summit, Figure founder Brett Adcock argues that humanoid robots will scale through a single general-purpose hardware platform. He expects them to arrive first in workplaces, then in homes, outdoors and in space. Vinod Khosla of Khosla Ventures argues that technical capability will arrive faster than adoption at scale, which regulation and society will slow.

## Key points

- **One chassis.** Adcock says Figure won't customize its hardware for individual customers, because specialized variants hurt reliability and transfer learning. A single platform, he says, is how costs reach about $20,000. Khosla compares this to Henry Ford's single-colour Model T.
- **Adoption order.** Adcock says Figure, which is under three years old with a few hundred engineers, is focused on shipping 100,000 robots. He expects robots in workforces first, then homes and outdoors such as farms, all within about 10 years. Khosla sees farm robots demonstrated within five years but treats 1% penetration as the real milestone.
- **Fusion and siting.** Khosla names Commonwealth Fusion, Helion and Realta as credible efforts and sees a path to 5,000 fusion plants by 2050. He argues that permitting, not technology, is what slows fission, and that 50 MW reactors at substations could reduce the need for new transmission lines.
- **Jobs and safety.** Khosla expects job displacement and wider income disparity. He says each country will need policy choices to share the abundance. Adcock says Figure's charter rules out military work and that its robots can run their neural networks on board without any network connection.
- **Capital discipline.** Khosla says his analysis showed that companies raising less money made more absolute dollars, because they tested their assumptions in increments. He expects about 80% of AI investments to lose money but winners to more than make up for it. Adcock says 95% of deep-tech hardware ventures fail.
- **Science explosion.** Khosla predicts that AI scientists will multiply research capacity by 10-100x within five years. He expects unsupervised robot surgery to be more than 10 years away, mostly because of the FDA.
