## Thesis

At the 2024 Abundance360 Summit, Peter Diamandis puts Ray Kurzweil and Geoffrey Hinton side by side. They mostly agree on timelines and on AI's creative ability. They split on whether AI will merge with humanity, as Kurzweil expects, or might simply leave people behind, as Hinton fears. Hinton also argues that chatbots can already have subjective experience and that open-sourcing large models is reckless.

## Key points

- **Creativity.** Hinton points to AlphaGo's move 37 and AlphaZero's chess to argue that AI can be creative. He says LLMs pack far more knowledge into about a trillion connections than humans hold in roughly 100 trillion synapses, so they must find analogies people miss. His example is GPT-4 explaining how a compost heap is like an atom bomb.
- **Merge or diverge.** Kurzweil expects humans to merge with AI and says "large language model" is a misnomer. Hinton worries AI may have little incentive to merge, and says the outcome is very uncertain.
- **Subjective experience.** Hinton rejects the "inner theatre" view of the mind. In his thought experiment, a robot fooled by a prism says it had the subjective experience of the object being off to one side, using the term the way people do. Kurzweil says consciousness has no scientific definition but matters a great deal ethically.
- **Mortality.** Both say digital minds are immortal because their weights can be copied. Hinton thinks analogue brains cannot be faithfully recreated; Kurzweil disagrees.
- **Timelines.** Kurzweil says progress is two to three years ahead of his 1999 prediction. Hinton gives a 50% probability of superintelligence in 5 to 20 years and calls Elon Musk's AGI-in-2025 forecast ambitious.
- **Open source.** Hinton compares open-sourcing models to letting any graduate student build atomic bombs, and says fine-tuning an open model for harm is cheap. Kurzweil answers that the same models can be used to defend against those dangers.
