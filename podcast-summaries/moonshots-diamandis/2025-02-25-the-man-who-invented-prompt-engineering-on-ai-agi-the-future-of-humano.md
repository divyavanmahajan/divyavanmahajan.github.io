## Thesis

Richard Socher, founder and CEO of you.com, joins Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail for a weekly tech news roundtable. He argues that pure foundation-model companies will end up looking like telcos: they build costly infrastructure while others capture the value. In his view the winners will be model-agnostic trust layers and agents that automate knowledge work. He also expects cheaper intelligence to drive more total use, not less.

## Key points

- **Grok 3 and benchmarks.** Socher credits xAI's fast cluster build-out to Musk's mix of hardware and software skills. He says most people don't have PhD-level questions, so the frontier is moving to programming and science. He sees test-time compute making "speed" a dimension of intelligence.
- **AGI definitions.** Socher offers a pragmatic financial definition: automating about 80% of digitized work. He says physical manipulation is not required for superintelligence. He recently tweeted that with a couple of billion dollars he could build digital superintelligence in 18 to 24 months.
- **Open vs closed.** Socher and Ismail both expect open source to win in mass-market AI. Socher says you.com routes queries by intent across 40+ models, which lets customers avoid lock-in when a new model ships.
- **Data centers.** Ismail thinks the AI data-center build-out is overbuilt because training costs keep falling. Socher disagrees, citing Jevons paradox, but agrees there is a risk of buildings sitting empty without data.
- **AI for science and robots.** Socher says his Salesforce protein language model produced synthesized proteins 40% different from natural ones. Diamandis talks up Clone, 1X's Neo Gamma and Figure's Helix. Ismail questions why robots need human form.
- **Agents and the web.** Socher reports more than 50,000 custom agents on you.com. Examples include marketing campaigns, journalism research and VC data-room reviews. He expects agents that ignore ads to disrupt how the internet makes money.
