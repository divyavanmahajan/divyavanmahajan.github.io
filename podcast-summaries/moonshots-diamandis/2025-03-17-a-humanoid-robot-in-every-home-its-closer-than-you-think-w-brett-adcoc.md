## Thesis

Recorded on stage at Abundance360 2025, Brett Adcock, founder and CEO of Figure, tells Peter Diamandis that AGI needs a humanoid body to act in the physical world. He argues that general-purpose robots are a neural-net problem rather than a controls problem. In his view, Figure's in-house Helix model has made enough progress on generalization that the company is now limited by data more than by technology.

## Key points

- **Why humanoids.** Adcock says a single humanoid platform can do everything a human can without hardware changes, and that it suits transfer learning across tasks with one foundation model. He puts the addressable labour market at roughly half of global GDP.
- **Speed and vertical integration.** Figure went from a cold start to shipping its first robot in 31 months and designs a new hardware platform every 12 to 18 months. Adcock says there was no supply chain for humanoids, so Figure builds motors, batteries, sensors, firmware and AI itself.
- **Customers.** Robots work daily at BMW's Spartanburg plant, placing sheet metal on fixtures. Adcock says BMW's cycle time fell from four minutes to about 40 seconds, and that Helix let Figure deploy at a second customer, a large logistics firm, in under 30 days.
- **Helix.** Adcock describes Helix as a vision-language-action model trained on about 500 hours of data. In the demo, two robots put away groceries they had never seen before. He calls it possibly "the most important AI update for robotics in human history."
- **Figure 3 and cost.** Adcock says the Figure 3 design is finished, about 90% cheaper than Figure 2, and goes into production this year. He keeps his estimate of $20,000 to $30,000 per robot at volume, which Diamandis frames as about $300 a month on a lease.
- **Workforce first, home later.** Adcock calls the workforce the bigger and easier business and the home "the Wild West" for safety. He claims demand for 100,000 robots from his two customers alone and plans alpha tests in employees' homes this year.
