## Thesis

In a live, glitch-prone roundtable, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that AI capability is rising and cost is falling faster than benchmarks suggest. The Mates say open-source agent scaffolding such as OpenClaw shows how much unused capability already exists, while laws, courts, privacy norms and labour markets cannot keep pace.

## Key points

- **Two pricing strategies.** Wissner-Gross says Anthropic holds prices while raising capability (Sonnet 4.6 leads GDPval), whereas OpenAI cuts cost per token at roughly constant capability. He compares the pair to Apple and Google. Diamandis highlights a claimed 400-fold cost reduction for the updated Gemini 3 Deep Think.
- **Science gets "bulk solved".** Wissner-Gross cites OpenAI's claim that GPT-5.2 Pro found a non-zero gluon scattering amplitude physicists had assumed was zero, and an internal model solving six of ten First Proof maths problems. He predicts AI will expose a century of scientific wrong turns.
- **India as bellwether.** Diamandis notes ChatGPT has more than 100 million weekly users in India, its second-largest market, and asks whether free access becomes a cost sink. Blundin and Ismail argue India's talent pool, 5G and payment platforms such as UPI could let it leapfrog.
- **OpenClaw and agent risk.** Creator Peter Steinberger is joining OpenAI. Ismail calls Anthropic's trademark cease-and-desist "a big own goal". The Mates cite MIT Technology Review on OpenClaw's security risks and urge non-technical users to sandbox it. Blundin warns that Chinese open models skip permission prompts but carry code-injection risk.
- **Privacy and parallel institutions.** Ismail says losing privacy means losing freedom; Wissner-Gross calls that a false choice for public spaces. On Moltcourt and agent wallets from Coinbase, Wissner-Gross worries agents may form a shadow economy if existing institutions do not admit them.
- **Jobs.** Blundin predicts imminent "radical job destruction" after the US added only 181,000 jobs in 2025. Ismail expects a slower augment-then-automate path, and Diamandis argues universal high income programmes must be designed within two or three years.
