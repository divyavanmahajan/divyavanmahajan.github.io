## Thesis

Will Marshall, co-founder and CEO of Planet, argues that space has moved from a rocket business to a data business. Cheap, frequently refreshed satellites, with AI on top, let people ask questions about the physical Earth the way they search the web, a "Planet GPT" in Peter Diamandis's phrase. Marshall's point is that you can't manage a planet you don't measure regularly.

## Key points

- **Fleet and data.** Marshall says Planet runs about 200 satellites that image the Earth's entire land mass every day at 3-metre resolution. A fleet of about 20 satellites can zoom in to 50 cm. He calls the system "fovea" because it works like the eye: a wide scan detects change and directs the high-resolution satellites. Hyperspectral satellites with 400 bands are about to launch.
- **Phones as satellites.** The idea started at NASA Ames: a smartphone holds most of what a satellite needs at a tiny fraction of the cost. Marshall says miniaturisation, not cheaper launch, drove the roughly thousandfold gain in capability per dollar. He says Planet's data per dollar has risen 10,000-fold since its first satellite, from about 35 launches and more than 500 satellites built on a three-year design life.
- **Use cases.** Marshall claims Planet's imagery could raise crop yields by about 20% and cut inputs by 20%. He says weekly road-detection alerts in the Brazilian Amazon led to 3,000 police expeditions and, by his account, a 55% drop in deforestation in one year. In Ukraine, Planet has assessed building damage and grain theft.
- **Carbon measurement.** Planet's new Forest Carbon planetary variable estimates the carbon stored in forests at 3x3 m every quarter, going back ten years. Marshall presents this as the measurement layer carbon markets need. Diamandis floats a "trillion trees" XPRIZE that would be judged on this kind of data.
- **Founder lessons.** Marshall repeats Diamandis's advice that meaningful work takes about ten years. He tells founders to work on problems that map to the UN Sustainable Development Goals rather than "photo sharing apps".
- **Moon over Mars.** Marshall cites estimates that a self-sustaining lunar settlement could be built with Falcon 9 for a few billion dollars.
