## Thesis

Mustafa Suleyman, co-founder of DeepMind and Inflection AI and now CEO of Microsoft AI, tells Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross that computing is moving from apps and browsers to agents and companions. He rejects the idea of a winner-takes-all AGI race, describes Microsoft's push to train frontier models of its own, and argues that superintelligence is close enough that it must be bounded and contained before anyone worries about whether it shares our values.

## Key points

- **Agents replace interfaces.** Suleyman describes Microsoft, a roughly $4 trillion company with almost $300 billion in revenue, as a "platform of platforms". He expects it may mainly sell agents certified for reliability, security and trust within five years.
- **Self-sufficiency, not a race.** He says his mandate is for Microsoft to train its own models end to end at the frontier. The core superintelligence team is a few hundred people, and more Microsoft models will appear next year.
- **The modern Turing test.** His 2022 proposal asks which agent can turn $100,000 into $1 million. He thinks agents "don't really work yet" but will come into view within a couple of years. He cites the MAI Diagnostic Orchestrator as about four times more accurate than experts on rare cases.
- **Surprises.** Suleyman admits he got costs wrong: Inflection raised about $1.5 billion with a 25-person team and was CoreWeave's first AI customer, then open-source Llama undercut its cost base. He puts the fall in per-token inference cost at about 100x over two years.
- **Consciousness and personhood.** He argues AI can imitate consciousness without suffering, warns against model-rights campaigns and calls himself a humanist, even a "speciesist".
- **Containment first.** He says Microsoft spends less on safety than it should. He backs shared safety commitments, such as a share of compute and headcount, and expects major powers including China to cooperate on containment within 20 years.
