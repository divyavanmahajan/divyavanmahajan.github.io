## Thesis

Recorded the day after Gemini 3 launched, Peter Diamandis opens with the claim that "Google is winning". Alexander Wissner-Gross and Dave Blundin argue the release is a real step change that people miss because weekly breakthroughs now feel routine. The Mates then link it to AI agents running businesses, AI-native software development, biosecurity and Diamandis's case for abundance.

## Key points

- **Big model smell.** Wissner-Gross calls Gemini 3 the biggest release since OpenAI's o3. He cites Google Workspace integration in agent mode, a one-shot 3D model of the MIT campus, and the Antigravity IDE built with former Windsurf staff. He says the gains on Humanity's Last Exam and ARC-AGI-2 do not look like benchmark gaming.
- **Agents as economic actors.** On Andon Labs' Vending-Bench Arena, where agents start with a simulated $500 to run a vending machine, Diamandis cites about 3,000% more profit than GPT-5 or Claude Sonnet. Wissner-Gross sees this as a step toward "zero human startups". Blundin estimates the non-human economy is already worth about a trillion dollars.
- **Competition drives Google.** Blundin argues Google improved its voice mode and shipped models like the transformer only under pressure from OpenAI. He calls Gemini 3 a 7-trillion-parameter-class model and expects 10x to 40x more raw compute next year.
- **Coding and capital.** Blundin finds Antigravity nearly identical to Cursor, which tripled its valuation to about $30 billion, and calls Cursor's position vulnerable. Wissner-Gross reads Jeff Bezos's $6.2 billion Project Prometheus as money moving toward science and engineering after superintelligence.
- **Biosecurity co-scaling.** On OpenAI's $15 million investment in Red Queen Bio, Wissner-Gross argues that safety should scale with compute ("defensive co-scaling"). Diamandis proposes airborne-pathogen sequencing at airports. Salim Ismail raises the privacy trade-off.
- **Proof of abundance.** In answer to a listener question, Diamandis works through household spending categories and Ismail cites vertical farming numbers. Blundin suggests tracking depression rates. Wissner-Gross argues the cost of intelligence, which he says falls about 40x a year, is upstream of all of it.
