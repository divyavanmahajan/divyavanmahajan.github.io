## Thesis

Recorded on Earth Day 2021 near Kennedy Space Center, this conversation launches the $100 million carbon removal XPRIZE funded by Elon Musk. Musk places himself in the middle of the spectrum on climate risk, but argues that even a small chance of nonlinear climate change is not worth running as an experiment. He wants the prize to reward working, affordable carbon removal that could scale to gigatons, not theory.

## Key points

- **Prize rules.** Diamandis says teams must physically remove 1,000 tons of CO2 per year, calculate the fully considered cost, be net negative and hold the carbon for at least 100 years. Judges must also be convinced the method can scale to a gigaton. Musk says the rules are guidelines and open to feedback.
- **Prize money.** Diamandis gives the split: $50 million for first place, $30 million across second to fourth, $15 million as $1 million awards to 15 leading teams, and $5 million for student teams. Musk says he would add money if a strong team went unrewarded.
- **Cost is scale.** Musk argues a method is not scalable unless it is cheap: 1-2% of GDP is affordable, 20% is not. He is sceptical that mass tree planting is simple, citing water and fertiliser costs.
- **Pricing carbon.** Asked who should pay, Musk argues CO2 is an unpriced externality and favours a carbon tax with rebates to protect low-income households.
- **Rockets and Mars.** Musk defends rocket emissions as needed to make humanity multiplanetary. He says Starship will be fully and rapidly reusable, can carry 100-200 tons to the Moon, and that Mars propellant would come from atmospheric CO2 and water ice.
- **Bigger risks.** Musk names advanced AI and population collapse as the two largest existential risks, and argues that persuading people to accept a worse life is a losing climate strategy, which is why Tesla set out to build better cars.
