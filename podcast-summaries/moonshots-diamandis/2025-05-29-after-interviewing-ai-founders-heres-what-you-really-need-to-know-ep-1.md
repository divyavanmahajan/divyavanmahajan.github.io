## Thesis

Recorded at Link Ventures near MIT, this is a fireside chat in which Dave Blundin interviews Peter Diamandis for an audience of AI founders. Diamandis argues that a founder's mindset (purpose-driven, exponential, abundance-oriented) predicts success better than money or technology. He also says founders now have to sell a vision long before they can demo it. Both men argue that education is falling far behind the pace of AI.

## Key points

- **The X Prize playbook.** Diamandis tells how he announced the $10 million X Prize in 1996 without the money and got about 150 rejections before Anousheh Ansari funded it. He says the 30 prizes since then, worth about $500 million, have driven about $10 billion in R&D, including the $100 million carbon removal prize funded by Elon Musk.
- **Mindsets over plans.** Diamandis lists four mindsets: purpose (a "massive transformative purpose"), exponential (aim for 10x, not 10%), abundance and longevity. Blundin says tech companies keep reinventing themselves, so founder and team dynamics matter more than the current business plan.
- **Education lags.** Citing Ray Kurzweil, Diamandis expects as much change from 2025 to 2035 as in the previous century. An audience poll favoured Thiel Fellowship and Y Combinator acceptance over Harvard and MIT degrees. Blundin points to Mercor as an example.
- **Founder brand and storytelling.** Blundin says Sam Altman showed that founders can sell a vision years before the product exists. Diamandis adds that a founder who doesn't believe in the mission can't sell it.
- **Quantum and abundance.** Alex Wissner-Gross calls quantum computing "a major disappointment thus far" but is bullish on quantum sensing, which SandboxAQ uses for GPS-free navigation. Diamandis predicts humanoid robots at $20,000-30,000, leased for about $300 a month, and a move towards a post-capitalist society.
- **Lessons from 27 companies.** Diamandis says his failures came from chasing quick money or picking co-founders he wasn't close to. Blundin describes the "Fred Wilson rule": back technical teams of three or more best friends.

## Quotes

> "Your job is to become a compelling storyteller to paint the vision with such clarity and such audacity that people are leaning in and they're believing you." — Peter H. Diamandis

> "Whenever I did something for money and not for purpose or passion, I failed miserably because doing anything big and bold is hard." — Peter H. Diamandis
