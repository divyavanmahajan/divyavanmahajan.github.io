## Thesis

In a quick reaction to OpenAI Dev Day, Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross and Emad Mostaque argue that the frontier labs are racing to become the everything app that owns human attention. Mostaque says falling token prices force OpenAI beyond chat subscriptions, and the group sees compute, chip fabs and energy as the real limits.

## Key points

- **Dev Day numbers.** Sam Altman reported 4 million developers, 800 million weekly ChatGPT users and over 6 billion API tokens per minute, up from 300 million in 2023. Wissner-Gross estimates that is about 6% of human spoken tokens, and Mostaque thinks AI tokens will soon overtake human speech.
- **Apps inside ChatGPT.** Mostaque frames the apps SDK as a WeChat-style super-app battle against Meta, Google and xAI. Wissner-Gross sees apps as a passing phase before every pixel is generated. Blundin rejects the claim that a million startups died, arguing strong AI teams simply pivot.
- **Agent Builder and Codex.** Blundin and Mostaque call node-and-line workflow builders a transitional design, expecting voice to take over. Diamandis reads an OpenAI employee's tweet that Agent Builder was built in under six weeks with Codex writing 80% of the PRs.
- **Sora 2 API.** Wissner-Gross says Sora 2 costs 10 cents per second and argues mechanical product design is being automated. Mostaque argues OpenAI needs agentic work, advertising and new verticals because chat is getting cheap.
- **Chips and data centers.** The hosts discuss the AMD deal for 6 gigawatts of GPUs, with OpenAI able to earn about 10% of AMD. Mostaque estimates about $50 billion of buildout per gigawatt. Blundin says the hard ceiling is chip fab capacity, and Wissner-Gross expects new data centers to run on co-located power off the grid.
- **Robotics.** Wissner-Gross sees Tesla's FSD 14.1 as a step towards a single vision-language-action model shared with Optimus. He also notes that DeepMind tests robot safety rules against Asimov's three laws.
