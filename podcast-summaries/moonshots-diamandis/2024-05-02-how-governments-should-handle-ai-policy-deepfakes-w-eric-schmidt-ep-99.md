## Thesis

Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, speaking with Peter Diamandis at Abundance360 2024, describes himself as both optimistic and fearful about AI. He expects everyone to soon have access to a "polymath", and AI to move from producing text to taking actions. He argues that a small number of very powerful frontier systems will be regulated, while open-source mid-sized models spread widely, and that scientists and industry should shape the safety rules before governments do.

## Key points

- **Language to action.** Schmidt says text will increasingly be turned into programs that run. His examples are an agent that organises a whole conference, or one that builds a search engine using your own ranking algorithm.
- **Danger thresholds.** Schmidt says a group of about 20 scientists he works with concluded that current LLMs are acceptable but future ones are worrying. They name recursive self-improvement, agents inventing their own language, and autonomous advanced mathematics as points at which systems should be unplugged.
- **China and chips.** Schmidt says export controls leave China at about the A100 level and seven-nanometre chips. He calls the gap temporary, because China can spend five times more on training.
- **Elections and deepfakes.** Schmidt argues that the way social platforms present content will decide how heavily they get regulated. He cites the Taylor Swift deepfakes as evidence that motivated users get around safeguards.
- **Drones and war.** Based on visits to Ukraine, Schmidt argues that cheap drones could make tanks obsolete and invasions harder, and that better targeting could reduce civilian casualties.
- **Models and regulation.** Schmidt says restricting training data produces more brittle models, so guardrails and red teaming work better. He expects red teaming to become an industry of its own, and points to AI uses in physics, biology and SandboxAQ's quantum simulation.
