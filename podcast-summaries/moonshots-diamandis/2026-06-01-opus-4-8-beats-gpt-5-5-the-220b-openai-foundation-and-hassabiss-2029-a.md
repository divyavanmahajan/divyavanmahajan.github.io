## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates argue that frontier AI is now a monthly release race between Anthropic and OpenAI, with benchmarks saturating faster than anyone can agree what AGI means. Much of the hour goes on who captures the resulting wealth: the OpenAI Foundation's stake, UBI-style schemes, state job dashboards, and a growing anti-tech backlash.

## Key points

- **Opus 4.8.** Diamandis cites Opus 4.8 at 61.4 on the Artificial Analysis index and 69.2 on SWE-bench Pro. Wissner-Gross calls it an incremental monthly update and wants benchmarks built on unsolved scientific problems. Blundin, who runs about 100 agents on EC2, says it handles parallel threads and "self-forking" much better.
- **AGI timelines.** Demis Hassabis now backs 2029, alongside Ray Kurzweil, and proposes an "Einstein test". Wissner-Gross calls this convenient goalpost-moving from a lab that is not leading, and says some form of AGI has existed since about 2020. Ismail calls the AGI debate noise without a clear definition of intelligence.
- **Agentic shopping.** Amazon is opening its Alexa-based assistant, which Diamandis says converts at 3.5 times keyword search, to all retailers. He contrasts this vertical play with Google's open agent commerce protocols. Ismail says the retail war is shifting to agent preferences.
- **OpenAI Foundation.** Diamandis puts the foundation's 26% stake at $130-260 billion, the world's largest, and notes a $250 million economic futures grant. Wissner-Gross predicts pressure for it to fund UBI. Ismail argues UBI is closer to libertarianism than socialism, while Wissner-Gross calls the scenario "privatized socialism".
- **Energy and quantum.** The hosts cite wind and solar passing natural gas at 22% of global electricity in April 2026, and IBM's $2 billion Albany quantum chip foundry with the Commerce Department. Wissner-Gross supports the foundry as a bet on quantum-accelerated AI.
- **Backlash and jobs.** The Mates discuss federal monitoring of "anti-tech extremism", California's AI workforce dashboard and robotics competition with China. Blundin says LinkedIn spot checks show laid-off big-tech engineers mostly joining startups.
