## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates argue that one week held two turning points: SpaceX's IPO, which the hosts say has public markets rewarding hard tech again, and a US government export-control order that took Anthropic's top models offline. Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail call the second the bigger story, because it settles who decides what level of AI companies may access.

## Key points

- **SpaceX IPO.** Diamandis says SpaceX opened at $135, reached about $2.89 trillion in market cap and made Elon Musk the first trillionaire. He frames it as three businesses: launch, Starlink cash flow and orbital AI compute. Blundin warns about lockup expiries, the Kessler debris risk flagged in the S-1, and a valuation he compares unfavourably with Google's cash flow.
- **Fable 5 and Mythos 5 suspended.** According to the hosts, a directive barring access by foreign nationals, after a reported jailbreak, led Anthropic to disable both models for everyone. Alexander Wissner-Gross expects a deal within weeks. Blundin says government control over model releases is now permanent.
- **Trust and on-prem models.** Diamandis and Wissner-Gross criticise reported data retention, silent downgrades and possible poisoning of AI-research queries. Ismail says 70% of elite lab researchers are foreign-born, and expects enterprises to run local models as a fallback.
- **OpenAI price cuts and IPO delay.** Wissner-Gross sees price cuts as mostly optics. On Sam Altman's comment that faster recursive self-improvement could favour delaying the IPO, he suggests technology may start to replace the need for capital. Ismail offers "Salim's law": each 10x drop in token cost brings 100x more experiments.
- **Physical limits.** Diamandis cites a 2.5-year wait for power transformers. Wissner-Gross and Blundin predict training on Earth, inference in orbit, and eventually data centres on the Moon.
- **Work and unrest.** Diamandis points to weak hiring of 22-to-25-year-olds and to youth-led revolutions in history. The Mates reject taxing AI directly and favour lowering the cost of living or a universal basic dividend.
