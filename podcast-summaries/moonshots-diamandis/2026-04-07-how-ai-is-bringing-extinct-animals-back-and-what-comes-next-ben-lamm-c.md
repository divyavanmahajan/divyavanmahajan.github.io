## Thesis

Recorded on stage at the 2026 Abundance360 Summit, Peter Diamandis frames Colossal as a platform for designing living products with AI rather than just a woolly mammoth project. Ben Lamm argues that tackling de-extinction forces the company to solve the hardest problems in biology, and that the resulting pipeline can be reused for commercial spin-outs. He says synthetic biology paired with AI will be humanity's most transformative technology, a view he admits others will dispute.

## Key points

- **Origin and scale.** Lamm recalls asking George Church what single project he would pursue with unlimited capital; Church answered mammoths. Diamandis says Colossal reached a $10 billion valuation in four years; Lamm cites 260 scientists (200 in the US, 60 in Australia) plus AI programmers.
- **Spin-out model.** Diamandis describes Colossal as a parent spinning out a dozen companies. The first, Breaking, uses a set of microbes, tuned with directed evolution, to break the chemical bonds of plastics rather than just shrinking them. Lamm says spin-outs get dedicated teams and share editing improvements through intercompany agreements.
- **Business case for de-extinction.** Lamm cites an EY estimate of about $1.7 trillion tied to consumer spending on extinct-animal products and education. He describes a UAE partnership on what he calls the world's first bio vault, a nine-figure initiative on each side, which positions countries as customers for preserving their species.
- **Cloning and wombs.** Lamm says Colossal bought ViaGen, which he credits with 15 of the 18 species ever cloned and about 78% efficiency against a typical 2%. He says its three artificial-womb projects do not work yet.
- **Gene drives.** Lamm puts the invasive species problem at about $5.4 trillion and pitches engineered gene drives, for example against the screwworm, as a humane alternative to poisoning and culling, with the ability to roll them back.
- **Pace of editing.** Lamm claims Colossal went from a few edits at 40% efficiency to hundreds of edits at 90%, and expects thousands in coming years.
