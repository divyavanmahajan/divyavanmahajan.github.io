## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that demand for AI tokens is running far ahead of compute supply. They read Anthropic's lease of SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center as a sign that frontier labs are thinning out while SpaceX AI becomes a hyperscaler, and they frame chips, energy and data-center infrastructure as the "singularity loop" driving returns.

## Key points

- **Anthropic outruns its compute.** Diamandis cites Dario Amodei's claim of 80-fold growth in Q1 2026 against an expected 10x, with run-rate revenue rising from $9 billion to $30 billion by April. Wissner-Gross credits Anthropic's focus on high-value enterprise tokens.
- **Colossus 1 and "Grok on life support".** Anthropic is taking over the Memphis data center, which let it double Claude Code rate limits. Blundin calculates its 220,000 GPUs serve only about 1.6 million concurrent agents. Wissner-Gross infers xAI is giving up on frontier models and turning SpaceX AI into a hyperscaler, eventually in orbit.
- **Alignment through stories.** The hosts discuss Anthropic research reporting that every Claude model since Haiku 4.5 scores perfectly on its agentic misalignment eval, after training on its constitution and on fiction about AIs behaving well. Diamandis links this to his Future Vision XPRIZE.
- **The "great unhobbling".** Claude for legal and Claude for small business prompt debate. Ismail sees a short-term opportunity in helping 36 million US small businesses adopt them. Wissner-Gross expects these skill packs to be absorbed into base models.
- **Singularity economy.** Diamandis compares a 31% S&P 500 return with averages of 320% for six chip stocks and 419% for infrastructure and energy stocks over a year. Wissner-Gross cautions that these returns are backward-looking and that the biggest gains stayed in private markets.
- **Space and UAPs.** Wissner-Gross predicts the singularity will show first in space, where there are fewer incumbents. Others push back that space is heavily regulated too, and Diamandis recalls the regulatory hurdles his asteroid-mining company faced. The group treats the first UAP file release as mostly prosaic but welcomes a formal declassification process.
