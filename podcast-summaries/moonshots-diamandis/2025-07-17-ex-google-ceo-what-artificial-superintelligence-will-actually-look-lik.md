## Thesis

Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt tells Peter Diamandis and Dave Blundin that AI is underhyped: it is a learning machine whose natural limit is electricity, not chips. He expects AI programmers and mathematicians soon, specialised "savants" in every field within five years, and digital superintelligence within ten. He also argues that the US-China race and open-weight proliferation call for a deterrence framework now.

## Key points

- **Energy as the constraint.** Schmidt cites testimony that the US needs 92 more gigawatts for AI, while new nuclear and small modular reactors won't arrive until around 2030. He warns that China has plenty of electricity and would be a strong competitor if it gets the chips.
- **Programming and math first.** Schmidt calls the "San Francisco consensus" (AI soon replacing most programming and math work) roughly right but thinks its dates are off by 1.5-2x. He says junior programmers go away first, and that MCP-connected LLMs threaten middleware and enterprise software vendors.
- **Geopolitics and risk.** Schmidt describes "mutual AI malfunction", a paper with Dan Hendrycks and Alex Wang that proposes deterrence modelled on mutually assured destruction. He also argues for tracking where chips and training runs are. DeepSeek, he admits, proved his "two years behind" estimate wrong. Open-weight models running on small servers are the proliferation problem he worries about most.
- **Jobs.** Schmidt expects automation to raise wages and total employment over the next five to ten years, helped by personal AI assistants and falling birth rates. Blundin says he is urging staff at his companies to retrain now rather than wait.
- **Moats.** Schmidt says the defensible software businesses will be built on fast "learning loops". He predicts about ten new Google- or Meta-scale consumer companies, and says brand matters less than it used to.
- **Purpose.** On the "drift" risk from his book Genesis, Schmidt says human agency must be protected, but he thinks people will keep finding purpose and work.
