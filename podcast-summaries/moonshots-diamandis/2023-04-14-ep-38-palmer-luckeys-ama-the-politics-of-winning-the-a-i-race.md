## Thesis

In an Abundance360 audience Q&A hosted by Peter Diamandis, Oculus founder and Anduril co-founder Palmer Luckey argues that leaving defense to less competent people is not a moral choice. In his view, more precise weapons make the use of force more accountable. He also gives a sober view of US competition with China, and advises investors to back mission-driven founders with relevant skills rather than passion alone.

## Key points

- **Precision and accountability.** Luckey argues that very precise weapons remove the excuse of collateral damage and make it easier for democratic institutions to regulate the use of force.
- **Non-partisan mission.** He says Anduril's CEO Brian Schimpf raises money for Democrats while he raises money for Republicans, and the shared national-security mission keeps them aligned. He calls political monoculture at tech firms a zero-interest-rate phenomenon.
- **Defense primes.** Luckey says cost-plus contractors such as Lockheed have no incentive to reuse existing technology. He cites Lockheed at about $100 billion in value on $67 billion in revenue.
- **US versus China.** He thinks the US is militarily strong for now. He says China gets far more than two or three times the value per dollar, and that rebuilding US advanced manufacturing will take decades. He quotes Putin saying the AI leader will rule the world.
- **Talent over passion.** Luckey calls Oculus luck and timing. He tells VCs to look at Anduril instead and to back mission-driven founders with something to prove.
- **Metaverse.** He expects the first breakthrough to come from small teams, not big players. He points to 55,000 Oculus dev kits and to VRChat thriving, and blames lawyers for slowing Horizon Worlds.
