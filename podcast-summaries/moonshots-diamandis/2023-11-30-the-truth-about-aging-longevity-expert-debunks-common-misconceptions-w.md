## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Jamie Justice, executive director of XPRIZE Healthspan, present the $101 million prize as a way to turn aging into a target for therapies and to open a regulatory and commercial path for them. Justice argues that judging teams on restored function rather than on a single disease captures what aging actually does. Diamandis compares the effort to the original Ansari XPRIZE, which he credits with opening up commercial spaceflight.

## Key points

- **Prize design.** Justice explains that teams must improve muscle, cognitive and immune function in people aged 65 to 80, with a treatment lasting a year or less. Any approach is allowed: drugs, repurposed compounds, biologics, gene or cell therapies, devices or nutrition.
- **Staged payouts.** Justice describes $10 million for about 40 teams around 2025 and another $10 million for 10 teams in 2026 to fund trials. Final awards are $61, $71 or $81 million for improvements equivalent to 10, 15 or 20 years. Diamandis adds $30 million for operations and a $10 million FSHD bonus funded by Lululemon founder Chip Wilson.
- **Regulation as the bottleneck.** Justice says the FDA is willing to engage but needs the field to bring it a consensus. She describes one aging-focused company that declined to compete because it saw no regulatory pathway, so it kept targeting rare diseases instead.
- **Economic case.** Justice cites research suggesting GDP falls by just under 6% for every 10% increase in the share of people over 60, mostly through lost labour supply. Diamandis cites a study co-authored by David Sinclair that values one extra year of global life expectancy at $38 trillion.
- **Healthspan gap.** Justice says Americans gained about 30 years of lifespan in a century, but there is roughly a 12-year gap between health-adjusted life years and lifespan.
- **Objections.** Diamandis and Justice dismiss worries about boredom, "immortal dictators" and inequality. Justice says accessibility is part of the judges' considerations. Diamandis notes that XPRIZE makes no claim on teams' intellectual property.
