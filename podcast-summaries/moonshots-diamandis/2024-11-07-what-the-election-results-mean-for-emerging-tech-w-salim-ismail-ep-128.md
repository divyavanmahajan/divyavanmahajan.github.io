## Thesis

Recorded the day after the 2024 US election, Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail, who both describe themselves as libertarian capitalists, ask what a pro-deregulation White House could mean for emerging technology. Their shared view is that government policy is defensive and reactive and cannot keep up with exponential change. They argue the US should set rules early for crypto, drones and biotech, and let markets and insurers manage AI and robotics risk.

## Key points

- **Bitcoin and tokenization.** Ismail would stop taxing everyday crypto payments as property transactions and would let financial advisers recommend Bitcoin. He points to Mattereum's legal stack for tokenizing real-world assets. He estimates the global economy at close to 600 trillion dollars, about half of it real estate that could be fractionalized.
- **AI cannot be regulated.** Ismail sees no mechanism to regulate AI because open-source and small models undercut any framework. He expects private AI auditing firms to fill the gap. Diamandis says the real AI arms race is between companies, citing xAI's 100,000-H100 cluster.
- **Energy.** Diamandis cites a projection that hyperscalers could consume all US electricity production by 2030, and points to Gen 4 nuclear contracts and perovskite solar. Ismail suggests placing data centers next to stranded renewables, such as a gigawatt wind farm in Kazakhstan.
- **Robots, jobs and insurance.** Ismail is not worried about unemployment. He cites Sweden, South Korea and Germany as countries with high robot density and low unemployment, and predicts that insurers, not regulators, will decide which robot use cases are allowed. Diamandis expects job displacement and favors universal basic income (UBI) experiments.
- **Biotech and drones.** Both want the FDA to weigh efficacy over safety and support a right to try therapies. Diamandis praises Zipline's start in Rwanda, where the country created a dedicated drone airspace corridor.
- **Rethinking democracy.** Ismail uses an "ice, water, steam" metaphor and argues nation states are the wrong container. He favors city-level self-government, sunset clauses on laws, and liquid democracy, where people delegate their vote by topic.
