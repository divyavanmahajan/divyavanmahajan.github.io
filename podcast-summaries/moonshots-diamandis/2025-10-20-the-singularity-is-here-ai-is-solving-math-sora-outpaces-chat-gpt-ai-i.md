## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross agree that humanity is already living through the singularity. Wissner-Gross argues it only looks like a sudden break from far away and feels smooth from the inside. They point to AI progress on frontier mathematics, AI-assisted chip design and the scale of data-centre spending, and they question Ray Kurzweil's 2045 date.

## Key points

- **Math on track to be solved.** Wissner-Gross had predicted math could be called "solved" once a model passed 10% on Frontier Math Tier 4. GPT-5 Pro has now scored 13%. He calls this the canary for the physical sciences, engineering and medicine, and says GPT-5 is being used to bulk-solve open Erdős problems.
- **AI designing its own chips.** Blundin highlights Greg Brockman's claim that OpenAI's models achieved large area reductions in chip layouts. He says self-improvement only needs math, algorithms and chip design. He links this to Leopold Aschenbrenner's Broadcom stake.
- **Smaller, cheaper models.** GPT-5 Pro set a record of 70.2% on ARC-AGI-1 at about $4.78 per task. Samsung's 7-million-parameter Tiny Recursive Model leads Wissner-Gross to argue that intelligence is compression. Ismail sees a shift from mainframe-style to distributed architectures.
- **Media and adoption speed.** Sora reached 1 million downloads in under five days, faster than ChatGPT. Diamandis cites AI-written content now exceeding human content online. Wissner-Gross rejects the idea that the web will drown in "slop", comparing it to fears about email spam.
- **Robots, energy and GDP.** Figure 03 gets palm cameras and a lighter chassis, and Diamandis says it targets a $20,000 price. The hosts discuss record US electricity prices and Army micro-reactors. Diamandis cites a figure that data centres and AI made up about 92% of US GDP growth in the first half of 2025. Ismail calls the Dallas Fed's 0.3% AI-impact forecast far too timid.
- **Kurzweil's timeline.** Blundin suggests Kurzweil is pushing the singularity date out to 2045 to avoid arguments over definitions. Wissner-Gross accepts the forecast but not the idea of a discontinuity, and says we have "line of sight" to what comes next.
