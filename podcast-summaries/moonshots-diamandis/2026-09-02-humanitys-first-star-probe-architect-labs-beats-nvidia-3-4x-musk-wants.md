## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross (Salim Ismail is stuck at airport security for the whole episode) use the launch of the Fermi Explorer interstellar mission as evidence that AI now does in a week, for a few billion tokens, technical work that used to take large institutions years. They apply the same lens to AI-designed chips, outcome-based AI pricing and Elon Musk's moves in energy and geoengineering.

## Key points

- **A $15 million star probe.** Starcloud CEO Philip Johnston describes a roughly 100 kg ride-share spacecraft, about 60% xenon, using off-the-shelf ion thrusters to get 99% of the way to Alpha Centauri in about 80,000 years. He plans to launch within three years.
- **AI found the trajectory.** Johnston says JPL veterans and six months of work failed to close the mass budget. Matt Pines' company Physical Superintelligence (PSI), using its open-source Get Physics Done package, found a "perihelion pump" manoeuvre in a week with about 10 billion tokens. Pines also announced a $58 million seed round led by Breakthrough Energy Ventures.
- **Cursor and the lab alliances.** After OpenAI cut Cursor off from its models following the SpaceX acquisition, Anthropic stepped in. Blundin reads this as OpenAI going after enterprise customers. Wissner-Gross argues the fight is really about who gets the reasoning traces.
- **Outcome-based pricing.** Following Salesforce and OpenAI, the hosts expect AI to be priced by results. Wissner-Gross predicts pricing will settle into flops, tokens and outcomes, much like CPM, CPC and CPA in digital advertising.
- **AI-designed silicon.** Architect Labs says its Redwood chip went from a two-person spec to a silicon-ready design in two weeks, with 3.4 times the performance per watt of Nvidia's Jetson. Wissner-Gross (an adviser) and Blundin (an investor through Link Ventures) call it a threat to Nvidia.
- **Energy and weather.** Musk warns that 15 GW of AI compute made in 2027 cannot be switched on that year, and says SpaceX and Tesla will cast turbine blades in-house. The hosts back his satellite geoengineering idea but flag the problem of who gets to control the climate.
