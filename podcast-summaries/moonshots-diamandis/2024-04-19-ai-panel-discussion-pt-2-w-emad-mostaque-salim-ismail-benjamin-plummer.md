## Thesis

Recorded at the 2024 Abundance360 Summit, this audience Q&A has the AI faculty answering rapid-fire questions on media, hardware, jobs and ownership. The common line, pushed hardest by Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail, is that AI is a land grab where many companies can win at once and where automation creates more work and more firms instead of mass unemployment.

## Key points

- **Movies from novels.** Emad Mostaque estimates about two years until a book can be turned into a film on a large cloud computer, since short scenes already work. Another panellist counters that editing is the hardest part to automate. Blundin argues short-form video, not feature films, is the real target.
- **Investing in India.** Blundin advises seeding teams of 10 to 20 AI-trained people and acting as their first customer. He compares the moment to the arrival of the relational database, only far larger, and says competition is not a concern yet.
- **One-person unicorns.** The Invisible team cites Sam Altman's one-person unicorn and Ronald Coase's theory of the firm. They say only 5-10% of knowledge work is outsourced today and suggest 25-50% by 2050. Guillaume Verdon pictures firms that are 80% AI and 20% human, with many more of them.
- **Chips and data centres.** Verdon says Nvidia's lead comes from its compilers and hardware and tells investors to watch AMD and then Intel. Mostaque says Stable Diffusion 3 runs faster on Intel chips than on Nvidia. Blundin says new data centres should sit next to power, not users.
- **Decentralization.** Blundin points to roughly 600,000 open models on Hugging Face. Mostaque cites 330 million downloads of Stability models, a new board seat at Render, and plans for about 100 national models with citizen-owned data.
- **Jobs and copyright.** Ismail uses bank ATMs as his example: branch costs fell tenfold, banks opened more branches and teller numbers held steady. Blundin expects copyright objections to fade, as they did for Google's web indexing.
