## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross treat the launch of ChatGPT Atlas as an opening shot in a fight with Google over points of control and personal data. Wissner-Gross argues that the browser matters less than the superintelligence behind it. The rest of the episode follows the money and power behind that intelligence: credit-funded data centers, custom chips, and energy timelines that the hosts see as the real limit.

## Key points

- **Atlas as a channel.** Blundin compares Sam Altman to Bill Gates: a larger installed base defended by a device, data centers and now a browser. Wissner-Gross finds the partly local agent mode the most interesting feature. In his web-chess test, the agent asked the site for hints and won.
- **White-collar work, one vertical at a time.** OpenAI is reported to pay over 100 bankers $150 an hour to train models on M&A, LBOs and IPOs. Blundin expects this in every field. Wissner-Gross sees thousands of labour categories still open to startups and does not expect one company to take the whole economy.
- **Debt for compute.** Meta's SPV is borrowing $27 billion at 6.8% for a Louisiana data center. Wissner-Gross describes credit funding the lower half of the AI stack and equity the upper half. The hosts also cover Oracle's planned 16-zettaflop system and Anthropic's deal for up to 1 million Google TPUs.
- **Chips and choke points.** Wissner-Gross notes that Tesla's AI5 is one chip for both data centers and robots or cars. Blundin argues that Anthropic, relying on Google TPUs, is in an awkward spot when rivals design their own silicon, and that TSMC, Samsung and Intel are the choke points.
- **Quantum and energy.** Wissner-Gross calls Google's Willow result real progress but is still waiting for a quantum speed-up for AI. On energy, the hosts discuss a claimed thousand-percent rise in US reactor costs, a DOE fusion roadmap targeting 2030-2035, and Amazon's X-energy SMRs. Blundin cites Eric Schmidt's call for 100 gigawatts by 2030.

## Quotes
