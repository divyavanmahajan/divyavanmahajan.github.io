## Thesis

On stage at Moonshots Live, Peter Diamandis and Emad Mostaque question ARK Invest's Cathie Wood and Circle's Nikhil Chandhok. Chandhok argues that AI agents will become full economic actors that need verifiable identity, cheap settlement and stablecoin rails. Wood makes a bull case for US growth, health care and Bitcoin, and blames regulation for slowing progress.

## Key points

- **Tesla-SpaceX merger.** Wood says ARK expects the two companies to combine, with Mars as Elon Musk's end goal and orbital data centres as his route to the cheapest AI compute. Tesla and SpaceX are ARK's top holdings. She calls the China exposure the main obstacle.
- **More agents than humans.** Chandhok expects more AIs than humans online next year. He says agents will need provenance, liability and work histories, and that public blockchain logs can provide them. Circle's Arc chain gives new agents a 25-cent credit to start.
- **Why build Arc.** Chandhok says USDC has settled over 100 trillion across 30 public chains. Arc adds payment finality, known validators, privacy and low cost (0.005 cents versus 89 cents on Ethereum, by his figures). He sees the Genius Act taking effect in January as a tailwind.
- **Health care and robots.** Wood calls health care the most undervalued application of AI. ARK's research puts humanoid robots at 200,000 times the complexity of a robotaxi, so it expects scaling a couple of years after Musk's late-2028 target.
- **Fear and regulation.** Diamandis cites polls showing 80% of Americans fear AI. Wood argues that without nuclear regulation, US electricity would cost half as much. She also says technology is always a net job creator, and Chandhok argues AI must visibly make housing, education and health care better.
- **Tokenization and Bitcoin.** Chandhok argues stablecoins export the dollar and that tokenized treasuries and stocks will trade around the clock. Wood keeps ARK's $1 million Bitcoin forecast, although she says stablecoins have taken over some of Bitcoin's expected role.
