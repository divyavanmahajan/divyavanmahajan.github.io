## Thesis

Emad Mostaque, former CEO of Stability AI and founder of Schelling AI, walks Peter Diamandis through his paper "How to Think About AI". His core claim is that AI models are like an endless supply of capable graduates, and soon robots, that cost only compute and electricity. People and companies that work with them will pull ahead. Because intelligence is turning into infrastructure, he argues it should be open and nationally owned rather than controlled by a few firms.

## Key points

- **AI Atlantis.** Using a phrase he credits to Nat Friedman, Mostaque describes a "new continent" of around 100 billion agents that work like interns. He says GPT-4-class costs have fallen roughly a thousandfold, and he predicts one- to three-person unicorns within a year or two.
- **Jobs.** Mostaque expects outsourced and entry-level screen work to be hit first. Mid-level workers become more productive, and leaders who direct AI become "multipliers". Diamandis argues that by the end of the decade companies will either be fully using AI or out of business, and Mostaque agrees.
- **Humanoid robots.** Diamandis cites Elon Musk's $10,000 target for Optimus and imagines renting a robot for $100 a month. He mentions forecasts of up to 10 billion humanoids by the 2040s. Mostaque says the robotics control problem is largely solved for about 90% of use cases.
- **Persuasion and ads.** Both expect personal agents to disrupt advertising. Mostaque warns that empathetic AI can also manipulate people, through hyper-personalised political calls or real-time voice and avatar filters, so it matters who the AI is loyal to.
- **Open, sovereign AI.** Mostaque says every nation needs its own sovereign AI and open, high-quality datasets, for example an open cancer model. He points to 300 million developer downloads of Stability's models and disputes the US-versus-China race framing in Leopold Aschenbrenner's essay.
- **Economics.** Mostaque claims current debt-driven economic models are near their end. He floats universal basic compute or jobs programmes and expects things to get very turbulent within about five years.
