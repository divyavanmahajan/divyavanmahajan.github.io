## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail treat the OpenAI-Musk fight as a symptom of a race for AGI, in which hundreds of billions of dollars are committed without a clear definition of the goal. Diamandis argues the race can only be guided, not slowed. Ismail agrees, but wants a broader, parallel conversation about where it leads. Both then argue that AI will rebuild healthcare.

## Key points

- **The OpenAI letter.** Diamandis summarises OpenAI's published letter as claiming that Musk wanted a for-profit model from the start and wanted to run it. Ismail reads the letter as a defensive move against a lawsuit that now includes Microsoft. The hosts also note that Meta asked the government to block the switch.
- **Capital without ROI.** The hosts play a clip of Sam Altman saying he doesn't care whether OpenAI burns $500 million or $50 billion a year. Diamandis cites SoftBank's $100 billion US AI pledge, Oman's stake in xAI, and an estimated $200 billion of 2024 spending by the big labs. Ismail compares the buildout to the early telcos.
- **Musk's speed.** Diamandis recounts xAI raising $6 billion and standing up a 100,000-H100 cluster in 122 days.
- **Odds and guidance.** Diamandis says Musk's estimate moved from 80/20 to 90/10 good versus disaster. Diamandis himself follows Mo Gawdat's view that smarter systems will be abundance-loving, and Ismail puts himself at 99.9% optimistic. Ismail plays down Anthropic's alignment-faking result with Claude 3 Opus as pattern matching, not intent. Diamandis points to the Asilomar conferences as a model of self-guidance.
- **Healthcare.** Diamandis shows a chart in which US administrators grew 3,000% between 1970 and 2015, against roughly 100% growth in physicians. He cites a New York Times report that GPT-4 scored 90% on diagnosis, versus 76% for doctors using AI and 74% for doctors alone. The hosts disagree on whether healthcare should be private.
- **Empathy and education.** Diamandis cites a JAMA study rating AI therapists more empathic than humans. Ismail says AI could replace the 40,000 English teachers one minister planned to hire.
