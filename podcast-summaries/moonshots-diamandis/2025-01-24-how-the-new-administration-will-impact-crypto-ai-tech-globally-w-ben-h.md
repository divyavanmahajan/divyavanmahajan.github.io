## Thesis

Ben Horowitz, co-founder of Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), tells Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail that the change of US administration is the most dramatic shift in tech policy he has seen. In his view it removes barriers to M&A, crypto and AI startups. He argues that the US competes with China best by keeping distributed, bottom-up innovation, not top-down control. He also gives his criteria for backing moonshot founders.

## Key points

- **Policy shift.** Horowitz describes the previous administration as hostile to tech, pointing to blocked M&A such as the FTC's challenge to Meta buying Within, and to de-banking of crypto firms. He welcomes the revoked 2023 AI executive order. Ismail argues that AI could not be regulated that way in any case.
- **Stargate and energy.** Horowitz supports attracting AI infrastructure investment to the US, though he calls the $500 billion Stargate figure "a Masa thing." He wants energy opened up, especially small modular nuclear reactors that can sit next to data centers.
- **AI and biology.** Horowitz compares AI to calculus for physics: a language that can finally model biology. He cautions that in vivo testing will still slow progress. Diamandis highlights Demis Hassabis's expectation of AI-designed drugs entering trials.
- **Backing moonshots.** Horowitz warns against startups padded with vice presidents and says founders must be able to raise money when things look bad. He asks whether he would want to work for the company himself, and prefers hungry teams that won't "call in rich."
- **China.** Horowitz sees China's advantage in merging state and private sector, while Ismail argues that strict rules block disruptive innovation there. Horowitz notes that the robot supply chain sits almost entirely in China.
- **Crypto's comeback.** Horowitz expects crypto to address proof of human, deepfakes, machine-to-machine payments and user-held keys with zero-knowledge proofs. Ismail predicts Bitcoin at $250,000-300,000 for 2025.
