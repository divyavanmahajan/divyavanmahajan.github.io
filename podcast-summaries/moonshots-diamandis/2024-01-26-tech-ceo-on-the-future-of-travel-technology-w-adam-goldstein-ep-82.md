## Thesis

Adam Goldstein, co-founder and CEO of Archer Aviation, tells Peter Diamandis that electric air taxis can turn 60-120 minute car trips into 5-10 minute flights. He expects them to cost about the same as an Uber Black at launch and less than car ownership within five to seven years. His central argument is that the hard part of hardware is building a business around it, not making it, which is why Archer designed its Midnight aircraft around a business model and partnered with automaker Stellantis.

## Key points

- **The vehicle.** Goldstein describes Midnight as a piloted, four-passenger aircraft with 12 electric engines and six independent battery packs. It is designed for up to 100 miles at 150 mph, with typical trips of 20-40 miles. He says it has zero single points of failure, against 200-300 on a helicopter, and its wing allows it to glide.
- **Enabling technologies.** He credits denser batteries (the biggest factor), lightweight composites and electric motors that scale down efficiently. Diamandis adds electric propulsion and compute.
- **Operations.** Goldstein expects Uber-integrated trips from simple vertiports that only need charging. He describes 90-second curb-to-air boarding, top-up charging between legs and full charges in 30-40 minutes. New York comes first, partly because of the roughly 30 million yearly Manhattan-airport trips he cites.
- **Regulation and launch.** He says US regulators are supportive, citing drone leadership lost to China's DJI. Archer targets FAA certification and operations in 2025, and plans to launch in 2025 regardless through Department of Defense contracts and international markets such as India via IndiGo's Rahul Bhatia.
- **Scale and capital.** The Covington, Georgia plant is planned for 650, then 2,300, aircraft a year. Goldstein models the Stellantis relationship on Apple and Foxconn. He says Archer raised almost $1 billion through its SPAC with 45 people, and calls the timing lucky.
- **Founder lessons.** Goldstein, a former banker and hedge-fund investor who founded Vettery, describes his job as vision, capital, people and risk. He says big missions are easier to hire and raise money for.
