## Thesis

Balaji Srinivasan, former Coinbase CTO and founder of the Network School, tells Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail that there will be many AGIs rather than one, and that AI amplifies capable humans instead of replacing them. Blundin pushes back, arguing that recursive self-improvement has begun and a single winner may be visible within months. Balaji frames the wider decade as China (the physical world) versus the internet (the digital world), with crypto as the internet's money.

## Key points

- **Polytheistic AI.** Balaji points to leapfrogging models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI and Chinese open models such as DeepSeek, Kimi and Qwen. He argues chaos, turbulence, cryptography and adversarial systems like markets put hard limits on any runaway superintelligence.
- **The counter-bet.** Blundin claims labs are diverting compute to internal self-improvement and that "we'll know" about a single winner in roughly six months, two years at most. Diamandis proposes making it a long bet.
- **Amplified intelligence.** Balaji says AI works "middle to middle": humans still prompt and verify. He argues smarter users get smarter output, AI mostly replaces the previous AI tool in a budget, and it creates jobs in verification and proctoring. He sees regulation, not AI, as the main brake on medicine.
- **China and the internet.** Balaji claims Western currency yields are rising while money flows to China and Bitcoin. Blundin raises China's ageing demographics; Balaji answers that China is automating its way around them.
- **Crypto as AI money.** Balaji argues programs can open wallets in fractions of a second, making crypto the natural currency for agents. He predicts most fiat currencies die once currencies compete globally, and sees retail banks displaced around 2035.
- **Advice.** For founders: build your own audience, consider Florida or Texas, move crypto to cold storage, join local tech communities and visit cities such as Shenzhen, Bangalore and Dubai. For employees: spend a month offline learning maths and computer science.
