## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin, Salim Ismail and Alexander Wissner-Gross recorded this episode the day Nvidia announced its investment in Intel. Blundin argues that the US could not let its only truly domestic leading-edge fab fail. Wissner-Gross compares the deal to Microsoft's 1997 investment in Apple. The rest of the episode follows the hosts' view that compute is the scarce resource of the moment and that AI progress is speeding up in coding, interfaces, health and robotics.

## Key points

- **Intel rescue.** Blundin says the deal follows a US government stake of 10%. In his view, it gives Intel capital to finish its 1.8-nanometer process, and he notes that TSMC holds 66% of the market for AI chips. He also says he copied Leopold Aschenbrenner's Intel options position and had the biggest one-day gain of his life.
- **Where to invest (not advice).** Blundin picks seed-stage AI startups and the data-centre buildout, naming CoreWeave as an example. Ismail picks NextEra for renewable power, and Diamandis mentions Google and uranium. Wissner-Gross declines to pick, arguing that markets driven by AI trading algorithms have already priced in the news.
- **Reasoning beyond verification.** Wissner-Gross says DeepMind's and OpenAI's golds at the ICPC coding contest point to the next leap after reasoning models. He predicts a "strawberry"-scale release, possibly by year end.
- **Interfaces.** Diamandis presents Meta's Ray-Ban Display glasses and EMG neural band. Wissner-Gross sees the band as likely the first mass-market brain-computer interface, while Blundin expects gestures to replace keyboards.
- **Chip geopolitics.** The hosts discuss David Sacks's warning that export controls could push the world toward Huawei. Ismail argues that politics and tariffs are the bigger factor, and Wissner-Gross cites Soviet-era divergence in computing architectures.
- **Science, health and robots.** Wissner-Gross describes DeepMind's progress on Navier-Stokes singularities and the Delphi-2M paper, which treats disease histories as token sequences. Diamandis covers Figure's raise of more than $1 billion and Tesla's Optimus 3 targets of one million robots a year.
