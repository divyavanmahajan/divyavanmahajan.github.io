## Thesis

In an audience Q&A recorded at the Abundance360 summit, Ray Kurzweil argues that LLMs are the biggest advance in communication since written language, and that they are the product of an exponential price-performance curve he has tracked for 40 years. He frames AI as an extension of the human mind rather than a rival. He repeats his timeline: computers pass the Turing test by 2029, and human intelligence is multiplied a millionfold by 2045.

## Key points

- **The compute curve.** Kurzweil shows his chart of computations per second per dollar, which he says ran from 0.000007 on the 1941 Zuse Z1 to about 50 billion today, unbroken by wars or recessions. He says it should not be called Moore's law: it predates Intel, and only 10 of the 80 machines on the chart are Intel-related.
- **Merging, not competing.** He argues that AI is already part of human intelligence through phones and will eventually be added to the brain directly. He says the share of thinking done in the cloud will grow over time.
- **Simulated biology.** He cites Moderna's vaccine design, which he says tested billions of mRNA sequences in two days. He predicts simulated trials will replace much human testing and that diligent people reach "longevity escape velocity" by the end of the decade.
- **Work and education.** Kurzweil notes that farm work fell from 80% of American jobs to 2% over 200 years, and expects LLMs to reshape education by measuring each student's progress.
- **Scepticism and perils.** He doubts quantum computing will matter soon, noting that 1,000 to 10,000 noisy qubits may be needed per accurate qubit. Each of his books has a perils chapter, and he names misuse of AI and engineered pathogens as real risks.
- **Method.** He says he invents by imagining a talk four years out that explains the solution, then working backwards. He also built a "Talk to Books" style replica of his late father from his writings.
