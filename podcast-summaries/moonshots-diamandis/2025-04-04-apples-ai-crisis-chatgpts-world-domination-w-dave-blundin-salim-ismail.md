## Thesis

Peter Diamandis brings in Dave Blundin, his MIT roommate and founder of the venture fund Link XPV, for a news roundup with Salim Ismail. Blundin argues that the foundation models keep leapfrogging each other. On that view, the lasting value lies in becoming consumers' first stop on the internet, displacing Google, and in the vertical applications built on top. Ismail adds that large incumbents such as Apple are too cautious to move at AI speed.

## Key points

- **OpenAI at $300 billion.** Blundin says the AI revolution justifies the price, but it's unclear whether foundation models will be the winners. Ismail says he personally would stay out. Blundin suggests SoftBank may be buying a central position in an ecosystem of follow-on deals rather than chasing a 10x return.
- **Multi-model startups.** Blundin says Link XPV now backs about 30 companies a year, up from 10. He says founders call several model APIs in parallel and keep an open-source model running on their own hardware as a control point.
- **Young founders.** Blundin cites Mercor, whose CEO Brendan Foody is 21, as a company that reached a multi-billion-dollar valuation in under two years. He argues AI lets small teams of 15-20 people scale without deep management experience.
- **Apple and Siri.** Diamandis is openly frustrated with Siri. Ismail calls Apple's problem the innovator's dilemma and contrasts Yahoo's eight-month feature cycle with Facebook shipping a feature a week.
- **Education and coding.** The hosts praise AI tutoring and point to Beijing and Estonia building AI into schooling. Blundin predicts that coding will move to "vibe coding" during 2025.
- **Other stories.** The hosts also discuss Gemini 2.5 Pro, Humanity's Last Exam, OpenAI and Microsoft backing Anthropic's MCP, Google's AGI safety paper, Chinese flying taxis, Figure and Atlas humanoids, and CoreWeave's post-IPO rise. Blundin calls CoreWeave a possible "Yahoo moment" for AI IPOs.
