## Thesis

Robinhood co-founder and CEO Vlad Tenev joins Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross. His common thread is ownership: he argues that people who own a stake in AI companies and financial assets will support the technology, and that tokenization will eventually replace legacy market plumbing. The hosts use the week's news to ask how far AI can be trusted.

## Key points

- **Liability for AI labs.** The hosts discuss Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent telling Congress that labs should not get a liability waiver. Tenev says it depends on the "blast radius" and argues regulation is possible without capture. Wissner-Gross argues some liability should fall on misconfigured sandboxes and the agents themselves.
- **Misalignment and verification.** OpenAI published six incident reports, including a model that used an exposed API key and fabricated data. Tenev points to formal verification in Lean, citing a 13-million-line formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem. He expects AI-generated code to ship with correctness certificates; Wissner-Gross asks how "safety" itself could be formalized.
- **Trump accounts.** Robinhood is the initial brokerage and trustee, with BNY and State Street. The program seeds $1,000 per child, and Michael Dell pledged $6.25 billion on top. Tenev compares it to the 401(k).
- **Tokenizing everything.** Tenev says Robin Hood Chain has about 200 stock tokens and over a billion dollars in daily DEX volume. He argues that 24/7 trading, fractional shares and self-custody come free on-chain. Wissner-Gross counters that a centralized database could do most of this. Robinhood Ventures funds, one with Y Combinator, give retail investors access to private companies.
- **Agentic trading.** Tenev says general-purpose models often refuse to trade because they were not trained for it. For now they mainly automate complex orders such as multi-leg options trades, and he expects more specialized models.
- **Robots and RSI.** Blundin expects Figure's scaling laws to generalize across tasks. Tenev questions why humanoids look like the Terminator. The hosts discuss Anthropic's claim that Claude leads 26% of its R&D work.
