## Thesis

For the 30th anniversary of XPRIZE, Peter Diamandis, co-founder Gregg Maryniak, Erik Lindbergh and XPRIZE CEO Anousheh Ansari tell the story of the first $10 million prize, from the idea in 1993 to SpaceShipOne's winning flight on 4 October 2004. They argue that an incentive prize can overturn a false assumption (here, that only governments can fly people to space). They also say the effort survived only because the team refused to give up through years of near-bankruptcy.

## Key points

- **Origin and design.** Diamandis says Charles Lindbergh's book on the $25,000 Orteig Prize gave him the idea. Maryniak credits him with choosing suborbital flight, which needs far less energy than orbit. The rules called for three people, 100 km, and a repeat flight with the same vehicle within two weeks, which the founders say was meant to push down costs.
- **Fundraising.** St. Louis backers of the "New Spirit of St. Louis" each gave $25,000. The founders then announced the prize in 1996 with 20 astronauts on stage, before the money existed. They say First USA later pledged $5 million and hole-in-one insurance covered the rest, which led to "$50,000 Fridays" of premium payments.
- **Lindbergh's flight.** Erik Lindbergh describes recovering from rheumatoid arthritis and recreating his grandfather's transatlantic flight in 2002. Part of the goal was to raise operating money for the foundation.
- **Ansari's bet.** Ansari says she saw the prize as a risk-mitigated way to fund innovation: teams are paid only on success. She describes it as an investment, not a donation.
- **The flights.** Mike Melville's X1 flight rolled 29 times on the way up and still landed safely. Brian Binnie won the prize on the Sputnik anniversary. According to the founders, Burt Rutan's SpaceShipOne was quietly funded by Paul Allen, and Virgin licensed the technology.
- **Lasting impact.** The guests credit the competition with helping change FAA rules for commercial launches. Diamandis says XPRIZE has since launched nearly $600 million in competitions.
