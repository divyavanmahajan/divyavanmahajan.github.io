## Thesis

Matt Fitzpatrick, CEO of Invisible Technologies and formerly head of McKinsey's QuantumBlack Labs, tells the Moonshot Mates that AI will not affect all industries equally. In his view, enterprise adoption is held back by data, focus and organisation rather than by model capability. Alex Wissner-Gross pushes back, arguing that generalist models and AI researchers could soon make much of the human training work redundant.

## Key points

- **Uneven disruption.** Fitzpatrick expects big changes in media, legal services and business process outsourcing. He thinks the hype is overblown for sectors such as oil and gas or real estate, where the core decisions stay with people.
- **Custom benchmarks.** Public coding benchmarks track general progress, Fitzpatrick says, but enterprises need evals on specific tasks that compare AI with expert humans. Dave Blundin says owning the benchmark for a niche is an opening for domain experts.
- **The Klarna lesson.** Salim Ismail cites Klarna's claim that its agent did the work of 700 staff, before the company went back to human agents. Fitzpatrick argues contact centres should always run a changing mix of humans and agents.
- **A playbook for 2026.** Fitzpatrick advises picking two or three use cases that matter, getting the data for those right, building a pilot rather than a strategy document, and paying outside vendors on results. He cites an MIT report that only 5% of enterprise models reach production. Ismail argues for AI-native units built at the edge of the company.
- **Timelines and RLHF.** Wissner-Gross gives two to three years as a conservative outer bound for AI researchers that match humans. Fitzpatrick replies that specialised tasks will keep needing human feedback and human-equivalence testing.
- **Case studies and predictions.** Examples include computer vision for Charlotte Hornets draft scouting, US Navy underwater drone swarms and Swissgear inventory forecasting. For 2026, Fitzpatrick predicts multi-agent teams, multimodal interfaces and RL gyms.
