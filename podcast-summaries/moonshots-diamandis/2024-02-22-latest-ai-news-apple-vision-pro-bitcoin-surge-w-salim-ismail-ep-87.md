## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail review the week's tech news: Apple Vision Pro, Sam Altman's reported $7 trillion chip-and-AI raise, Google Gemini, deepfakes and Bitcoin. They argue that the convergence of these exponential technologies will reinvent industry after industry. Their advice to companies is to put AI at C-suite level now, and they see Bitcoin as a hedge against debt-driven fiat currencies.

## Key points

- **Vision Pro.** Diamandis credits Apple's App Store and ecosystem. He says around 700 native apps let the headset avoid the fate of unused XR devices. Ismail thinks the real value lies in specialist B2B uses such as surgery, training and inspection.
- **The $7 trillion raise and Gemini.** Diamandis welcomes Altman's reported ask, and Ismail argues it could enable many times that in economic impact. On search, Ismail notes that putting ChatGPT into Bing did not dent Google's dominance. He says only a paradigm shift will break search habits.
- **Deepfakes and AI for truth.** The hosts discuss the FCC ban on AI-generated robocalls after a fake Biden call. They propose an XPRIZE for AI that can classify claims as fact, opinion or disinformation. Ismail suggests putting such checks at platform "choke points" like X.
- **Chief AI officer.** Diamandis advises every company to appoint a chief AI officer who understands the business and the tools. Ismail goes further and imagines AI CMOs and CFOs. With his "1995 IBM problem," he argues that staff already use generative AI even when CEOs think they don't.
- **Vesuvius Challenge.** Diamandis highlights Nat Friedman's challenge, in which AI decoded X-rays of scrolls buried by Vesuvius in 79 AD.
- **Bitcoin.** Ismail argues that fiat systems grow on debt while technology is deflationary. He cites Jeff Booth's claim that every dollar of global GDP growth came with $4 of new debt. He also argues that the Lightning Network has solved Bitcoin's scalability problem. Bitcoin was near $50,000 at recording, and both hosts predict higher prices.
