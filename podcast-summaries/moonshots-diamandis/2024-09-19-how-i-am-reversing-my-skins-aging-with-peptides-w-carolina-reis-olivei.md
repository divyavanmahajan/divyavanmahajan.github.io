## Thesis

Carolina Reis Oliveira, co-founder and CEO of OneSkin, argues that the anti-aging skincare industry has not kept up with longevity science, and that a measurement-first approach (lab-grown skin plus epigenetic age readouts) lets a small startup build products that target a root cause of aging instead of symptoms. Peter Diamandis, who discloses that he is an adviser to the company and that OneSkin sponsors the show, predicts that traditional skin creams and the beauty industry are about to be disrupted.

## Key points

- **Market size.** Oliveira puts skincare at over $200 billion, with anti-aging around $80 billion and, in her estimate, growing to about $150 billion by 2032.
- **Pivot from testing to discovery.** The founders started out growing human tissue to replace animal testing, then built a platform to measure whether existing products changed skin age. Oliveira says most did not, and some made it worse, which led the company to search for its own molecules.
- **Screening approach.** Oliveira describes starting with a university library of 200 antimicrobial peptides, finding four hits, then using an algorithm to generate about 700 variants; over 900 peptides were tested in total. The resulting OS-01 peptide is 10 amino acids long, which she says is small enough to penetrate the skin, and is covered by a patent on its motif.
- **Claimed results.** According to Oliveira, OS-01 cut the senescent-cell load by up to 40% in lab tests, and a 12-month human study showed a 3.3-year reduction in measured skin age; a larger cohort study is under way.
- **Roadmap and partnerships.** The team of about 30 in San Francisco is focused on peptides for the near term, sees cell reprogramming as still hard to bring to consumers, and works with AI companies that generate candidate molecules while OneSkin's platform tests and validates them.
- **Competing approaches.** Oliveira views red light therapy as complementary and is cautious about exosome products, citing reproducibility, stability and penetration problems.
