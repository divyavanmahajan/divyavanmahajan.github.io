## Thesis

Recorded at the 2025 Abundance Summit, Peter Diamandis talks with Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, who now runs CloudKitchens. Kalanick describes his career as "digitizing the physical world". He says innovators win by finding valuable truths that nobody else knows yet, building trust with the people affected by the change, and reducing risk instead of chasing it.

## Key points

- **Atoms-based computers.** Kalanick maps the parts of a computer onto the physical world: manufacturing stands in for the CPU, real estate for storage, and logistics for the network. In his view, Uber digitized the network layer. He says CloudKitchens aims to make prepared, delivered meals cost close to cooking with groceries from the store.
- **Founding and staging.** Kalanick says the Uber idea came from Garrett Camp in Paris. Uber ran in San Francisco for a year before expanding to New York. He says CloudKitchens' "parallel multicontinental deployment" went too wide, and that kitchens could not make money in India.
- **Resistance to change.** Kalanick calls resistance to change a natural mechanism. He argues that building trust while making change turns "adversaries into advocates" and lets a company move faster.
- **Innovation formula.** Kalanick says innovation equals progress divided by risk, where risk means time, money and reputation. He sees iteration as a way to lower risk. Truth, trust and passion are his current company's values.
- **Bits AI vs atoms AI.** Kalanick separates chatbots ("bits AI") from AI that acts in the physical world, such as Waymo cars and humanoids. He expects that the second will need new kinds of models. He predicts AI will disrupt traditional consulting.
- **Advice for founders and investors.** Kalanick says founders should quit only if they stop believing, are not the right person, or risk serious harm to themselves. He argues that investors should focus on capital allocation and "do no harm." On whether AI will replace jobs, he says he is cautiously optimistic, partly because "robots don't have bank accounts."
