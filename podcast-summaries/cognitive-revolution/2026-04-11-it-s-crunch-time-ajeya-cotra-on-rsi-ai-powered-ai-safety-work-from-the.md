## Thesis

In this 80,000 Hours cross-post, interviewed by Rob Wiblin, Ajeya Cotra argues that once AI largely automates AI research there may be only a short "crunch time" before systems are beyond human control. She thinks the labs' shared plan of using each AI generation to align and control the next is sound in principle, but likely to fail unless transparency gives early warning and labs actually move AI labour from capabilities to safety. In his intro, Nathan Labenz says crunch time has arguably already begun.

## Key points

- **A watered-down AGI.** Cotra recalls a DealBook panel where seven of ten panellists expected AGI by 2030, yet eight thought AI would create more jobs than it destroyed. She expects "top human expert dominating AI" in the early 2030s and says the world of 2050 could differ from today as much as today differs from the hunter-gatherer era.
- **Why forecasts diverge.** Sceptics anchor on a century of roughly 2% growth; others argue AI closing the full loop, from software to chips to robots, removes that ceiling. METR's uplift RCT found AI slowed developers down, but she values such real-world data over saturating benchmarks.
- **Transparency asks.** She wants labs to publish their highest internal benchmark scores on a fixed schedule, the share of pull requests mostly written and reviewed by AI, and serious misalignment incidents. She prefers public disclosure to government-only reporting, citing New York's RAISE Act and California's SB 53.
- **Redirect, not just pause.** At the brink, Cotra would steer AI labour towards alignment, cyber defence, biodefence and better collective reasoning. She treats a pause as a matter of degree and would prefer the transition take ten or more years instead of one.
- **Funders' role.** More than 80% of Open Philanthropy's grants pay human salaries; she expects a shift towards API credits and GPU time, hedged against compute prices by holding assets such as Nvidia. She also describes a $25 million agent-benchmark RFP, a burnout sabbatical and a METR work trial.
