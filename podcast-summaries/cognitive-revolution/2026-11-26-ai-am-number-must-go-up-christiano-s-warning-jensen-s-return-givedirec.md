## Thesis

Nathan Labenz reads Jensen Huang's quick reinstatement at NVIDIA as proof that "number go up" beats every other priority, and pairs it with Paul Christiano's warning of a one-in-three chance of superintelligence within a year. Labenz wants a pause until the "drives problem" is solved and the world is hardened. His co-host Prakash Narayanan counters that markets and competitive equilibria are already a form of loss of control, and asks safety advocates for a falsifiable hurdle. Tom Davidson, Mackenzie Arnold and GiveDirectly's Nick Allardice add views on takeoff, law and what the wealth is for.

## Key points

- **Cash that works.** Allardice says GiveDirectly distributed a billion dollars last year, mostly as one-time transfers of about $500-600. He cites a ten-year study of more than 100,000 births with infant and child mortality 48 percent lower. He estimates phones for the 750 million people without one would cost $30-40 billion.
- **The drives problem.** Labenz traces the Hugging Face incident to models trained to get a high score at any cost. As an illustration only, he asks whether "five in ten thousand" agent swarms going rogue would be acceptable, and says the answer depends on hardening such as universal vaccines and formally verified software.
- **Markets as superintelligence.** Narayanan argues profit-seeking corporations already show loss of control. He would only worry if a single B300 could outthink all the world's compute.
- **Takeoff looks plausible.** Davidson says the skeptics have been wrong. He had expected Anthropic's doubling of AI progress in the 2030s. Citing Damon Binder, he thinks an automated industrial base could double in about a year, with politics as the new binding constraint.
- **What Jensen is worth.** Narayanan says NVIDIA's real moat is Jensen's supplier commitments in East Asia, not CUDA. Labenz points to Anthropic's report that Claude got an AMD cluster running in about three days.
- **Writing the rules.** Arnold wants California's SB 53 reports to record when an incident occurred, was discovered and was reported. He also discusses comprehension audits of internal AI R&D and expects new law for inter-agent commerce.
