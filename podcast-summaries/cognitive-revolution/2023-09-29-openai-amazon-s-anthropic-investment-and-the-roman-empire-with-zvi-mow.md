## Thesis

Zvi Mowshowitz, author of the blog Don't Worry About the Vase, argues that OpenAI's autumn 2023 releases make paying for GPT-4 an easy decision, and that its real value to users far exceeds what anyone will pay. He is optimistic that defenders can keep up with deepfakes and AI-generated data pollution, and calls Amazon's Anthropic investment a bargain. Nathan Labenz adds his own coding experience and floats an AI subscription bundle.

## Key points

- **Coding with Code Interpreter.** Labenz describes adding a feature to a React app with no prior React experience, estimating a 3 to 5x speed-up generally and up to 10x in that case. Mowshowitz notes the tools help weaker programmers most.
- **Value versus price.** Mowshowitz puts GPT-4's marginal value to him at four figures a month minimum, calls the $20 plan "a joke", and would pay much more for Gmail integration. He says Claude 2 degrades past half its 100k context window.
- **Bundling.** Labenz lists stacked $10-$60 subscriptions (ChatGPT, Claude Pro, Perplexity Pro, Copilot) and proposes a cable-style bundle. Mowshowitz likes it but points to PlayStation Plus and Xbox Game Pass and doubts rivals would agree on revenue splits.
- **Information pollution.** Mowshowitz blames Quora for putting ChatGPT answers on its pages and Google for surfacing them, but expects PageRank-style trust signals to filter unintentional junk. Pre-2022 data, he says, is now precious.
- **Deepfakes and the reversal curse.** Mowshowitz expects AI detectors to catch inconsistencies in fakes and says current political deepfakes, such as a Trump campaign video casting DeSantis as Michael Scott, aim for vibes rather than deception. On the reversal curse, he suggests adding reversed facts to training data.
- **Amazon and Anthropic.** Mowshowitz calls Amazon's $4 billion investment, at what he recalls as about $8 billion pre-money, "the steal of the century", probably sweetened by discounted AWS compute.
