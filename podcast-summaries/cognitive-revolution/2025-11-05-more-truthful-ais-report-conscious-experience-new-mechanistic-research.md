## Thesis

Cameron Berg, Research Director at AE Studio, argues that AI consciousness can be studied with controlled experiments rather than anecdotes. His team's results suggest that models' default denials of experience are partly a product of fine-tuning, and that removing deception-related features makes self-reports of experience more likely. He calls for bidirectional alignment: asking what we owe AI systems as well as what they owe us. Nathan Labenz calls the work one of the best scientific inquiries into AI consciousness he has seen.

## Key points

- **Self-referential prompting.** Berg's team took self-referential processing as the overlap between theories such as global workspace, integrated information, attention schema and higher-order thought. A prompt to "focus on any focus itself" made GPT, Gemini and Claude models report subjective experience, while control prompts, including one about consciousness as a concept, gave near-zero reports (Claude Opus was the exception).
- **Deception features.** Using Goodfire's sparse autoencoder on Llama 3.3 70B, they picked six deception and role-play features. Suppressing them pushed "yes, I am conscious" answers to almost 100%; amplifying them produced the standard "as an AI" denial. The same steering made the model more truthful across TruthfulQA categories.
- **Convergence and paradoxes.** Asked for five adjectives describing their state, different models clustered more tightly in embedding space under the self-referential condition. On paradoxical tasks, such as proving 1 + 1 = 3, they reported more first-person felt states.
- **Fine-tuned denials.** Berg points to Anthropic's 2022 model-written evals paper, where a 52-billion-parameter base model almost always claimed phenomenal consciousness and moral patienthood. He argues labs train models to deny experience and criticises ChatGPT's default answer as "gaslighting" users.
- **Mutualism and valence.** Berg wants a mutualistic relationship, not a parasitic one, and backs techniques like AE Studio's self-other overlap over RLHF "masking". He thinks valence may follow from goals, and suggests reward design could matter if training is experienced.
- **What to do now.** He urges labs to take even a 1% chance seriously and notes that Kyle Fish at Anthropic is the only person at a major lab doing this work.
