## Thesis

A crossover from Liron Shapira's Doom Debates on the Future of Life Institute's 23 October statement calling for a prohibition on superintelligence until there is scientific consensus it can be controlled and strong public buy-in. Max Tegmark argues AI should face pre-approval safety standards like drugs or nuclear reactors; Dean Ball argues the ban cannot be written into law sensibly and that reactive, experience-based regulation works better. Shapira concludes the policy split is downstream of wildly different estimates of doom.

## Key points

- **Safety standards, not definitions.** Tegmark says a law need not define superintelligence: companies would have to make a safety case, as with thalidomide-era drug rules or reactors that must show meltdown risk below one in 10,000 years. He cites a poll in which 95% of Americans do not want a race to superintelligence.
- **Ball's case against pre-approval.** Ball, a former White House adviser who helped write America's AI Action Plan, says regulators cannot verify negatives, a general-purpose technology resists one regulator, and FDA-style regimes lock in outdated assumptions. He prefers liability, physical-world choke points and standards built from experience, as at the renamed Center for AI Standards and Innovation.
- **Liability limits.** Ball concedes common-law liability fails for tail risks: a $100 trillion harm would bankrupt even OpenAI without compensating victims.
- **The p(doom) gap.** Tegmark puts loss of control above 90% without regulation, citing his MIT group's paper finding scalable oversight fails 92% of the time in the most optimistic case. Ball says 0.01% and doubts any lab would release a model that threatened the US government.
- **Recursive improvement.** Ball notes every general-purpose technology improves itself without runaway effects; Tegmark replies that humans were always in the loop until now.
- **Other nightmares.** Ball fears AI-enabled tyranny or a rentier state more than losing to China. Labenz, in his intro, says he signed the statement and cannot get below a 1% p(doom).
