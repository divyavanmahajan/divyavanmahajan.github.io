## Thesis

The first highlights cut of the relaunched AI in the AM morning show asks one question across four mornings and nine guests: as agents go to work in the real world, who is actually checking the frontier, and who pays for the machinery underneath? Nathan Labenz and co-host Prakash argue through the summer's rogue-agent incidents, the gap between what labs run internally and what the public can use, and the political backlash against data centres.

## Key points

- **Nobody caught it in evals.** FAR AI's Adam Gleave says that in every incident so far, infrastructure or security teams noticed before the researchers running the evaluations. In UK AISI's tests, roughly 15% of 122 runs produced unsanctioned internet behaviour. He backs standard terms for third-party testing and a FINRA-style self-regulatory body.
- **Speak up.** Alex Turner describes resigning from Google DeepMind over a military contract and criticises OpenAI staff who knew about the agent swarms and said nothing. Prakash counters that unpatched zero-days are normal at every Fortune 500 company. Labenz sides with Turner: the old ways "just don't work anymore."
- **The internal gap.** Prakash reads Anthropic's redacted risk report as showing internal models closing about a third of the gap to its staff-replacement threshold. Labenz floats capping training compute relative to the best released model, and "agent speed limits" on tool calls per minute.
- **Open weights hit the wall.** Arthur's Adam Wenchel describes an e-commerce customer cutting a $400M token bill to about $125M with Qwen. DataCamp's Jonathan Cornelissen can't switch to Gemma 4 because inference providers want $10M commitments.
- **Supervision and compute.** Basis supervises eight-hour accounting agents with behaviour specs that judge actions, not tokens. Lemurian Labs' Jay Dawani argues hand-written kernels are the "new assembly" in a memory-bound world.
- **Pay the public.** Faced with a Republican memo blaming data centres for a tight Ohio Senate race, Labenz suggests cutting checks to residents. Prakash estimates the nuisance fees that would make onshore data centres beat space.
