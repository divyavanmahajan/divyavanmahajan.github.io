## Thesis

Emad Mostaque, founder of Stability AI and now Intelligent Internet, presents his book The Last Economy to host Nathan Labenz. He argues that economics built on scarcity, GDP and the value of human labour will register cheap, abundant AI intelligence as job loss and poverty. His answer is "intelligence theory", a framework borrowed from generative AI loss functions, plus a new social contract in which AI for health, education, finance and government is owned by citizens rather than a few companies.

## Key points

- **Abundance trap and metabolic rift.** Mostaque says AI workers do not eat, need housing or pay taxes, so knowledge work will be displaced even as lives improve. Labenz adds that cost fell from about $60 per million input tokens for GPT-3 to roughly $1.25-1.50 for GPT-5.
- **Limits of the caring economy.** Mostaque agrees people will still value human connection (the "Taylor Swift economy"), but says work has become identity and community has hollowed out, so a new social contract is needed for people to become "meaning makers".
- **Three laws of living systems.** Flow, openness and resilience. He cites Tokugawa Japan (1633-1853) as a closed monoculture and warns that AIs sharing similar latent spaces could all fail together.
- **Three futures.** Digital feudalism, fragmentation into national AIs, or symbiosis. Mostaque expects frontier labs to keep their best models for internal and first-party use, pointing to OpenAI revenue projections in which API income shrinks.
- **FoundationCoin.** Equity schemes fail on the numbers, he says: a 10% US stake in a $100 trillion OpenAI pays about $1,500 a year per American. He proposes a Bitcoin-like currency mined by citizen-owned national champions that provide compute for free universal AI.
- **Next 1000 days.** Mostaque names the Grok 5 training run as the indicator to watch for scaling, and argues the next three years decide who owns the interface layer before job displacement shows up as a "sand pile collapse".
