## Thesis

In this ChinaTalk cross-post, Zixuan Li of Z.ai (Zhipu AI) describes a Chinese lab that sees itself as an upstart chasing US leaders rather than a peer. He argues that open weights are a practical necessity for global adoption, since Western enterprises will not send data to a Chinese API, and that current architectures are approaching a wall that more data alone will not cross.

## Key points

- **From chat to coding.** Li says Z.ai once optimised for Chatbot Arena (ranking roughly 6th to 9th in 2024) but shifted in 2025 to coding and agentic use, which is where GLM 4.5 and 4.6 earned recognition through tools like Claude Code and Kilo Code.
- **Open weights as marketing.** Li says Z.ai's flagship was closed in 2024; after DeepSeek R1 it learned it could open-source and still earn revenue via APIs and its subscription-based GLM coding plan.
- **Small, hands-on core team.** Li puts the core training team at 100 to 200 people, many of them PhD students, and says team leads and even the founder run experiments themselves because the field changes too quickly to delegate.
- **Markets and compute.** Overseas services run from Singapore while training stays in China. Li says India has the most overseas users, but the US generates 50% of overseas revenue.
- **Role-play and translation.** Li credits post-training data for GLM 4.6's strength at long-prompt role-play, and says its Chinese-English translation, including memes and emoji, is on par with Gemini 2.5 Pro.
- **A wall, and fast releases.** Li says GLM is a 355B-parameter model and that 90% of small-scale experiments fail. Z.ai open-sources models within hours of finishing training, which he admits leaves partners little time to integrate.
