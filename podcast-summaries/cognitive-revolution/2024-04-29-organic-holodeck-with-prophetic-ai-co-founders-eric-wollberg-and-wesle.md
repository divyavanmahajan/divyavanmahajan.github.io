## Thesis

This is a cross-post from the Emergent Behavior podcast, where the AI-voiced host Ate-a-Pi interviews Prophetic AI co-founders Eric Wollberg and Wesley Berry. They argue that lucid dreaming is simply prefrontal cortex activity during REM, so a closed-loop device can induce it on demand. Their headband, the Halo, reads EEG, has a model choose ultrasound targets, and stimulates until it sees the signature of lucidity. They see lucid dreaming as the first of several brain states the same hardware could deliver.

## Key points

- **How the Halo works.** Wollberg explains that the headband waits for the EEG signature of REM, then switches on transcranial focused ultrasound (TFUS) aimed at the prefrontal cortex and watches for a gamma spike that signals lucidity. It targets one REM cycle a night, about 20 minutes, and stops when REM ends.
- **The Morpheus 1 model.** Berry says Morpheus 1 was trained on open-source simultaneous EEG and fMRI data from people who were awake, to model an active prefrontal cortex. The EEG samples at 1000 Hz, the ultrasound runs at about 500 kHz, and pulses come at about 40 per second. He treats spatial targets as more important than timing.
- **Data pipeline.** Wollberg describes a collaboration with Martin Dresler's lab at the Donders Institute, running about four lucid-dreaming neuroimaging sessions a week, plus a planned "qualia factory" imaging lab. Berry expects reinforcement learning from user ratings and EEG responses to personalise the device at scale.
- **Demand and roadmap.** Wollberg reports $2.4 million in refundable reservations with $0 spent on marketing, and 3,500 beta sign-ups in five days. Stimulation tests start in the spring, and he hopes to launch with focus and positive mood alongside lucid dreaming.
- **Safety and regulation.** Prophetic makes no medical claims. Wollberg puts its output at 100 to 200 milliwatts against a regulatory threshold of 720, "less than a sonogram".
- **Privacy and critics.** Wollberg argues for "noetic sovereignty" over dream content, and dismisses purists who think lucid dreaming should only be earned through practice.
