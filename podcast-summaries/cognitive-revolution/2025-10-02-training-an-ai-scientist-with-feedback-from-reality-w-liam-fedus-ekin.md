## Thesis

In this a16z crosspost, Anjney Midha talks with Periodic Labs co-founders Liam Fedus (co-creator of ChatGPT) and Ekin Dogus Cubuk (formerly at Google DeepMind). They argue that LLMs will not advance physics and chemistry by scaling on internet data alone. Models have to iterate against real experiments, so an automated lab becomes the reward function. Their North Star is a high-temperature superconductor, and the commercial path runs through advanced manufacturing.

## Key points

- **Nature as the reward function.** Fedus traces ChatGPT's path from RLHF on human preferences to more precise math and code graders. He says Periodic replaces those graders with physical experiments, so the experiment corrects any error in the simulator.
- **Why literature is not enough.** Fedus gives three reasons: the data is noisy (one reported property spanned many orders of magnitude), negative results are rarely published, and science needs the ability to act and iterate. Cubuk adds that out-of-domain performance can improve on a power law so shallow that it is "basically useless."
- **Superconductivity as North Star.** Cubuk notes the best ambient-pressure superconductor today sits around 135 Kelvin. Reaching higher would require autonomous synthesis and characterization along the way. The first lab uses powder synthesis, which cheap robots can do.
- **Team and culture.** The team is roughly 30 people, about half ML researchers and half physical scientists. They hold weekly cross-teaching sessions and keep a "no stupid questions" culture. An advanced degree is not required; Fedus says mission alignment is the biggest hiring filter.
- **Commercial path.** Fedus describes copilots for engineers in space, defense and semiconductors, sold land-and-expand on well-scoped problems. He argues these customers need mid-training and high-compute RL, not just retrieval over their documents.
- **Academia.** Cubuk announces an advisory board, including ZX Shen and Steve Kivelson (Stanford), Mercouri Kanatzidis (Northwestern) and Chris Wolverton, plus a grant program for academic work.
