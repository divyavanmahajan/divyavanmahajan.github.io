## Thesis

Roy Lee, founder and CEO of Cluely, argues that only output matters: if AI can do a task, people should let it, and norms that police how work gets done (exams, coding interviews, disclosure of AI use) will become obsolete. Nathan Labenz shares much of the AI-maximalist view but keeps pressing on trust, authenticity and the value of effort, where Lee concedes that some things are valued precisely for the human input.

## Key points

- **Product and strategy.** Lee describes Cluely as a desktop "pane of glass" overlay with access to system audio and microphone, feeding real-time notes and suggestions. He sees it as a land grab for the interaction pattern, pointing to Apple's Liquid Glass, so that Cluely holds the user data when a much more capable model arrives.
- **Norms that will dissolve.** Lee predicts that people will trade data privacy for efficiency, and that competition with China will erode copyright and training-data restrictions.
- **The Columbia story.** Lee built Interview Coder, used it through an Amazon interview, posted the video, and was suspended after Amazon complained. He refused to apologise, citing Google's claim that AI writes 90% of its code.
- **Output over input.** Lee is fine with overemployment and rejects Yuval Noah Harari's proposal that AI must always identify itself, calling it unenforceable. When Labenz raises a child's Father's Day drawing, Lee agrees that in such cases the human effort is itself the output that matters.
- **Assessment and learning.** Lee wants hiring based on past work evaluated by AI rather than coding riddles, mentioning Mercor. He argues that "cheat on everything" still means learning whatever the AI cannot yet do.
- **Marketing and hiring.** Cluely runs an in-house film studio and UGC campaigns aimed at college students. Lee offers comp packages of up to a million dollars to attract full-stack engineers.
