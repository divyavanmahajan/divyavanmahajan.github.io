## Thesis

Joel Predd, Ben Boudreaux and Matt Chessen of RAND's Center for the Geopolitics of AGI argue that uncertainty about superintelligence is too deep to commit to one national strategy. Committing early forecloses other paths, so they propose a "freedom of action" strategy: invest now in capabilities that serve many strategies, especially those with long lead times, and in tools that show where the world actually stands.

## Key points

- **Seven archetypes, five pivots.** Chessen lists dominance, co-development, preparedness, moratorium, deterrence, continuity of society and acceleration, each with a "pathology" such as value lock-in or global surveillance. Five questions sort people into camps: is danger close, is coexistence feasible, is restraint feasible, is a decisive advantage reachable, is suppression feasible.
- **Priors drive outcomes.** Predd says hundreds of RAND tabletop games show players' going-in beliefs determine results. Unlike Los Alamos before Trinity, nobody can run a calculation today on whether danger is close.
- **Dominance is hard to sustain.** Predd cites Phil Root's game theory: a race defaults to mutual sabotage unless AI yields a "wonder weapon"; some argue Mythos-class cyber models were the first. Boudreaux adds that theft, distillation or diffusion can erode any lead.
- **Co-development objection.** Labenz says a wise humanity would pick co-development with a moratorium fallback. Chessen replies that humanity is not a unitary actor and co-developing with China risks locking in non-democratic values.
- **Cost and signalling.** Predd says every archetype except acceleration means a society-wide reorientation on the scale of the nuclear era or post-9/11. Chessen describes a sprint to cost a 106-item Security Architecture Catalog and warns that chip controls may have made a domestic chip industry existential for China.
- **Human backstops and red lines.** Boudreaux wants credible shutdown capacity, AI-free spaces and an "AI Sabbath". Chessen expects a rough patch in cyber over 12 to 24 months as Mythos-class capabilities reach open-weight models, and names rushing AI into nuclear command and control as a red line.
