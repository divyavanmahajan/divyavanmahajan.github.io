## Thesis

Andy Masley, director of Effective Altruism DC and a former physics teacher, argues that fears about AI's energy and water footprint are largely overblown at the level of individual use, and that AI's climate impact will mostly come from how it is used rather than from data centers. Nathan Labenz broadly agrees. Both see the large data center buildout as a real but manageable challenge, with local issues such as air pollution and electricity prices deserving the most attention.

## Key points

- **Per-prompt math.** Masley estimates a median ChatGPT prompt at 0.3 to 0.6 watt-hours, or about 0.3 grams of CO2 including training and hardware: roughly one second of a microwave. You would need about 1,000 prompts a day to raise your emissions by 1%.
- **Everyday comparisons.** A 20-mile sedan trip (3-4 kg CO2) equals about 10,000 prompts; Labenz adds that a hamburger or a hot shower lands in the same range and a cross-country flight near a million.
- **Water myths.** Masley traces the "bottle of water per prompt" claim to a Washington Post article and puts the real figure near two milliliters. Most of the projected "half of the UK's water" figure is power-plant withdrawal that is returned, not consumed.
- **Chips and grid scale.** An eight-GPU H100 node costs about $300,000 but uses about $35,000 of electricity over four years; Masley says the carbon is mostly in the electricity. Labenz works out that a 1 GW data center equals about a million homes, and an 80 GW buildout would add a little over 1% to global energy use of about 6,000 GW.
- **Real local concerns.** Masley ranks air pollution from on-site gas turbines, citing Memphis, as his top worry. Citing Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, he says data centers did not drive the 35% rise in average US electricity bills since 2020.
- **Desert siting.** Masley says Arizona golf courses use around 30 times the water of the state's data centers, which generate about 50 times more tax revenue per gallon.
