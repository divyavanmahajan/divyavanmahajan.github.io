## Thesis

Sherif Mansour, Head of AI at Atlassian, argues that "AI slop" (output that is technically correct but generic, because everyone uses the same models) is avoided only when teams inject their own taste, knowledge and workflows into AI teammates. He tells Nathan Labenz that people will shift from doing the work to architecting how humans and agents do it, and that chat is a universal but often poor interface that specialist software will build on rather than replace.

## Key points

- **Mostly non-technical users.** Mansour says most Atlassian users now work in HR, finance, marketing and legal, and that about three and a half million people use its AI capabilities.
- **Taste, knowledge, workflow.** In his procurement example, a team gives a Rovo agent its Confluence contract-review checklists and SharePoint content, then places it in a Jira workflow where it drafts a response before a human reviews it.
- **RAG plus the Teamwork Graph.** Field-level permissions make enterprise RAG hard, Mansour explains. For "what did my team do last week", Atlassian traverses a graph of teams, Jira items, Confluence pages, Figma designs and pull requests, weighted by collaboration signals; RAG still suits questions like the annual-leave process. Old content is decayed by activity after Rovo once surfaced a stale goal of his.
- **Gateway and cost discipline.** Atlassian runs well over 70 AI features through an AI gateway mixing cloud and locally hosted open-source models. Mansour sees general-purpose models commoditizing for 80-90% of knowledge work and warns customers against using LLMs where deterministic automation will do.
- **Skeptical of the one-person unicorn.** Small AI-native startups he meets still orchestrate agents in workflows and keep hiring. Atlassian has deliberately shifted hiring toward AI-native juniors, since behavior change is hardest for senior staff.
- **Browser and adoption.** He compares The Browser Company acquisition to how Slack and Teams diverged from consumer messengers like ICQ. On adoption, he rejects mandates as threatening and cites an AI builders week where about a thousand product, design, engineering and research staff blocked time together to tinker.
