## Thesis

Micha Kaufman, founder and CEO of Fiverr, argues that AI raises the floor for everyone: easy tasks disappear and average work is no longer enough, so people who master AI will replace those who do not. He also worries that AI trained on everyone's output without credit or pay will stop people from creating. Fiverr Go is his attempt at a different design, where creators own and earn from models of their own style. Nathan Labenz praises the values-driven approach but questions whether it can last once AI matches human quality.

## Key points

- **The wake-up memo.** Kaufman's internal memo says AI is coming for everyone's job, his own included. He compares the current wave to the dot-com era ("pets.com" becoming "pets.ai") and expects 999 of 1,000 AI startups to fail. Anyone "still working like it's 2024" is doing something wrong.
- **Freelancers adapt faster.** Kaufman says freelancers without a corporate safety net have picked up AI faster than Fiverr's own staff. The marketplace grew from 6 categories in 2010 to close to 800 and serves about 90% of the Fortune 500.
- **Why people hire people.** His framework has three reasons: buying back time, getting a competitive edge from someone with better taste, and handing off complex multidisciplinary work. Since everyone has AI, it alone confers no edge.
- **Fiverr Go.** It has a personal assistant trained on each seller's profile and past conversations, which Kaufman says converts better than sellers do themselves. It also has creator models for about 60 categories, released only where they score at least 90% on matching the creator's style. He points to the 1710 copyright act and Michelangelo's workshop to argue that creators need credit and a share of the reward.
- **Leading and hiring.** Kaufman went back to coding and built in 3 hours what a team had estimated at 3 weeks. Asked by Labenz about junior roles, he says how well candidates use current AI tools matters more than seniority, and that you can learn most of what you need online without a university degree.
