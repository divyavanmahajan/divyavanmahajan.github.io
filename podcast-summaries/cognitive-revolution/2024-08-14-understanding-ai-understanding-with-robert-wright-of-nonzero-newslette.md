## Thesis

In this crossover, Robert Wright of Nonzero interviews Nathan Labenz, prompted by Labenz's debate with Martin Casado of a16z. Both argue that language models have real, if alien, forms of understanding that can be defined without settling consciousness. Labenz says the burden of proof sits with those who claim there is nothing to worry about, but he is not yet calling for a pause.

## Key points

- **Understanding without experience.** Wright defines understanding as processing information broadly the way human brains do and says multimodal models make Searle's Chinese Room argument "dead". Labenz points to Anthropic's 10 million-plus sparse autoencoder features behind Golden Gate Claude and to edits that changed which sport Michael Jordan played.
- **Beyond the human ceiling.** Asked whether training on human text caps capability, Labenz cites AlphaGo, protein folding, AI weather forecasts and Sora's intuitive physics as signs that systems can go past human data.
- **Hyperscale pauser.** Labenz wants AI doctors deployed fast but caution about trillion-dollar training runs. Given responsible scaling policies and monitoring for deception, he is not worried about a GPT-5 class model.
- **Open source and China.** Wright asks why open weights are released while chips are restricted. Labenz, a self-described China dove, fears the chip ban could push China towards an AlphaGo-style war AI without human values, and calls the Llama 405B release an olive branch.
- **Where values come from.** Claude refused to write a denial-of-service script even for a hypothetical Myanmar resistance fighter. The purely helpful GPT-4 Labenz red-teamed in 2022 suggested targeted assassinations, which suggests values come from later training.
- **Mixture of architectures.** Labenz explains that Mamba-style state space models run in constant time per step while attention costs grow quadratically. Hybrids work best, KANs may follow, and interpretability methods such as the Othello GPT probe have transferred.
