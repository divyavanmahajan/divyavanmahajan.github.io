## Thesis

Zvi Mowshowitz, author of Don't Worry About the Vase, tells Nathan Labenz that Gemini Advanced has become his default model and that Gemini 1.5 Pro's long context opens up real uses. He is unimpressed by Sora as a product. His bigger worry is that capabilities are outrunning control: a GPT-5-level model could make today's agent scaffolding work, while safety plans like defence in depth will not hold up against smarter systems.

## Key points

- **Gemini as default.** Mowshowitz, given early access, says Gemini answers the question asked with less padding, and likes its Google button for checking sources. Labenz found Claude the most direct on a contract negotiation.
- **Context hyperinflation.** Labenz traces context windows from 4,000 tokens to 1,000,000 for Gemini 1.5 Pro, with 10,000,000 teased. Mowshowitz wants to load all 50-plus of his weekly posts, or a 2-hour video, and query them.
- **Sora skepticism.** Mowshowitz thinks Sora's creative and commercial value is overestimated and that its physics is "vibey" heuristics, not a real engine. He suspects OpenAI timed the announcement to bury Gemini 1.5; Labenz notes Sam Altman denied this. Labenz sees value for Waymark in animating small businesses' still photos.
- **Application-layer safety.** Labenz describes AI calling apps that will place ransom calls and clone a voice from 15-30 seconds of audio. Mowshowitz says getting 90 of 100 apps to refuse helps a little, but over-refusal also pushes users toward permissive tools.
- **Open source and live players.** Mowshowitz notes his Manifold market on Mistral keeping a top model closed resolved yes within 2 days. He calls Altman's reported $7 trillion chip plan in the UAE inconsistent with the compute-overhang argument, calls Anthropic's sleeper agents paper scary, and doubts claims about Chinese models such as Ernie.
- **Fast inference.** Labenz reports Groq's LPU serving Mixtral at 500 tokens a second for about 27 cents per million tokens; Mowshowitz warns cheaper inference may encourage larger models.
