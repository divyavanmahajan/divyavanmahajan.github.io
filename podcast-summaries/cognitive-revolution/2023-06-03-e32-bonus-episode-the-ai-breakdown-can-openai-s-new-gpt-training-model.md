## Thesis

This bonus episode replays a 1 June 2023 edition of Nathaniel Whittemore's The AI Breakdown, introduced by Nathan Labenz. Whittemore surveys OpenAI's product, policy and roadmap news, then argues that its new research on rewarding each reasoning step, rather than only the final answer, is rare in offering better performance and better alignment at once.

## Key points

- **News brief.** Whittemore reports that Japan's education minister said the country would not enforce copyright on AI training data, which he links to Getty Images' lawsuit against Stability AI. He also covers Australia's AI consultation, the GPT4Tools paper and Paragraphica, a lensless camera that generates images from location and weather data.
- **Product and policy.** He lists the ChatGPT iOS app (now in 152 countries), shared links and plugin search, plus OpenAI's "Governance of Superintelligence" post, ten $100,000 democratic-inputs grants and a $1 million cybersecurity grant programme.
- **Altman's roadmap.** Citing Humanloop's Raza Habib, Whittemore says Sam Altman told developers OpenAI is heavily GPU-limited, which delays the 32k context window, fine-tuning and multimodality. Priorities were cheaper and faster GPT, longer context, a fine-tuning API and a stateful API. Altman reportedly said plugins lack product-market fit outside browsing.
- **Process supervision.** OpenAI's paper compares outcome supervision with process supervision on the MATH dataset: 78% versus about 71% of problems solved. OpenAI describes this as a "negative alignment tax" and says it gives more interpretable reasoning.
- **Alignment reading.** Whittemore quotes a Twitter commentator who called the work a step away from "paperclip maxing", likening it to teachers asking pupils to show their work.
- **Caveat.** As Nvidia's Jim Fan notes, the method needs much more human labelling. OpenAI released 800,000 step-level labels across 70,000 solutions to 12,000 problems.

## Quotes

> "For challenging step by step problems, give a reward at each step instead of a single reward at the end." — Jim Fan, read by Nathaniel Whittemore

> "it's not every day that we get something that shows both improved performance of AI models as well as better alignment." — Nathaniel Whittemore
