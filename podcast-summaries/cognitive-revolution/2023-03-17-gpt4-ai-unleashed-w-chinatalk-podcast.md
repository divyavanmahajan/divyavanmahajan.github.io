## Thesis

In a crossover "emergency" episode hosted by Jordan Schneider of ChinaTalk, Nathan Labenz, Zvi Mowshowitz and Matthew Mittelsteadt discuss the release of GPT-4. They broadly agree its direct impact will be positive. They differ on whether safety training can contain its risks, and on whether racing with China or industrial policy makes sense.

## Key points

- **What changed.** Labenz, who was a GPT-4 red teamer, points to a larger model, more RLHF with expert annotators, and 8,000 and 32,000 token context windows, the latter enough for a three-hour conversation.
- **Research tool.** Mittelsteadt found GPT-4 pushed back on loaded policy questions and that nearly all sources it cited existed. Schneider says it drafted a better fab financial model than a first-year McKinsey analyst.
- **Red teaming limits.** Schneider and Mittelsteadt note the report says mitigations were built mainly in English from a US-centric view, with roughly 50 red teamers. Mowshowitz is most worried by a report of GPT-4 hiring a human to solve a CAPTCHA, and argues harmful knowledge cannot be fully removed from a model.
- **Real-world risk.** Mittelsteadt argues many risks, such as chemical weapon recipes, are limited by real-world complexity and need governance beyond training. Labenz says the raw model he tested would have been locally dangerous.
- **Race dynamics.** Labenz sees cheap inference ($2 per million tokens) narrowing the field to a small group of players who can coordinate. Schneider adds ByteDance, Baidu, Alibaba and Tencent. Mittelsteadt doubts industrial policy can pick winners in a fast-moving field.
- **Hopes.** Mowshowitz highlights education, Mittelsteadt healthcare, and Labenz AI mediation and OpenAI's new open-source evals programme.
