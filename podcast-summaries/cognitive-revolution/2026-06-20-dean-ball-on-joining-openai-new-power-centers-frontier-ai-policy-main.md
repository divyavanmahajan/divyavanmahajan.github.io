## Thesis

Dean Ball, primary drafter of America's AI Action Plan, argues that frontier labs are a new centre of political and economic power, comparable to early modern finance, and that he cannot think clearly about governing them from outside. He announces he is joining OpenAI to lead a forward-looking policy team, while warning that secretive, centralised government control of frontier AI makes policy brittle and that broad diffusion is the best check on it.

## Key points

- **Action Plan scorecard.** Ball estimates the plan is 30% to 40% implemented after about 11 months, citing nuclear, FERC grid reform and military adoption. He says global export controls imposed with 90 minutes' notice confirm foreign partners' fear that the US will "turn off the models".
- **Secrecy and supply chain risk.** The Department of War's supply chain risk designation of Anthropic is before the DC Circuit, yet the NSA reportedly kept its contract and honoured Anthropic's red lines. Ball worries more about the cyber executive order's classified 30-day pre-deployment testing, arguing a society works as an information processing system that secrecy cripples.
- **States and surprises.** He notes California's SB 53, New York's RAISE Act and Illinois' SB 315 have converged on near-identical transparency language. World-simulation models gaining object permanence led him to expect dexterous robot manipulation solved within eight months.
- **The Fable ban.** Ball says Fable demolished a 70-page rebuttal to his FERC Order 1000 testimony, and calls the government's shifting justifications post hoc.
- **The OpenAI role.** His team will look 6 to 12 months ahead, separate from Chris Lehane's Global Affairs, and he keeps an independent writing voice. On recursive self-improvement his prior is continuity, but even a 10% or 20% chance of a discontinuous leap warrants planning; he backs an FTC "no-action letter" for narrowly scoped slowdown coordination.
- **Leverage and values.** He favours character over corrigibility, citing Confucian li and ren, and equity for households rather than government. Lab leverage comes from national-security value (Project Maven cut targeting teams from 2,000 people to 20) and broad diffusion. He says he would have resigned over the supply chain risk designation.
