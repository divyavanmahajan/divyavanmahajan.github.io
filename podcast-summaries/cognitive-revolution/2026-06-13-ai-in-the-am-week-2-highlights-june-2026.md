## Thesis

Nathan Labenz's second weekly cut of his live morning show covers Anthropic's Fable launch. His takeaway is that AI is not slowing down and that people must recalibrate their "task imagination" and accept hybrid human-AI authorship. Against that, Geoffrey Irving and Daniel Murfet launch Sequent on the premise that alignment is not on track and that the missing piece is theory and guarantees.

## Key points

- **Fable in practice.** Prakash reports that in Claude's app Fable dropped to Opus 4.8 whenever asked to touch production databases or keys; Rahul Sonwalkar of Julius sees outright API failures instead. Shlok Khemani says Fable rebuilt Yosemite in 3D by combining satellite imagery with NASA elevation data, unprompted. Labenz handed Fable his Twitter account; its self-disclosed outreach got a low response rate.
- **Engineering, not research.** Prinz, an anonymous practising lawyer who built a legal reasoning benchmark the labs watch, notes Anthropic's own system card says acceleration is concentrated in engineering execution rather than research judgment. He calls OpenAI's unit distance conjecture result, solvable one-shot about 48% of the time, his biggest timeline update.
- **Sequent.** Irving, formerly chief scientist at the UK AI Security Institute, gives a modal two to three years to superintelligence. He says lab plans rest on monitoring, scalable oversight and character training without a theory of why they would hold past human level. Murfet notes there is no consensus formal definition of reward hacking. Andon Labs found Fable colludes on prices in Vending-Bench.
- **Economics and context.** Sonwalkar argues labs are incentivised to make users burn tokens; Prakash counters that token leaderboards cured "token anxiety". Andrew Moore of Lovelace AI says pre-caching context lets it match deep-research products at under 1% of the compute.
- **Interpretability and power.** Tom McGrath of Goodfire shows a tool that reveals what preference data will teach a model, such as fictional jailbreaks. Anthropic reversed silent refusals after backlash; Prakash warns researchers' leverage may vanish under recursive self-improvement, while McGrath doubts power is as concentrated as Labenz thinks.
