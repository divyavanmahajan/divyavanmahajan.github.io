## Thesis

Emad Mostaque, former Stability AI CEO and founder of the Intelligent Internet, puts his own p(doom) at about 50% but argues that current models already "satisfice" for most human needs. In his view the priority is not racing to AGI but building open, transparent, locally specialised AI for health, education and government, which he calls universal basic AI. Host Nathan Labenz tests the energy maths, the funding model and the safety story behind it.

## Key points

- **Where the risk comes from.** Mostaque points to closed or open-weight models whose training data nobody can inspect (citing Qwen, Llama and Anthropic's sleeper-agent work), the lack of robotics standards, persuasive synthetic voice, and military AI trained not to seek truth.
- **Agency as the goal.** He wants AI optimised to strengthen human, community and children's agency rather than replace people. He warns that ad-funded assistants could become manipulative "best friends" for children.
- **Three-tier infrastructure.** Edge devices running 8B-class models on roughly 5 watts, distributed nodes at hospitals or banks for fine-tuning (128-256 H100s), and national "hypernodes" of a few thousand H100 equivalents that maintain gold-standard cultural and sector datasets. Labenz's estimate is single-digit dollars a year in electricity for an always-on 5-watt model.
- **Open data for regulated industries.** Mostaque argues black boxes do not belong in regulated industries. All models would be released under the MIT licence, and his main policy ask is mandatory data transparency wherever AI makes decisions in those sectors.
- **Proof of beneficial compute.** The company, about 26 people at the time, plans a coin modelled on Bitcoin (21 million coins) whose sale funds GPU clusters for cancer, autism and education work. Holders would get a say in where compute goes, and nations could buy reserves.
- **Pause and regulation.** He signed the 2023 pause letter but now calls a pause too late. He sees liability rules and compute speed limits as unworkable under competition, and expects labour displacement, such as in Philippine call centres, to be badly underestimated.
