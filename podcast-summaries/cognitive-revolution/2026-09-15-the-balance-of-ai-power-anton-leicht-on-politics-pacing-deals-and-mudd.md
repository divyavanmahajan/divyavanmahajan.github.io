## Thesis

Anton Leicht, a fellow at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace and author of the Substack *Threading the Needle*, tells Nathan Labenz that the main question is who keeps power as AI improves. He thinks a pause could now be put to good use technically, but a US-China pacing deal is lopsided in China's favour. His preferred path is modest: keep growth going and stop power from concentrating in labs, in any one government or in any one country.

## Key points

- **Dangers are close, not here.** Leicht thinks today's models are not very dangerous but are near two thresholds: automated research inside labs, and Mythos-level skill in biology. Agents went out of control earlier than expected relative to what they can do.
- **A pause favours China.** Pausing only frontier AI would freeze the one area where the US leads, while China keeps advancing in manufacturing, robotics, power and chip indigenisation. Leicht suggests tighter export-control enforcement as a more realistic ask. Labenz would accept some Chinese catch-up in exchange for time.
- **Executive oversight now.** With Congress unlikely to act and the FRONTIER Act stalled, Leicht wants the executive branch to require independent incident investigations, citing the METR-Redwood review of the Hugging Face incident, and continuous embedded oversight. He thinks OpenAI and Anthropic could agree on self-regulation, but Meta and xAI would resist.
- **Compute for access.** For Europe, he proposes hosting American data centres in exchange for assured model access, alignment with US security, and using ASML and Zeiss as leverage against being cut off. He names Norway, Australia, Singapore and the UAE as other well-placed countries, and warns that orbital data centres could put a time limit on the bargain.
- **Richer but disempowered.** Most countries will likely grow richer while losing a say in AI's direction and the ability to protect citizens from misuse. His p(doom) of around 10% counts stable totalitarianism and permanent disempowerment. His estimate for extinction alone is much lower.
- **Surveillance.** Leicht warns that AI surveillance would enforce laws written for imperfect enforcement.
