## Thesis

Flo Crivello, founder of Lindy, calls himself an accelerationist on almost everything, from immigration to self-driving cars, but argues that advanced AI is different: a new form of life that could plausibly end in human extinction. He and Nathan Labenz welcome the White House executive order as a reasonable first step, while Crivello argues that real control has to come from choke points in chip manufacturing rather than from application-level rules.

## Key points

- **Accelerationist elsewhere.** Labenz criticises the Cruise shutdown and describes an eight-hour Tesla FSD trip with his 90-year-old grandmother; Crivello, who worked at Uber, says regulatory capture and cartels are "killing everything", but that AI is the exception.
- **AI as literal life.** Crivello places AI on a long march of increasing self-organisation of matter, from atoms to cells to brains, citing the book "Into the Cool", and sees no reason that march stops at humans.
- **Executive order.** He is "pleasantly surprised" that the order uses FLOPs and training compute (Labenz notes the 10 to the 26 threshold) but expects researchers to find loopholes through algorithmic improvements.
- **Kill switch at the silicon layer.** Crivello names ASML, TSMC and NVIDIA as choke points and proposes a GPS-like chip in every H100 so a central authority can shut down GPUs. Regulating applications cannot work, he argues, because any kid in a garage can build one.
- **Timelines.** He defines AGI as a seed AI that can recursively self-improve, says GPT-4 is the core component and the remaining gap is engineering, and points to NVIDIA's Eureka paper and Lindy writing its own integrations. He calls for ending open-sourcing of GPT-4-level models such as a future Llama 3, and expects ASI within 30 years.
- **China and consciousness.** Crivello thinks coordination with China is possible because there are few players in an iterated game. On subjective experience, he answers with the Zen "mu", rejecting the question.
