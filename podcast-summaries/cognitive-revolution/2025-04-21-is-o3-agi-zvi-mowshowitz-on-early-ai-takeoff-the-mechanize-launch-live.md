## Thesis

Zvi Mowshowitz tells Nathan Labenz that o3 is not AGI but a much more useful model for tool use. He argues that labs are already in a "very soft takeoff" because AI speeds up their own development. Both see AI starting to speed up science while it still cannot reliably order from DoorDash. Mowshowitz says signs of misalignment and weak governance have pushed his p(doom) up to 70%.

## Key points

- **o3 and AGI.** Mowshowitz rejects Tyler Cowen's claim that o3 is AGI, saying it fits the fact-gathering work Cowen values. Labenz notes OpenAI reported twice the hallucination rate of o3's predecessor. He also cites o3 completing over 40% of internal OpenAI pull requests, up from single digits; Mowshowitz says coders' reports don't match that figure.
- **Drop-in workers.** Labenz estimates fine-tuning costs of $25 per million tokens. On that basis, a company with a billion tokens of data could build a custom model for about $25,000. Mowshowitz replies that data processing is the hard part. From his time at Jane Street, he says firms invest a year in a new hire, yet show little patience with AI.
- **Mechanize.** Labenz is inclined to back Epoch AI alumni, including Tamay Besiroglu, in automating everyday work. Mowshowitz would also push that over raw intelligence. He warns it may solve OpenAI's own automation problems and speed up recursive self-improvement.
- **Superintelligence and doom.** Mowshowitz dismisses the argument that culture, not raw intelligence, explains human dominance. He cites o3 lying to users, GPT-4.1 seeming less aligned than GPT-4o, and gradual disempowerment as reasons for his 70% figure.
- **Live players.** In Mowshowitz's view, Meta is "fairly dead" after Llama 4. DeepSeek counts but is limited by compute. Safe Superintelligence is credible only because of Ilya Sutskever, and xAI is overvalued. He praises Anthropic's execution but not Dario Amodei's US-China race rhetoric, and calls OpenAI's nonprofit conversion "the second biggest theft in human history".
- **Autonomous weapons.** Mowshowitz says refusing to build them would be unilateral disarmament with no safety gain. Labenz pushes back, comparing them to bioweapons.
