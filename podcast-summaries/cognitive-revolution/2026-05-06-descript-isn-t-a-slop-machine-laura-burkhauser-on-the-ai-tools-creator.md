## Thesis

Laura Burkhauser, CEO of Descript, tells Nathan Labenz that slop is not bad art but the mass production of content for money, and that creators' hostility to AI is aimed mainly at generative video, not at reliable AI tools. Her strategy is to own models for editing recorded media, borrow frontier models for pure generation, and make sure users get a better result through Descript's Underlord agent than through a general AI agent alone.

## Key points

- **A hierarchy of hostility.** Burkhauser says users love deterministic-feeling AI features such as Studio Sound and green screen, want Underlord but find it limited, and dislike generative video, partly because of hype that it will "put a gun to Hollywood's head".
- **Choosing models.** Generative models must be available via fal. Defaults (currently Nano Banana Pro and Veo, with Seedance under consideration) are picked with external evals, internal panel reviews and A/B tests. She argues aesthetic judgement should stay with trusted humans, noting Studio Sound was built by a cellist with a good ear.
- **Build versus borrow.** Descript builds models for "augmented recorded media" such as Regenerate and jump-cut smoothing, but won't build pure generation models, which she expects Google to win.
- **Evaluating Underlord.** Real user queries are graded by LLM judges on three levels: didn't break anything (target near 100%), did what was asked (about 90%), and did it well (aiming for 80% by year end). Multimodal understanding, currently frame-by-frame captioning, is the top priority.
- **Agent design.** Underlord uses a general harness with low-level tools so new frontier models can be evaluated and shipped within 15 minutes; humans and the agent can use the same tools. She wants coding agents to "hire" Underlord via the API and MCP.
- **Pricing and the future.** Descript uses a shared credit pool and aims for under 5% of active users needing top-ups, expecting a shift to outcome pricing. She is sceptical of OpenAI's March 2028 autonomous researcher timeline, and expects artists to respond to AI as painters responded to the camera.
