## Thesis

Speaking to Latent Space, Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan explain why the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative is making the Biohub the main focus of its philanthropy. Their argument: major scientific advances follow new tools, so CZI should build tools, data and models rather than chase individual cures. Zuckerberg adds that the timeline for curing and preventing disease depends more on the pace of AI than on biology alone.

## Key points

- **Operate, don't just grant.** Zuckerberg contrasts NIH-style grants to individual investigators with CZI building its own institutes, where biologists, engineers and AI researchers from Stanford, UCSF and Berkeley sit together.
- **Data compounds.** Chan says the Human Cell Atlas took about 10 years to reach 125 million cells via CELLxGENE, with CZI contributing roughly 25% and the ecosystem 75%. The billion cell project now takes months at a fraction of the price.
- **Frontier biology plus frontier AI.** Zuckerberg contrasts AlphaFold, built on decades of public data, with designing measurement tools specifically to ground models. He calls this an "inversion": generating datasets to train models rather than for scientists to read.
- **AI talent and compute.** The Evolutionary Scale team (ESM3) is joining, with Alex Rives running the overall program. Zuckerberg says CZI was probably first to build a large-scale compute cluster for biology and plans to release frontier models.
- **Wet-lab feedback loop.** Chan says model predictions must be validated experimentally and fed back. Wet-lab throughput cannot match tens of thousands of benchmark tests, but models could de-risk bigger scientific bets.
- **Towards an omni model.** Zuckerberg expects virtual cell models to merge into a biological "omni model" over 5 to 10 years. Chan sees the clinical payoff as N-of-1 precision medicine, delivered through partners.
