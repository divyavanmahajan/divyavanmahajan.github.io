## Thesis

In this news roundup, recorded for Liron Shapira's Doom Debates, Nathan Labenz puts his P(doom) at "10 to 90%", weighted towards the low end, and accepts Shapira's challenge to state the stakes more plainly to his audience. Across image generation, coding jobs, valuations and alignment research, Labenz argues that no single fix will make AI safe. He expects a defence-in-depth patchwork, and says international trust matters more than technical verification.

## Key points

- **Risk and candour.** Labenz says he has "lost a little trust" in Dario Amodei over Anthropic's recent walkbacks. In his view a superpower race to weaponise AI could multiply the baseline risk by 10x.
- **GPT-4o images.** Waymark has not yet integrated image generation because outputs did not look like customers' own businesses. Labenz thinks GPT-4o crosses that threshold. He adds that since text-to-speech arrived, the work Waymark sends to its voice-over provider has fallen by more than 90%.
- **Coding jobs.** Responding to Amjad Masad's "learn to code" reversal, Labenz guesses (not as literal figures) that we may see 10 to 100 times more software built by 10 to 20% as many dedicated engineers. He cites Cursor's small team.
- **OpenAI's $300B valuation.** Labenz calls Gary Marcus's question about whether OpenAI pays off a fair one, and frames the valuation as a long-shot bet, for example a 90% chance of zero against a 10% chance of $3T. Shapira cites projections of revenue rising from about $14B to $100B a year by 2029.
- **Alignment approaches.** Shapira sees Emmett Shear's Softmax and its "organic alignment" as weakened by its analogy to cells and ants, which need to cooperate because they are weak. Labenz encourages any distinctive idea as another layer of defence. He is critical of Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence for sharing nothing.
- **Interpretability and treaties.** Labenz praises Anthropic's new interpretability paper but notes its replacement model reproduces only about 50% of model behaviour, and feature labels are subjective. On Kat Woods' verification ideas, he argues trust between great powers, not satellite monitoring, is the bottleneck.
