## Thesis

This is part 2 of Nathan Labenz's appearance on the 80,000 Hours podcast, interviewed by Rob Wiblin and recorded in late 2023. Labenz argues that the gap between what AI can do and what decision makers understand is itself a risk, and that the present is striking enough to warrant attention without forecasting. He also argues that the AI debate has turned tribal, and that the loudest anti-regulation voices may provoke the heavy-handed rules they fear.

## Key points

- **Capabilities people miss.** Labenz cites Med-PaLM 2, preferred over human doctors on 8 of 9 dimensions, GPT-4V outperforming humans on cases from 69 clinical pathological conferences, MultiOn's browser agent passing California's online driver's test, and AlphaFold assigning structures to hundreds of millions of proteins.
- **Self-driving and vision.** He says Tesla, Waymo and Cruise data suggest roughly human-level safety or better, based partly on an 8-hour trip in a friend's FSD car. He expects GPT-4V to unlock web agents, as screenshots cost about 1 cent for 12 images, far less than bloated HTML.
- **Discourse got worse.** Both blame the shift on rising government involvement and Twitter dynamics. Labenz criticises Marc Andreessen's "enemies" list and the hostile reaction to a voluntary responsible AI pledge signed by 35+ VC firms.
- **Regulation and liability.** Labenz supports rules that AI must identify itself, thinks Section 230 should not cover AI, and argues control is only feasible at the frontier, where clusters of H100s costing about $30,000 each leave a physical footprint.
- **Mundane harms.** He worries about spear phishing, information pollution, careless face recognition in policing and virtual friend apps built to maximise engagement.
- **Staying informed.** He recommends Zvi's weekly newsletter, Last Week in AI, The AI Breakdown, Latent Space, ChinaTalk and Dwarkesh, plus hands-on use of ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity.
