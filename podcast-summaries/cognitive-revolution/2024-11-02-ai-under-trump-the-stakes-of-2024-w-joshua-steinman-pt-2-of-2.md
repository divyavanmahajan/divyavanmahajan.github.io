## Thesis

Nathan Labenz argues that an AI arms race with China is a political choice, not an inevitability, and that avoiding it should decide the 2024 vote; he says he will vote for Kamala Harris, mainly as a rejection of Trump. Joshua Steinman, Trump's NSC senior director for cyber policy from 2017 to 2021, counters that competition with the Chinese Communist Party is "the physics of the environment" and that pressure, not trust-building, is the realistic policy.

## Key points

- **Labenz's case against Trump.** In his opening monologue Labenz agrees with Trump supporters on nuclear power, abundance and high-skilled immigration, but objects to Trump's escalation with China, undisciplined decision-making and zero-sum outlook during a possibly pivotal AGI window around 2027.
- **Trust versus pressure.** Labenz criticises the Biden-era chip ban as premature escalation and prefers earlier benefit sharing over the Aschenbrenner "situational awareness" plan of racing to decisive strategic advantage. Steinman reads this as giving China the tools Labenz fears most.
- **Energy and AGI scepticism.** Steinman says AI company leaders he knows worry more about energy than trade policy, and doubts AGI exists at all. Labenz replies that humans are a weak AGI and points to Slaughterbots and drone warfare in Ukraine as the trajectory.
- **Working for Trump.** Steinman describes Trump as one of the smartest people he has met and says media coverage routinely misrepresented the administration.
- **Arms-control precedent.** Labenz cites Reagan-era arms reduction treaties and calls the nuclear status quo a "terrible failure"; Steinman credits Soviet collapse instead and says words on paper mean nothing.
- **Steinman's plan.** Pressed repeatedly for a plan, Steinman offers continued pressure on the CCP and reshoring semiconductor production; Labenz calls that one move on the path to an arms race.
