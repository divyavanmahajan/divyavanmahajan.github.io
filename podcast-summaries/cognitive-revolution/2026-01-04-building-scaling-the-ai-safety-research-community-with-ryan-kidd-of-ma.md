## Thesis

Ryan Kidd, co-executive director of MATS, the largest AI safety research mentorship programme, argues that because informed experts still disagree sharply on timelines and risk, the field should run a broad portfolio of research bets rather than go all-in on one agenda. He sees today's models as safer than early theorists predicted but not safe, and says the binding constraint on the field is people good enough to lead and manage research, not money.

## Key points

- **Timelines.** MATS anchors on forecasts such as Metaculus, giving a median for strong AGI around 2033 with roughly a 20% chance by 2028, and Kidd urges weighting concern toward the earlier scenarios.
- **State of safety.** Models understand human values better than expected and have not shown sustained, spontaneous coherent deception, but sycophancy, situational awareness and dangerous capabilities are growing. He cites a MATS and Anthropic study where an AI found $4.6 million of smart-contract exploits.
- **Safety is capabilities.** Kidd says all safety work is capabilities work, as RLHF showed, and argues AGI will be built anyway under market pressure, so the goal is to lower the "alignment tax" and leave coordination to governments.
- **Talent archetypes.** From interviews with 31 hiring managers MATS defines "connectors" who invent paradigms, "iterators" who run experiments, and "amplifiers" who manage teams. Iterators are most hired today, but he expects amplifiers to become most in demand within a year or two as coding agents lower the technical bar.
- **Getting in.** About 7% of stream applicants are accepted; the median fellow is 27, 20% are undergraduates and about 15% hold PhDs. Of 446 alumni, 80% now work in AI safety. Tangible research output and trusted references matter most.
- **Money and compute.** Frontier-lab safety roles pay the same as other lab roles, about $350k for juniors; nonprofits pay less. MATS budgets $12,000 of compute per fellow and finds compute rarely limits research. The summer 2026 cohort will have about 120 fellows.
