## Thesis

This is a cross-post from ChinaTalk. Jordan Schneider and Ilari Makela interview USC professor David C. Kang, co-author with Xinru Ma of "Beyond Power Transitions". Kang argues that power transition theory comes from European history. From about the year 1000 to the 19th century, East Asia was a stable hegemonic system in which China dominated without conquering Korea, Japan or Vietnam. Nathan Labenz notes that AI never comes up. He shares the episode because so much AI strategy assumes a US-China Thucydides trap.

## Key points

- **Internal decay, not conquest.** Kang says almost every East Asian dynastic change came from internal causes. The Song was the exception, and it was not a weak declining power: about 50 million people and a 3 million-man army, against perhaps 1 million Mongols. He borrows a line he cannot source: "Empires die from suicide, not murder".
- **Shared norms over raw power.** Kang argues tribute relations and a shared Confucian bureaucratic culture let the smaller states live alongside China. He says the Vietnam-China border agreed around 1084 is in the same place today.
- **The Imjin War.** In 1592 Hideyoshi invaded Korea with 300,000 troops and 700 ships, against the Spanish Armada's 130 ships and 20,000 troops. Kang notes that Chinese troops controlled the peninsula afterwards and went home within about a year.
- **Pushback.** Schneider and Makela argue the stability rested on China being far stronger than everyone else. They say Japan challenged China as soon as industrialisation let it. Kang replies that Japan was never measurably as strong as China, and that the Westphalian model only lies thinly over Asia.
- **No balancing coalition.** Kang says Asian states will not join a US containment coalition. Vietnam and Malaysia have no US guarantee, yet behave much like US allies, which he takes as evidence against the idea that the US is what keeps the peace.
- **Taiwan.** Kang says the Taiwan problem exists only because the Westphalian system recognises nation states alone. He thinks Taiwan should take from Ukraine that the US is very cautious about fighting a nuclear-armed power.

## Quotes

> "The countries in Asia are not going to join a US containment coalition against China. That's not how it's gonna work." — David C. Kang

> "These internal dynamics are, in many ways, way more consequential than external." — David C. Kang
