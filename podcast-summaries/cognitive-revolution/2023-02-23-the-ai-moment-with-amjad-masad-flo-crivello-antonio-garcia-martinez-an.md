## Thesis

This 30-minute cut from the Moment of Zen podcast asks whether the 2023 AI moment differs from earlier hype cycles. Amjad Masad (Replit) and Flo Crivello argue that large language models bring a real jump in generality, starting with software creation. Antonio Garcia Martinez is the sceptic, dismissing AGI doom and asking where the actual products are.

## Key points

- **Third great convergence.** Crivello, citing Denny Britz, describes ImageNet (2012), transformers (2017) and LLMs (2022) as successive convergences. He calls AI underhyped and says his AGI timelines are shortening and his concerns growing.
- **Doom versus tool.** Garcia Martinez calls the Yudkowsky view and transhumanism "bullshit", comparing Kurzweil's singularity to Christian eschatology. Masad argues people ascribe agency to things, and that LLMs are a powerful new type of computer, not agents with goals.
- **Coding changes shape.** Masad would still teach his kids to code because generated code will not be 100% accurate, so reading and debugging matter more. Dan Romero expects the 10x engineer to become a 100x engineer; Crivello says 1x engineers become 10x too.
- **Model weaknesses.** Crivello says models lack systematic thinking: GPT-3 might reformat dates correctly 99.8% of the time but fail 0.2%. He expects models to learn to write code for repeated tasks because it is cheaper and more reliable.
- **Bounties and the future of work.** Masad links AI to The Sovereign Individual's picture of freelancers forming companies on the fly. Crivello predicts a billion-dollar company with one or two people. Garcia Martinez says bounty systems are the part of Web3 that does not work.
- **Where value lands.** Crivello puts GPT-4 training at about $100 million and argues cheaper content makes distribution more valuable. He predicts all knowledge work will be automated within 20 years. Masad is bearish on AI-first companies and bullish on infrastructure, dev tools and growth-stage firms such as Notion.
