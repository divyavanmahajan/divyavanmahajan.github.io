## Thesis

Daniel Miessler, a cybersecurity veteran and creator of the open-source Personal AI Infrastructure (PAI) framework, argues that AI's real value lies in scaffolding rather than raw models, and that AGI will arrive as a product able to replace an average knowledge worker, probably around 2027. Because he expects most corporate jobs to disappear, he wants AI to "activate" people's own ideas and creativity, with a personal assistant organised around each person's goals.

## Key points

- **Labour outlook.** Miessler says the bar for replacing rote knowledge work is low and that companies would ideally employ no one; he expects pressure for UBI around 2028 or 2029. He argues Claude Code's success comes from its harness, since frontier models are close in ability.
- **PAI's design.** Built on Claude Code, PAI starts with TELOS, an interview capturing your problems, goals and capabilities. Skills, about 12 hooks and roughly 5,000 to 15,000 tokens of start-up context let his assistant, Kai, know who he is and route tasks across Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, Gemini, Codex and others.
- **Self-improving loop.** Hooks score his sentiment on each response, summaries feed a file-system memory (he dislikes RAG as lossy), and an upgrade skill reads Anthropic changelogs and blogs to propose improvements. A "permission to fail" principle reduces hallucination and faked work.
- **Portability.** Because PAI is Markdown, skills and context files, Miessler says it could move to OpenCode if Anthropic's lead or leadership changed; he backs Anthropic for its human-first focus on harnesses.
- **Security as AI stack versus AI stack.** He describes tools that profile employees for spear-phishing and spin up hundreds of campaigns from one prompt, and says defenders must run equal AI stacks; insiders should win because they see logs and configuration directly.
- **Bounded trust.** He no longer skips Claude Code permissions, layers prompt-injection defences, and rates his trust in the system at about 60%.
