## Thesis

In a cross-post from Sarah Hastings Woodhouse's Consistently Candid podcast, Nathan Labenz explains why he is worried about AI but not a hardcore doomer. He puts p(doom) at 10 to 90 percent, which he calls radical uncertainty, and argues that even a small chance of a good outcome is worth fighting for. His stance is to accelerate adoption of current models while being open to pausing further hyperscaling.

## Key points

- **Underused capability.** Labenz says models already beat doctors on differential diagnosis as judged by other doctors, and score higher on empathy. On the LMSYS leaderboard, GPT-4 rose from 1186 to 1287, roughly a 70/30 preference. Adoption lags because tools are hard to discover; his parents had never heard of Devin, which finished a stock-data task for him.
- **Domain work over scale.** He cites OpenAI's custom legal model with Harvey and Google's Med-Gemini as gains from extra training rather than more compute. He expects one more scaling step to be safe, then favours a pause. Woodhouse raises algorithmic efficiency (GPT-2 for $20); Labenz replies that data scale, such as Llama 3's 15 trillion tokens, keeps basement actors far behind.
- **Red teaming in public.** His volunteer group cloned Trump, Biden and Taylor Swift voices on calling-agent apps and ran fake ransom and fundraising calls. Renaming clones "formula 45" and "formula 46" beat filters. He proposes AI classifiers that flag only egregiously criminal use.
- **Eight reasons for hope.** Claude 3 understands human values well; interpretability is going well (the Golden Gate Bridge demo); a basement breakthrough looks unlikely; lab leaders have engaged with safety arguments; governments have responded, including the Biden order's 10^26 and 10^23 (biology) thresholds; GPT-4 sits in a useful but controllable sweet spot; and China seems to see the threat, which he calls the weakest reason.
- **Uncertainty as the sensible view.** Woodhouse says she is around 50 percent. Labenz notes Anthropic and other leaders also express radical uncertainty, and urges worried people to act in varied ways rather than as a monoculture.
