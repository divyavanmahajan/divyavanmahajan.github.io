## Thesis

Ben Todd, co-founder of 80,000 Hours, whose book of the same name has been rewritten for the AI era, argues that a career (about 80,000 hours of work) dwarfs every other lever an individual has for doing good. He says the most pressing problems now are loss of control of AI, AI-driven concentration of power and engineered pandemics, and that people should plan for when their own impact peaks rather than for a precise AGI date.

## Key points

- **Planning across scenarios.** Todd plans for three cases: AI R&D automated within one to four years, a slower path driven by chip build-out into the 2030s, and a plateau. He argues career capital still matters on short horizons: a year that makes you 20% more productive pays back in four or five years.
- **Top problems.** He estimates only 1,000-2,000 people work full-time on loss-of-control risks against perhaps 100,000 to a million advancing capabilities. Concentration of power is newer on the list and, in his view, even more neglected; he cites AI-enabled mass surveillance as one route.
- **Four kinds of work.** Technical research and engineering (he notes METR has around 20 useful projects but capacity for two or three), government and policy, communications, and organisation-building.
- **Working at AI labs.** Todd calls it complicated: labs are strong places for alignment research but may speed up risks. He urges people to question convenient conclusions, seek friends who will challenge them, and write down pre-commitments, since people become like their colleagues.
- **Policy and funding.** He favours groundwork for a possible strategic pause, compute tracking, an "off switch", transparency and red lines. Funding is plentiful, including pledged Anthropic founder equity.
- **Pandemics and neglected ideas.** Todd lists wastewater monitoring, gene-synthesis screening, better PPE stockpiles, air filtration and rapid vaccines as builder-friendly projects, and flags digital minds, space governance and gradual disempowerment as under-explored.
