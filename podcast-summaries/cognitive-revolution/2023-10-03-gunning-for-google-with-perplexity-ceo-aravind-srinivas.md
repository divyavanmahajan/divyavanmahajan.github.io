## Thesis

Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas tells Nathan Labenz that if LLMs become a commodity, the advantage goes to whoever owns the best data. So Perplexity is replacing every borrowed part of its stack with its own crawler, index and models. He also argues that charging users directly keeps the company aligned with them, whereas Google works for its advertisers.

## Key points

- **Traction.** Srinivas says Perplexity serves multiple millions of queries a day, 6 to 7x more than in March. By SimilarWeb estimates that is about 40x smaller than ChatGPT, and roughly 15 to 20% of Bard's traffic. He calls moving from weekly to daily use the hardest step.
- **Owning the stack.** Perplexity uses several search APIs but no longer depends on any single one. Srinivas says the goal is an index of the best 1 to 10 billion pages, not the whole web, and that training its own models is the only long-term option.
- **Copilot versus default search.** The default search is fast. Copilot asks clarifying questions and queries the web live. Srinivas admits even the team cannot say when users should pick which mode. To cut costs, Copilot's router moved from GPT-4 to a fine-tuned GPT-3.5.
- **The web as an API.** He predicts AI assistants will become the main way people reach the web. Platforms such as Amazon or Instagram will wall off their data and build their own assistants, leaving Perplexity the market for research and knowledge. He points to Neeva as proof that a Google-style UI with summaries on top fails.
- **Subscription over ads.** Pro gets unlimited GPT-4 Copilot, a choice of GPT-4 or Claude 2, and unlimited file uploads. Citing Jeff Bezos and a leaked Google email about Ruth Porat's targets, he argues that ad revenue pulls a search engine away from its users.
- **Spending and speed.** The team is 25 to 30 people with a $25M Series A, and spends more on compute than on staff. Srinivas calls generative AI a "war zone": incumbents ship fast too, so for a startup shipping fast is the only option.
