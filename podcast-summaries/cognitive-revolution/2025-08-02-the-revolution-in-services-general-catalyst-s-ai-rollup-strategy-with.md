## Thesis

Marc Bhargava of General Catalyst argues that the $16 trillion services market, long ignored by venture capital because of 5-10% margins, can be rebuilt by AI-enabled rollups: vertical software companies that automate 30-70% of tasks and then acquire the service firms they serve. He frames it as revenue growth with the same staff, not cost cutting. Nathan Labenz is persuaded on the near term but questions how smooth the transition will be for workers and smaller firms.

## Key points

- **Picking verticals.** Bhargava says the team screened 70 service categories down to 10 where at least 30% of tasks are automatable, citing 50-70% of call center workflows at Crescendo and 80% automation in bookkeeping.
- **Founding teams and deals.** Each company pairs applied AI engineers with industry veterans who have done M&A. Pilot customers often become acquisition targets, with sellers rolling over around 30% of their equity instead of selling to debt-financed private equity.
- **Growth target, not savings.** His rule of thumb: spend 20% more on software, engineers and AI tools and aim to double revenue within 3 years. He points to accountant shortages and baby boomer retirements as reasons firms can take on 2-3 times more clients.
- **Hybrid workflows.** Bhargava expects services to stay human-in-the-loop, with some steps automated, partly because fields such as medicine are regulated. He keeps existing workflows, for example an HOA manager emailing an agent for a board deck.
- **Where the debate sits.** Labenz guesses only 10-20% of traditional firms make it into these compounders and cites evidence of AI beating doctors. Bhargava believes most firms can join and puts the odds of all model labs closing their models at "sub 3%".
- **Why talk publicly.** Bhargava says publicity attracts applied AI founders and offers owners a "third way" besides private equity or a strategic buyer.
