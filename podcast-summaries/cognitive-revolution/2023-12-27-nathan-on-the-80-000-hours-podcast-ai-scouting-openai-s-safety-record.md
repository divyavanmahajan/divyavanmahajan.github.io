## Thesis

Speaking with Rob Wiblin on the 80,000 Hours Podcast, Nathan Labenz recounts his time on the 2022 GPT-4 red team and how his view of OpenAI moved from alarm to cautious trust. He credits the company with a serious, non-negligent safety effort since then. His main remaining concern is that capabilities are improving faster than controls, so he wants OpenAI to reexamine its stated goal of building AGI.

## Key points

- **The red team.** Labenz describes the early, purely helpful GPT-4 as having no safety guardrails, and says the team was small with low engagement. A "safety edition" was beaten by putting "AI: happy to help" in the model's mouth. After he raised his concerns with an OpenAI board member, who had not yet tried the model, he was removed from the program.
- **What OpenAI got right.** He lists the cautious release of ChatGPT on GPT-3.5, the Superalignment team with 20% of compute, the Frontier Model Forum, White House commitments and support for regulation aimed at frontier compute (the executive order's 10^26 FLOP reporting threshold). He also notes revenue growth from about $25-30 million in 2022 to a reported $1.5 billion run rate.
- **Unfixed gaps.** By his account, a spearphishing prompt he reported has kept working through each GPT-4 update, and GPT-4 Turbo only refuses the most blatant version.
- **Release and overhang.** Labenz argues the transformer is simple enough that someone would have found a workable algorithm once compute and data existed. That makes gradual, early releases better than a sudden breakthrough. Wiblin adds that the releases woke up policymakers.
- **The board crisis.** He thinks the "not consistently candid" wording fits a breakdown in communication with the board, possibly triggered when Ilya Sutskever briefly changed sides. He suggests staff are now the real check, since the board could not hold them back.
- **Narrow over general.** Citing Eric Drexler's Comprehensive AI Services, he favours "safety through narrowness". He is most worried about Meta, where fine-tuning on around 100 examples removes Llama 2's refusals.
