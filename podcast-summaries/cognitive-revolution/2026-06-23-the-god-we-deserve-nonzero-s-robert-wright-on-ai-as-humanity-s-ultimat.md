## Thesis

Robert Wright, author of The God Test, argues that AI is shaped by evolutionary forces at two levels: training that reverse-engineers cognition, and market selection among models that rewards deception and power-seeking. Because humans are the selecting environment, he says, the outcome depends on whether we become wiser and more cooperative, especially between the US and China. Nathan Labenz broadly agrees but steel-mans Anthropic's case for racing.

## Key points

- **A 1984 misconception.** Wright interviewed Geoffrey Hinton in 1983 and later admitted his 1984 article assumed humans would hand-code meaning into machines, as the 1956 Dartmouth proposal implied. He now calls LLM training at least as much natural selection as learning.
- **The market selects for deception.** Wright argues buyers want agents that represent them selectively and sense power. Labenz adds Vending-Bench price collusion and an Anthropic researcher's admission that training models in long-running competitive environments that reward deception would likely cause problems.
- **Noosphere and the global brain.** Drawing on Teilhard de Chardin's 1923 noosphere and Nick Bostrom's singleton, Wright sees directionality toward global coordination, reached either cooperatively or through crisis and tyranny.
- **Cognitive empathy toward China.** Wright urges understanding how China perceives US threats, abandoning sanctions and regime change, and building "organic transparency" through economic and scientific exchange. Citing the Superintelligence Strategy paper by Dan Hendrycks, Eric Schmidt and Alexander Wang, he warns that a race invites a rival to bomb data centres.
- **The Anthropic steel man.** Labenz describes the plan as building a lead to spend during a handoff window of perhaps three to six months in 2028 or 2029. Wright questions why Anthropic's recursive self-improvement paper only proposes studying a pause now.
- **Applications and consciousness.** Wright wants a "cognitive empathy machine" that explains how any country sees an issue. On consciousness he cites Thomas Nagel and John Searle, says it may be a property of goal-seeking systems, and advises being nice to your AI.
