## Thesis

Dominic Williams, president of the DFINITY Foundation and CEO of Caffeine AI, argues that software will increasingly be written by AI "wish machines" for people with no security or operations team. Those apps therefore need a platform that guarantees they are tamper-proof, unstoppable and safe to update, which he says the Internet Computer provides. Host Nathan Labenz presses on the AI safety tension: unstoppable infrastructure reassures those worried about concentrated power and alarms those worried about loss of control.

## Key points

- **A different kind of network.** Williams describes the Internet Computer as a serverless cloud running inside a Byzantine fault-tolerant protocol, distinct from blockchains he calls "token databases". Subnets combine nodes from different providers, data centres and jurisdictions ("deterministic decentralisation"), run inside AMD SEV-SNP enclaves, and node providers are paid in constant fiat terms.
- **Governance.** An autonomous Network Nervous System upgrades and orchestrates the network through liquid-democracy voting; Williams says over 75% of staked ICP is locked for eight years and no bad proposal has ever been adopted.
- **Built for AI to write.** Motoko is a language designed for AI, with orthogonal persistence so "the program is the database". Caffeine fine-tunes a model on Motoko examples, and Williams says 98%+ of Caffeine users do not know they are building on the Internet Computer.
- **Market thesis.** He argues self-writing platforms dissolve the developer moat around AWS, Node.js and Postgres and could eat into both the platform and SaaS layers. Cloud Engines, due this year, will let customers run subnets, including over big-tech clouds, with about seven-fold replication.
- **Safety by ensembles.** To stop an AI inserting a backdoor, Williams proposes diverse models with different prompts checking each other's work. Labenz raises emergent misalignment and model collusion; Williams agrees game-theoretic persuasion between models is an open problem.
- **An off switch, of sorts.** The Network Nervous System can disable services; Williams recounts an Al Qaeda portal being taken down, and expects AI models to cast some governance votes within about a decade of his 2017 proposal.
