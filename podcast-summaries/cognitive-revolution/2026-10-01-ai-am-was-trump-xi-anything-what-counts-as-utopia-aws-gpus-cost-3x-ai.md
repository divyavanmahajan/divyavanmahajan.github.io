## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and co-host Prakash Narayanan argue that "real things are starting to happen". The week brings tentative US-China contact on AI incidents, measurable compute markets, and AI agents diagnosing children whose genetic cases had gone unresolved. Labenz closes by saying that pacing frontier AI, which he still supports, has a real cost for families waiting for answers.

## Key points

- **A slight thaw, with caveats.** Ed and Jeremie Harris of Gladstone AI, drawing on interviews with about a dozen State Department diplomats, warn that crisis hotlines with China have often gone unanswered. Without vetted verification, they argue, the only ask available after a serious incident might be to switch off every cluster above a certain size. They urge verification startups to engage the intelligence community early, because vetting takes years.
- **GPU price indexes.** Steve Hou of Silicon Data says hyperscalers charge two to three times what neoclouds charge for similar GPUs because of bundled services and enterprise stickiness. His index combines quoted and transaction prices but does not yet factor in benchmarked chip performance. A recent rise in his proprietary-LLM token index likely reflects a shift towards pricier models, not price increases.
- **OpenAI DevDay.** Narayanan says GPT-6.1 Sol is "Astra for the price of Sol". He argues that Ultrafast's lower latency matters for keeping builders in flow. Labenz expects Dots to suit people who do not want to maintain their own agent, and sees Sign in with ChatGPT as a help for trial costs, recalling about a dollar per new customer at Waymark.
- **Co-writing a novel.** Joel Borgen says he planned The Receipt Horizon himself and used models for critique and drafting, then cut about 14% of the text. A viola-clef test failed on every model until Astra.
- **Rare-disease diagnosis.** Daniel McKinnon of Gamow Labs explains that his son Owen's 91-kilobase enhancer deletion was missed because labs filter structural variants far from the gene. A human specialist found it, and his AI pipeline later rediscovered it. On his RareBench benchmark, a traditional tool scores about 10% and vanilla Opus 5.5 in Claude Code about 50%. Gamow has bought a lab to run functional studies on variants of uncertain significance.
