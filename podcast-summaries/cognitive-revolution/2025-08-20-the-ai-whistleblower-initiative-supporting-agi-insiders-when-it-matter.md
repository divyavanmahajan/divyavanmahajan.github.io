## Thesis

Karl Koch, founder of the Berlin-based AI Whistleblower Initiative, argues that the few thousand people with real insight into frontier AI development face unclear legal protections, internal policies they don't know about, and no obvious place to get advice. His organisation offers anonymous expert and legal support and runs a campaign asking labs to publish their whistleblowing policies. Host Nathan Labenz, who was dismissed from the GPT-4 red team after escalating concerns to OpenAI's board, says he would have used such support.

## Key points

- **Three channels.** Koch separates internal, external (regulator) and public whistleblowing. He cites SEC figures showing more than 70 percent of people who go to the SEC first raised concerns internally. The EU AI Act comes under the EU whistleblowing directive in August 2026, while US protection is a patchwork.
- **Survey findings.** Roughly half of surveyed insiders had low confidence in judging risks, over 90% knew no whistleblower support organisation, and 55% did not know whether their company's policy existed or where to find it.
- **Labenz's experience.** The process took about 3 months in late 2022. OpenAI cited his talks with outside safety contacts as grounds for dismissal and made what he calls an overt threat to Metr's access. He credits OpenAI's latest system card with much stronger red-teaming, including chain-of-thought access for Apollo and Metr.
- **Retaliation record.** Koch cites Apple's long dispute with Ashley Gjøvik, the Timnit Gebru and Margaret Mitchell firings at Google, and Theranos whistleblower Tyler Shultz needing $400,000 for legal costs.
- **Publish Your Policies.** Only OpenAI has published its policy. The campaign, backed by over 30 organisations including Transparency International, Stuart Russell, Larry Lessig and Daniel Kokotajlo, asks labs for policies plus metrics such as report counts, retaliation complaints and appeals. Koch says 100% of surveyed insiders support publication.
- **Third Opinion.** Insiders can ask questions anonymously through an open-source tool used over Tor. The initiative gets answers from independent experts and refers people to pro bono lawyers. It has also supplied hardened devices.
