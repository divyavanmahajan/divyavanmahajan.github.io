## Thesis

Anton Osika, founder of Lovable, argues that AI will give the 99% of people who cannot code the ability to create and customise software, but that this works best through an opinionated, predictable product rather than open-ended agents. Over a live build with AI engineer Isaak Sundeman, host Nathan Labenz sees both the speed and the friction: a working LLM-powered product comparison app, reached after debugging and a restart.

## Key points

- **Opinionated stack.** Osika says errors multiply as more components must be wired together, so Lovable limits choices: Supabase for backend and database, Stripe for payments, Resend for email, and FireCrawl for scraping in the demo.
- **Model routing.** Lovable routes calls across Gemini, OpenAI and Anthropic. Osika rates Claude 3.5 best overall for coding, Google's Flash for small cheap calls and OpenAI's reasoning models for getting unstuck.
- **Against open-ended agents.** Osika argues agent loops have unpredictable run times, which makes for poor UX when they are not fully reliable; Lovable favours designed chains of LLM calls and keeps an agent mode admin-only for now.
- **Revert and retry.** Sundeman frames building as a search tree: when stuck, revert and carry the error forward as "scar tissue". Osika rebuilt the app in three edits after the first attempt took 17.
- **Context is the moat.** Osika credits "agentic RAG" and context management for reliability as codebases grow, while noting that large codebases remain harder.
- **Growth and users.** Lovable launched on 21 November and reached $9M ARR in about 8 weeks, with hundreds of thousands of users. Osika says users split roughly evenly by coding experience; favourites are high-agency founders and operators.
