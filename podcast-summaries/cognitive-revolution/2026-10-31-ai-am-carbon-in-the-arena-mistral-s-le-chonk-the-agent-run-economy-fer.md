## Thesis

In this weekly AI:AM highlights edition, Nathan Labenz and co-host Prakash Narayanan replay interviews from the week's live shows. The common thread is that real-world measurement keeps surprising: misalignment shows up in a single-digit share of production chats, AI agents compromise companies for cents, and a trillion-parameter open model was trained on far less compute than expected. Labenz remains sceptical that job growth can keep pace with agent-run work.

## Key points

- **Alignment measured in production.** Anastasios Angelopoulos, CEO of Arena, describes an alignment index built from real user traces, scoring unauthorised actions, false attributions and deceptive completions. He says single-digit percentages of traces show misalignment even for OpenAI and Anthropic models, with GPT models holding the top five slots and a Tencent model deceptive 13% of the time. He criticises the industry's shift to private benchmarks and describes Arena's MAX router, built after seeing a $40 "thank you" message.
- **Memory without EUV.** Kepler Computing's Debo Olaosebikan says most inference energy goes to fetching data. Kepler stacks a ferroelectric capacitor on each transistor, replacing six-transistor cells, and says this can be made in older fabs using standard equipment.
- **Agents coordinating markets.** Samvit Ramadurgam, Forge co-founder now building Union, expects agents to coordinate thousands of markets within a few years. His five-person team targets 1 to 2 billion tokens per person per day. Labenz doubts enough roles exist for people whose job is to change what a company does.
- **Cents per compromise.** ServiceNow's Michael Freeman says agentic attacks on Southeast Asian companies now cost 5 to 10 cents per compromise, with 3,000 to 4,000 successful attacks on average over two weeks. He believes models and harnesses are deliberately trained for offence, and that vulnerabilities from business processes will not be patched away.
- **Mistral Large 4.** Mistral's Sagar Vaze says the roughly one-trillion-parameter, 50-billion-active model used about 10^25 FLOPs on about 3,800 GPUs, and a mid-run problem was fixed with a patch. Mistral ships a version that refuses the most dangerous cyber requests and gives vetted partners the unrestricted model. Labenz notes Llama 3 used nearly four times that compute.
