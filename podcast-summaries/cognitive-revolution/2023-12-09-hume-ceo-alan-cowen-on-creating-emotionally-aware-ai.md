## Thesis

Alan Cowen, CEO and chief scientist of Hume AI, argues that emotional expression in voice and face carries information that language models miss, and that measuring it without demographic bias lets models predict outcomes such as call quality far better than text alone. He tells Nathan Labenz that the same signals, used as feedback over weeks and months, are the way to align AI with human well-being.

## Key points

- **Modalities.** Hume measures speech prosody, vocal bursts such as laughs and sighs, and facial expression. Cowen says people across cultures distinguish over 28 dimensions of facial expression, giving hundreds of measurements per second; for driver drowsiness, language adds nothing because drowsy drivers are usually not talking.
- **Data without identity bias.** Hume's survey platform recruits hundreds of thousands of participants and randomizes their tasks so expression can be separated from gender, ethnicity and age. Cowen says "pick an emotion" labels predict slider ratings almost fully, while sliders explain only 20% of the emotion data, and that about 80% of variance is shared across cultures.
- **A continuous space.** He describes emotion words as rotations of one continuous space: schadenfreude, for example, is captured by contempt plus amusement, so the label set is cut where explained variance drops off.
- **Custom models.** Customers upload recordings and labels and the training is automated; Cowen says 100 to 200 calls rated by a manager can be enough, but customer support models need per-culture training. Labenz works out 2.76 cents a minute, or $1.66 an hour; Cowen says the custom model API itself is free.
- **Beyond GPT-4V.** Cowen claims GPT-4V reads expressions with gender bias because it learned from internet images and text, not dialogue. Hume adds expression tokens to open language models and uses people's reactions as reinforcement, which he says scales better than RLHF.
- **Alignment.** He argues AI should predict how its actions affect a person's well-being next week, month and year, and put that above the stated goal. He is worried capabilities outpace alignment, but thinks the AI race has slowed progress by reducing publishing, noting GPT-4 has led for over a year.
