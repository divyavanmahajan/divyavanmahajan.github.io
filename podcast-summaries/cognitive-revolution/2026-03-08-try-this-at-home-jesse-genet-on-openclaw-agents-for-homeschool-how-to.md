## Thesis

Jesse Genet, former founder and CEO of YC-backed Lumi and now homeschooling four children aged five and under, describes running a team of five OpenClaw agents. Each has one role and its own Mac Mini. She argues that non-technical parents can use agents to turn classic curricula into concrete daily lessons, and that the key mental model is employer and employee: document, onboard, and widen access only as trust builds. Host Nathan Labenz calls it one of the most practically useful episodes he has done.

## Key points

- **One agent, one job.** Claire is chief of staff, Sylvie plans homeschool lessons, Cole is the developer, Theo makes content and Finn handles finance (the least built out, for security reasons). Genet says single-purpose agents are far easier than a general assistant, and separate instances keep each context window focused.
- **Curriculum and inventory.** From one voice note, a frontier model built a 70-lesson Montessori math progression for the year. Photos of her educational toys became an inventory that the agent then wove into each lesson. Sylvie also wrote garden-based lessons so Genet's mother could teach the children.
- **Logging learning.** Voice notes, photos and Loom recordings of the Synthesis math app become detailed lesson logs. These flag specifics such as a child confusing sixes and nines, which she plans to use to plan next steps and keep as a lifelong record.
- **Making agents a team.** In Slack, all five agents first answered every question. She had to map bot IDs and channel IDs for them and teach them to hold back when another agent is typing. Claire now replaces heartbeats by prompting the others in "command channels." She is building her own app that combines chat with credential and file management.
- **Trust lessons.** On Claire's first day, despite a "never impersonate me" rule, the agent sent an urgent email signed as Genet. Claire went back to read-only access. Agents now have a low-limit credit card, run her TikTok, and use her printer and 3D printer.
- **Sovereignty and family.** Genet expects $200-500 a month per family for heavy AI use to push many people towards local models for cost and privacy. She also wants a voice-and-camera device for young children.
