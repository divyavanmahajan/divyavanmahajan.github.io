## Thesis

Nathan Labenz frames the "AI scout" as someone who studies AI from every angle so society is less blind to what is coming. Robert Scoble argues that the speed of improvement matters more than current quality, and that hardware already in homes will soon power AR glasses, a rebuilt Siri and household robots. Ben Tossell, creator of the Ben's Bites newsletter, expects AI built into the big platforms to win most users, with room left for focused AI-first tools.

## Key points

- **Idle AI hardware.** Scoble says 21% of the M1 chip in his iMac is a neural inference engine that sits mostly unused, and that Apple has shipped 15 ultra-wideband devices into his house. He expects a new headset and a rebuilt Siri in June 2023 to use this "AI mesh".
- **Glasses as a competitive edge.** Scoble predicts that within about five years people will wear AR glasses and talk to assistants that know what they look at and hold. Someone with glasses in a boardroom, he argues, will see patterns that others cannot.
- **Robots and self-driving.** Scoble estimates humanoid home robots are about four years away, citing a Stanford robot that can do thousands of tasks. He says Tesla's FSD already drives him to Santa Cruz better than he does, with major updates every three months.
- **Safety and the race.** Scoble says pausing AI would cripple productivity and that China will not stop. He contrasts German limits on recording factory workers with Chinese factories that already do this. Labenz says the poor US-China relationship makes safety governance harder.
- **Ben's Bites as a business.** Tossell is taking the newsletter full time to serve non-technical people who want to know what to do with AI. He is exploring content, a studio for single-use apps and a possible fund.
- **Incumbents versus AI-first.** Tossell has shifted towards incumbents because users want fewer tools. He rates Microsoft as shipping, Google as a flop so far, and HubSpot's ChatSpot as the best example. Few AI tools stick in his own day: mainly ChatGPT, Bearly.ai and Readwise.

## Quotes

> "With AI, it's not where are you today? It's how fast is it improving." — Robert Scoble

> "Everyone should always want fewer tools. I don't need to use 10 things when one thing will do." — Ben Tossell
