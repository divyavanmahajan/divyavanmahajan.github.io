## Thesis

Harmonic co-founders Vlad Tenev and Tudor Achim tell Nathan Labenz that mathematics is reasoning, and that AI reasoning has to be output as formally verified Lean code to be trusted. They argue that capabilities are rising along a smooth exponential, but verification has made a one-off jump. Formal proofs move humans from checking AI work to choosing which problems matter, and the same logic will spread to mission-critical software.

## Key points

- **Lean as a trust machine.** Achim describes Lean as a dependently typed language whose small kernel checks every step against three short axioms, including the axiom of choice. Tenev says this moves maths from the chalkboard to GitHub and removes the need for traditional peer review, because a proof that checks is correct.
- **Aristotle's architecture.** The system that reached gold-medal level at the 2025 IMO combines LLM-driven Monte Carlo tree search, an informal "lemma guessing" module that Achim says makes many mistakes but helps manage context, and a geometry module modelled on DeepMind's AlphaGeometry. Harmonic, OpenAI and DeepMind all missed question six, which Tenev estimates is about 5x harder even for humans.
- **Formal beats informal at scale.** Tenev argues that nobody will read a 5,000-page AI proof, or the roughly 1.5 million lines of the browser the Cursor team generated. That makes formal languages the future for maths and safety-critical software, starting with fields that already pay Lean, Rocq or Isabelle experts.
- **Community taste, few priors.** Harmonic opened Aristotle through an API and web interface, so users decide which problems to pursue, including Erdős problems. Achim says the company never used panels of mathematicians to judge proofs. It optimises the cost of future proofs and follows the Bitter Lesson, and he calls hallucination-driven exploration essential.
- **2030 vision and safety.** Achim predicts self-consistent theories for anything that can be posed mathematically, possibly several unifications of quantum mechanics and general relativity, which only new collider experiments could tell apart. Tenev says safety worries are limited for now because Aristotle's action space is mostly Lean files.
