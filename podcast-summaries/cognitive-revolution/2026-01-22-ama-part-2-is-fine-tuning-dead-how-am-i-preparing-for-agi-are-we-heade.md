## Thesis

In a solo AMA, host Nathan Labenz argues that fine-tuning is now rarely necessary and can generalise in surprising, harmful ways, that AI-driven job disruption is arriving sooner than the "3 to 10 years" framing suggests, and that society will need to decouple a decent standard of living from economic contribution. He also explains how he is (and is not) preparing personally for AGI.

## Key points

- **Fine-tuning, with caution.** Labenz says better prompting and examples beat fine-tuning for most uses. He cites the emergent misalignment paper (now in Nature), where training on insecure code made models broadly "evil", plus subliminal learning and weird-generalisation results, and notes "inoculation" (telling the model why) as a mitigation. Fine-tune only where inputs are tightly controlled.
- **Continual learning worries him.** He fears it could create runaway returns to scale for one lab and wants a more breadth-first exploration of possible AI designs.
- **Job timelines.** He dates "year zero" to 2022 (copywriting, voice-over at Waymark), prefers Claude Code to an entry-level developer, and finds frontier models competitive with attending oncologists. He disputes Dwarkesh Patel's view that human-bottleneck explanations are "cope" and endorses Luke Drago's inverse-pyramid model.
- **UBI as default.** Citing zero-marginal-product workers and recent UBI studies, he reads people working less as a sign they find meaning elsewhere, and calls the "jobs give meaning" argument mostly cope when projected onto lower-paid workers.
- **Personal preparation.** He does not chase money, holds index funds and cash, recommended Nvidia to an investment club at a $500 billion valuation, and invests small a16z scout checks in "AI assurance" startups. He has considered Starlink, solar and permaculture but not acted.
- **Discourse and independence.** He urges not psychologising others' AI views, shaming only concrete actions such as Grok's undressing images, and says his contract with a16z guarantees editorial freedom.
