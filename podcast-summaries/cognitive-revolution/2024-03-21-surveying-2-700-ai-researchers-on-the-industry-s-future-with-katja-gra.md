## Thesis

Katja Grace of AI Impacts walks Nathan Labenz through her survey of researchers who published at six top AI venues in 2022. Labenz reads the main result as radical uncertainty: few respondents are confident of either a very good or very bad future. Grace adds that this uncertainty is itself a kind of consensus, namely that extremely bad outcomes have enough probability to be worth taking seriously.

## Key points

- **Method.** Grace's team wrote to about 20,000 authors, paid $50 per response and got about 2,700 replies. Because asking for probabilities by year gives later answers than asking for the year of a given probability, half the respondents get each framing and the fitted distributions are averaged.
- **Shorter timelines.** The aggregate gives high-level machine intelligence a 10% chance by 2027 and 50% by 2047, down from 2060 in the 2022 survey. Full automation of labour moved by four or five decades but remains much later.
- **Narrow tasks.** 35 of 39 tasks have a 50% chance within 10 years. The longest are installing house wiring (17 years), writing an ML paper (19), publishable maths theorems (22) and millennium prize problems (27). Labenz notes that lab leaders such as Sam Altman and Shane Legg expect things sooner.
- **Extinction risk.** Medians across three similar questions were 5%, 10% and 5%. Grace explains that answers cluster at 5% and 10%, so small shifts move the median, and cautions against the "51% say at least 10%" headline.
- **Safety and concerns.** 80% think it unlikely users will understand the true reasons behind AI decisions by 2028, most want more safety prioritisation, and views on speeding up versus slowing down are split. More than 80% see deepfakes and false information as worth substantial or extreme concern.
- **Ideas for future surveys.** Labenz suggests asking about step changes when stronger models plug into existing scaffolding, and about US-China chip restrictions; Grace is wary of adversarial policy topics.
