## Thesis

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch tells Nathan Labenz that code completion was step one of a pattern every product will adopt, and that Vercel's v0 brings the same idea to user interfaces. He argues that companies should build AI into their own products instead of handing customers to ChatGPT, that writing code by hand will matter less as "Software 2.0" grows, and that open source models will catch up with proprietary ones.

## Key points

- **From ghost text to v0.** Rauch says GitHub Copilot convinced him every product would need "ghost text". v0 generates three or four UI options from a prompt, then iterates with the user, and he says it has produced hundreds of thousands of generations for a small waitlist.
- **Better than his own code.** When Rauch rebuilt his site rauchg.com with v0, he says the output was more accessible than his hand-written code, for example adding a screen-reader label to the Twitter icon.
- **Using AI well is a skill.** He advises pasting full error messages and phrasing prompts as questions, uses Perplexity, Metaphor and Phind for different tasks, and cites an Angry Birds clone where ChatGPT could not reason about the passage of time.
- **Own the customer.** Rauch compares GPTs to selling on Amazon: limited presentation and no direct customer link. He expects many vertical assistants and points to Vercel's AI SDK.
- **Software 2.0 and Cloud 2.0.** Drawing on Andrej Karpathy's essay, he pairs vector databases, LLMs and retrieval with Cloud 2.0, and predicts AI attention may "ossify" Software 1.0 choices such as JavaScript and Python.
- **Open source and risk.** Citing Next.js's growth, Rauch says it is costly to bet against open source and names regulatory capture as the main threat. He expects AI to amplify harms such as impersonation, but calls the "nuclear grade weapon" framing wrong; Labenz says he may "take the under" on Rauch's AGI timeline.
