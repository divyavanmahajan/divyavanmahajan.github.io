## Thesis

Reporting solo from a two-week trip to China in July 2026, under Chatham House rules, Nathan Labenz takes aim at the "but China" argument that ends many American AI policy debates. He concedes that Chinese models have weaker safeguards than the best American ones, but argues that Chinese safety research closely tracks Western work, top leadership talks openly about loss of control, and the Chinese government regulates AI far more actively than Washington.

## Key points

- **The gap is two companies wide.** Labenz says OpenAI and Anthropic carry the American safety average. Concordia AI's airiskmonitor.net, which covers 47 frontier models, and Adam Gleave's jailbreak work at FAR.AI rank them hardest to break, with Gemini and Grok easier and Chinese models easier still.
- **The 45-degree line.** Zhou Bowen of the Shanghai AI Lab proposed at WAIC 2024 that capability and safety should grow together. Labenz thinks China is below that line today. He notes that Chinese regulation attaches to the service rather than the model.
- **Research converges.** Concordia's database shows Chinese safety papers rising from a couple a month in 2023 to 50-60 a month by mid-2026. At a Tsinghua safety hub launch, speakers named Constellation and LISA as models. Papers on self-replication, evaluation faking and DeceptionBench echo Western work.
- **Leadership rhetoric.** Labenz reads passages from Xi Jinping's WAIC keynote on keeping AI under human control and argues that, next to JD Vance's remarks in Europe, Xi sounds like the safety hawk.
- **More active governance.** The Cyberspace Administration of China keeps a registry of approved services. In 2023 Beijing reportedly held back chatbot launches for about six months while standards were written, and it recently banned minors from AI companions.
- **Blind spots.** Labenz thinks Chinese officials can take down open models at home, but releases are permanent abroad. Chinese signatories also never published the frontier risk frameworks promised at Seoul, and he found no work on Confucian or other tradition-based alignment targets.
