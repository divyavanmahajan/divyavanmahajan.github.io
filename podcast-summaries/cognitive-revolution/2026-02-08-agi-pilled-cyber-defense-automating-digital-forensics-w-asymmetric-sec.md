## Thesis

Alexis Carlier, founder and CEO of Asymmetric Security, tells Nathan Labenz that if AGI makes intelligent labour almost free, cyber defence should be redesigned around deep forensic investigation. Today that work is done by a small number of expensive experts and only after a breach. He argues defenders have to deliberately pull the "jagged frontier" of AI capability toward defence, because the data and expertise needed to train defensive models do not appear by default.

## Key points

- **Threat landscape.** Carlier estimates about 80% of attacks come from relatively unsophisticated, financially motivated actors, and 70-80% start with social engineering or phishing rather than zero-days. Nation states are a small share by volume but are very hard to stop: China focuses on IP theft, Russia on political disruption, and North Korea places remote workers in Western tech firms to fund the regime. Ransomware is highly damaging, and he cites Jaguar's reported $2 billion UK government loan after an attack.
- **Rules-based detection fails.** Static detection rules flag normal behaviour, such as travel or bulk downloads, and miss slow exfiltration. Deep forensics catches both, but CrowdStrike's elite team is only about 60 people.
- **AI-plus-human investigations.** Asymmetric starts with business email compromise. An agent analyses the logs first and human investigators check its work. Off-the-shelf models reach roughly 90% accuracy, which speeds work up but is not enough to automate it. Investigations that took two days to a week now take a few hours.
- **Services first, via insurers.** Following CrowdStrike's early playbook, Asymmetric sells services to build trust and collect proprietary data. Cyber insurers' pre-approved incident-response panels are its main distribution channel. He sizes incident-response services at about $40 billion and the detection market it could replace at up to $500 billion.
- **Defence can be differentially accelerated.** Carlier argues that forensics, unlike pentesting, is not inherently dual use: people who specialise in it are not especially strong attackers. Offence can practise on any target, but defence lacks data and expert graders. He wants similar data and eval projects in biosecurity and AI safety.
