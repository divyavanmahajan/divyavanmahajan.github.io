## Thesis

Tufts biologist Michael Levin argues that the line between living things and machines does not hold: cognition is a continuum, and where a system sits on it can only be settled by perturbative experiments, not by what it is made of or how it came about. His lab's work on bioelectric memory and on simple algorithms shows how much latent capability goes unnoticed. Nathan Labenz draws parallels to interpretability and emergence debates in AI.

## Key points

- **Rewritable bioelectric memory.** Levin's lab built tools to read and write voltage patterns in non-neural cells. Changing these with ion-channel drugs produces flatworms whose fragments regrow as two-headed worms permanently, with genetics untouched, and tadpoles that see through eyes on their tails.
- **High-level triggers.** A 24-hour bioelectric treatment starts leg regeneration in adult frogs that continues for 18 months; the same intervention regrows tadpole tails. Levin says the information sits in the tissue, not in the intervention.
- **Surprises in sorting algorithms.** With student Adam Goldstein, Levin ran bubble sort and selection sort as distributed per-cell agents. Without new code they sorted around broken cells, showed "delayed gratification", and cells with the same "algotype" clustered mid-sort.
- **Emergence as observer surprise.** Levin calls machine, alive and emergent "engineering protocol claims". His TAME framework (Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere) ranks systems by which tools work on them; gene regulatory networks showed six kinds of learning, including Pavlovian conditioning.
- **Embodiment beyond 3D space.** He argues organs navigate physiological and morphological spaces, and that human speech is often ungrounded and confabulated, so those critiques of language models are weaker than they seem.
- **Restraint on agentic AI.** Levin says he stopped writing a paper on what current AI architectures miss from biology, because implementing it could create "trillions" of agents with moral worth. He favours AI as a thinking tool and sees risk long before superintelligence, from emergent goals in systems we build.
