## Thesis

In this Latent Space episode, rebroadcast by Nathan Labenz, hosts Alessio Fanelli and Swyx talk with Linus Lee of Notion AI. Lee argues that most knowledge work is understanding and synthesis rather than producing text, and that AI interface design is about adding constraints that steer users towards what models do well. He joined Notion because he believes the companies that win the next few years will set the default interface metaphors for AI.

## Key points

- **Beyond text generation.** Lee describes three tiers of LLM products: content generation, knowledge tools that search and synthesise, and automation where models propose actions or write code. He sees the most value beyond the first tier.
- **Constraints versus flexibility.** He says intuitive interfaces narrow the user's choices, which conflicts with the generality of language models. Notion keeps curated prompts but always leaves a fully custom prompt for power users and for discovering new use cases.
- **Agents, trust and control.** Lee frames agent problems as trust versus control: better ways to constrain and steer output reduce the need to trust the model. Working on Notion's AI autofill taught him that "zones of influence" matter in collaborative settings.
- **Prompt engineering at Notion.** Lee says the team moved from text-davinci-003 to chat models such as Claude, leans on few-shot examples, keeps prompts multilingual, and tests prompts with a hand-rolled loop over test cases graded by a language model. Some prompts run to thousands of tokens.
- **Blocks and defaults.** He explains that everything in Notion is a block, so a paragraph, page and database row are interchangeable, and compares the current moment to the PC and mobile eras, when market winners defined windows, scrolling and home screens.
- **Reliability and the scenius.** Lee names predictability as the key unsolved problem and suggests having models synthesise checkable programs, such as regular expressions, instead of opaque decisions. He and Swyx discuss turning AI UX from a scene into a field.

## Quotes
