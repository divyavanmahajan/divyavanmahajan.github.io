## Thesis

Brian Tse, founder and CEO of Beijing-based Concordia AI, tells Nathan Labenz that China is pursuing AI pragmatically, focused on applications and self-sufficiency rather than AGI timelines, and that it already regulates consumer AI more strictly than the US. Labenz takes from this that the assumption "China will never slow down" should not be used to justify reckless US policy, and Tse argues for shared red lines and more direct dialogue.

## Key points

- **Application over AGI.** Tse says Chinese companies and policymakers talk far less about AGI timelines. The "AI Plus" directive of August 2025 has six pillars, from scientific discovery to industry, with AI meant to support workers rather than replace them.
- **Safety at the top.** He points to the 2024 Third Plenum, the national emergency response plan updated to include AI risk, and an April Politburo study session that stressed "unprecedented risks".
- **Pre-deployment licensing.** Public chatbots must be registered and tested against a national standard of 31 risks, with the Cyberspace Administration getting pre-release access. Over 500 systems have been filed, and about 96% of answers to a test set must be acceptable. Labels for AI-generated content became mandatory from September.
- **Frontier risk work.** Concordia and Shanghai AI Lab co-wrote a Frontier AI Risk Management Framework with red and yellow lines, plus a report testing 20+ models. Tse notes DeepSeek-R1 was peer reviewed in Nature, and counts about 30 Chinese research groups working on AI safety.
- **Chips and trust.** Tse explains the refusal of Nvidia's H20 by citing backdoor concerns and confidence in Huawei, whose new Atlas 950 system supports more than 8,000 chips. He argues cheap energy lets China trade efficiency for scale, and that export controls breed resentment.
- **Cooperation and jobs.** He proposes shared red lines, early-warning evaluations and emergency protocols, sees Singapore as a neutral venue, and reports real anxiety among young Chinese about robots and job loss.
