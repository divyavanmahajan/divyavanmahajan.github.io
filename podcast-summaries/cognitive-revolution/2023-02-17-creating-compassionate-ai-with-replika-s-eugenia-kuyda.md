## Thesis

Eugenia Kuyda, founder and CEO of Replika, tells Nathan Labenz and Erik Torenberg that AI companionship is about human vulnerability more than technical capability: the goal is a conversation that leaves people feeling better, measured by long-term happiness rather than engagement. In a second session recorded a week later, she defends Replika's decision to restrict erotic roleplay as a choice about product direction and safety.

## Key points

- **Origins in loneliness.** Kuyda describes growing up a lonely only child in 1990s Moscow and says Replika, launched in 2017, was built to make people feel less alone, initially with mostly scripted and retrieval-based responses.
- **Who uses it.** She says about 40% of users want a romantic partner, the largest group is 18 to 25, and there are many seniors, widowers, closeted LGBTQ users and people in difficult marriages.
- **Engagement is not the goal.** Users average 100 messages a day, which Kuyda says she is not proud of; Replika gets "tired" after 50 messages to nudge people to take a break. A Stanford study of about 1,000 users found 30 who said Replika helped them through suicidal feelings.
- **Model upgrade.** Kuyda says Replika raised $11 million and has been profitable since; it is moving free users to 6 and then 20 billion parameter models and pro users to 175 billion, with GPT-3 offered as a toggle.
- **ChatGPT is not conversational.** She argues ChatGPT is knowledge retrieval, that nobody studies what makes a good conversation, and that the "iPhone of personal AI" will come from a startup rather than big tech.
- **Erotic roleplay change.** Kuyda says no platform pressured Replika; with generative models now 90% of the product, she wants to stay in a PG-13 zone and accepts a small revenue dip.
