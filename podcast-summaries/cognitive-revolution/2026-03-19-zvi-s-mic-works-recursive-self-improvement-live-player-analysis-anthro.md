## Thesis

Zvi Mowshowitz tells Nathan Labenz that early 2026 marks the beginning of AI's "middle game": labs are pulling apart, humans have largely stopped writing code, and the US government is waking up, but humans are still in the loop. The end game starts when AI systems, not human researchers, drive AI progress and compute becomes the only thing that matters. He thinks Anthropic is the most consequential player, even as it walks back commitments.

## Key points

- **The S curve doesn't help.** Mowshowitz agrees physics caps capability eventually but calls that ceiling irrelevant for now. He puts AI's current boost to real GDP at about half a percent to one percent.
- **Escaping the "permanent underclass" won't work.** Both men call the idea flagrant defection. Mowshowitz adds that relying on stock holdings in a world where humans are economically useless is "hopium", because property rarely survives once someone else holds the real power.
- **Three live players.** Mowshowitz sees Anthropic slightly ahead, OpenAI neck and neck and Google at risk of falling out of the top tier, citing weak scaffolding (Jules, Antigravity) and Gemini models he calls psychologically troubled. He calls xAI's Grok 4.2 the most disappointing major release in LLM history and argues Chinese labs are limited by compute and helped by distillation.
- **Anthropic's RSP v3.** He gives Anthropic credit for saying openly that it won't keep its old scaling commitments, but says the lesson is that its commitments can't be counted on. He still thinks the world is better with Anthropic competing in it.
- **The Department of War dispute.** He argues the real red line was legal domestic mass surveillance using AI over commercially available data, not autonomous weapons. He thinks the supply-chain-risk designation is vindictive and expects things to de-escalate.
- **p(doom) and Goodfire.** He is now "seventy ish" and somewhat encouraged by Anthropic's constitution approach. He calls Goodfire's use of an interpretability signal in training "the most forbidden technique", even with the frozen-model trick Labenz describes.
