## Thesis

Logan Kilpatrick, formerly OpenAI's developer relations lead and now product manager for Google's AI Studio, tells Nathan Labenz that Gemini 1.5 Flash makes long-context and vision use cases cheap and fast enough for production for the first time. He argues the main constraint at Google is shipping products fast enough to keep up with DeepMind's research. He also expects many competitors rather than a single winner, and sees large openings for startups.

## Key points

- **Culture.** Kilpatrick says OpenAI built strong walls between research and other teams after GPT-4, and staff there focused on ChatGPT. At Google, he says, DeepMind treats developers as a main route to the world; a DeepMind doc on Flash onboarding led to fixes within 24 hours.
- **Cost curve.** He compares the original GPT-4 at $30 per million input tokens with Flash at 35 cents under 128k tokens and 70 cents above. Labenz notes Flash's Elo of 1232, about 50 points above GPT-4 0314. Labenz fed about 250 emails, a quarter million tokens, into Flash for a client character sketch in about 45 seconds for under 20 cents.
- **Vision becomes practical.** Kilpatrick says Flash can output bounding boxes, enabling UI automation and replacing custom segmentation models, and suggests an "open Astra" could be built from off-the-shelf glasses and the API.
- **Which product to use.** AI Studio is the playground and API-key entry point; Vertex is for enterprise needs such as legal or privacy requirements. Many thousands of internal Google teams build on the same Gemini API. Flash fine-tuning was announced, with no charge for training and no extra inference cost.
- **Rumours and benchmarks.** Kilpatrick says almost no AI rumours are true. He wants leaderboards that weigh cost and latency, not just human preference.
- **Competition and AGI.** Labenz expects about 10 frontier labs; Kilpatrick expects more players and stronger open source. He says most people have never used GPT-4, so even AGI would change the world slowly.
