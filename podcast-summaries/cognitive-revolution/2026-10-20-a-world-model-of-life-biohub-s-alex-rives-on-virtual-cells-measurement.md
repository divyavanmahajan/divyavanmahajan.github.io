## Thesis

Alex Rives, Head of Science at Biohub and creator of the ESM protein language models, tells Nathan Labenz that the models now called virtual cells cannot yet simulate a cell. He argues progress needs three things together: better modelling paradigms, data at scale, and, most fundamentally, new tools to observe what matters. A full "world model of life" is far off, but he thinks an information-level model of the cell is attainable in about five years.

## Key points

- **A world without disease.** Rives calls himself practical: Biohub's target is a world without cancer, infectious disease, or chronic and degenerative disease, reached through fundamental science and programmable medicines that can "read and write the programs of biology". He frames life as a program encoded in the genome, with disease unfolding in regular ways.
- **Data regimes and inductive bias.** In the limit of infinite data and compute, Rives says architectures converge into function approximators. Protein biology, with sequencing at scale, is near that limit, which is why ESM worked; cell biology is not, so inductive bias still matters. The near-term goal is a probabilistic transition function from a cell state plus inputs to a distribution of next states.
- **The $1.8 billion data push.** The Virtual Biology Initiative, launched in April, now has a flagship partnership with the Department of Energy, the NIH, Google DeepMind, Isomorphic and Meta. Rives expects a roadmap centred on contextual, spatial maps linking molecular measurements to imaging "at the scale of hundreds of billions or more cells".
- **Measurement drives paradigm shifts.** He describes Biohub's laser phase plate, which brings phase contrast to electron microscopy, as making individual macromolecules visible inside cells through cryo-electron tomography. Biohub also images transparent zebrafish to track every cell from embryo to organism.
- **Transfer across evolution.** On the "in mice" problem, Rives says model organisms mislead in both directions. ESM and AlphaFold worked by training across all of evolution, and he argues models can learn from conserved building blocks even where programs are rewired.
- **Empirical AI.** He expects biology to follow the pretraining, elicitation and RLVR recipe, but with the lab as verifier. He predicts first drafts of cell atlases of health and disease within one to two years, warns that health records alone yield "a bunch of correlations", and is undecided on fusing biology and language latent spaces.
