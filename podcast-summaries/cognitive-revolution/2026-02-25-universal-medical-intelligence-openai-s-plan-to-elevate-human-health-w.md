## Thesis

Karan Singhal, who leads Health AI at OpenAI, tells Nathan Labenz that models now perform at a level worth trusting for health questions, and that the job has shifted from laying foundations to scaling impact. He argues that physician-built evaluations, strict privacy, free access and real-world trials can make medical expertise far more universal, while health gives safety research concrete feedback. Labenz, drawing on his son's cancer treatment, says frontier models kept pace with the attending oncologist.

## Key points

- **Product strategy.** Singhal says over 230 million people a week use ChatGPT for health and wellness questions. ChatGPT Health lets users connect medical records, wearables and Apple Health; ChatGPT for Healthcare is a HIPAA-compliant version for clinicians, launched with eight leading institutions after nine waves of red-teaming over six months.
- **Access and incentives.** Singhal says ChatGPT Health offers a reasoning model free, without rate limits, and that ads are not coming to it. Connected health data is encrypted, kept separate from other chats and not used to train foundation models.
- **Evaluation.** HealthBench, built with more than 250 physicians, covers 5,000 conversations and about 49,000 criteria, with global health as its largest theme. On HealthBench Hard, GPT-4o scored zero and OpenAI's models now reach about 40%. A worst-of-N metric tests consistency across repeated samples.
- **Real-world evidence.** In a study with Kenya's Penda Health, patients of clinicians using an AI copilot had statistically significant improvements in diagnosis and treatment. Singhal says change management mattered as much as the tool.
- **Safety approach.** Rather than one expert writing a spec, OpenAI turns the views of many physicians into evals and training data, and trains models to express uncertainty and escalate to care when needed. Singhal says OpenAI has not seen large-scale drift of chain of thought into unreadable neuralese.
- **Alignment link.** Singhal treats health as grounding for scalable oversight, which he splits into rater scaling and value oversight, and for shaping model personas that act in the patient's interest.
