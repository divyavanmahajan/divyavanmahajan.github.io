## Thesis

Matthew Harvey Sanders, founder and CEO of Longbeard, tells Nathan Labenz that the objective function of Catholic AI is fidelity to the magisterium, the Church's teaching. He argues the Church has historically been open to new technology, should help define human flourishing as an alignment target, and should favour open source and "subsidiarity" over concentrated AI power. Labenz presents the episode as evidence that groups with different values use the same techniques to align AI to their own.

## Key points

- **Openness with caution.** Sanders says recent popes have good advisers and see AI as perhaps the most powerful tool humanity has built. Labenz notes Pope Francis called AI a "true cognitive industrial revolution" at the G7 in June 2024.
- **Post-work transition.** Sanders expects about 80% of jobs in the classical GDP economy to disappear and worries more about the transition than the end state. He imagines people doing other work in what he calls an "Etsy economy".
- **Consciousness.** He does not believe current AI is sentient and separates intelligence from sentience, citing Yann LeCun. He says Catholics have no agreed definition of consciousness, but that a conscious AI would not shake the Holy See's faith.
- **Magisterium AI.** The product serves users in 165 countries. It launched with about 600 magisterial documents, which proved too few for the long tail of personal questions. Sanders says it now runs on the open model OSS via the inference provider Groq, costs $3.99 a month with a free tier, and the company has 22 staff.
- **Why train from scratch.** Sanders says fine-tuning did not hold up across the long tail of their domain, so Longbeard is training its own models with a stealth partner, filtering data and trading off languages and chain of thought. Labenz warns that fine-tuning can cause unpredictable behaviour elsewhere, citing the emergent misalignment paper.
- **Flourishing over ethics.** Sanders proposes evals for human flourishing, since people agree on that more easily than on an ethical framework. He thinks pausing AI is unrealistic while the US and China compete.
