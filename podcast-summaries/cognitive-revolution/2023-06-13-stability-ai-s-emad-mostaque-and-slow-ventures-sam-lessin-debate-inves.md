## Thesis

Slow Ventures' Sam Lessin argues that generative AI is a sustaining innovation: it extends what incumbents with data, IP and distribution already do, so few seed-stage AI startups will produce venture-scale returns. Stability AI founder Emad Mostaque agrees on much of this, but argues that value can be created where there are unmet gaps, such as education, mental health and regulated or emerging markets. Host Nathan Labenz says he mostly shares Lessin's view on investing.

## Key points

- **Extender, not reshuffler.** Lessin contrasts AI with crypto, which he calls a deck reshuffler; pitches to be "the Adobe of AI" fail, he says, because Adobe will be the Adobe of AI. Mostaque compares the current stage to the iPhone 2G/3G era.
- **Reasoning versus next-token guessing.** Mostaque calls the models reasoning machines rather than fact machines; Lessin says calling next-token prediction intelligence is "a real stretch".
- **The end of the open web.** Lessin predicts AI will collapse the information economy of the web, with data going private behind walls. Mostaque points to licensed and national broadcaster datasets and retrieval-augmented models.
- **Entertainment and companions.** Both expect AI to reshape entertainment and relationships. Mostaque cites Character AI's two hours of daily use and Replika's Valentine's Day shutdown; Lessin says IP holders and distributors capture the value and companion apps lack moats.
- **Regulated industries and the global South.** Mostaque cites adaptive-learning tablets that he says teach 76% of children literacy and numeracy in 13 months. Lessin accepts leapfrog effects abroad but doubts change in US education or health care.
- **Predictions.** Asked by Labenz, both expect US primary care prices to rise, name Stack Overflow and BPO as likely casualties, and expect marriage and birth rates to fall. Mostaque describes Stability's model as open base models plus national data and consulting partners, focused on the global South.
