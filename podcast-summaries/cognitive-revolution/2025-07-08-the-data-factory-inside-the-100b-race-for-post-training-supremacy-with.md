## Thesis

Manu Sharma, founder and CEO of Labelbox, argues that data is the third pillar of frontier AI next to compute and talent, and that post-training is where the effort now goes. The work has shifted from simple labelling to domain experts building rubrics, graders and RL environments ("gyms") that teach models long-horizon tasks. Host Nathan Labenz frames the episode against Meta's $15 billion deal with Scale AI.

## Key points

- **Post-training budgets are growing.** Sharma says base models already hold the knowledge, but labs must teach them to do useful long-horizon work, such as professional software development, which is why spending is moving to post-training.
- **From traces to rubrics.** Labenz summarises the shift from supervised fine-tuning on human reasoning traces to RL against gold-standard answers and rubrics; Sharma agrees and adds that reliable auto-graders aligned with domain experts are a work stream of their own.
- **Expert pay follows a power law.** Sharma says each frontier lab is probably spending over $1 billion a year on data, and Labelbox's top contributors earn well north of $250,000 a year, tapering to around $40,000-$50,000 in other domains and countries.
- **Context engineering over fine-tuning.** For most enterprise tasks Sharma recommends feeding models the right context, as Cursor does for code, rather than fine-tuning, which freezes an investment while base models improve. Where fine-tuning happens, he sees Qwen, Llama and Mistral.
- **AI interviews at scale.** Labelbox runs well north of 2,000 AI interviews a day with its Zara interviewer, with satisfaction scores of 4.6 to 4.7, and Sharma says it also assesses language fluency.
- **Sovereign AI and computer use.** Sharma tells governments to work backwards from the citizen services they want rather than collecting data first, and says computer-use data collection "is coming" because current models fail on spatial understanding and long sessions.
