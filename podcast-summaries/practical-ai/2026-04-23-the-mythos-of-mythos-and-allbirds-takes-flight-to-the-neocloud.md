## Thesis

In a hosts-only episode, Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson look past the hype in four April 2026 stories. Their common thread is caution: a shoe company buying GPUs, a withheld frontier model and developer leaderboards for token spend all make headlines, but the hosts argue the durable questions are about expertise, governance and what actually gets measured.

## Key points

- **Allbirds becomes a neocloud.** Whitenack explains that Allbirds sold its footwear business to American Exchange Group in March 2026 and is rebranding as AI compute infrastructure; Benson notes the shares jumped at least 700%. Whitenack is sceptical: roughly $50 million is a drop in the bucket and the company brings capital, not AI expertise.
- **What a neocloud is.** Whitenack defines it as GPU-first cloud built for AI training and inference, citing CoreWeave, Together AI and Lambda Labs. Benson expects more growth at the "far edge", in embedded devices, than in neoclouds.
- **Mythos and Project Glasswing.** Benson describes Anthropic withholding Mythos after it found thousands of vulnerabilities, and giving access to about 40 companies. Whitenack recalls the gated GPT-3 release and doubts it is world-ending, but sees a tailwind for AI governance and control.
- **Token-maxing.** Benson describes Meta gamifying developer token spend with a leaderboard that people gamed. Whitenack thinks his own company spends too little on AI, cites Jensen Huang's suggestion that a $500k engineer should spend $250k on tokens, but calls token usage a vanity metric.
- **Chat logs are discoverable.** Whitenack discusses a federal ruling that forced a defendant to hand over Claude outputs used to prepare legal materials, treating the AI as a third party and waiving privilege. He warns founders against pasting lawyers' drafts into AI tools.
