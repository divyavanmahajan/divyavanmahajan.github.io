## Thesis

In this hosts-only news roundup, Daniel Whitenack argues that open models, many of them from China, have caught up with closed frontier models for most practical uses, and that smaller specialised models are where the progress is. Both hosts expect AI policy to change under a second Trump term but are unsure how. They also ask whether AI clones and shopping agents will bring people together or push them further apart.

## Key points

- **Policy uncertainty.** Citing a Time article, Daniel notes the promise to repeal Biden's AI executive order (covered in episode 244). Chris says he hopes the new administration stays mostly hands-off, and argues that repealing the executive order or the CHIPS Act would be a mistake. Daniel mentions Intel's CHIPS Act award of up to about $7.9 billion.
- **Qwen and model security.** Daniel points to Alibaba's Qwen 2.5 models topping open leaderboards, and to Qwen Turbo extending context from 128K to 1 million tokens. He separates self-hosting downloaded weights from sending data to a Chinese API. Chris says that in defence work, Chinese models are ruled out by policy.
- **Smaller and specialised.** Daniel says gains from more data and bigger models have levelled off. He cites DeepSeek R1 Lite Preview beating o1-preview on some benchmarks, Hugging Face's small vision model, and the 350M/500M-parameter OuteTTS.
- **Meeting clones.** Daniel describes getpickle.ai, which joins calls as a polished video clone of you. Chris worries it reverses the tolerance for real home life that remote meetings built during COVID.
- **AI commerce.** Daniel covers Stripe's agent toolkit (with LangChain, CrewAI and Vercel's AI SDK) and Perplexity's shopping assistant and merchant programme. He also recommends Docling for document parsing and Observers for logging prompts to DuckDB or Argilla.
