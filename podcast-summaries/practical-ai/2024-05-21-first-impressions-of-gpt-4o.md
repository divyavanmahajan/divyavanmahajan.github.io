## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson share first impressions of OpenAI's GPT-4o ("Omni"). Benson argues that the biggest change is not raw capability but speed: fast voice replies make the assistant part of the conversation rather than a separate task. Both hosts conclude that durable value comes from solving a specific problem with domain expertise, not from wrapping a commodity LLM API.

## Key points

- **Speed changes behaviour.** Benson says GPT-4o became the most-used app on his phone and a "third party" at the kitchen table. Whitenack notes millisecond voice responses, lower prices and fewer token penalties for non-English languages.
- **Live demo, mixed results.** Whitenack asks GPT-4o questions on air; it gives fluent answers but twice cuts off, once with a heavy-load error.
- **Privacy in physical space.** Benson asks how always-listening voice use fits state recording laws, citing Sam Altman's suggestion to leave it on all the time. Whitenack links this to Alexa, Meta AI glasses, an airport "virtual assistant" kiosk in Boston and delivery robots.
- **Gadgets and wrappers at risk.** Benson warns that products such as the Rabbit R1 and Humane AI Pin can go from cool to obsolete after one announcement, and admits open models felt frustrating after a week with GPT-4o.
- **Where value lies.** Whitenack cites an Andreessen report saying about 75% of enterprise AI budgets go to engineering integrations rather than models, and points to Farsight AI's financial workflows. Benson favours small fine-tuned models in the 7-8 billion parameter range.
- **Resources.** Hugging Face's $10 million ZeroGPU compute programme, the AI Quality Conference and AI Engineer World's Fair (June 2024, San Francisco), and Salman Khan's book "Brave New Words".
