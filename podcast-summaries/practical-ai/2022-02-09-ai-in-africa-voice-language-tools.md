## Thesis

Kathleen Siminyu, Kiswahili machine learning fellow at Mozilla, argues that progress in NLP for African languages comes from grassroots communities such as Masakhane rather than from markets. She says data access is the main bottleneck, datasets should capture dialect and gender diversity, and language communities should set licensing terms so they benefit from what is built on their data. Guest co-host Joyce Nabende of the Makerere AI Lab leads the conversation.

## Key points

- **Masakhane's origin.** Siminyu says Jade Abbott and Laura Martinus founded Masakhane at the 2019 Deep Learning Indaba in Nairobi with a replicable notebook that let anyone train a machine translation benchmark for their language in an hour or two. It has since been registered as the Masakhane Research Foundation; Whitenack counts 1,304 members in its Slack.
- **Bible data and its limits.** Early work used the JW300 dataset of Jehovah's Witness Bible translations. Siminyu describes "biblification" of outputs and notes the organisation later pulled the data, saying it had not given permission.
- **Why it grew.** She credits Abbott's energy, distributed leadership, room for beginners (anyone multilingual can label data), mentorship from experienced researchers, and strong female leadership. Her worry is that new funding decisions could weaken that distributed model, and she wants a pathway for members who want to build companies.
- **Lab impact.** Nabende says her Luganda speech and translation researchers get faster help on the open Masakhane Slack than by emailing authors.
- **Kiswahili on Common Voice.** Siminyu is working with language boards and linguists to collect text and recordings that cover coastal, Nairobi, Tanzanian and DRC variants, with subsets for fine-tuning and evaluation, and deliberate outreach to women because speech recognition performs worse on women.
- **How to help.** She asks researchers to mentor junior native speakers instead of only paying them to create data, and urges communities to choose licences that centre their needs.
