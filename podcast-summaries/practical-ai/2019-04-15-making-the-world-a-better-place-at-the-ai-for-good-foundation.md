## Thesis

James Hodson, head of the AI for Good Foundation, argues that researchers rarely work on social problems because those problems have neither funding nor data, and academic careers reward fast publication. The foundation's job is to fix those incentives by connecting researchers with UN agencies, NGOs and data owners, and by pointing them at problems that have no working solution yet rather than at solved benchmarks.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Hodson says the foundation came out of 2014 Stanford workshops and started in 2015, using the UN's 17 Sustainable Development Goals as its agenda. It is a membership-funded public charity; he previously ran Bloomberg's AI research lab.
- **Structured volunteering.** A vetted "global volunteer force" forms small task forces, for example with UNESCO, each overseen by a faculty mentor. Hodson says this was added after early projects lost volunteers once the kickoff excitement faded.
- **Food Security AI Challenge.** Data from seed producers, logistics firms and markets was shared with participants and workshops at the Santa Fe Institute and KDD. Hodson says models raised annual seed-yield improvement from about 1% to 1.5%, but adoption depends on cultural and market change, not the technology alone.
- **Climate and small data.** Hodson notes almost no machine learning research is cited in the latest IPCC report and that many climate data sets have only tens of samples. He plans an Earth Day summit in Anchorage at KDD to bring the communities together.
- **Know your data.** Reusing data collected for another purpose can hide unknown measurement errors, and Hodson warns that a failed promise can lose a stakeholder's trust for good.
- **Unsolved over incremental.** Hodson contrasts adding 0.1 BLEU on French-English with a breakthrough for an under-resourced language used by 350 million people. Benson closes by challenging listeners to start their own AI for good side projects.
