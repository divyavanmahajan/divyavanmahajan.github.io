## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack review AI news from October 2018, from a GAN-painted portrait heading to auction to an assistant designed to look human. Their common thread is that AI is spreading into creative, educational and medical work, and Benson argues it works best when it adds to human capability rather than replacing it.

## Key points

- **AI portrait at Christie's.** Whitenack describes a portrait by the art collective Obvious, made with a GAN trained on 15,000 portraits from the 14th to the 20th century, with an estimated price of $7,000 to $10,000. Benson cites a study in which listeners judged AI music by whether they believed a human composed it.
- **Human-looking assistants.** Benson covers Magic Leap's assistant Mica, seen through Magic Leap One glasses; users lean back when she leans in. Whitenack notes the design principle that almost-human interfaces come across as creepier than clearly artificial avatars.
- **Graph machine learning.** From his work with SIL International, Whitenack points to Helena Deus's trip report from the International Semantic Web Conference, the Allen Institute's Semantic Scholar, and DeepMind's open-source Graph Nets library for TensorFlow.
- **MIT's College of Computing.** Benson reports a $350 million gift from Blackstone CEO Stephen Schwarzman within a $1 billion commitment and 50 new faculty positions, to bring AI to all disciplines. Whitenack hopes MIT will release resources publicly.
- **Mammogram density model.** Benson describes an MIT and Massachusetts General Hospital model trained on about 58,000 screenings. Whitenack stresses that radiologists can accept or reject its rating, so it augments their work.
- **Community and resources.** Whitenack promotes CSV Conf in Portland (May 2019); Benson says his Atlanta deep learning meetup draws 60 to 120 people. Resources include the TensorFlow Neural Network Playground, IBM AI Fairness 360, Towards Data Science and a Forbes explainer on reinforcement learning.
