## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson debate whether current AI spending is a bubble like the dot-com era. Both see crazy valuations and companies with unproven business models, but argue that real earnings, diverse revenue streams and decades of research put AI on firmer ground. Their verdict: no single classic bubble, but possibly many small ones, with the winners being companies that solve real business problems.

## Key points

- **The case for a bubble.** Whitenack cites a source saying over 50% of VC funding in Q2 2025 went to AI companies, at revenue multiples of 15x to 30x and more, well above typical SaaS rounds. He also notes a New York Times report that Nvidia is now worth $5 trillion.
- **The case against.** Fed Chair Jerome Powell told Fortune that, unlike the dot-com boom, AI companies "actually have earnings". Whitenack adds that revenue spans chips, cloud and consulting, and that investment relative to GDP is still lower than in railroads or electrification.
- **Concentration risk.** Benson says AI-related stocks account for about 75% of S&P 500 returns, 80% of earnings growth and 90% of capital spending growth, concentrated in a few firms. The cloud provider may boom while customers see no ROI.
- **Deep roots.** Benson points out that the neural networks behind today's AI are about 40 years old; his parents worked on them in the early nineties. Whitenack compares the speculation to prospecting for gold or oil: the value is known, only its location is uncertain.
- **Layoffs and cognition.** Benson raises Amazon's cut of 14,000 middle managers and asks how AI-driven unemployment feeds back into the economy. He prefers AI as a pair programmer over pure vibe coding, citing research on cognitive decline in heavy AI users.
- **AI-washing.** Whitenack notes many products labelled AI are rules engines or regression models, which makes the market hard to read.
