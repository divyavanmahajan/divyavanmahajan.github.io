## Thesis

Kirsten Lum, co-founder and CPO of Storytellers AI, argues that small organizations under-use machine learning not for lack of data but because they do not know how to hire, judge their data readiness or integrate the work. A data scientist in a small company has to cover the whole cycle at shallow depth, run projects end to end and earn trust by delivering measurable results.

## Key points

- **From English to Python.** Lum has an English degree and came to data science through startups and marketing analytics. Her first project turned an Excel bidding process that took 30 hours a week into a Python script that ran in eight seconds.
- **Why small firms hold back.** She names three barriers: not knowing how to hire, uncertainty about data infrastructure and quality, and not knowing how to feed model output into existing systems. She also says the field lacks literature written for non-data-scientist leaders such as small-company CEOs.
- **Shallow but complete.** Instead of the T-shaped profile, Lum says the job spans infrastructure, ETL for features, training, simple batch or real-time deployment, and monitoring. She advises gradient boosted trees as the default for tabular data and a clear baseline for knowing when a model is done.
- **Project management and people.** Lum recommends a strong end-to-end project framework, starting with a Google Sheet for a quarter before moving to tools like Trello, and treating colleagues' goals and KPIs as the way to earn trust.
- **Results as defence.** Becoming the person who raised email open rates 50% stops the stream of ad hoc spreadsheet requests, she argues, and A/B testing is what lets a small team show its impact.
- **Career advice.** Lum steers new graduates towards mid-size or larger companies to learn the workflow, unless a startup is led by an executive with deep data science experience.
