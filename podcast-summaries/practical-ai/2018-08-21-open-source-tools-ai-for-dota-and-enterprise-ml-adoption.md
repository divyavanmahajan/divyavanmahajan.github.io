## Thesis

In a news and learning-resources episode, Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson argue that open source tooling may explain why enterprises use cloud ML services less than expected. They also look beyond the technology, at how AI changes the purpose of companies and national strategy, and recommend hands-on tutorials and real projects as the way to learn.

## Key points

- **Enterprise adoption survey.** Whitenack highlights O'Reilly's State of Machine Learning Adoption in the Enterprise report: embedded data science teams build most models, and he reads only about 3% as using cloud ML services. Both hosts expected a higher figure; Benson expects uptake to grow as companies settle their AI strategy.
- **Open source alternatives.** Benson covers AutoKeras, an open source Python library based on neural architecture search, presented as an alternative to paying Google $20 an hour for AutoML. Whitenack recommends Netron, a neural network visualiser that supports ONNX, Keras, CoreML and TensorFlow Lite, as an example of interoperability.
- **AI and corporate purpose.** Benson summarises a Harvard Business Review piece on how AI affects business strategy, including exploiting existing advantage, long-term trade-offs and a moral or spiritual call to action. Whitenack, motivated by his Christian faith, says builders shape the morality of AI.
- **Julia and AI nationalism.** Whitenack congratulates the Julia team on version 1.0. Benson presents Ian Hogarth's "AI Nationalism" post, which argues AI policy may become one of the most important areas of government policy; Whitenack points to the Facebook congressional hearings as evidence of limited government understanding.
- **OpenAI Five.** Whitenack describes OpenAI's team of five neural networks beating 99.95th percentile Dota players, as he understands it two games out of three, noting the team play and drafting challenges.
- **Learning resources.** Whitenack recommends the PyTorch tutorials for their reusable patterns. Benson shares a Lifehacker article on getting started in machine learning and robotics, with advice to "cross the streams" and take on a real assignment such as a hackathon.
