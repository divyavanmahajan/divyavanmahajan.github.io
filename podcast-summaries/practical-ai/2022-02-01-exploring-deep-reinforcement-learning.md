## Thesis

Thomas Simonini, a developer advocate at Hugging Face who moved into AI from a law and political science degree, describes deep reinforcement learning as learning from actions and rewards. He argues it is becoming more sample-efficient but still overfits its environments, and that its value depends on AI staying open and accessible. Chris Benson hosts with guest co-host Natalie Pistunovich.

## Key points

- **Career path.** Simonini self-taught deep learning, wrote articles in 2018 that grew into a free course of 10 articles and eight videos, worked on dynamic pricing with RL at Dataiku, then moved to games and Hugging Face.
- **Why Hugging Face works.** He says the pipeline API lets non-specialists use a trained model in two lines of code, and that an active community on the forum and Discord answers questions.
- **Limits.** Citing Alex Irpan's 2018 article "Deep Reinforcement Learning Doesn't Work Yet", Simonini names sample inefficiency and, above all, poor transfer and generalisation: his Super Mario agent failed when he changed the background colour.
- **Learning path.** He advises learning deep learning and CNNs first. His course needs only Python and goes up to PPO; next steps are Unity ML-Agents for games, or OpenAI's Spinning Up and CleanRL for research.
- **Games.** Studios mostly use hand-built behaviour trees and RL is used mainly for game testing. Simonini is experimenting with NPC dialogue using GPT-3 and Replica voices, including a Hugging Face murder-mystery demo, and mentions curriculum learning for complex tasks.
- **Access and ethics.** He wants open-source models, free education and legislation that makes AI auditable for bias. He opposes military use of RL but expects it to happen, and urges practitioners to consider consequences.
