## Thesis

Marta Martinez-Cámara and Miranda Kreković, computer scientists at EPFL who co-founded GirlsCoding.org, argue that girls stay away from computing because they do not know what it is and rarely see people like them doing it, not because they lack talent. Speaking with Chris Benson at Applied Machine Learning Days, they explain how their workshops shifted from teaching code to providing role models.

## Key points

- **Origin.** Martinez-Cámara says they were usually the only women in classes, conferences and internships, and decided to reach girls aged 9-16 before university. The first workshop, with about 10 girls, ran in her home village in Spain.
- **Role models first.** Kreković says the girls' reaction to her recorded video and later visit led them to switch: more than half of each workshop is now "power talks" about mentors' jobs and historic women scientists.
- **Marketing matters.** Martinez-Cámara describes a public coding workshop with dark advertising about cars and dinosaurs that attracted only boys; both say girls arrive neutral and leave positive about studying computer science.
- **Format and tools.** Workshops start with an unplugged game where a mentor plays a literal-minded robot making a Nutella sandwich. Ages 9-12 use Scratch; 12-15 use Python in Google Colab, so no installs or licences are needed. Topics include machine learning, Instagram filters and chatbots.
- **Growth.** Kreković says volunteers grew to more than 50 mentors, with 30 at the latest event, plus a Logitech workshop. Martinez-Cámara plans to expand into German-speaking Switzerland, and former mentors want to run workshops in France, Italy and Croatia.
- **Parents.** Kreković nearly dropped coding as the only girl in her class until her engineer parents encouraged her; Martinez-Cámara recalls her truck-driver father telling her she could be the first woman driver.
