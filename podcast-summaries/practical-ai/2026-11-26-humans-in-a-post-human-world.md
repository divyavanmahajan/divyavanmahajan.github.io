## Thesis

In a solo episode drawn from his book in progress, Humans in a Post-Human World, Chris Benson argues that humans have always valued themselves by their productivity, and that exponentially improving AI and robotics will break that link. He says human-AI teaming is only a short-term answer. He asks listeners to start a non-partisan conversation about how people, and the economy, will work once humans are no longer the most productive option, and once they may no longer be the most intelligent or conscious beings either.

## Key points

- **Education is behind.** Benson says his 14-year-old daughter Athena uses AI to boost her learning and gets straight A's. But schools still test for skills the future won't need, and they still treat AI use as cheating.
- **Teaming won't last.** Benson says his own field, software engineering, has changed completely in a year. As AI keeps improving, the human role of providing vision and orchestration will shrink. That raises questions about wages, the right to work, and who buys products in a market economy.
- **Shared concerns among elites.** He cites a Wired article from June 16, 2026 on Peter Thiel's "Dialog" society, where more than 200 members predicted mass labour displacement, a swing back towards unions and government programmes, attacks on data centres and defendants choosing AI lawyers. Benson does not expect an AI winter.
- **Consciousness as computation.** Benson finds active inference and the free energy principle (Karl Friston, Andy Clark) the most convincing theory. On that view consciousness is ordinary computation, so silicon-based consciousness would not surprise him.
- **The view from the bug.** Drawing on his work as a licensed wildlife rehabilitator, and on HAL in 2001 and the aliens in The Three-Body Problem, he argues that fear of superintelligence is fear of being treated the way people treat animals they see as disposable.
