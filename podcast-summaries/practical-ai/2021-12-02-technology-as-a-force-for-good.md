## Thesis

In this bonus episode from Me, Myself, and AI, hosts Sam Ransbotham and Shervin Khodabandeh talk with Paula Goldman, Chief Ethical and Humane Use Officer at Salesforce. Goldman argues that AI ethics is going the way security went in the 1990s, from an immature field to a standard professional discipline. She says it works best when technology, human judgement and process are combined, rather than treated as a separate effort in a corner of the company.

## Key points

- **The role.** Goldman describes two jobs: helping teams anticipate the impact of technology as they build it, and setting policies so customers do not abuse Salesforce products. Before Salesforce she worked under eBay founder Pierre Omidyar, investing in technology for underserved populations.
- **Benefits and risks.** She cites Salesforce research projects such as ReceptorNet for spotting breast cancer and Shark Eye for beach safety. She also points to research by Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru showing facial recognition is less accurate on darker-skinned people.
- **AI plus humans.** Asked by Khodabandeh whether AI can make outcomes fairer, Goldman says human loan and hiring decisions are also biased, and the answer is AI combined with human judgement and guardrails.
- **Safeguards in the product.** Salesforce tools prompt users, for example warning that a zip code field can correlate with race. Its "consequence scanning", adapted from a UK nonprofit's method, asks scrum teams about intended and unintended consequences and adds the resulting ideas to the backlog.
- **Ecosystem and regulation.** Goldman says companies alone would not scale this work. She sees the debate over Europe's draft AI law and privacy and social media laws, together with civil society, as largely healthy.
- **Teaching and hiring.** Ransbotham relabels a dataset column as race to make students think again about their results. Goldman, citing DJ Patil, suggests adding an ethics question to hiring exercises and says teaching should stress that data usually represents a person.
