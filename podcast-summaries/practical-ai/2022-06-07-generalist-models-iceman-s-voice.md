## Thesis

In this hosts-only news episode, Daniel Whitenack frames DeepMind's Gato as a natural continuation of large language model work: one transformer model that handles many tasks and data types because the input context tells it what to do. Both hosts then use the AI-recreated voice of Val Kilmer in Top Gun: Maverick to argue that generative media is arriving fast and that ethics and intellectual property questions are becoming urgent.

## Key points

- **Multidisciplinary thinking.** Benson says his job is to fuse the views of specialists, and argues that practical value lies at the intersection of disciplines, which academia does not reward. Whitenack points to the agriculture ideas from the AI for Africa series and to field linguistics as examples.
- **What Gato is.** Whitenack describes it as a single multimodal, multitask, multi-embodiment model that can caption images, chat, play video games and control robot arms. He links it to the shift from fine-tuning language models to training them on many tasks through structured prompts.
- **A path to AGI?** Asked by Benson, Whitenack says the goal line keeps moving. Gato is general across the tasks AI researchers have defined, such as sentiment analysis, but those tasks cover only a small part of the world's complexity.
- **Iceman's voice.** Whitenack explains that Kilmer, unable to speak after throat cancer treatment, had his voice reconstructed with AI models. Benson notes it was trained on his old film audio and expects such media to become pervasive, contrasting it with the film's choice to avoid CGI in flight scenes.
- **Rights and estates.** Whitenack asks who is paid when a studio rebuilds an actor's voice, and what he would do if someone cloned his voice from 180 episodes of the show. Benson wonders whether voices become estate assets, citing Tolkien and Tom Cruise.
- **Responsible AI resources.** Whitenack recommends a TensorFlow team talk and tools on responsible AI, including model cards and dataset cards, aimed at developers.
