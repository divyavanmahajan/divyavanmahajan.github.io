## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson review a week of AI news and argue that practitioners should keep expectations about AI realistic, between hype and doom, while accepting that the morality built into AI comes from its creators. They close with practical learning resources on pandas, gradient descent, Vim and capsule networks.

## Key points

- **Open challenges.** Whitenack points to National Geographic's AI for Earth request for proposals, with grants he believes reach up to 200 thousand and a deadline around October, and Intel's AI Interplanetary Challenge.
- **Eye tracking and personality.** Benson describes a University of South Australia study of 42 participants that linked everyday eye movements to personality survey results, which he files as "interesting but slightly creepy".
- **Accessibility.** Whitenack highlights Abhishek Singh's project that uses a camera to translate sign language into text for Amazon Alexa, and encourages listeners to pick AI-for-good projects. Benson mentions his interest in AI for animal advocacy.
- **Business and interpretability.** Benson cites a Forbes article framing AI as "digital transformation 2.0", reshaping how whole businesses work rather than single jobs. Whitenack recommends "The Mythos of Model Interpretability", noting its relevance under GDPR.
- **Kissinger's warning.** Benson summarises Henry Kissinger's Atlantic essay "How the Enlightenment Ends", which places him alongside Elon Musk in warning of long-term dangers. Whitenack calls for moderated expectations and better communication with influencers; Benson says current deep learning does not feel threatening, though he leaves a door open for distant developments.
- **Learning resources.** Whitenack recommends a Real Python pandas performance guide and a "Mastering Vim Quickly" e-book; Benson suggests a Medium introduction to gradient descent and O'Reilly's introduction to Geoffrey Hinton's capsule networks.
