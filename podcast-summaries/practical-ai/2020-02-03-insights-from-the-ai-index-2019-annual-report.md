## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack walk through the AI Index 2019 report from Stanford's Human-Centered AI Institute and partners such as McKinsey Global Institute, OpenAI and MIT. They read its data as evidence that AI is expanding on every front (research, compute, investment, jobs) while the field shifts towards harder benchmarks, industry-led research and attention to ethics.

## Key points

- **Research output.** Benson notes China has passed Europe (and earlier the US) in publication volume, though the field-weighted citation impact of US papers is still about 50% higher. Whitenack highlights small countries with high per-capita deep learning output, such as Singapore, Switzerland, Israel and the Netherlands.
- **Conferences and diversity.** NeurIPS reached roughly 13,000 attendees, and the Women in Machine Learning workshop has 20 times more alumni than in 2015. Whitenack asks for better live streaming to cut travel and widen access.
- **Technical performance.** Training time and cost for image classification have dropped sharply. Whitenack points to the report's timeline of human-level milestones, from Othello in 1980 to diabetic retinopathy detection in 2019, and to GLUE being surpassed and replaced by SuperGLUE.
- **Investment and jobs.** The report cites over $70 billion in AI investment, more than $37 billion of it in startups. Benson says AI jobs are nearing 1% of all US jobs, up from about 0.3% around 2015; Whitenack notes India and Singapore lead AI hiring growth.
- **Academia to industry.** Both hosts describe PhDs and faculty leaving for industry. Whitenack worries academia is drifting from practical problems; Benson says industry-university partnerships at Lockheed Martin are being rebalanced.
- **Autonomy and ethics.** Autonomous vehicles drew the largest share of investment, and California has licensed testing for over 50 companies with more than 2 million miles driven. Benson closes on the rise of AI ethics principles; Whitenack recommends IBM's AI Fairness 360 toolkit.
