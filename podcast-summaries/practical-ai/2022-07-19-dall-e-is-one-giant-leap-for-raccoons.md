## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson respond to claims that large AI models are becoming sentient. Using the "stochastic parrots" paper, Whitenack argues that fluent output is coherence in the eye of the beholder, not evidence of intent. Both see DALL-E 2 as a predictable result of combining existing components across modalities, and Whitenack says practitioners should explain that history rather than feed the hype.

## Key points

- **The sentience claim.** Whitenack raises a Google engineer's claim that a dialogue model is sentient; both hosts reject it. Benson says people tend to infer a bigger jump than each milestone represents.
- **From n-grams to transformers.** Whitenack traces language models from n-gram statistics through recurrent networks to large transformers, whose output became coherent enough to mislead readers.
- **Stochastic parrots.** Whitenack reads from the paper by Timnit Gebru and co-authors: generated text is not grounded in communicative intent, and humans project meaning onto it.
- **How DALL-E 2 came about.** Whitenack explains that the original DALL-E was transformer-based, and DALL-E 2 combines CLIP text-image embeddings with diffusion models that learn to remove noise. He mentions Google's Imagen and a Cosmopolitan cover made with DALL-E. Benson calls this a normal path of technology innovation, not a surprise.
- **Next modalities.** Whitenack predicts more work mixing audio with images, and describes ideas from deaf community partners: translating between some of the world's 400 sign languages and searching sign language video by signing.
- **Enjoy the moment.** Benson urges people to appreciate current progress without stretching it into sentience, which he sees as a long way off.
