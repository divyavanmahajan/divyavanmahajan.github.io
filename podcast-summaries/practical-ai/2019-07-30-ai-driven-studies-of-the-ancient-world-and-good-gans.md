## Thesis

In this news round-up, Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson see a common thread in recent stories: AI is moving toward semi-supervised and unsupervised methods that need little labelled data. They tie this to studies of the ancient world, "good" uses of GANs, game-playing bots and Yann LeCun's case for unsupervised learning, and they question how much compute modern models really need.

## Key points

- **Lost languages and ancient games.** Whitenack describes MIT and Google Brain research that deciphered Linear B into Greek using cognates, LSTMs and attention, with no parallel data. He also covers a Netherlands researcher proposing "archaeoludology", which would use machine learning to reconstruct the rules of ancient games and generate new ones.
- **Bots in games.** Benson reports that DeepMind's AlphaStar plays anonymously on the public StarCraft II ladder, limited to a camera view and to human-level action rates. He also covers a poker bot that looks only two or three moves ahead and learned to bluff.
- **Compute and its cost.** Whitenack notes that the poker bot trained on a 64-core server for about $150, in contrast to the energy bill of large NLP models. Benson mentions a Georgia State professor who compresses trained models so inference is cheaper.
- **Good GANs.** Whitenack highlights researchers who use GANs to generate synthetic tumour imagery to train cancer-detection models. Benson admits their earlier deepfakes episode was gloomy and points to creative and educational uses.
- **Unsupervised learning.** Benson cites LeCun's argument that most human learning is self-supervised. Whitenack adds that data problems dominate real projects and that most datasets are biased or messy.
- **Resources.** Benson recommends Google Cloud's Deep Learning Containers. Whitenack recommends the numpy-ml repo, the free spaCy advanced NLP course and fast.ai's code-first NLP course.
