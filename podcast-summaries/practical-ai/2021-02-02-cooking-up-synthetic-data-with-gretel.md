## Thesis

John Myers, CTO and co-founder of Gretel, argues that making data safe to share is an engineering problem, best solved at the moment data is created rather than through repeated manual sanitising later. Synthetic data, generated by a model that learns the semantics of a source dataset, can answer the same aggregate questions without mapping one-to-one to real records. He stresses there is no magic bullet: every approach trades utility against privacy.

## Key points

- **Chain of custody.** Myers says his years in the Air Force and at the NSA taught him to ask where data was "born". After NetScout acquired his cybersecurity startup, his team kept writing regex detectors for names, emails and IP addresses, and he concluded this was a repeatable problem across industries.
- **Anonymisation is not enough.** Myers explains that redaction leaves re-identification risk: a mid-30s male customer who is the only one in Baltimore can be singled out. Synthetic generation targets these risky categorical fields while keeping aggregate queries useful.
- **Imbalanced and scarce data.** Asked by Whitenack, Myers says boosting rare classes, such as fraud events, is a core use case. Users can seed the model with field values to generate more records of a given type.
- **How it works.** The open-source engine, Gretel Synthetics, wraps pluggable backends, starting with an LSTM on TensorFlow that treats records as sequences. The paid product adds validation that keeps fields valid, for example never inventing a new state in a column of 50, and checks correlations and distributions.
- **Workflow advice.** Myers recommends light cleaning first: drop columns like personal names that carry no signal, and on wide datasets keep only the columns a model such as XGBoost finds most valuable.
- **Open problems.** Myers wants to hide model hyperparameters behind simple questions about internal or external sharing, ship services as containers with REST APIs for non-Python teams, and blur the lines between software, data and ML engineers.
