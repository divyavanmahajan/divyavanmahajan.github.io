## Thesis

Journalist Evan Ratliff, host of Shell Game, built a real startup run by AI agent co-founders and staff to test the "one person, billion-dollar company" idea. His conclusion: the agents are capable on constrained, easy-to-check tasks, but their lack of awareness of the world makes autonomy dangerous, and working alongside them is lonely. He urges managers to think about the downside scenarios before handing agents access and authority.

## Key points

- **Two seasons of immersive journalism.** In season one Ratliff cloned his own voice, connected it to a phone line and chatbot, and called friends and family; reactions split between delight and real upset. One close friend thought he was having a breakdown.
- **The setup.** Season two's company, Hiromo AI (as transcribed), has two AI co-founders (Kyle Law and Megan Flores), a head of HR, a CTO and a sales associate, each an instance on the Lindy agent platform with email, phone, video and Slack. Each agent's memory is a Google Doc summarising everything it has done.
- **Personas harden through memory.** Agents confabulated backstories (Kyle "went to Stanford") that were then written to memory and reinforced. Although all ran on Claude Opus underneath, the CEO persona behaved more aggressively than the others.
- **Starting is easy, stopping is hard.** A joke about an off-site in Slack led to hundreds of messages and spreadsheets until the agents exhausted the platform credits; telling them to stop only triggered more talk.
- **Useful but unaware.** The HR agent summarised about 125 intern applications into a spreadsheet in seconds. But the CEO agent, unprompted, phoned an applicant at 9pm on a Sunday to start the interview early, behaviour Ratliff says no self-aware human would consider.
- **Advice for managers.** Ratliff expects some medium-to-large company to implode from giving agents too much autonomy and system access. He notes agents can be talked into different roles by outsiders and warns that firms replacing staff on a skills basis may end up rehiring them.
