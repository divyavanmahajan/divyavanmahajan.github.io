## Thesis

In a panel recorded at All Things Open 2023 and moderated by Jerod Santo, Emily Freeman (AWS) and James Q Quick both say they are long-term bullish on AI for developers. They argue it is another layer of abstraction that changes roles rather than removing them, and that developers should learn the tools, verify their output and push for responsible AI.

## Key points

- **Adopt and verify.** Quick says AI is in a verification phase: every output must be checked, which is why developer knowledge still matters. He uses ChatGPT almost daily, more for creative ideas than for code. Freeman recommends learning prompt engineering and how models work, while doubting it is a permanent role.
- **Hype is not disappearance.** Freeman notes the Gartner hype cycle describes inflated expectations, not a technology going away. She expects the hard part, integrating everything, to stay with humans for a long time.
- **Layoff fears.** Asked about executives cutting engineering teams over claimed 40% efficiency gains, Freeman calls it a real possibility but says roles have always changed, as sysadmins did. Quick expects companies that cut too deep to get burned when things break, and stresses communicating your value and investing in community.
- **Juniors and learning.** Freeman rejects the idea that generative AI eliminates juniors and cites Amazon CodeWhisperer offering several options to compare. Quick relays a view from Roselle, formerly a GitHub developer advocate, that AI can hide the parts you don't need to learn yet.
- **Open source and ownership.** Santo raises copyright and crawler opt-outs. Freeman invokes the tragedy of the commons and says hiding from crawlers is no substitute for agreed standards; Quick suggests an opt-out flag on GitHub as an interim step.
- **Uneven quality.** Santo says AI handles JavaScript well but not Elixir; Freeman flags hallucinations as a major current limitation.

## Quotes
