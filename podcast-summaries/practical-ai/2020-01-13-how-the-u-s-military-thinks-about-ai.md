## Thesis

Greg Allen, Chief of Strategy and Communications at the Department of Defense's Joint Artificial Intelligence Center (JAIC), describes the JAIC as the focal point for implementing the 2018 DoD AI strategy. In his account, its job is adoption rather than research: applying AI that already works in industry and academia to defence problems, under the law of war and the Defense Innovation Board's AI principles.

## Key points

- **Background and strategy.** Allen wrote the 2017 report "Artificial Intelligence and National Security" for IARPA, published through Harvard's Belfer Center. He lists the strategy's five pillars, from delivering capabilities for mission impact to leading in military ethics and AI safety.
- **Projects and tools.** National mission initiatives include humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, predictive maintenance, cybersecurity, intelligent business automation, warfighter health and joint warfighting. Allen says the JAIC adapts open source tools within a DevSecOps process rather than giving developers stripped-down software.
- **Division of labour.** By Allen's rule of thumb, problems solvable in zero to five years fit the JAIC, while five-to-20-year research problems fit DARPA and the service laboratories.
- **Competitors.** Allen notes the 2018 National Defense Strategy names Russia and China as strategic competitors, and says China's 2017 AI strategy describes AI as a "leapfrog" technology.
- **Ethics.** Allen says the law of war (military necessity, proportionality, distinction, humanity, honour) governs use of force, and that Directive 3000.09 on autonomy in weapons dates from 2012. He summarises the Defense Innovation Board principles (responsible, equitable, traceable, reliable, governable), developed over 15 months, as being about confidence that technology does what is intended, citing the GCAS auto-recovery system; Benson notes it is a Lockheed Martin system.
- **What's next.** Allen expects the next few years to focus on adopting computer vision and NLP, with transfer learning a longer-term interest, using AlphaGo's inability to generalise as the example.
