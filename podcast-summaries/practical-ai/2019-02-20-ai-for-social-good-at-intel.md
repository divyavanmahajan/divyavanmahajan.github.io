## Thesis

Anna Bethke, who proposed and now leads AI for Social Good at Intel, argues that a company can do humanitarian AI work that also serves the business. Recorded with Chris Benson at Applied Machine Learning Days in Lausanne, she describes how existing computer vision, NLP and edge hardware can be reapplied to poaching, disability and child safety.

## Key points

- **Creating the role.** Bethke studied aerospace engineering at MIT, volunteered with Delta Analytics (tagging Open Media Foundation town hall transcripts with LDA) and pitched the role to five or six managers about a year after joining Intel as a data scientist.
- **Business case.** She names three benefits: marketing, hiring and retention of staff who want impactful work, and hardware insight. Large 3D medical images for tumour segmentation, for example, show Intel what memory its hardware must support, unlike small ImageNet images.
- **TrailGuard AI.** With Resolve, Intel put a Movidius vision processing unit in motion-triggered cameras. An SSD network detects people or vehicles on the device, cutting false alarms for park rangers and letting units run for a year or more.
- **The Wheelie.** Working with Hoobox, a RealSense 3D camera and a NUC let quadriplegic users steer a motorised wheelchair with facial gestures such as smiling or raising eyebrows, processed locally for speed.
- **NLP projects.** Hack Harassment used LSTMs to detect harassing speech online. For the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, Intel built models to locate reports, route them to the right authorities and prioritise them.
- **Advice.** Bethke suggests volunteering with groups like Delta Analytics or DataKind, building a data science background, networking heavily and attending AI for Good tracks or DSSG.
