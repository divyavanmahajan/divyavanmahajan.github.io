## Thesis

Emily Robinson, Senior Data Scientist at Warby Parker and co-author with Jacqueline Nolis of "Build a Career in Data Science" (Manning), argues that data science is a young, loosely defined field with no standard path or interview format. Candidates do better by understanding its sub-specialties, building original work to prove ability, and judging employers as carefully as employers judge them.

## Key points

- **No standard path.** Robinson contrasts software engineering, where resources like "Cracking the Coding Interview" exist, with data science interviews that range from no coding to whiteboarding, take-home projects or maths derivations. She expects specialisation, citing Airbnb's split of roles into inference, analytics and machine learning.
- **Breaking the experience paradox.** Robinson says the market is good for people with a year of experience but harder for bootcamp graduates and career changers. She recommends an original portfolio on GitHub and a blog, not another Titanic Kaggle script.
- **Assess the employer.** Robinson cites Robert Chang, who spent his first year at the Washington Post doing data engineering because there was little data infrastructure. She advises asking about data engineering and team size, and avoiding "unicorn" postings that cover all three specialties.
- **Basics over deep learning.** Robinson says AI pressure comes mostly from peers. Her brother David Robinson advises getting comfortable manipulating, summarising and visualising data and building simpler models.
- **Resumes and first months.** Robinson insists resumes still matter: one page, white space, easy to scan in 10 seconds. In a new job she recommends asking questions with curiosity and learning before trying to "fix" things.
- **Influence and failure.** Robinson suggests starting with descriptive analysis before churn models, finding executive champions, checking in often, explaining risks up front and balancing risky prediction work with safer infrastructure or dashboard projects.
