## Thesis

Bharat Sandhu, Director of Azure AI and Mixed Reality at Microsoft, tells Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack that techniques come and go, so AI projects should start from a business goal (cost, operations or revenue) with stakeholders committed up front. He describes Azure AI as the layer that packages open source tools and Microsoft Research results for both machine learning experts and ordinary developers.

## Key points

- **Business before technology.** Sandhu, who has spent close to 11 years commercialising incubation businesses at Microsoft (unified communications, Azure IoT, big data, AI), advises internal AI incubators to define KPIs and secure stakeholder buy-in for what may be a year-long project.
- **Mixed reality as applied AI.** He defines mixed reality as a common development platform spanning virtual and augmented reality, and cites HoloLens hand tracking, trained partly on synthetic 3D hand poses, and on-board speech recognition.
- **The Azure AI stack.** Sandhu describes Azure Machine Learning for professional data scientists (Jupyter, TensorFlow, PyTorch, large-scale training), pre-built Cognitive Services for other developers, and dogfooding in Teams transcription and PowerPoint Designer.
- **OpenAI infrastructure.** He says Microsoft built a cloud supercomputer for OpenAI with about 300,000 CPU cores and 10,000 GPUs, and that optimisations such as ONNX Runtime for training reach customers even without GPT-3 access.
- **Image captioning and accessibility.** Microsoft researchers pre-trained a captioning model on a "visual vocabulary" of objects before fine-tuning on full sentences, improving results used in the Seeing AI app for blind and low-vision users; Sandhu also mentions Immersive Reader.
- **Research to product.** Sandhu calls the research-engineering link "loosely coupled", with pipelines to production and a responsible AI review covering consent and bias. Customer examples include Nestle detecting phishing emails and Scandinavian Airlines detecting fraud.
