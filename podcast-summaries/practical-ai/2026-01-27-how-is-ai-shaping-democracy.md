## Thesis

Security expert Bruce Schneier, drawing on his book Rewiring Democracy (co-written with Nathan Sanders), argues that AI amplifies the intentions of whoever wields it. Its concentration in a few US companies is a market and political choice rather than a property of the technology, and cheaper public models can spread its benefits. He expects AI to make existing problems such as inequality and job insecurity worse, so those problems need political solutions.

## Key points

- **More than deepfakes.** Schneier's book covers five areas: elections, legislation, government administration, courts and citizens, with examples from Japan, Brazil, France, Chile, Germany and Taiwan. Most people's daily AI is predictive (maps, social feeds), not chat.
- **"Compared to what?"** Schneier judges AI against the alternative: an AI that listened in a Canadian emergency room wrote better handover reports than doctors did, and in places without doctors the choice is an AI or nobody. Germany's party-position chatbot worked well because its dataset was narrow.
- **Public AI.** He points to the Swiss model built at ETH Zurich without corporate funding, Singapore's Sea Lion for Southeast Asian languages and a French model tuned on French law, and urges Canada to build its own rather than host OpenAI. DeepSeek, he says, showed frontier-class models need not cost hundreds of millions.
- **Positive examples.** Takahiro Anno used an AI avatar to campaign for Tokyo mayor, then won a seat in Japan's upper chamber with Team Mirai. CalMatters' Tip Sheet flags anomalies in politicians' records for journalists. Brazil's courts use AI for scheduling and moved faster, though lawyers now file more cases.
- **Disruption ahead.** Schneier expects apprentice professions (law, medicine, accounting, architecture) to lose their junior tiers and calls for decoupling life from employment, for example health cover tied to citizenship or a universal basic income.
- **Builders' leverage.** He urges AI engineers to use their bargaining power as a moral compass, citing the Google walkout over Project Maven, and says current LLMs are on a plateau.

## Quotes
