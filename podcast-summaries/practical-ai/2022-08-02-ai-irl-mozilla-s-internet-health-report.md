## Thesis

Solana Larsen, editor of Mozilla's Internet Health Report, and Bridget Todd, host of Mozilla's IRL podcast, tell Daniel Whitenack that the 2022 report focuses entirely on AI because AI amplifies the internet's existing harms. They argue that the mystique around AI hides the people who make decisions about it, and that people harmed by AI systems hold knowledge developers lack.

## Key points

- **A single-topic report.** Larsen explains that after four broader editions covering topics from undersea cables to open-source codes of conduct, this is the first report devoted to one subject, built around the question of who has power over AI.
- **Demystifying AI.** Larsen says treating AI as magic serves those who use it to exert power; Todd recalls Larsen's advice never to write "AI does this", because people program it.
- **Data ownership.** Larsen describes gig workers tracking their own data to reverse-engineer platform algorithms, and a DAIR Institute fellow using geospatial data to document the spatial legacy of apartheid in South African townships.
- **Global South as an afterthought.** Todd cites Shmyla Khan of Pakistan's Digital Rights Foundation, who says technology is built without imagining users like her; Larsen adds that such groups are asked to fix platforms after harm is done.
- **Refusing and redesigning.** Todd points to Laura Nolan, who refused to keep working on Google's Project Maven. Larsen cites dermatology datasets made up almost entirely of white skin, and argues for community-built datasets and tools.
- **Accountability and research.** Larsen quotes a delivery-worker leader in Quito who says Ecuador's government welcomes gig platforms with few demands. Whitenack notes how fast AI research becomes products, and Larsen says the report visualises big tech's influence over benchmark datasets and research.

## Quotes
