## Thesis

Retired Lt. General Jack Shanahan, who stood up Project Maven and the DoD Joint AI Center (JAIC), argues that fielding AI at scale is mostly an organisational problem: culture, data, authorities and modern software practice matter more than the model. He says the Department of Defense must start with low-risk uses, test rigorously, and update fielded models continuously, because future conflicts will be software- and API-driven.

## Key points

- **From drone video to two startups.** Shanahan says Project Maven began because analysts could not keep up with record volumes of full motion drone video. After about 18 months he was asked to stand up the JAIC, and he describes both as "AI startups in the Pentagon".
- **Eight non-technical enablers.** He lists mandate, vision, alignment, obstacle clearers, bureaucratic enablers, resources, authorities and talent management, and says the same problems face industrial-age companies asking whether AI is worth the investment.
- **Start low-risk, fix data first.** The JAIC began with predictive maintenance and medical projects and avoided "killer drones". Shanahan says data was what most often stopped DoD AI projects, and that Maven put models into combat operations within a year.
- **Ukraine and software-defined warfare.** He points to Ukraine moving its government to the cloud and fielding around 100,000 drones, while Russian electronic warfare forces constant adaptation. He credits Nand Mulchandani's Software-Defined Warfare report as a blueprint: without digital modernisation, AI will not scale.
- **Human-machine teaming and testing.** Shanahan describes a "bell curve" in which nuclear launch decisions stay with the president, some tasks belong to machines, and most are shared. He cites AlphaGo's move 37 and the 737 MAX, and calls for test and evaluation and a risk management framework for military AI.
- **Net assessments.** His testimony to the Senate AI Insight Forum puts techno-economic net assessment first: the intelligence community must judge China's compute, models, talent and operational concepts, which are harder to observe than tanks.
