## Thesis

Don Beyer, a Democratic congressman from Virginia and AI PhD student at George Mason University, tells host Chris Benson that almost nothing in AI policy is the same as two years ago. He argues that Congress is incremental and slow, so states should be free to experiment, while the long-term answer is a "new Geneva Convention" with China, Europe and others.

## Key points

- **Mythos as a wake-up call.** Beyer says Anthropic's Mythos showed that existing cybersecurity protections can be unravelled, and credits Anthropic for giving early access to a handful of defenders. He expects a wholesale rethinking of what to protect and how.
- **Federal versus state.** He is sympathetic to Republican Jay Obernolte's case against a patchwork of state rules, but notes that Congress has passed only one AI bill (Ted Cruz's Take It Down Act) out of 80 recommendations from the bipartisan House task force, while over 700 state bills exist.
- **Shared ground.** Beyer sees bipartisan agreement on surveillance, citing concern over DOGE copying Social Security and tax records, as well as on sexual imagery and job displacement, which could hit America's 18 million white-collar workers within two to five years.
- **Abundance and redistribution.** He calls himself an AI optimist, pointing to Nick Bostrom's book on an AI utopia and fusion start-ups such as Helion and Commonwealth Fusion. He relays Geoffrey Hinton's suggestion to start with free universal healthcare rather than UBI.
- **Weapons and Anthropic.** Beyer criticises Pete Hegseth's clash with Anthropic over autonomous weapons, citing Dean Ball's argument that it threatened private property, and notes the Defense Department turned to OpenAI.
- **Incremental bills.** He backs the AI Foundation Model Transparency Act, which would require the biggest model makers to safety-test, and says existential risk worries many constituents.
