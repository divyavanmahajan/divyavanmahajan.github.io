## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson use AI Now's "A New AI Lexicon" essay series to look at how AI vocabulary shapes perception. Both argue that jargon and marketing terms make AI seem mystical and exclusive, when most systems are ordinary, practical machinery. They also cover how loose framing lets models drift into uses nobody evaluated.

## Key points

- **Electric brain.** Whitenack walks through the essay "An Electric Brain", written by an Oxford doctoral researcher, which breaks Cantonese terms into their characters: "computer" reads as "electric brain", and one reading of "artificial intelligence" points to manual labour or manpower. Benson compares that practical reading to how practitioners prefer "machine learning" over the glossier "AI".
- **Demystifying models.** Whitenack tells trainees that a model is a parameterised function: the saved file is just serialised numbers plus code that says how to use them. Benson calls a model a filter that maps inputs to outputs until it is retrained.
- **Loaded terms.** The hosts point out that "black box" implies something unknowable and carries a negative connotation. Benson says "cognitive" makes him cringe. Whitenack cites an NLP Highlights episode where Lisa Beinborn struggled to match neural network layers to brain-imaging signals.
- **Smart and function creep.** Benson argues that "smart" usually signals that a product now uses your data, which consumers may not understand. On function creep, he says teams reuse a model built for purpose A on purpose B because it costs nothing extra, skipping due diligence. Whitenack's example is building-entry face recognition being reused to check mask wearing.
- **Provider responsibility.** Benson recalls AWS pulling back from selling facial recognition to law enforcement. Whitenack suggests licence clauses, for example drawing on UN guidance against oppressing indigenous populations, as a self-regulatory lever.
- **Resource.** Whitenack recommends Jeff Heaton's free 500-page, code-first book "Applications of Deep Neural Networks with Keras" on arXiv.
