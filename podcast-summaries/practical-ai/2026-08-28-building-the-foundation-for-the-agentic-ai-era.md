## Thesis

Angie Jones, vice president of the Agentic AI Foundation and formerly an engineering leader at Block, argues that agent standards must live in a neutral home so that competitors can build on them with confidence. Drawing on her work teaching Block's whole company to use agents, she also argues that adoption works best through a small group of champions who embed what works into the systems everyone uses.

## Key points

- **Teaching 12,000 people.** At Block, Jack Dorsey asked Jones to teach the whole company, including finance, marketing, design and HR, to use agents, starting with the internal agent Goose. Her approach: stay realistic about AI's strengths and weaknesses, and start with concrete wins such as getting data in seconds through an MCP server instead of filing a report request.
- **The 1-9-90 rule for engineers.** Rather than lift 3,500 developers at once, Jones recruited 50 champions (30% of their time) from every major repo and platform. They tested techniques and baked what worked into the repos as AGENTS.md files, skills and context engineering, then ran brown-bag sessions for their teams.
- **Why a foundation.** OpenAI, Anthropic and Block formed the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation at the end of 2025. Jones says no one wants a standard "cooked in one kitchen", for example PayPal, Stripe and Visa working together on agentic commerce.
- **The projects.** AGENTS.md (OpenAI), Goose (Block), MCP (Anthropic), Agent Gateway (Solo) for controlling and observing agent traffic, and A2A, Google's agent-to-agent protocol, which had just joined.
- **Global differences.** Jones notes that mobile-first China has a strong need for agent-to-agent protocols between apps, and that the EU AI Act has created a working group on transparency. Working groups are open to anyone.
- **Speed over tradition.** She says members are paid innovators who need the standards for their products, so they move faster than a traditional foundation and collaborate despite public rivalries.
