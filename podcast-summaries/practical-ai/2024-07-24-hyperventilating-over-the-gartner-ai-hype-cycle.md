## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack, Chris Benson and MLOps Community founder Demetrios Brinkmann pick apart Gartner's 2024 Hype Cycle for AI in a deliberately light-hearted way. Their shared view is that the chart mixes overlapping terms, places some items oddly, and leaves out several things practitioners actually talk about every day. Brinkmann closes by proposing his own buzzwords, including "Broccoli AI": unglamorous AI that is good for an organisation.

## Key points

- **Cloud AI services in the trough.** Brinkmann calls it the biggest surprise that cloud AI services (Bedrock, SageMaker, Vertex) sit at the bottom of the trough of disillusionment, since that is where the money is being made; Chris suggests hype measures talk, not usage.
- **AI engineering at the peak.** Gartner puts AI engineering at the top, just above prompt engineering, responsible AI and "AI TRiSM" (trust, risk and security management), a term Daniel, who works in that area, had never heard used. Chris had called prompt engineering as a job "a passing fad".
- **Odd placements.** Brinkmann argues synthetic data should still be climbing, and Chris thinks quantum AI is even further off than Gartner's "greater than 10 years". Computer vision is the only item on the plateau of productivity.
- **What is missing.** The group notes there is no multimodal AI, RAG, small language models, AI gateways or routers, vector databases, ethical AI or GPU hardware on the chart. Chris argues RAG, and Daniel agents, are already heading into the trough.
- **Invented terms.** Brinkmann offers free range AI (trained without guardrails), EQ AI, unsustainable AI (built for a demo rather than scale, citing Devin, Rabbit and Humane) and Broccoli AI, such as traditional ML for fraud detection. Daniel adds "AI anti-nepotism" for multi-model strategies.
