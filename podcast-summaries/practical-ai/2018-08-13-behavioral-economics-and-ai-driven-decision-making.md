## Thesis

Mike Bugembe, former chief analytics officer of JustGiving, argues that the main use case for data, whether analytics or AI, is decision making, and that getting value from it is a people problem rather than a data problem. He describes how JustGiving taught staff behavioural economics, tied data work to business strategy and organised its team so that models were actually used.

## Key points

- **Data at JustGiving.** Bugembe joined in 2010 as a team of one. The platform, which has raised more than $3 billion, held data on over 20 million people from more than 100 countries, including which causes they support and why.
- **Decisions first.** He frames Facebook's EdgeRank and Google's PageRank as automated decisions, and says companies should list their key internal and external decisions before chasing a signature algorithm (JustGiving's was "GiveRank").
- **Behavioural economics.** Citing Daniel Kahneman and Dan Ariely, Bugembe used anchoring demos (Rwanda's population versus 80 million) to show human bias, but also stressed data's limits. Whitenack suggests framing AI as augmenting experts, and Bugembe agrees.
- **Strategy and use cases.** Bugembe reduces company strategy to one sentence with numeric goals, scope and an advantage, then picks decisions such as who should receive a campaign email after the Haiti earthquake. Good AI use cases are repeatable, use consistent inputs and actions, and have measurable outcomes.
- **Adoption.** He applies BJ Fogg's model (behaviour = motivation x ability x triggers): staff need incentives, usable outputs and clear communication. JustGiving trained the whole data team to communicate.
- **Team roles and lessons.** He describes four roles: business analysts, data engineers to cut data-prep time, data scientists, and engineers who make models production-ready. His main regret is not educating the whole organisation early, citing Facebook's two-week data academy.
