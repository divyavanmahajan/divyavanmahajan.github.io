## Thesis

Scott Meyer, founder and CEO of Chipp.ai, argues that the main barrier to AI at work is fear, not technical skill. Because AI adoption is bottom-up, he says companies should let each person build many small, single-purpose assistants under a clear "umbrella of safety", and share what works. Hosts Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson probe the tooling landscape, business models and privacy.

## Key points

- **The usage gap.** Meyer cites almost 50% of Americans using AI weekly against 7% of businesses, and recommends a monthly lunch and learn so employees share practices horizontally.
- **Start with what you hate.** He tells beginners to build a "digital protege" for tedious tasks and to prompt with his RIPE framework: role, instruction, parameters, example. He compares good prompters to parents of three-year-olds who give one step at a time, and suggests having the AI ask you questions first.
- **Value moves to the experience.** Meyer maps tools on horizontal versus vertical and closed versus open axes. He says Chipp's cost per API call fell 90% in eighteen months, so value lies in the customer relationship and ease of use, not the model.
- **Software as an individual sport.** Users build apps in about sixty seconds for narrow tasks, such as grant introductions or checking IRS status. Meyer says the average admin user saves sixty minutes a day, cutting admin work from ninety to thirty minutes.
- **Rules remove fear.** His privacy pyramid starts with stating what may not be shared, adds automatic redaction of personal data (a service from Whitenack's Prediction Guard), and tops out with models run in your own cloud for regulated sectors.
- **Field examples.** A Sioux Falls HVAC firm loaded its equipment manuals for technicians; others built a Canadian tariff checker and contractor supply lists. Meyer also cites a World Bank study in Nigeria where six weeks of ChatGPT tutoring equalled two years of education.
