## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson argue that generative AI has moved past its heavy R&D phase into scaling and adoption. In their view, 2024 will reward software engineering, cost-efficient models and products that differentiate, rather than companies that simply claim to "have AI". They also review how 2023 changed their own daily work.

## Key points

- **Adoption curve.** Whitenack cites a survey showing 15 to 20% of enterprises had an AI prototype or integration, and expects rapid expansion in 2024. Benson describes "AI by API" from Microsoft, Google and AWS as the default for second- and third-tier companies.
- **AI in daily work.** Benson says 2023 was the year AI entered all his workflows, from coding to his animal nonprofit. Whitenack switched from Vim to VS Code with Codeium, and points to continue.dev for using open models such as WizardCoder or Code Llama.
- **Spicy takes.** Whitenack predicts winners will hybridise traditional machine learning, such as a cheap CPU-based sentiment model, with generative AI. Benson predicts renewed interest in AGI and consciousness research, driven by fear and coming mostly from neuroscience.
- **Consensus predictions.** Whitenack distils five common forecasts: RAG improvements, open models beating GPT-4, AI augmenting rather than replacing work, more multimodal models and a shift to small language models. Benson agrees with all five and says he currently gets better Rust from Gemini.
- **Economics.** Whitenack highlights Hugging Face CEO Clem's prediction that a hyped AI company will go bankrupt or be sold cheaply as compute costs bite, and Jerry from LlamaIndex's point that shipping LLM apps reduces to software engineering fundamentals.
- **Fear and policy.** Benson notes widespread public fear, fed by Yann LeCun and Geoffrey Hinton holding opposite views, alongside the US executive order and the EU AI Act.
