## Thesis

In a short news-and-resources discussion, Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack argue that AI is moving faster than public understanding and oversight. They point to government facial recognition as the clearest example, and welcome published ethics principles, such as Google's, as a model for other organizations.

## Key points

- **National AI strategies.** Benson recommends a Medium post giving an overview of national AI strategies, with short summaries for a couple of dozen countries on a timeline, as a single place to see what countries such as China and Denmark have announced.
- **Facial recognition and government.** Whitenack mentions reports of facial recognition used at concerts in China to identify people with outstanding warrants; Benson says he is not comfortable with that approach and notes the uproar over Amazon's facial recognition work with US law enforcement. Whitenack highlights the ACLU's call for a moratorium on government use.
- **Setting expectations.** Whitenack describes a spectrum where some people think AI can solve everything and others find it creepy, and says both views need tempering. Benson adds that most people hear about AI in the news without a basis to evaluate it.
- **Ethics frameworks.** Whitenack recommends "Doing good data science" by DJ Patil, Hilary Mason and others. Benson walks through Google's "AI at Google: our principles", including being socially beneficial, avoiding unfair bias, safety testing, accountability and privacy design.
- **AI in space.** Benson notes CIMON, a small flying robot with a cartoon-like face, launched to the International Space Station on June 29 to interact with astronauts.
- **Learning resources.** The hosts point to Bloomberg's free Foundations of Machine Learning course, Google's Machine Learning Crash Course using TensorFlow APIs, and a Siraj Raval video on choosing a laptop for deep learning on a budget.
