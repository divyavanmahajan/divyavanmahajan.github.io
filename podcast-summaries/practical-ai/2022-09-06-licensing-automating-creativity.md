## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson look at AI spreading into creative work, jobs and government. Their common thread is that whoever releases a model carries responsibility for how it behaves and how it is used. Whitenack points to Hugging Face's Open RAIL licences as a practical step: open access combined with explicit restrictions on use.

## Key points

- **AI art and attribution.** Benson cites a Vice story about an AI-generated artwork that won a state fair fine arts competition, to the anger of artists. He sees AI as another tool, like Photoshop, but expects some competitions to require work done by hand. Whitenack notes that Stable Diffusion's openness, via Hugging Face Spaces and DreamStudio, has pulled attention away from DALL-E.
- **Automation of jobs.** Benson cites a Register article claiming call centres could save $80 billion by switching to AI, and argues that economics drives automation in every industry. Whitenack describes an AI assistant taking orders at a White Castle drive-through in rural Indiana.
- **France's pool detector.** French authorities used satellite imagery to find tens of thousands of undeclared swimming pools. Benson cites about 200 euros of tax per pool and roughly 10 million euros in new revenue. Whitenack notes the system first mistook solar panels for pools, with a 30% error rate, and argues that practitioners must evaluate model behaviour before release.
- **Cost of training.** Whitenack quotes a tweet saying Stable Diffusion used 256 A100s for 150K hours, about 600K at market price. That is trivial for governments but within reach of small teams, not just big tech. Benson also discusses Nvidia's public back-and-forth with the US government over A100 and H100 chips in China.
- **Open RAIL licences.** Whitenack explains that models fit neither software nor content licences because their behaviour and biases matter. Open RAIL licences, used for BLOOM and Stable Diffusion, add use restrictions, for example no harassment and no medical advice. Benson worries that explicit lists will miss unexpected harms. Whitenack replies that the BLOOM FAQ calls the list a start.
- **NormConf.** Whitenack recommends a free online conference on the unglamorous side of ML work.
