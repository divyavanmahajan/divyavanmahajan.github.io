## Thesis

The first episode of the "Spotlight on AI in Africa" series features Joyce Nabende and Mutembesa from the Makerere University AI Lab in Kampala. Both argue that the main barrier to local AI innovation is a lack of good, localised training data. They say the lab's distinctive approach is to collect that data itself and build models together with the people who will use the results.

## Key points

- **Open for Good Alliance.** Whitenack introduces the alliance, formed in 2020 with about 13 members including Canada's IDRC, GIZ's FAIR Forward and Makerere. Nabende says it grew out of work with Mozilla and FAIR Forward on Luganda speech recognition, which first needed localised text and voice data.
- **Lab history and focus.** Mutembesa says the lab began around 2009-10 with doctoral researchers returning from abroad. It worked on food security crops: crowdsourced crop disease data from farmers' phones, automated disease classification and spectrometry-based diagnosis. It now also covers health, infrastructure and languages.
- **Building with users.** Nabende describes breast and prostate cancer tools based on MRI and ultrasound, where clinicians help label lesions as models are built. For farmers new to smartphones, the lab set up a call centre to follow up on crowdsourcing.
- **Bias and ethics.** Mutembesa says selection criteria for data collection are set with national regulators, aim for geographic coverage and gender balance, and are checked afterwards by looking at impact. He says about half or more of participating farmers became village information points.
- **From research to production.** Mutembesa says research projects quickly meet strong demand once tested with small groups of farmers or clinicians. He links this to Africa's jump straight to the mobile era.
- **Community and skills.** Nabende points to Radiant Earth's satellite data repository, Masakhane's NLP community and Data Science Africa's training workshops, and previews series topics including bias, feminist AI and COVID-19.
