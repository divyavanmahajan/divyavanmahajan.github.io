## Thesis

Nicholas Mohnacky, CEO and co-founder of bundleIQ, tells Chris Benson that his company's goal is to "engineer eureka moments": vectorise the information a person creates and receives, then suggest relevant connections while they work. He argues that a narrow model built on one person's data does not need GPT-3's breadth, and that depending on a third-party black box is a weakness investors will probe.

## Key points

- **Using GPT-3, then moving off it.** Mohnacky says bundleIQ joined the GPT-3 beta and used it mainly as a benchmark and for training. Because it is a black box that could be cut off, the company has built its own ensembles of filtering and summarisation models and is moving away from it.
- **Narrow beats broad for this job.** Benson suggests a business does not need the scope of a model trained on much of the internet. Mohnacky agrees: each model is tied to one user's data and the people they work with, so it is more explicit and less creative by design.
- **The AI sidekick.** Mohnacky describes a manager writing a rejection letter and getting a nudge pointing to a similar email sent months earlier. bundleIQ avoids the word "assistant" and calls the tool a Robin-style sidekick.
- **Data sources.** The product covers a notes workspace with an AI in the editor, Gmail, Google Docs next, and a "books" integration. Mohnacky says the team graphed all seven Harry Potter books in about six minutes.
- **Unsupervised by default.** Everything so far is unsupervised. Mohnacky says manual knowledge graphs do not scale to 10,000 or 200,000 documents, and the team is debating whether to add user feedback.
- **Next markets and a long view.** Mohnacky names climate policy, citing the roughly 3,000-page IPCC report and an upcoming meeting with a large international law firm. Longer term, he speculates that knowledge workers could own and be paid for their intellectual property.
