## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson walk through the 2021 AI Index from Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence, together with the Allen Institute's Delphi model for moral judgements. Benson argues that generative AI, more than sentient AI, is the main short-term concern because it makes people question what is real. Whitenack focuses on what the numbers suggest about research, talent and training costs.

## Key points

- **Delphi.** Whitenack describes the Allen Institute for AI training Delphi on the Commonsense Norm Bank, 1.7 million examples of ethical judgements (including Dear Abby advice), and reaching 92.1% accuracy as rated by Mechanical Turk workers, above GPT-3. Benson notes it stumbles on time-of-day questions and reflects today's status quo.
- **Drug discovery and industry shift.** Whitenack cites more than $13.8 billion invested in AI drug design, four and a half times 2019. Benson notes that 65% of North American AI PhDs went into industry, up from 44.4% a decade earlier; Whitenack links this to scarce academic posts and higher salaries.
- **Generative risk.** Benson says generative text, audio and images are hard to tell from human output and, citing polarisation among his own friends in the US, worries that targeted, convincing content can break down a shared sense of reality.
- **Faster training.** Whitenack highlights ImageNet training time falling from 6.2 minutes in 2018 to 47 seconds in 2020, enabling more experiments, though he warns faster training may simply mean more training runs and energy use.
- **Citations and geography.** Both note an "AI summer" in citations from 2019, and China leading in citations, which Whitenack ties to its publication policy versus corporate secrecy in the US. Brazil, India, Canada, Singapore and South Africa show the fastest AI job growth.
- **Gaps and resources.** Benson asks why the 222-page report says little about automation and jobs; Whitenack guesses roles are changing rather than disappearing. He recommends the Hugging Face course on transformer models.
