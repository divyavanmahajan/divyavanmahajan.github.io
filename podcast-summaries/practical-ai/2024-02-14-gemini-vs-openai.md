## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack argue that Google's Gemini launch shows a model alone is not enough. The tooling, extensions and fixes built around GPT-4 are what keep it ahead in head-to-head tests. Whitenack also argues that useful LLM data analysis is a hybrid approach: the model writes Python or SQL, and conventional code does the actual computation.

## Key points

- **FCC robocall ruling.** The hosts discuss the FCC decision against AI voices in robocalls, which followed a voice clone of President Biden. Benson calls it the right call and connects it to the US executive order. Whitenack points out that legitimate synthesized voices, such as Google's demo of an AI booking a restaurant, sit in a grey zone.
- **Gemini naming.** Benson explains that Bard has been rebranded as Gemini. Gemini Pro is the free tier, roughly equivalent to GPT-3.5, and Gemini Advanced costs $20 a month and runs the Ultra model against GPT-4. He says most reviews still favour GPT-4 and that Google had only about 100 private beta testers.
- **Rough edges.** Whitenack says one of Gemini's own example prompts failed for him. Both hosts note that Anthropic, Cohere and open models are largely missing from the comparisons.
- **Other releases.** The hosts mention Unbabel's Tower translation models, the 1-billion-parameter MetaVoice text-to-speech model trending on Hugging Face, and Apple's MGIE image-editing model.
- **How LLMs analyse data.** Whitenack explains that ChatGPT's analysis feature generates and runs Python, while tools like Defog, Vanna AI and SQLCoder generate SQL. He predicts more hybrid setups, for example an LLM extracting parameters for Prophet or ARIMA forecasts.
- **Teachers.** Benson apologises to teachers for an earlier comment about schools banning ChatGPT and offers to support teachers who want AI in their classrooms. Whitenack recommends promptingguide.ai from DAIR.AI.
