## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson talk with Representative Don Beyer of Virginia, who is working towards a master's in machine learning at George Mason University. Beyer argues that Congress moves slowly and needs bipartisan agreement, so the realistic goal is to pick the handful of AI bills that actually protect people, while keeping existential risk on the agenda and pointing AI at problems such as suicide prevention, education and healthcare.

## Key points

- **Back to school.** Beyer describes failing a Coursera AI course years ago because he knew no Python or linear algebra, then starting pre-calculus at GMU; he has finished six courses, has 11 to go, and typed 93 pages of notes for his Java course.
- **From 200 bills to a handful.** A 24-member task force appointed by Speaker Mike Johnson and Hakeem Jeffries is reviewing about 200 AI bills. Beyer groups the issues as deepfakes and copyright, reliability of large language models, and safety, with Section 230 as the warning of what happens when Congress does nothing.
- **Growing interest.** The AI caucus once drew six members to lunch; Beyer says about 150 attendees now come, many of them staff. He praises Biden's executive order and the new NIST Safety Institute led by Elizabeth Kelly.
- **International coordination.** Beyer has met EU parliamentarians at least eight times over the EU AI Act, contrasts their "regulatory superpower" stance with the U.S. focus on innovation, and co-sponsored a bill with Ted Lieu and Ken Buck that bars AI from deciding on a nuclear launch.
- **Risk ladder.** He lists job loss (citing ambient clinical documentation), misinformation, bioweapons and existential risk, and says he is more cautious than the computer scientists who dismiss machine consciousness.
- **AI for hard problems.** Rather than more enterprise tools, Beyer wants predictive models for suicide risk, noting about 50,000 U.S. suicide deaths a year, and he pushes back on the EU ban on emotion recognition.
