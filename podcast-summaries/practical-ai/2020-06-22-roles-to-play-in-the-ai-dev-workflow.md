## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack walk through the stages of an AI project to show that there are many ways into the field, not one data-scientist path. Benson argues that AI development is best treated as part of agile software development, and Whitenack argues that teams that both explore and ship a solution own its robustness better than teams that split the two.

## Key points

- **Context and news.** Benson shares that his mother-in-law died of COVID, and both hosts ask listeners to apply AI skills to COVID, racial justice and ethics work. Whitenack highlights Streamlit's $21 million Series A and GPU-accelerated training in Windows Subsystem for Linux.
- **Career myths.** Benson, who mentors people moving into AI, calls it a myth that you need a PhD or must be a data scientist, comparing today to the early web, when one role quickly split into many.
- **Scoping and solution architecture.** Whitenack describes a fuzzy first phase of defining the problem and data, using a manufacturing camera example. Benson adds that a neural network should only be chosen if cheaper approaches do worse, and that domain expertise matters.
- **From notebook to production.** Whitenack contrasts Jupyter notebook proofs of concept with Docker containers on Kubernetes and GPU infrastructure. Benson asks both software developers and data scientists to drop their biases about each other's tools.
- **AI engineering and domains.** Benson expects most deployment to move to the edge, such as autonomous vehicles, and cites his own path from server-based work to warehouse robotics to defence. Whitenack notes that manufacturing vision and web NLP have their own tooling.
- **Fairness resources.** Whitenack recommends Google's ML crash course on bias types, IBM's Fairness 360, Google's responsible AI practices and Driven Data's Deon checklist. Benson adds the US Department of Defense AI ethical principles.
