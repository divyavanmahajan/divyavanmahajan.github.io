## Thesis

Ksenia Se sets up her own OpenAI dot at DevDay 2026 to test whether an always-on assistant can close the "capability overhang", the gap between what AI can do and what people actually do with it. Her view is that much of a dot's usefulness comes from the system around the model: its own cloud computer, connected apps, carried-over context and permission checks. That same system is also what could lock users in.

## Key points

- **The setup.** A dot runs on GPT-6 Astra, has its own cloud computer and browser, and OpenAI says its plugin ecosystem reaches more than 4,000 apps. Her dot, a lavender owl called Turrie, was designed from what ChatGPT knew about her.
- **Inbox results.** It started with 14 archived work emails, then moved to larger batches. When she phoned it, it reported 4,567 emails archived across both inboxes. Within three hours she had also replied to 15 emails that had waited three weeks.
- **Proactive but bounded.** An OpenAI documentation engineer told her his dot spotted a product change the docs no longer reflected. OpenAI says the background research is read-only; any action is checked against user instructions, custom rules and built-in safeguards.
- **Cost and routing.** Dots currently use only Astra, which makes them expensive. OpenAI told her it is working on routing between models but has not announced it as a shipping feature.
- **Why the acquisitions matter.** She links Ona (persistent cloud environments), Sky (a Mac assistant that acts through apps) and Rockset (data infrastructure) to the direction of Dots.
- **Competition and lock-in.** Grok Bot coordinates several bots, Meta's Muse works through WhatsApp, and Anthropic is combining Cowork and chat. At OpenAI, Sam and Thibault favour one dot per person. Recalling Microsoft's history, she warns that moving a team's habits and context is much harder than switching models.
