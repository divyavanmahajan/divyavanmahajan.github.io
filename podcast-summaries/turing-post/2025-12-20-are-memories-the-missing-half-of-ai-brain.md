## Thesis

Shawn Shen, a former Meta Reality Labs researcher who co-founded Memories.ai, argues that cognition has two parts, intelligence and memory, and that AI has mostly built only the first. Glasses, robots and cameras that see the world also need to remember what they saw. He describes memory as an indexing and retrieval system, which he says makes it lower risk than generative intelligence.

## Key points

- **Memory as a system.** Shen says Memories.ai's Large Visual Memory Model is still transformer-based. It is trained as an all-in-one embedding model that maps video, audio, text and actions into one space, paired with a retrieval system. The team is working on a world-model architecture so it can track people and objects over time.
- **Encode for machine.** Video compression was designed for humans to watch. Shen says his team trains compression and indexing together so that only AI reads the output.
- **On device.** Humanoids cannot stream video to the cloud all the time, so the models have to be small and low-power. That is why Memories.ai partners with Qualcomm. The constant trade-off is accuracy against efficiency.
- **Demand-led roadmap.** Shen says that since launch they have had more than 100 to 200 inbound requests and sent 60 to 70 contracts. Customers ask for human and object tracking and multimodal speaker diarisation. He sees Twelve Labs and Coactive as aimed more at studios and publishers.
- **Forgetting and safety.** Text memory tools like Mem0 and Letta decide what to keep through context engineering. Shen expects forgetting to emerge from training, as in ChatGPT, if enough life-logging video data becomes available. Ksenia Se asks whether a system that never forgets is dangerous. Shen replies that it only rearranges data and does not create new ideas.
- **AGI and books.** Shen expects AGI in 10 to 20 years. He recommends The Mom Test for researchers who are turning into product builders.
