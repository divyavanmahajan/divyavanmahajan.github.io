## Thesis

Ulrik Hansen, president and co-founder of Encord, argues that compute and model size are not the main bottleneck: data is. Most models are good enough and swappable, he says, and what separates working systems from failed ones is how well teams orchestrate data and build feedback loops that carry human corrections back into training.

## Key points

- **From quantity to quality.** Hansen says Encord started in 2021 automating bounding-box annotation, and the work has since shifted to post-training and alignment, with frontier labs hiring PhDs. He puts robotics roughly where language models were in 2017-2018.
- **Tesla versus Waymo.** Hansen, a Tesla owner, says every driver intervention is sent back to Tesla, which works like a built-in thumbs-up/thumbs-down button. That gives Tesla a compounding data advantage, while Waymo collects its own data with extra sensors: slower and more expensive, though possibly safer. Waymo's lead with regulators is the open question.
- **Embodied AI is harder.** Robots run on GPU-constrained edge hardware and may lack Wi-Fi, so failures such as a laundry-folding robot's are hard to feed back. Digital tools such as chat and coding get instant feedback. Teleoperation helps, but Hansen says fully autonomous robots cannot rely on it.
- **Humans as preference providers.** He sees AI doing roughly 80% of the baseline work while experts, such as an investment banking analyst, own edge cases and policy. He splits the market into services-first firms that supply experts to frontier labs (he places Scale here) and software-first firms like Encord that sell tooling to Fortune 500 and applied AI teams.
- **Synthetic data needs a hybrid.** Physics-based simulation works well for self-driving edge cases. Models training on other models' output risks collapse, "the snake eating its own tail", so he favours combining real and synthetic data.
- **Cheap and expensive intelligence.** Hansen expects facts and pattern work to become nearly free, while taste, creativity and vision stay valuable. He also expects brands to matter more for trust, and sees a shift to a "connection economy". His book pick is Mason Currey's *Daily Rituals: How Artists Work*.
