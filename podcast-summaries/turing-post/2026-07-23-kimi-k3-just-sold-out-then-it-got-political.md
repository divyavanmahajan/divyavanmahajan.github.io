## Thesis

Ksenia Se reads four days of news as one story: Moonshot pausing Kimi K3 subscriptions, Xi Jinping endorsing open-source AI, Alibaba promising an open Qwen 3.8, and reports of a possible US ban on Chinese models. In her view, three logics make openness China's strategy: distribution, hardware and diplomacy. The bottleneck for open AI is shifting from capability to compute to permission.

## Key points

- **Sold out.** Three days after launch, Moonshot paused new subscriptions because demand was pushing its GPUs to their limits. It also split its membership into separate app and Kimi Code tiers. Se explains that agentic coding sessions run for hours with large contexts, so each coding subscriber is an ongoing compute obligation.
- **A shortage as an IPO pitch.** Moonshot is preparing a Hong Kong IPO at a potential valuation above $30 billion, with annual recurring revenue reportedly at $300 million in June, up from $200 million in April. Se argues a sellout proves demand. Releasing the weights on July 27th lets large customers serve themselves, although Databricks' CEO says even it is running out of GPUs hosting such models.
- **One ecosystem escalating.** Alibaba, which holds roughly 36% of Moonshot, teased a 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen 3.8 with an unaudited open-weight promise. At WAIC, Xi framed open models as a public good, and 29 countries agreed to form a Shanghai-based cooperation organization (WAICO).
- **Three-level plan.** Commercial: a closed Chinese API has no path to Western customers, but open weights do. Industrial: free models create inference demand, where Huawei and domestic chips can compete. State: the US offers intelligence as a subscription, China as infrastructure.
- **Limits of a ban.** Citing Axios, Se says the Trump administration may ban Chinese models. A ban could reach APIs, app stores, procurement and compliance teams, but not weights that have already been downloaded.
