## Thesis

Sarah Bird, Chief Product Officer of Responsible AI at Microsoft, rejects the idea of "making AI responsible": humans stay responsible, and the job is to build AI that is trustworthy for a specific task in a specific context. Her biggest current concern is that agentic coding is replacing the software development lifecycle that responsible AI practice was built around, so human oversight needs new tools to keep pace with machine speed.

## Key points

- **Trust is contextual.** Bird says she might trust AI to draft a paragraph she will edit but not yet to make clinical decisions; "fit for purpose" is the test users should apply, along with checking a provider's data and privacy guarantees.
- **Open-source tooling.** At Build, her team released Assert, which turns high-level risk concerns into detailed tests, and an agent control specification that any runtime can implement to enforce agent policies. They also open-sourced Clarity, a tool for thinking through risks. Bird says Microsoft does not want to compete on responsible AI.
- **Responsibility is layered.** Model providers should prevent novel dangerous capabilities, while only the application developer (for example a bank) can test fitness for its use case. She calls "fix it in the model" appealing but unworkable, noting her own team generates harmful content to train guardrails.
- **Risk scales from chat to physical AI.** Chat leaves room for human correction; agents calling tools across organisational boundaries can take one-way actions, and physical systems raise the consequences further.
- **Three priorities.** Emerging dual-use risks (cyber, bio, chemical, psychosocial), governing agents that now act as the interface between organisations, and the new software lifecycle.
- **Education and optimism.** She cites the New South Wales Department of Education's red/yellow/green "stoplight" policy for AI use in assignments. Bird says her p(doom) is low and wants less focus on sci-fi scenarios and more on present practice. Her book pick is *The Culture Map*.
