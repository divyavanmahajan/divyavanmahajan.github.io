## Thesis

Peter Norvig, co-author of *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* with Stuart Russell, argues that today's neural nets with attention are roughly on the right path: they need logic and better world modelling added on top, not a rebuild. He also tells Ksenia Se that education should change now that AI can do the old assignments, and that he worries about concentrated power in AI.

## Key points

- **Insiders are bad forecasters.** Norvig once thought the open internet could not work because only a few hundred people could set up a web server properly. He missed that demand produces tools, such as packages from Netscape or Microsoft, that make hard things easy. He says we should ask end users what tools they need.
- **Neural nets plus clever algorithms.** AlphaGo beat the world champion by combining neural nets with Monte Carlo tree search. Norvig expects that mix to continue, with logic learned on top of a neural core, the way humans learn reasoning after perception.
- **The disagreement with LeCun.** Norvig agrees with Yann LeCun on what models should do better. He sees language models as world models already, because they know a cat chasing a rat is more likely than the reverse. LeCun wants to start over; Norvig expects small tweaks to get there.
- **Agents, then and now.** The publisher put an "intelligent agent book" banner on the 1995 edition. Norvig says the book underplayed how to build agents and how swarms of them interact.
- **Three levels of feedback at Google.** Search used billions of clicks, tens of thousands of human-rater comparisons and dozens of in-depth user studies. Norvig says it would have been incomplete without any one of them.
- **Education and power.** He urges CS departments to optimize for minors rather than majors, and suggests problem-setting and oral exams in place of essays. In his Stanford human-centered AI class, every student built an app, including lawyers and sociologists. Smaller labs such as DeepSeek and Kimi make him hope power will be more widely distributed.
