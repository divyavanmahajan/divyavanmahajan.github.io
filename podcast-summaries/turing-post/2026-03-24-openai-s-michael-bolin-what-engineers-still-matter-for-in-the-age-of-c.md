## Thesis

In the second part of his interview with Ksenia Se, Michael Bolin, lead for open-source Codex at OpenAI, argues that agents will take over most code writing, and that this changes what an engineer does rather than making the role pointless. For now, the human contribution is taste, judgement and asking the right questions. He warns that handing those over too early is the main way the agent era could go wrong.

## Key points

- **Building for agents.** Bolin says switching from writing tools for developers to writing them for agents is "a bit different, but not wildly different". It is arguably easier, with one customer who has fewer opinions.
- **Identity beyond typing.** Bolin has long taken pride in writing a lot of code, yet he enjoys working through agents, because the typing was always a means to the artifact. He says he builds more now, mainly because prototypes that once took a whole weekend have become cheap.
- **Skills that matter.** He says he does not claim to know what the job will look like. His current advice: be able to read code, develop taste about what "better" means, move between levels of abstraction, and stay curious enough to question the model about why code is the way it is.
- **Agents will write the code.** Bolin rarely types code himself now, except for security-sensitive parts, and even those he has the agent check many times. He expects that one day Codex will have better taste than he does, but cannot say when.
- **The failure mode.** His worry is that people stop applying taste too early and end up with projects that are bloated or falling over six months later. Ping-ponging random requirements at a machine will not produce artifacts built to last.
- **The human loop shrinks.** Over time, harnesses may learn to run their own periodic architecture reviews, replacing today's nightly cron job, which would leave less for the human to do. His favourite book is Neal Stephenson's *Cryptonomicon*.
