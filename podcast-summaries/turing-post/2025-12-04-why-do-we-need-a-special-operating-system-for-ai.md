## Thesis

Renen Hallak, co-founder and CEO of VAST Data, tells Ksenia Se that enterprises are moving agents from sandboxes into production. He argues this needs a new software layer, an "AI operating system," between new hardware and agentic applications. It would handle data, compute, security and policy, things older storage, database and orchestration systems were never built to do.

## Key points

- **Bigger, but slower.** Hallak says VAST has seen hundreds of enterprise projects move to production in the past year. He thinks enterprises underestimate how big the change will be and overestimate how fast it will come. In ten years he expects every kind of work to be augmented, and questions such as what money means to be open.
- **Why start in 2016.** Seeing neural nets recognise cats convinced him that data access was the limiting factor. VAST designed for exabyte scale from the beginning. Its customers have moved from hedge funds and life science institutes to AI clouds and now enterprises.
- **From storage to OS.** Each layer came from customer requests: storage, then the VAST database for metadata on unstructured data, then data-driven compute orchestration across GPUs. What is still missing, he says, is security, observability, reproducibility and compliance.
- **Path to thinking machines.** Hallak argues agents need to fine-tune their own models of the world, talk to each other, and act in the physical world through robots. All of this needs policies about who can see which data, plus the ability to answer why an agent gave a certain answer.
- **Control, like raising kids.** He does not believe in p(doom). He compares AI to raising children: give them values and observability, but leave room for unexpected results.
- **How the company runs.** VAST has no five-year plan, only a one-year plan it revises every quarter. Hallak credits first-principles thinking from his maths training and has recently read biographies of Elon Musk and Leonardo da Vinci.
