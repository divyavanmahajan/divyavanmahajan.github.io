## Thesis

Eric Ries, author of *The Lean Startup*, presents his new book *Incorruptible* as its missing second half: the more successful a company becomes, the more it attracts people who want to make money without creating value, which he calls corruption. He argues builders must protect their mission structurally and early, and that as AI and institutional decay force a wave of replacement, the values built into new institutions are up for grabs.

## Key points

- **"Always too early until it's too late."** Ries says lawyers, investors and bankers routinely tell founders to postpone mission protection until they have lost the leverage to do it. He addresses the book even to "future builders" who have nothing to protect yet.
- **The spreadsheet problem.** The most important decisions show negative ROI because their returns are intangible and their costs tangible. He cites private equity takeovers that ruin product quality.
- **Financial gravity.** Organisations converge on the same behaviour because a "gravitational" force pulls them toward mediocrity and extraction. Successful companies become gravitational actors themselves and can "bend the light" toward a mission-driven economy.
- **Love and trust as strategy.** Ries credits Airbnb founder Brian Chesky, interviewed during the pandemic, with the idea that businesses are built on love and trust. He calls trustworthiness the most underrated asset in business, citing research on retention, customer loyalty and cheaper capital.
- **Institutional turnover.** Younger generations no longer defer to institutions that keep failing. Ries warns that disruption does not carry over civic values, pointing to the spillover effects of local news deserts after Google and Facebook replaced newspapers.
- **AI: augment, not replace.** He says the AGI goalposts keep moving and criticises executives rewarded for AI-driven layoffs. Because LLMs are best at summarising, he sees a possible AI "mission guardian" that checks whether a company's legal and operational systems match its stated mission. He credits James O'Toole's *The Enlightened Capitalists* as a key influence.
