## Thesis

Clem Delangue, co-founder and CEO of Hugging Face, argues that agents are about to widen the pool of people who can train, fine-tune and run models, which would return control to builders and reduce their dependence on closed APIs. He calls open source safer and economically necessary, and warns that US lobbying against it would concentrate power and cost the country its lead.

## Key points

- **ML Intern and 100 million builders.** Delangue says default coding agents are poor at building AI, but with better harnesses and Hub tools, Hugging Face's ML Intern fine-tunes small models, builds datasets and passed the team's researcher interview test in half an hour. He expects AI builders to grow from low millions to perhaps 100 million.
- **Building changes perception.** Buyers of Hugging Face's almost 10,000 Reachy Mini robots come to like AI robots once they assemble them and build apps. He calls fear-based AI marketing misleading; sales emails followed Project Glasswing within days.
- **Open source and security.** He argues open repositories are patched faster, closed access widens the gap between attackers and defenders, and companies citing "safety" for staying closed often have business reasons, which he says is legitimate if stated openly. Mistral and Cohere, he says, benefited from releasing work openly.
- **Agents as users.** He would not be surprised if Hugging Face had more agent users than human users by the end of the year; the team is prioritising CLIs, APIs and token efficiency.
- **Local and specialised models.** Today 99% of workloads are calls to large proprietary APIs; he expects that to shift toward something like 5% frontier API use, with the rest on specialised open or local models (Hugging Face's team includes llama.cpp). He calls comparing open weights with full API systems "apples to oranges"; training on your own data is the lasting differentiator.
- **Scale and constraints.** Hugging Face has 15 million builders, almost 3 million public models, almost a million datasets and about 200 staff. Robotics needs more datasets (hence Hugging Face Buckets) and builders; LeRobot is the most used open robotics library. His book pick is Camus's *The Myth of Sisyphus*.
