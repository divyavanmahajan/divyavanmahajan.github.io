## Thesis

Mario Rodriguez, Chief Product Officer at GitHub, argues that a jump in model capability around December 2025 turned agent delegation from a correction process into a creation process, and GitHub's activity grew sharply as a result. He frames GitHub's job as "low floor, high ceiling": making creation accessible to newcomers while giving skilled developers more power, with the human always in the loop.

## Key points

- **Compounding growth.** Rodriguez says more commits lead to more PRs, Actions runs and security scans; agents opened 17 million PRs in March alone. GitHub is moving load to Azure because one data center hit its limit, and it had to ask outside providers to add network capacity on the US West Coast.
- **Creation drives progress.** Citing an MIT design idea passed on by GitHub's head of design John Maeda, he argues lowering the floor is a GDP story, because growth comes from human creation. His example: there were probably other Mozarts who never had a piano.
- **From UI to UX to AX.** The new Copilot app introduces "canvases", interfaces that are bidirectional: the agent can read and change the UI, and UI actions such as a "summarize" button can drive the agent.
- **Against parallelization hype.** Rodriguez rejects the idea that everyone must run 50 agents; he keeps one to three creative tasks going and compares macro delegation to drawing a CAD file and handing it to a 3D printer.
- **Cost and routing.** On the June 1 move to usage-based billing, he points to the "auto" setting's semantic routing (small models such as MAI Code One Flash for simple tasks, frontier models for hard ones) and Chronicle, which stores sessions and suggests cost savings.
- **Prototype versus production.** Sloppy, vibe-coded prototypes are fine for exploration, but professional software needs quality, maintainability and security. Copilot was named "Copilot, not Pilot" in 2021 because the human stays central.
