## Thesis

Ben Goodger has worked on Firefox, Chrome and now ChatGPT Atlas. He tells Ksenia Se that putting ChatGPT inside the browser makes it easier to ask questions, and that agents acting for you are the next step. For OpenAI, he says, the browser is the environment where AGI can take action. It has to win trust step by step through transparency and control.

## Key points

- **More curiosity.** Goodger says Atlas has made him ask more questions, because he no longer has to turn a thought into a search query or explain to a separate ChatGPT tab what he is looking at.
- **How agents use the web.** ChatGPT can reach sites through connectors (direct API access) or through agent mode, which reads the DOM or uses computer-use vision. His advice to site owners is to improve accessibility, because what helps screen readers also helps agents.
- **Safety by design.** Ksenia Se raises Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta" for agents. Goodger lists the safeguards: agent mode is opt-in, users can run it signed out, sensitive sites like email stop the agent if you switch tabs, and there is a big red stop button. He compares building trust to teaching a child to ride a bike.
- **Current use cases.** Goodger names research, shopping (coupon codes, prices over Black Friday) and boring tasks like building a Google Form. Atlas uses the user's existing ChatGPT plan.
- **Strategy and roadmap.** He compares this moment to Netscape 1.0. Atlas launched on macOS, with Windows and mobile in progress and releases every week. Long term, he sees it becoming a management console for your life.
- **Book.** Goodger picks Steinbeck's East of Eden and says AI is the next step after drag-and-drop and the web in letting people tell machines what they want in their own words.
