## Thesis

Mati Staniszewski, co-founder and CEO of ElevenLabs, says the company started partly because Polish films dub every character with a single narrator's voice. He expects real-time translated media streams this year. He thinks earbud-style live translation will arrive in two to three years and be usable anywhere within five. The hard parts are carrying emotion and context across languages, and making models small and fast enough.

## Key points

- **A three-step pipeline.** Staniszewski describes speech-to-text, then an LLM for translation, then text-to-speech. He says speech recognition is mostly solved for major languages and points to ElevenLabs' Scribe model. Translation struggles with niche phrases and context such as sarcasm. Emotion is the missing piece because there is little speech data annotated with how something was said, so ElevenLabs works with voice coaches to build that data.
- **Where it works now.** He cites Hippocratic, which automates nurses' patient follow-up calls, and EliseAI, which live-translates customer-support calls between languages, though not yet with the original emotion. Live sports dubbing remains hard.
- **Dubbing Lex Fridman.** He says each podcast took one to two weeks, mostly on translation that also had to keep the original length; Spanish runs 30-40% longer than English when spoken.
- **Latency and interruptions.** End-to-end response time is about one to 1.2 seconds, and ElevenLabs' text-to-speech takes 70 milliseconds. The system combines context and silence signals and pre-generates part of the LLM response. A planned speaker-embedding check should filter out other voices.
- **Pipeline vs multimodal.** Staniszewski recommends the three-step stack for enterprises because it is easier to control. He sees end-to-end multimodal models as more natural but less stable, and expects them to suit emotional, no-action use cases over the next few years.
- **Strategy.** He says ElevenLabs shows rather than tells, through creators and developers on one side and deep enterprise deals such as Deutsche Telekom on the other. It has not open-sourced its models to protect its research advantage against better-resourced competitors.
