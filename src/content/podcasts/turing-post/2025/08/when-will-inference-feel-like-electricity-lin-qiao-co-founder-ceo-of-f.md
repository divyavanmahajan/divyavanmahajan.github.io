## Thesis

Lin Qiao, co-founder and CEO of Fireworks AI and former head of PyTorch at Meta, argues that inference cost is what holds GenAI applications back. In GenAI, she says, finding product-market fit and building a viable business are separate problems. The fix is to close the gap between the data foundation models are trained on and each product's own data, while hardware efficiency improves over time.

## Key points

- **Product-market fit can bankrupt you.** Qiao describes startups with waiting lists of millions of users that cannot open access because GPU costs would exhaust their money. She compares GenAI apps to an iceberg mostly under water, and expects far more apps to surface if costs fall tenfold.
- **The alignment gap.** Labs curate training data for their own goals, while app builders optimise for engagement using product data. Qiao calls this mismatch the root cause of gaps in accuracy, latency and cost.
- **Betting on inference early.** Fireworks was founded in 2022 by seven people who had spent years at Meta. They went all in on inference because, she says, it scales with the number of users, while training scales with a small pool of researchers. Its "3D optimizer" tunes quality, speed and cost together across more than 100,000 configurations, much as a database query optimizer picks a plan.
- **Agents and enterprises.** Qiao calls 2025 the year of agents (coding, hiring, SRE, customer service). Fireworks focuses on open models, but its private benchmarks will recommend a closed model when it performs better.
- **Open models are converging.** She says Chinese labs (DeepSeek R1, Kimi K2, Qwen 3, GLM) innovate despite having fewer GPUs, because talent and data are spreading evenly. Text models are converging, while closed models still lead in multimodal. She doesn't see Meta's open-source stance as final.
- **Cost outlook.** Qiao says Fireworks already delivers improvements of four to more than 10 times, and she expects the industry to reach 100 times more efficient infrastructure, following the path CPUs took. Asked for a book, she credits instead the people who pushed her past her own inner doubts.
