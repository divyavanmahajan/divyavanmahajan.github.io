## Thesis

Linda Tong, CEO of Webflow, tells Ksenia Se that AI agents and chatbots now account for a growing share of website traffic. In her view sites need an agent-optimized layer, much as they once needed mobile versions. Optimizing for AI answer engines (AEO) is extra work on top of SEO, not a replacement for it. She thinks the bigger opportunity is what comes after a site is built: web experiences that change for each visitor.

## Key points

- **Bot traffic is rising.** Tong says the human-to-bot split varies widely, from 90% human on some sites to 90% bots on others. She expects bots to be the majority of traffic by the end of this year. Her advice is not to block good bots, because LLMs need to index a site in order to reference it.
- **What agents need.** She lists three things: a simple, structured view of site architecture and brand, links that tie together a brand's presence across the web, and fresh, authentic, opinionated content, because recency is weighted heavily.
- **Actions through MCP and APIs.** Today companies mostly use MCP servers to let agents act, layered on top of the human user journey. Tong sees this as a midterm fix. She suggests publishing a manifest of allowed actions and guardrails, and expects agentic platforms hooked directly into those actions to win.
- **Hybrid, not dual, interfaces.** Because people don't fully trust agents yet, she expects humans to stay in the loop, using visual interfaces while agents handle routine tasks.
- **SEO is not dead.** Tong compares the "SEO is dead" claim to "radio is dead". She says AEO weights authenticity, Reddit and Quora references, freshness and readable structure, and that each model (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude) weights these differently, which is why Webflow has published best practices rather than an official guide.
- **From creation to dynamism.** She argues that static websites copy the limits of physical buildings. AI now makes it practical to personalize a site by location, return visits and visitor history.
