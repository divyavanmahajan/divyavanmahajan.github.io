## Thesis

Ammaar Reshi, who leads product and design at Google AI Studio (DeepMind), argues that the hard problem is no longer whether models can code but how to let people who think in terms of problems, not apps, build what they imagine. His approach: abstract away the infrastructure, make the model a co-creator, and design agent interfaces around the check-ins people already use with human teams. He is self-taught and believes nothing is impossible, "just time and iteration."

## Key points

- **An unusual path.** Reshi started by writing iPhone app reviews as a teenager using his dad's phone, taught himself design, and was hired full-time at Palantir after blog posts led to interviews. He later worked at Brex and ElevenLabs.
- **Speed is the bottleneck.** Two years ago he would have said models needed to get better at code; now he says they are good enough, and speed matters most, especially for building on a phone. The Flash series is AI Studio's most popular and default model; compute capacity also limits free use.
- **Teaching users what's possible.** Users should not need to know what an NPM package is; asking for "slick animations" should pull in Framer Motion. A "tap, tap, tap" feature helps people build long, rich prompts step by step.
- **Abstraction over integration.** If someone wants an app to store their movies, AI Studio should add Firebase without mentioning Firebase or OAuth. Teams start from the problem, not the integration.
- **Agents as a team.** Reshi expects single chat threads to become something like Slack, with "@all agents" check-ins, daily stand-ups and agents taking Kanban tickets, organised around transparency, verification and coordination.
- **Spreading inside Google.** He runs sessions teaching PMs at YouTube and elsewhere to vibe code prototypes, and open-sources most of his demos. He says the gap between a demo and a product is scale, security, privacy and red teaming. His AGI test is solving new problems without documentation and continuing to learn after training.
