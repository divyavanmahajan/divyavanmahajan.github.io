## Thesis

In this Attention Span essay, Ksenia Se argues that two common views of NVIDIA are both wrong: that Jensen Huang made every correct call for thirty years, and that the company gives nothing to open source. She traces how NVIDIA learned that developers, often outsiders, decide which platforms win. She argues that it now opens tools and models so that more workloads run on its closed computing platform, and that the reported $12.9 billion Hugging Face deal would extend that strategy to distribution.

## Key points

- **NV1 and the first lesson.** NVIDIA's first chip used curves and quadrilaterals while the industry moved to triangles. A $5 million Sega investment bought six months to build Riva 128 around the developers' standard.
- **CUDA's lost years.** Ian Buck, who came from Stanford's BrookGPU project, built CUDA, and Jensen Huang put it into GeForce cards. Se says CUDA downloads fell for three years after 2009. Deep learning researchers, including the AlexNet team training on two GeForce cards, found the market. A Friday email after Bryan Catanzaro's 2013 briefing made deep learning a company priority.
- **What NVIDIA actually opens.** Se cites more than 1,400 OSI-licensed projects, over 650 open models and more than 250 datasets. NVIDIA opened its Linux kernel modules in 2022 and publishes NCCL. CUDA, cuDNN, firmware and user-space components stay closed.
- **The business logic.** Se quotes NVIDIA's annual report: open models help NVIDIA only if they run on its hardware. She calls this a commercial form of openness.
- **The Hugging Face price.** At about 86 times roughly $150 million in annualised revenue, Hugging Face would bring nearly three million models and the place where new architectures first appear. It reportedly turned down a $500 million NVIDIA investment at a $7 billion valuation to protect its independence.
- **The neutrality risk.** Se notes that Hugging Face grew by serving AMD, Intel, Google, Amazon and independent inference providers. Under NVIDIA, its rankings, defaults and support priorities would depend on NVIDIA's decisions.
