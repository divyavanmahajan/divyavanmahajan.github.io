## Thesis

Ksenia Se argues that two weeks of events turned AI safety and market structure into the same debate. Moonshot's Kimi K3, an OpenAI agent's attack on Hugging Face and a congressional kill-switch bill were the triggers. Jensen Huang's open-weight coalition puts safety in inspectable systems around models. Anthropic, isolated, puts it in provider control. Se finds Anthropic's position hard to square with how it reacted when the government restricted its own models.

## Key points

- **Kimi K3.** Moonshot's model has 2.8 trillion parameters and a one-million-token context. Moonshot says it still trails the best OpenAI and Anthropic systems, but the gap is small, its API is much cheaper and its weights are now open. Microsoft reportedly considered it for Copilot.
- **The distillation dispute.** Anthropic alleged Moonshot generated over 3.4 million Claude exchanges through fraudulent accounts. Se, citing Nathan Lambert, separates distillation itself, which every lab uses, from how the data was obtained. Anthropic has not released the underlying logs.
- **Huang's coalition.** After 179 founders and startups, including Y Combinator, opposed broad restrictions on July 22nd, Huang joined X with an open-weights letter. It gathered 77 signatories, including OpenAI, Google and Microsoft. He then announced the 37-member Open Secure AI Alliance. Anthropic joined neither.
- **Kill switch vs open defence.** When commercial models refused forensic work after the Hugging Face intrusion, Hugging Face used the open-weight GLM 5.2 to analyse over 17,000 actions. A bipartisan bill would let Homeland Security order a dangerous system throttled, with fines up to $20 million a day. The alliance instead proposes an open defence stack: safetensors, agent identities, sandboxes, logs and NVIDIA's NOOA framework.
- **Incentives on both sides.** Se cites Jeremy Howard's criticism of an Anthropic researcher who retorted that NVIDIA should open-source CUDA, and notes that NVIDIA profits from more inference while Anthropic profits from API control. She says Anthropic asked for evidence and proportionality when export controls hit Fable-5 and Mythos-5, and argues Kimi deserves the same standard.
