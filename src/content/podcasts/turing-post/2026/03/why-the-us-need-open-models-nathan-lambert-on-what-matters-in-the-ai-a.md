## Thesis

Nathan Lambert, a research scientist at the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) and author of the Interconnects newsletter, tells Ksenia Se that open models will not catch up with closed frontier models, because resources decide the outcome. He argues that is the wrong yardstick. Open models are the platform for academic research and long-term experimentation, and the US risks handing that influence to China.

## Key points

- **A steady gap.** Lambert expects the best open models to stay roughly six to nine months behind the best closed ones, possibly falling further behind. Compute, spent on training or on synthetic data, is the deciding resource. He says Ai2 spent millions of dollars on synthetic data for OLMo 3, while frontier labs spend at a vastly larger scale.
- **Why China ships open weights.** He calls DeepSeek fairly ideological and the spark for China's ecosystem. He says Chinese companies have told him open weights are their only option, since US customers will not send data to a Chinese API. He credits Chinese labs with permissive licences, unlike US releases that come with vague extra terms.
- **US players.** Lambert sees NVIDIA's Nemotron as the closest US effort, with the clearest business model: selling more GPUs. Fully open projects such as Ai2's remain a niche.
- **Where research is heading.** In Lambert's view, tool-use post-training is the most dynamic area, but open training environments (for example Prime Intellect's Verifiers) are at an early stage. Pre-training data is the hardest part to release for legal reasons. Hybrid linear-attention architectures are spreading after Mamba's early problems were fixed.
- **Practical reality.** Lambert mostly uses closed models himself and says Ai2 needs to use its own models in order to improve them. He expects enterprise adoption to be slow, because serving and fine-tuning an open model is costly.
- **The pitch.** For the US, Lambert frames open models as owning the engine of AI innovation for decades and removing the "is this a Chinese model?" question for startups.
