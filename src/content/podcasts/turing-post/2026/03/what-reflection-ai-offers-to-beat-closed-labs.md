## Thesis

In the second part of his conversation with Ksenia Se, Reflection AI co-founder and CTO Ioannis Antonoglou explains why the company moved from autonomous coding to building a general, open-weight agentic model from scratch, with both pre-training and reinforcement learning. His argument is that the West lacks a strong open base model to do frontier RL on, and that being open does not hold a lab back. In the show notes, Ksenia Se says she left the conversation more sceptical, given the $2 billion raised and few concrete results so far.

## Key points

- **AlphaGo as a preview.** Antonoglou says AlphaGo already looked like modern training: large-scale compute, a human-data phase similar to pre-training, an RL phase similar to post-training, and human testers flagging mistakes.
- **AGI without breakthroughs.** He defines AGI as an agent that can do on a computer what a human can do. By that definition, he argues, it needs better engineering and better combinations of existing methods, not major new breakthroughs.
- **The pivot.** Reflection started by applying RL to coding. Antonoglou says Llama 4's weakness showed that the Western ecosystem had no strong open base model to post-train, and he concluded Reflection was the only lab positioned to build one. He likens the goal to a "US DeepSeek". Applications will come later.
- **Bottlenecks.** He names three: hiring (he cites people from Gemini, OpenAI, Meta and Apple), compute (funded by a Series B of more than $2 billion in October) and engineering at scale. He compares building these models to building a rocket.
- **Measuring success.** Antonoglou says adoption is the only metric that really matters, alongside unseen internal evaluations. The aim is first to ship the best open models, then close the gap with closed labs. His main research interests are long-horizon credit assignment and RL-aware pre-training across the stack.
- **Advice and origins.** He tells young researchers to join startups and take risks. Shane Legg won him over at DeepMind with the goal of building computers that think like humans.
