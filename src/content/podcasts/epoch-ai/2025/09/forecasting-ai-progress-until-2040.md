## Thesis

Jaime Sevilla, director of Epoch AI, and Yafah Edelman, its head of data and analysis, extend Epoch's trend lines to 2040 and argue that even the default "trends continue" scenario is extreme. Their modal story has three eras: fast scaling to about 2030, AI that can do every cognitive task by roughly 2035 with around 10% annual growth, and a shift to hyperbolic growth around 2038-2040, where they say their models break down.

## Key points

- **Compute scaling slows.** Edelman expects training compute growth to drop from about 5x a year to 2.5-3x within two years, because training runs are no longer getting longer. Using xAI's Grok 3 and Grok 4 as examples, both still expect runs near 1e29 FLOP (about 1,000x today's) by 2030.
- **Coding and science by 2030.** Edelman would not be surprised if people stop writing code by the end of the decade. Sevilla expects engineers will still review AI code; Edelman argues that review becomes pointless when buyers cannot see the difference. Sevilla expects AI to solve at least one famous maths or physics problem.
- **Economics.** Sevilla sees AI revenues doubling yearly to hundreds of billions by 2030, lifting growth from about 2% to 4%. Edelman's rule of thumb ("Yafah Math") is that investment runs at about 10x a field's revenue, and each further 10x of infrastructure takes an extra year.
- **Cognitive automation around 2035.** Edelman gives 2035 (90% interval roughly 2027-2045/2050) and Sevilla 2034. Sevilla notes that only about 30% of US work time is remote-only, so going past 10% growth needs robots.
- **Labour disruption.** Edelman expects AI to de-skill many jobs before it fully automates them, followed by high unemployment and strong political reactions, pointing to the speed of the Covid stimulus.
- **Three cruxes after 2035.** Both name robot manufacturing speed, returns to more scientific labour and returns to intelligence. Edelman sketches a path from $100 billion to $10 trillion of robots (about 100 million units) in roughly three years. Sevilla says his median timeline is up to a decade later than this modal story.
