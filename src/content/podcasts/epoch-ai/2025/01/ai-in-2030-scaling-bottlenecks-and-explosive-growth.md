## Thesis

Jaime Sevilla, Tamay Besiroglu and Ege Erdil of Epoch AI argue that AI progress follows predictable "straight lines", mainly growth in training compute, and that none of power, chips, data, latency or hardware failures should stop a roughly 10,000x scale-up over GPT-4 by the end of the decade. The real limit, Erdil says, is whether people will pay for it. They argue the prize, automating human labor, justifies the spending and could speed up growth well beyond what economists expect, though Erdil expects that to take longer than this decade.

## Key points

- **What drives progress.** Besiroglu estimates pre-training compute explains close to 50% of recent gains, with algorithms and data quality making up the rest. Training compute has grown about 4-5x per year since deep learning took off. Erdil notes that algorithmic progress has also been smooth, more than halving the compute needed for a fixed level of performance each year.
- **Power and chips.** Sevilla estimates GPT-4-class training uses 15-30 MW and 2030-scale runs 5-15 GW; training spread across several data centers would ease the power problem. Besiroglu says TSMC needs about a 10x increase in output from roughly 4 million H100 equivalents, in line with its stated 30-60% annual expansion.
- **Data.** Sevilla says wider crawling (Common Crawl covers about a fifth of the web) and multi-epoch training give about 25x more data against a needed 100x. He expects multimodal and synthetic data to close the gap. Erdil warns that generating synthetic data costs a lot of compute.
- **Latency and failures.** Erdil finds latency becomes binding only beyond about a 10,000x scale-up. Using Llama 3.1 data (one failure per 50,000 GPU hours), he says GPU failures are not a real constraint.
- **Economics.** Besiroglu points to roughly $70 trillion a year in global wages as the prize. Erdil and Besiroglu put the chance of 30%-per-year growth at about 50% if AI can do all remote and physical work. Erdil argues human wages could then fall sharply while returns on capital and land rise.
