## Thesis

Sam Schillace, Microsoft's Deputy CTO and the creator of Writely (which became Google Docs), sees GPT-4 as the start of a second version of the computer industry. Until now computing has been about syntax, and models open the door to semantics. His practical lesson for AI engineers is to let the model handle the fuzzy thinking and to keep the overall plan in code. The episode opens with swyx and co-organiser Ben Dunphy announcing the AI Engineer World's Fair, with Microsoft as presenting sponsor.

## Key points

- **The event.** The first AI Engineer Summit had more than 5,000 in-person applications, about 400 attendees and over 20,000 viewers on the live stream. The June World's Fair in San Francisco plans more than 100 talks across nine tracks. swyx says he wants it to become a vendor-neutral KubeCon for AI.
- **First contact with GPT-4.** Schillace got access in September 2022, and the model composed and transposed fiddle tunes written in ABC notation. He describes Bill Gates being convinced once GPT-4 beat a champion student on the AP Biology exam.
- **Schillace's laws.** These came out of building Semantic Kernel. Examples are "break it into pieces" (pipe a reasoning paragraph into a second prompt that only extracts the answer) and "ask smart to get smart". He now admits that "text is the universal wire protocol" may not survive multimodality.
- **Code exoskeleton.** Prompts are disconnected, like frames of claymation, so memory matters and metacognition is vulnerable to stochastic noise. His Textbook Factory, about 200 lines of Python plus six prompts, reliably builds a full-year textbook. He wants models to emit short plans that call trusted building blocks such as Power Automate.
- **Engage early.** Schillace says the right time to build is while a technology is still controversial and painful to work with. He criticises executives who hold opinions on AI but have not used it for more than 10 minutes.
- **Microsoft's portfolio.** He covers MSR's Phi small models, AutoGen, Azure OpenAI and AI Studio, and Founders Hub credits of up to $150,000.
