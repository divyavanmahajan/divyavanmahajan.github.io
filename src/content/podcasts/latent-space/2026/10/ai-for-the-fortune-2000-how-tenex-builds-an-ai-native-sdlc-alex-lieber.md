## Thesis

Alex Lieberman, co-founder of applied AI partner Tenex, and CJ Hess, its first hire and special projects lead, tell swyx at AI Engineer New York that AI is raw intelligence and the hard part is the last mile of diffusing it through enterprises. Their model pays engineers by output, standardises engineering work in a repo-based "meta harness", and starts client agent work by standardising the process before automating it.

## Key points

- **Paying for output.** Tenex pays engineers by story points. Lieberman says the company is 18 months old, has 75 people (90% engineers or AI strategists), and the spread between highest- and lowest-paid engineer is about 4x. Sandbagging has not been a problem because AI strategists are incentivised on retention; the hard part is paying for non-coding time.
- **The meta harness.** Hess describes a structured set of docs, plans and decision logs checked into Git, with a CLI that agents use to drive work. He estimates engineer output roughly doubled this year, and the team recalibrates story points at least quarterly. Opus 5.5 is the default model, with Codex used for review; Hess works through Amp.
- **Token spend.** Hess puts raw token spend at roughly one to two times employee spend, but says costs fell sharply recently. Lieberman argues per-task costs have dropped enough that runaway token use is less of a risk than six to eight months ago.
- **The enterprise adoption ladder.** Of roughly 100 enterprises Tenex talks to, Lieberman says 90% have rolled out Claude or Codex, about 60% have done training, closer to 50% have coding agents, and about 5% have agents in production for non-engineering work. Common asks are back-office agents and data permissioning, for which Tenex now sells its own gateway.
- **Vertical AI and SaaS.** Lieberman is bearish on vertical AI agents such as Harvey; swyx pushes back, citing proprietary data and tailored interfaces. swyx says his conference was quoted $40,000 a year for a SaaS tool and replaced it for $1,000. Lieberman argues large systems such as Salesforce and SAP gain value because ripping them out is too risky.
- **Jobs.** Hess frames automation as reallocating people to the next problem; Lieberman calls "AI will only take the work you dislike" a cope and urges workers to take agency in reskilling.
