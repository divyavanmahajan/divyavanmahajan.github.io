## Thesis

Joseph Krause, CEO of Radical AI, argues that what makes a new material real lies after discovery: synthesis, microstructure, processing, supply chain and qualification, none of which fit in a text string the way small molecules do. His company therefore builds self-driving labs that run whole research campaigns and feed experimental data, including failures, back to a multi-agent AI scientist. He says the field is constrained by experiments, not compute.

## Key points

- **Automated lab versus self-driving lab.** Krause compares an automated lab to lane-keeping and a self-driving lab to a Waymo: it designs and runs campaigns itself. The hard parts include custom grippers for alloy "buttons" stuck to trays, an operating system that tracks samples and kills bad experiments early, and tool vendors that long refused software access.
- **Throughput.** Radical has made about 1,200 high-entropy alloys in recent months, around 300 never reported before and roughly 10 with exciting performance. Krause cites DARPA and GE Aerospace's MACH programme, about 500 alloys in a year, as the previous benchmark; Radical aims for 100 alloys a day. Each experiment costs about $60-300 depending on elements.
- **Humans still in the loop.** PhD metallurgists still run plasma-torch casting while models learn their intuition, and annotate microscope images to teach the AI scientist. Characterisation is fully automated. Krause says the AI scientist explores element families human researchers avoided, and one PhD can now run 10 campaigns at once.
- **Long timelines remain.** FAA and military qualification takes about 10 years, so Krause expects first uses in defence and space within three to five years. He describes removing hafnium, whose price has risen 10-15x because China dominates supply, from the aerospace alloy C-103.
- **Competing with China.** Krause wants the US to answer China's state-backed scale-up hubs with national-lab self-driving labs, the Genesis Mission and public-private partnerships rather than copying its model.
- **Open models, proprietary data.** Radical open-sourced TorchSim and Matrix, a vision-language model fine-tuned on Qwen to read lab data, which Krause says improved general scientific reasoning by about 5-16%.
