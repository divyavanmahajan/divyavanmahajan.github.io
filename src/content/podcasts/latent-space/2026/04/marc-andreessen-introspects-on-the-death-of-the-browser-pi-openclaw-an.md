## Thesis

Marc Andreessen tells swyx and Alessio Fanelli that this AI wave differs from earlier booms because the technology now works. LLMs, reasoning, agents and recursive self-improvement have each arrived, drawing on 80 years of research. He sees Pi and OpenClaw as a major architectural breakthrough, but argues that both utopians and doomers underestimate how slowly a heavily regulated economy will absorb AI.

## Key points

- **Not a dot-com rerun.** Andreessen says the 2000 crash was really a debt-fuelled telecom overbuild that took 15 years to fill. Today's buyers are cash-rich firms such as Microsoft, Google and Meta, every GPU dollar earns revenue at once, and the supply chain is sold out for three to four years. He says Michael Burry's Nvidia short was "180 degrees wrong": older chips are earning more as software improves.
- **Demand outruns supply.** He has friends spending $1,000 a day on tokens to run OpenClaw. Shortages of CPU and memory may slow the fall in inference prices, which he thinks will push work to local and open-source models. He calls DeepSeek's R1 paper a gift to the world and expects today's dozen leading model labs to shrink to a handful.
- **Agents are Unix.** He describes an agent as a model plus a shell, file system, markdown files and a cron loop. Because its state lives in files, the agent can switch models, move itself and add features to itself. He links this to the early web's human-readable protocols and "view source", and expects programming languages and user interfaces to matter less once bots are the main users of software.
- **Missing protocols.** He expects stablecoins to fill the web's unused 402 "payment required" status code, making AI crypto's killer app. He backs World's biometric proof of human because bots now pass the Turing test; cheap drones are the physical version of the same asymmetry.
- **Founder plus AI.** Andreessen suggests AI could take over managerial work and let founder-led firms scale. He then lists barriers: 900 hours of training to become a California hairdresser, a dockworkers' union that blocked automation, and K-12 schooling he calls a government monopoly.
