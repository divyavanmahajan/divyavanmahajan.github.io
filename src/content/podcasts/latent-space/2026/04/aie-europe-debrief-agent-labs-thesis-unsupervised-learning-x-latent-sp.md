## Thesis

In this annual crossover with Unsupervised Learning, Redpoint investor Jacob Efron interviews swyx just after AI Engineer Europe in London. swyx's central claim is that 2025 was the year of coding agents and 2026 is the year they "break containment" into everything else, while the coding market itself stays a stable two-player contest with room for agent labs that do the last mile.

## Key points

- **Harnesses are settling.** swyx says OpenClaw, harness engineering and context engineering dominated the conference, and that Skills (a markdown file plus scripts) look like a minimal, stable format for packaging agent integrations.
- **The agent lab playbook.** Start on frontier models, specialise for a domain, then train your own once you have enough workload and data. He cites Cursor's Composer 2 and Cognition's SWE-1.6 being chosen unsubsidised, plus fast inference on Cerebras.
- **Agents as customers.** He relays Vercel CTO Malte Ubl's figure that 60% of traffic to Vercel's admin architecture is bots, and a survey in which Claude recommended Resend for email in about 70% of cases.
- **Coding market size.** swyx puts Claude Code at about $2.5 billion ARR, with OpenAI and Cursor around $2 billion each, and expects the status quo of two big players to hold unless Microsoft or others change the economics.
- **SaaS squeeze.** He describes weighing replacing a $200K-a-year event-management SaaS with a custom build costing about $2,000, but says the hard part is bringing the team along.
- **Changed minds.** swyx now thinks open-model share is rising among top companies, highlights "dark factories" (zero human review), and points to world models and memory as the next frontiers. Efron says post-training can be worth it even if thrown away in three months.
