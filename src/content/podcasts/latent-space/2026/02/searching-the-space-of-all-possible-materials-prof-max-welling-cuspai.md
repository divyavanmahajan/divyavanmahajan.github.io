## Thesis

Max Welling, known for variational autoencoders and equivariant neural networks, says physics links all his work. He argues that materials are the foundation of nearly everything, from GPUs to batteries to carbon capture. AI now lets scientists search the space of all possible materials instead of relying on slow cycles of hypothesis and experiment. His startup CuspAI aims to give chemists that search engine, while keeping chemists in charge of choosing materials.

## Key points

- **Why CuspAI exists.** Welling started the company about twenty months earlier, with co-founder Chad Edwards, out of concern about climate change. He argues that staying within two degrees would need decades of removing CO2 at about half today's emission rate, which is an unsolved materials problem. CuspAI has about forty people and has raised $130 million.
- **Platform design.** A generative model proposes candidates. A multi-scale, multi-fidelity digital twin filters them from cheap checks to expensive ones, and the survivors go to experiments. Agents now search the chemical literature and orchestrate computations. Welling says the design is "not rocket science"; the moat is data and the hard work of building it.
- **Experiments as a "physics processing unit."** He wants high-throughput experiments and self-driving labs treated as nature computing for you, working alongside data centres.
- **Humans in the loop, partner-led.** Automation comes step by step: first tools such as easier DFT calculations, then agents. CuspAI only invests in a direction once it has an industrial partner, for example a long-term partnership on removing PFAS from water. Welling rejects the idea of a fully "dark lab" in the near term and wants a breakthrough material before full automation.
- **Equivariance and the bitter lesson.** Building symmetry into networks cuts the data they need. He admits that data augmentation sometimes works better because constraints make optimisation harder: it is a trade-off between data and inductive bias.
- **New book.** His book "Generative AI and Stochastic Thermodynamics" argues that diffusion models and non-equilibrium physics share identical mathematics. He hopes it will be out around his ICLR keynote in April.
