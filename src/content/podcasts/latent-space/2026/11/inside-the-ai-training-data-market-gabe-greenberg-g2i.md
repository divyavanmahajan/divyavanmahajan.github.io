## Thesis

Recorded at AI Engineer CODE, G2i CEO Gabe Greenberg tells swyx that the human-data market has moved from large gig-work labelling projects to staff-level engineers building RL environments and hard, targeted tasks. Frontier labs buy fast and without RFPs. They now want partners who can find gaps in their models, and Greenberg says they choose vendors on quality, not price.

## Key points

- **Scale and customers.** Greenberg says G2i did 15 million last year and will do about 100 million this year, with no VC backing, selling mainly to five or six frontier labs. Only one lab has a mature in-house human-data operation.
- **From volume to taste.** In 2023-24 projects used up to 6,000 mid-level engineers. Now the work is complex dataset building by staff-level engineers. Greenberg says tasks must sit just beyond what the model can do today: too hard and they become evals, too easy and they are worthless.
- **How labs buy.** Fortune 100 procurement takes 6 to 12 months or more. Labs, Greenberg says, start with pilots of a few million dollars, and a researcher can spend $10 million in 60 seconds. Work is priced by average handling time (AHT), for example staff engineers at $250 an hour.
- **Exclusive versus off-the-shelf.** Contrary to swyx's assumption, Greenberg says many labs accept data that is also sold to other labs because it costs less. Off-the-shelf data is the main mode. Labs avoid running data operations in-house because of the overhead, legal exposure and complexity of managing thousands of contractors.
- **Raw company data.** Greenberg says a dying startup can sell its Slack, email and other data. Buyers now want at least a few terabytes and more than 10 headcount, with the same projects showing up consistently across tools such as GitHub and Linear.
- **Next areas.** He predicts growth in robotics, 3D world models, CAD, mechanical engineering and chips, plus legal, finance and computer use. He also pitches Orch (orc.ai), G2i's Rust-based multi-agent orchestration platform.

## Quotes
