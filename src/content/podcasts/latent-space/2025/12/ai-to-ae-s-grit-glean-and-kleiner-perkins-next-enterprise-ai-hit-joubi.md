## Thesis

Joubin Mirzadegan, Kleiner Perkins partner and host of the Grit podcast, argues that selling AI into the enterprise takes design partners, technical salespeople and a real commitment to distribution, not just product. He is applying those lessons to Roadrunner, a KP incubation that replaces legacy configure-price-quote (CPQ) software, which he says breaks under the consumption-based pricing AI is bringing.

## Key points

- **Glean's playbook.** Mirzadegan says enterprise search was an "eye roll" category when Arvind came to Mamoon. Arvind built Glean for his previous company, Rubrik, betting that what Rubrik needed was what everyone would need.
- **Windsurf's sales machine.** Swyx cites growth from zero to about $100M ARR in seven to eight months. Mirzadegan credits a founder commitment to "Google-class product and Salesforce-class distribution", a market pulling for coding tools, and fast hiring, and gives credit to Liam on his team.
- **Hiring sales leaders.** He warns against hiring from brand-name logos. Across KP's top eight companies, 38 of 40 executives in five roles report to a CEO for the first time. Salespeople now need to be more technical and to have worked at companies of a similar stage.
- **Why CPQ is broken.** Two dinners with KP's network of 35 tech CIOs named CPQ as their top problem. Mirzadegan says Salesforce CPQ's data model predates many SKUs and consumption pricing; Salesforce, with about 95% share, has end-of-lifed it, which he sees as a two-year window.
- **Roadrunner today.** The team is nine people, led by co-founders AJ and Eugene, building with four "hairy" design partners through shared Slack channels and weekly stand-ups. The long-term idea is LLM recommendations on how to structure deals from historical quotes, cutting administrative work for AEs and deal desks.
- **Habits and grit.** Mirzadegan works out daily and eats the same lunch to avoid decisions, and defines grit around passion, after Angela Duckworth.
