## Thesis

Deedy Das, now a partner at Menlo Ventures after years at Glean, argues that durable advantage in AI comes from doing the hardest work in the stack: deep enterprise search, frontier models, or tedious infrastructure that engineers underestimate. He thinks the model layer is currently more defensible than the app layer, and worries that vibe coding is weakening the skills of trained engineers.

## Key points

- **Glean.** Das says Glean (valued at about $7 billion, with several hundred million in revenue) did years of unglamorous work before LLMs. Enterprise ranking is hard because query volume is too low for click feedback, freshness matters more, and queries are not head-heavy. Adoption came from taking over new tab pages and a Chrome extension that replaced native search bars.
- **Labs versus Glean.** He argues labs earning billions have little reason to staff the sales, FDE and customization work deep enterprise search needs, and that rate-limiting APIs (as Salesforce did with Slack) makes little business sense.
- **Anthropic.** Menlo first invested at a roughly $4 billion valuation, when the company had no revenue. Das calls it the fastest-growing software company ever (zero to $100 million, then to $1 billion, with a public projection of $9 billion) and singles out Claude Code as a key product innovation. The hosts cite Menlo survey data showing Anthropic's enterprise LLM API spend share rising from 12% to 32% while OpenAI fell from 50% to 25%.
- **Anthology Fund.** The $100 million fund run with Anthropic has backed about 40 companies, including OpenRouter, Goodfire, Prime Intellect and the voice dictation company Wispr, with checks from $100K to $20 million.
- **OpenRouter and research bets.** Das liked OpenRouter as a thorny problem with a product-led motion and details such as provider-level stats. He describes Goodfire's interpretability work as "brain surgery for LLMs" and says text diffusion models offer 80-90% of the quality at one-tenth the cost and latency.
- **Vibe coding.** Das compares coding agents to a slot machine and "a cigarette for your brain"; swyx counters that fast agents can help engineers read code while they still do the writing.
