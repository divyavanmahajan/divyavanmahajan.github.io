## Thesis

Alex Lieberman and Arman Hezarkhani, co-founders of Tenex, argue that AI has made the best engineers much more productive, but hourly billing gives them a reason to work slowly. Tenex pays engineers by output, measured in story points. The founders say gaming is controlled through hiring and opposing incentives, and that the business is limited by how many good engineers it can hire, not by AI tools.

## Key points

- **Origin story.** Lieberman says Hezarkhani had to cut his previous startup Parthian's engineering team by 90% and rebuilt the process to be AI-first. Output of production-ready software then rose about tenfold, which prompted the question of how to pay such engineers.
- **Guarding against gaming.** Hezarkhani admits story points are easy to inflate. He relies on hiring people who are "long-term selfish" or who simply enjoy good work. Lieberman adds that technical strategists are paid on net revenue retention and sign off every engineering plan before a sprint.
- **Results.** Hezarkhani expects more than a handful of engineers to earn over $1 million next year. The team cites a retail-camera project with quantised models on Raspberry Pi and Jetson Nano devices, prototyped in two weeks. Other examples are a Snapback Sports app built in a month that reached 20th on the App Store, and a fitness-coach app prototyped in about four hours to win a deal.
- **Stack.** Hezarkhani prefers TypeScript front and back end with shared types, because compiler errors let agents such as Claude Code or Codex correct themselves. The team switches agents based on feel rather than formal evals.
- **Hiring.** Take-home tests are deliberately "unreasonably difficult"; about 50% of candidates never reply, but the process can finish in a week. Engineer Dan's answer to the interview question was that controlling entropy, meaning compounding error, is the main barrier to autonomous coding.
- **MCP.** Hezarkhani sees MCP as rebranded APIs used to raise money. swyx defends the parts of the spec that go beyond API wrappers.
