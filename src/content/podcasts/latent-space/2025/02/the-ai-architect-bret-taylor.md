## Thesis

Bret Taylor, CEO of Sierra and chairman of the OpenAI board, argues that startups should move up the stack and build agents that solve a specific business problem, priced by outcomes rather than seats or tokens. He also argues that coding tools are stuck at a local maximum: once machines write most code, the hard problem becomes specifying and verifying what they produce.

## Key points

- **Product and engineering together.** Taylor recalls rewriting the Google Maps front end over a weekend, cutting the bundle to 20K. He argues that new technology waves reward small teams that combine product and engineering, because requirements depend on what the technology can actually do.
- **Where the value goes.** He expects frontier models to stay with a few heavily capitalised companies and tools to drift towards open source. Domain agents such as Harvey, Cursor and Sierra are the new SaaS. His analogy: sell the latte, not the roasted beans.
- **Outcome-based pricing.** Sierra, which builds customer-facing agents for brands such as Sonos, ADT and Sirius XM, charges a pre-negotiated rate each time the agent resolves an issue. He says customer experience teams at some customers now call themselves "AI architects".
- **AI-native software development.** Taylor wants new programming systems built around formal verification and automated review, so that people do not have to read every generated line. He cites Rust's compile-time memory safety as a modest example of this.
- **Incomplete specifications.** Prompts and PRDs never cover every case, so agents will fill the gaps. He sees the product insight in each domain as the loop through which people review and correct agent behaviour.
- **OpenAI.** Taylor describes mediating the November 2023 board crisis and says he holds no OpenAI equity. He calls Microsoft OpenAI's most important partner and says the mission of building AGI that benefits humanity drives priorities such as deep research and coding.
