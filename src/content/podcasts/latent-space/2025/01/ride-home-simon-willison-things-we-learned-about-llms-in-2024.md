## Thesis

Recorded with Brian McCullough of the Techmeme Ride Home, this crossover has Simon Willison, with swyx, walk through his year-end review. Willison argues that 2024's progress came from models getting cheaper, faster, multimodal and runnable on laptops rather than from a step change beyond GPT-4. He remains sceptical of autonomous agents because LLMs believe whatever they read, and he wants better interfaces and sharper criticism.

## Key points

- **The GPT-4 barrier fell.** Willison counts 18 organisations with models that beat the original GPT-4, some running on his laptop, such as Microsoft's Phi-4 as a 14 GB download. DeepSeek V3 was reportedly trained for $5.5 million, which he calls a bombshell.
- **Prices crashed.** He cites a 100x drop in OpenAI prices since GPT-3. Captioning his 68,000 photos with Gemini 1.5 Flash 8B would cost $1.68, and he hears that Google Gemini and Amazon Nova are not run at a loss. swyx estimates a 1000x cost drop for GPT-4-level intelligence over the year.
- **Agents and gullibility.** Willison says the word "agent" has no shared definition. Prompt injection remains unsolved, illustrated by Claude Computer Use downloading malware. He trusts coding agents and research assistants such as Gemini Deep Research, not agents that spend money for you.
- **Interfaces got harder.** He compares default chat UIs to a Linux terminal, praises ChatGPT Canvas, tldraw's Make It Real and Claude Artifacts, and wants LLMs to build ad-hoc UIs whose input they can then see. swyx counters that features like Gemini in Google Sheets make things easier.
- **Slop and credibility.** Willison defines slop as AI content that is unrequested and unreviewed, and argues credibility is the one thing an LLM cannot have.
- **Outlook.** Willison thinks OpenAI has lost its clear lead (o3 aside), recommends Ollama, LM Studio and MacWhisper, and wants regulation focused on uses and privacy. swyx predicts 2025 is the year of AI wearables.
