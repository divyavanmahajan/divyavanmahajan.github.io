## Thesis

In this crosspost from Cognitive Revolution, Nathan Labenz interviews swyx ahead of the AI Engineer Summit. Swyx argues that the AI engineer is a software engineer specializing in the post-model stack, a role created by scarcity of LLM researchers rather than by technology. He is cautious on agents and issue-to-PR coding, while Labenz takes the bullish side on timelines.

## Key points

- **Supply and demand.** Swyx estimates there are perhaps 5,000 good LLM researchers in the world, so most companies must instead hire software engineers who move "left" into tokenizers, embeddings and vector databases. His free seven-day Latent Space University course runs from the GPT API to building an agent.
- **Buy first, then build.** Swyx's rule is to adopt tools such as Humanloop, Guardrails and LangChain first, then rip out what you outgrow; he calls criticism of LangChain unfair given the company is under a year old.
- **Hiring.** Beyond core skills, swyx looks for entrepreneurial engineers who ship their own projects, citing the Vercel team as a model.
- **Agents in gradations.** Swyx says his most useful agent is SavvyCal/Calendly, which uses no LLM; Code Interpreter and Codium AI's test generation (an $11 million seed) are early working loops. He puts issue-to-PR agents like Sweep about five years out; Labenz bets on two to three years, citing Med-PaLM M reports preferred to radiologists 40% of the time.
- **OpenAI and its ecosystem.** Labenz worries OpenAI will absorb adjacent products such as fine-tuning tools and voice; swyx compares this to Apple "Sherlocking" and advises startups to prove they are strong engineering teams.
- **AI bundle and horcruxes.** Labenz pitches a $100-a-month bundle of 1,000 AI apps, noting Waymark spends 15 cents per free user; swyx points to Setapp and warns SaaS firms want to own customers. Swyx also expects always-on AI pendants acting as digital twins.
