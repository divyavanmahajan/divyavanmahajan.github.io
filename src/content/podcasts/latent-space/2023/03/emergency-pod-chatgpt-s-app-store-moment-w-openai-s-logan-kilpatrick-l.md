## Thesis

Recorded as an unplanned Twitter Space hours after OpenAI announced ChatGPT plugins, swyx argues this is the "App Store moment" for AI, and that ChatGPT running generated code makes it a general compute platform rather than a chat app. Guests from OpenAI, Lindy and the developer community debate who wins on such a platform, how plugins get paid for, and what remains for standalone products.

## Key points

- **Plugins are easy to write.** swyx notes a plugin is roughly 10 lines of JSON: an English description plus an OpenAPI spec, which he calls far simpler than Chrome's Manifest V3. He highlights Greg Brockman's demo of ChatGPT editing a video with FFmpeg.
- **Partners as presumptive winners.** swyx says OpenAI picked favorites (Expedia, Kayak, OpenTable, Wolfram, Zapier) and offers instant access to 100 million users. He also observes that no Google products are among the launch plugins.
- **Thin moats.** Nader Dabit describes building a GPT-3 travel itinerary app that drew about 100,000 visitors a day and selling it within about 24 hours; he expects OpenAI to build better versions of such apps into the platform.
- **OpenAI's view.** Logan Kilpatrick says Zapier already connects to about 5,000 services, rollout is deliberately slow to study new harms, and plugin descriptions and samples sit in the context window. No API with plugins enabled exists yet.
- **Lindy's position.** Florent Crivello says plugins lower the barrier to entry but look like a developer product; Lindy builds an integration in about 15 minutes from API docs and requires a user-issued token for any write action. He wants Lindy to be a neutral "Switzerland".
- **Monetization.** Participants float Spotify-style royalties, usage-based pricing and paid validation tiers, but reach no answer.
