## Thesis

Logan Kilpatrick, OpenAI's first developer advocate, says his job is less about publicity, since OpenAI has plenty of inbound interest, and more about documentation that helps developers build on the API. He argues that products built LLM-first will have the short-term edge, but that founders who are only reselling the OpenAI API will not last; differentiation, often through unique data, is the real question.

## Key points

- **DevRel as developer experience.** Kilpatrick says his focus is docs and a documentation-first culture, credits Ted and Boris for the OpenAI Cookbook, which became the top trending repo on GitHub, and says much of what ChatGPT does could already be built with prompt engineering on text-davinci-003.
- **Hallucinations in context.** He calls ChatGPT a research preview while stressing the underlying GPT-3.5 model is not, and argues that generative output can be wrong much of the time because regenerating costs almost nothing, unlike safety-critical vision for self-driving cars.
- **Prompt engineering is an art, not a job.** Kilpatrick thinks the "prompt engineer as a career" meme, including a $350,000 Anthropic listing the hosts mention, is overblown as tools like LangChain and prompt collections catalogue good prompts.
- **Moats and data.** He says LLM-native products beat bolted-on features for now (Google cannot easily put a prompt under its search bar), cites Jasper's interface as its differentiator, and is "super bullish" on embeddings, repeating Boris's estimate that $50 million could embed the whole internet.
- **Model upgrades and nondeterminism.** Kilpatrick warns that new models bring breaking changes, that comparison tooling does not yet exist, and that outputs vary even at temperature zero, which he attributes to GPU floating-point behaviour.
- **Personalisation next.** He expects personalised tutors, cheaper mental health and legal help, and models that ask clarifying questions; one host notes the sycophancy of models that accept a question's premise.
