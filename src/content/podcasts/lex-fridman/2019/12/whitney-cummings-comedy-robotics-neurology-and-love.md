## Thesis

Whitney Cummings, who built a robot replica of herself called Bearclaw for her Netflix special "Can I Touch It?", tells Lex Fridman that the fear of AI is mostly held by wealthy men, while vulnerable groups could see robots as help, protection and emotional support. She ties this to her interest in neurology: understanding brain chemistry, codependency and addiction, she says, makes people easier to forgive and explains why relationships with robots and animals can feel safer than relationships with humans.

## Key points

- **Building Bearclaw.** Cummings spent six months having her face moulded for the robot. She says it looks more realistic than generic robots because human faces are asymmetrical, and that audience screams led her to read about pathogen avoidance and the uncanny reaction.
- **Who robots help.** She argues robots could tutor children of poorer families, perform surgery in underserved places (citing her work with Operation Smile) and support shy or grieving people. Her research found sex-robot buyers were often disabled people or people exploring their sexuality.
- **Surveillance.** Cummings calls herself pro-surveillance because people behave better when watched, comparing it to Santa Claus, but says using data such as mouse movements to predict Parkinson's and raise insurance rates is unethical. Fridman notes many Chinese citizens support surveillance but not its abuse.
- **Animals and robot abuse.** She expects factory farming and captive whales and dolphins to be viewed with embarrassment, and suggests that people who abuse robots, like those who abuse animals, may show antisocial tendencies.
- **Neurology and codependency.** After her parents' strokes and her brother's viral encephalitis, she learned which brain regions affect behaviour. She says codependents walk on eggshells, which is why judgment-free robots appeal to her.
- **Social media and love.** She treats social media as an addiction, mutes much more successful people, and lets Bearclaw produce half her content. She defines love as consistent dopamine and oxytocin with the same person and says it should be conditional, a daily action.
