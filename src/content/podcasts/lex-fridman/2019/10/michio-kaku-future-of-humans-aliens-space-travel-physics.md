## Thesis

Theoretical physicist Michio Kaku tells Lex Fridman that the numbers make alien civilizations near-certain, and that humanity's own future lies in merging with technology: brain-to-brain networks, digitized minds sent across space on laser beams, and genetic fixes for ageing. He presents string theory as the only surviving candidate for unifying quantum mechanics and relativity, while being explicit that several of his ideas are personal speculation he cannot prove.

## Key points

- **Aliens and the Kardashev scale.** Kaku cites about 4,000 known exoplanets and roughly one in five stars hosting Earth-sized planets, and expects humans to pick up alien signals this century. He extends the scale beyond types 1-3 to type 4 (dark energy) and type 5 (the multiverse), and thinks aliens would mostly ignore us because we have nothing they want.
- **Multiverse and string theory.** He describes a "bubble bath" of universes in 11-dimensional hyperspace, which he says reconciles the Buddhist and Christian views of creation he grew up with. String theory, he argues, is the only remaining candidate for unifying the theory of the small and the theory of the big, though it could still be wrong.
- **Brain-net and robots.** Kaku points to recorded mouse memories and planned memory chips for Alzheimer's patients, and predicts a "brain net" carrying emotions and sensations that would upend entertainment. He expects robots to reach monkey-level intelligence by the century's end and argues we should merge with them.
- **Technology has a moral direction.** Unlike most scientists, he says, he believes the internet spreads knowledge and democracy, and that democracies do not go to war with each other.
- **Immortality.** He describes digitizing personalities (the connectome project) and laser-beaming minds to avatars on the Moon or Mars, and expects AI pattern recognition over billions of genomes, plus CRISPR-type tools, to locate and repair the genetic errors behind ageing.
- **Mars and fusion.** Kaku expects humans on Mars in the 2030s, laser-driven chip starships to Alpha Centauri at 20% of light speed, and fusion from seawater as the backbone of a type 1 civilization. He cites Carl Sagan's estimate that humanity rates about 0.7.
