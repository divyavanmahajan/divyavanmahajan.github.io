## Thesis

Greg Brockman, co-founder and CTO of OpenAI, tells Lex Fridman that no one can stop a technology like AGI from being invented; the only real freedom is to set the initial conditions under which it is born. He argues that the generality, competence and scalability of deep learning make AGI possible within our lifetimes, and that OpenAI's charter, capped-profit structure and caution over GPT-2 are attempts to make sure its benefits reach everyone.

## Key points

- **Three arms.** Brockman describes OpenAI as capabilities, technical safety (learning human preferences from data, as with recognising cats and dogs) and policy, which asks whose values a powerful system should serve.
- **Why deep learning gives hope.** He argues past AI winters taught the wrong lesson: the 1959 perceptron was dismissed and the 1980s revival was driven by more compute. He still sees missing pieces, especially reasoning.
- **OpenAI LP.** Brockman says building AGI will take many billions of dollars, so OpenAI created a capped-return company governed by the nonprofit board, with a fiduciary duty to the charter. The charter commits OpenAI to help, not race, a value-aligned project that is ahead.
- **Measurement before regulation.** He tells lawmakers it is the time for measurement, through bodies like NIST, and that existing regulators such as the highway safety agency should handle narrow AI like self-driving cars.
- **GPT-2 and responsible disclosure.** Brockman calls withholding the full model a test case, comparing it to how the security community built disclosure norms, and expects telling bots from humans to become a losing battle; he would draw the hard line at deception.
- **Scale plus ideas.** Citing OpenAI Five, where PPO run at massive scale (around 100,000 CPU cores) produced long-term planning its creator John Schulman did not expect, he says progress needs both compute and ideas. He and Ilya Sutskever were starting a reasoning team.
