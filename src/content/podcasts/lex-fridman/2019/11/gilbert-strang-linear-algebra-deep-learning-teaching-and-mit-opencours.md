## Thesis

Gilbert Strang, the MIT professor whose OpenCourseWare linear algebra lectures have been watched by millions (Lex Fridman among them), argues that linear algebra has surged in importance in his lifetime because data arrives as matrices. He says undergraduate maths overdoes calculus at the expense of linear algebra and, increasingly, probability and statistics. He frames deep learning as an automated search for rules in data, built largely on matrices plus a simple non-linearity.

## Key points

- **OpenCourseWare.** Strang recounts that an MIT committee asked to find a way to market MIT's teaching came back to President Vest with the idea of giving it away instead. His 18.06 videos from around 2000 are simply recordings of the class.
- **Four fundamental subspaces.** His favourite teaching idea is the column space, row space and the two perpendicular spaces (including the null space), which together form the fundamental theorem of linear algebra. He admits he cannot picture ten dimensions but says adding and scaling vectors works the same there.
- **Singular values.** Strang argues the singular value decomposition, every matrix as rotation, stretch, rotation, has become fundamental because data matrices are rectangular, so eigenvalues on square matrices are not enough. The first pieces carry the important part of the data.
- **Deep learning.** He describes neural networks as learning a rule from known inputs and outputs, using linear algebra plus a piecewise linear function with a fold. Folded a million times, this yields complex functions, an idea engineers already use in the finite element method. Without signal in the data, he says, there is no rule to find.
- **Maths and public life.** Fridman notes Andrew Yang's "MATH" slogan and the lack of engineers in Congress. Strang recalls testifying to a House committee around 2000 as president of SIAM.
- **Teaching.** Strang's favourite matrix is the tridiagonal 2, -1 matrix, the linear algebra counterpart of the second derivative. He dislikes grading and hopes students find teachers who still enjoy the subject.
