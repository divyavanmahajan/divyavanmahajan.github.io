## Thesis

Colin Angle, CEO and co-founder of iRobot, tells Lex Fridman that technology alone does not make a robotics business: a robot has to solve a frequent, disliked chore and deliver clearly more value than it costs. He sees the Roomba as only a first step toward a home that maintains itself, built on low-cost manufacturing, vision-based sensing, human partnership and a privacy stance customers can trust.

## Key points

- **Why robotics companies fail.** Fridman lists Anki, Jibo, Mayfield Robotics and Rethink Robotics as recent failures. Angle jokes that he had no success until he started selling vacuum cleaners, and notes that 85% of toys fail to reach a second season.
- **Scale and the home.** Angle says iRobot has sold 25 million robots, reaching about 10 percent of US homes, and that Roombas are the top-selling vacuums in the US, not just among robot vacuums.
- **Less autonomy, more partnership.** After years of making robots autonomous, Angle now wants robots that act on context, such as "clean the kitchen", using a semantic map labelled by the user. Knowing where things are, he argues, is what would justify giving a home robot an arm.
- **Cost and sensing.** Angle describes the shift from machining aluminium parts to injection-moulded plastic, where cost tracks weight. iRobot avoided lasers and spent years on vision-based navigation, betting on cheap cameras and phone-grade processors.
- **Vision vs lidar.** Asked about Elon Musk's lidar stance, Angle says homes are visually harder than roads but far easier on safety; for cars he would still consider a cheap lidar as a backup sensor.
- **Privacy.** Angle cites iRobot's privacy manifesto, GDPR applied globally and on-robot image processing. He says privacy labels on packaging would currently reduce sales, and calls for a shared, understandable privacy standard.
