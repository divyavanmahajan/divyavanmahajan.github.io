## Thesis

Stanford physicist Leonard Susskind tells Lex Fridman that he does physics by visualising first and turning insights into mathematics later, and that years of practice rewire intuition so that quantum mechanics feels natural. He sees string theory as a tool rather than a separate field, and he expects machine learning and evolved machines, more than introspection, to shed light on intelligence and consciousness.

## Key points

- **Intuition and ego.** Susskind says Richard Feynman confirmed his own intuitive, visual style rather than changing it. He argues good scientists need the arrogance to take on nature and the humility to expect to be wrong. He did not learn physics existed until he was almost 20.
- **Quantum computers.** Storing the quantum state of 400 qubits classically would take more information than the universe could hold, Susskind says. He thinks factoring-style speedups are rare and that the main value will be simulating chemistry, materials, quantum field theory and quantum gravity.
- **Machine learning.** Susskind advises a group of machine-learning physicists at Google X. He says nobody really understands why machine learning generalises so well, and notes the similarity between tensor networks and neural networks.
- **String theory.** Susskind says the theory began as a valid description of hadrons in the late 1960s, then returned as a theory of gravity. He argues its main contribution is mathematically rigorous models showing that gravity and quantum mechanics can coexist, which nobody now doubts. He rejects Gerard 't Hooft's deterministic substructure.
- **Arrow of time.** Susskind describes entropy as a statistical idea that only applies to large systems. Small systems can be run backward in the lab, but chaos makes this impractical for large ones; he says this is not time travel.
- **Cosmology and open questions.** Susskind says anti-de Sitter space could in principle be simulated on a quantum computer, but our expanding de Sitter universe is not understood. He calls the black hole image a triumph that confirms rather than reveals, and sees an intelligent agent behind the universe as a real but possibly unanswerable question.
