## Thesis

Larry Lawton, a former Gambino associate and jewel thief who spent 12 years in federal prison, argues that crime comes down to people's habits and mistakes rather than technology. He describes prison, and especially long stretches in solitary, as abusive and mostly pointless. His case is that rehabilitation, unannounced inspections and police who see themselves as part of the community would work better than more laws.

## Key points

- **GTA 6 versus reality.** In the first part, recorded recently, Lawton critiques the GTA 6 trailer. He says no real operation would let strangers walk past armed guards or take calls on speaker in front of a crowd. Only "a moron" robs a 7-Eleven, he says: you rob where the money is.
- **How a heist works.** Lawton says he cased over 1,000 stores and robbed 25 or more. He planned the exit first, then worked backwards, and timed jobs so that sun glare on the glass blocked the view inside. Fences paid him 25-30% on jewellery and a flat $2,000 per standard Rolex. Everything was melted down within 24 hours.
- **Getting caught.** FBI agent Matt Mullen hunted him for six years and placed him using convenience-store camera footage. Lawton says he was offered about two years and could keep $6.1 million if he informed. He refused because of his father's lesson that your word is all you have.
- **The hole.** Lawton went in at 34 and came out at 46. He describes 11 months in solitary "for investigation", being four-pointed and beaten, and coming close to suicide. He cites 1.9 million prisoners in the US against 1.69 million in China.
- **A cheap fix.** He proposes a team made up of a former inmate, a guard, a warden and medical staff, with power to inspect any prison unannounced. Anyone who blocks them would be fired. He contrasts this with accreditation visits that are scheduled two weeks in advance.
- **Reality Check.** After a judge sentenced two young people to a programme he did not yet have, Lawton built one. He cites a 90% success rate in keeping kids out of jail, measured by Eastern Florida State College.
