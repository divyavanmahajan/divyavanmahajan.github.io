## Thesis

Military analyst Michael Kofman (Carnegie Endowment) and West Point graduate and Afghanistan veteran Preston Stewart tell Lex Fridman that the war, now in its fifth year, turned around in fall 2025: Ukraine is advancing locally while Russia's grinding infiltration tactics have hit diminishing returns. Kofman argues that Russia's political goals no longer match its military means, so it is leaning on strategic bombardment of Ukrainian cities and has no real interest in a ceasefire.

## Key points

- **The kill zone.** Kofman says drones cause about 80% of losses and each side flies perhaps 8,000-10,000 FPVs a day, but minefields and artillery still matter. Destroying a tank takes about 30-40 drones, an infantry fighting vehicle 15-20. Combat videos only show the hits.
- **Not an autonomous war.** Almost every drone is flown by a two- or three-person team. Fiber optic drones, which cannot be jammed, are maybe 15% or less of the total. Tactics and technology change on a three-to-four-month cycle.
- **Russian infiltration and false maps.** Stewart describes Russian soldiers moving forward alone or on motorcycles, at very high cost, and units claiming ground on the strength of a flag video. Kofman says Russian maps put their lines 10-15 km ahead of reality. Fire support gets planned around those claims, and later costly assaults try to take the ground already reported as held.
- **Command culture.** Stewart contrasts Western mission command with Russian top-down orders. Kofman explains "Bakhmut syndrome", holding a city for political reasons after it stops making military sense, and the "Wagnerization" of the Russian army with convict assault units after Prigozhin's mutiny.
- **Endurance.** Russia spends 44% of its budget on the war, about 9-10% of GDP. North Korea at one point supplied 40% of its artillery ammunition. Ukraine depends on Western money and intelligence, with Europeans now paying for US weapons.
- **Trump and peace.** Both guests criticize the administration for giving up leverage and acting as a neutral referee. Kofman expects Putin to wait on the winter strike campaign and the European elections, and warns that a weak ceasefire could lead to a third war, as happened after Minsk.
