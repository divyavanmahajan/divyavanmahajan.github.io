## Thesis

Khabib Nurmagomedov, who retired from the UFC at 29-0, argues that champions come from hard conditions, deep coaching benches and a near-fanatical commitment to training, and gives most of the credit to his late father. He says staying at the top is harder than getting there, and that his faith kept success and money from changing him.

## Key points

- **Why Dagestan wins.** Khabib describes a Makhachkala school gym with one shower for 70 people that produced more than 20 world champions. He credits fierce competition, a clear ladder from regional to world level, generations of wrestling coaches going back to Ali Aliyev, and centuries of mountain life under invading empires.
- **Hating to lose.** He trained four to five hours a day, booked flights around his sessions, and sometimes slipped out at night for a third workout by the sea. His father paid him to read and copy out a book on the world's countries.
- **Pressure and strategy.** He explains his top game as forcing opponents to waste energy escaping, and cites his grandfather: a dog that latches onto one weak spot wins. That is why he never stood and struck with Conor McGregor.
- **Conor and Gaethje.** He calls the 2018 McGregor fight a real rivalry that went beyond the sport, and says one call from his father mattered more than millions of fans. His hardest fight was his last, against Gaethje, after his father's death, through injuries and with a promise to his mother to retire.
- **Legends and coaching.** He recalls the reigning Pride champion Fedor Emelianenko entering the 2004 Dagestan combat sambo championship and drinking tea from a thermos between fights. He praises Javier Mendez, calls Daniel Cormier a top-10 fighter, and says time is running short for Islam Makhachev to chase Jon Jones's and Demetrious Johnson's records.
- **Football and faith.** A Real Madrid fan, he refuses to rank Cristiano Ronaldo above Messi. He says faith was the only thing that helped after losing his father, and his advice to young people is the Quran's first command: read.
