## Thesis

Naturalist Paul Rosolie, author of the new book Jungle Keeper, says his organisation Jungle Keepers has learned that the land it protects is also home to the Mashco Piro, an uncontacted tribe whose only wish is to be left alone. With loggers and now narco-traffickers pushing in, he argues that the remaining land must be bought within roughly 18 months or both the tribe and the ecosystem will be lost.

## Key points

- **First filmed encounter.** In October 2024, two months after the tribe killed two loggers, Rosolie's team travelled overnight upriver after a community called for help. About 50 warriors with seven-foot arrows came onto a beach, and anthropologist Romel called out "Nomole" (brothers), which appears to be their name for themselves. Rosolie shows footage he calls a world first.
- **Peace, then violence.** The warriors took boatloads of plantains, rope and a Jungle Keepers shirt while their women raided the farm behind the group. The next day around 200 of them attacked a boat, and an arrow went through a local man, George, who survived after a medevac.
- **Buying the jungle.** Jungle Keepers protects 130,000 acres of a roughly 300,000-acre watershed. Rosolie says it needs about $20 million for the rest, which is mostly tribal territory, and that it turns loggers into rangers.
- **The narco threat.** After Rosolie flew a drone over a clearing, traffickers chased his boat. A police officer who escorted them was shot dead that night, and gunmen later ambushed the team's driver looking for Rosolie and JJ, who now have a bounty on them. He describes airstrips hidden under the canopy.
- **Jane Goodall's help.** Rosolie credits Goodall, who died on 1 October, with launching his career: she offered to endorse his first book after every New York publisher had turned it down, and HarperCollins then signed it. Later, Dax's 10-year funding commitment arrived four days after Rosolie had quit conservation.
- **Animals and writing.** He describes catching a 19-foot anaconda, seeing the "Mist River" from a 160-foot ironwood, and rescuing a drowning spider monkey. He journals daily and writes on paper before moving to a laptop.
