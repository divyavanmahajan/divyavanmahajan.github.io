## Thesis

Civil War historian Gary Gallagher argues that slavery, and specifically its expansion into federal territories, caused the war. Most Union soldiers, though, fought to save the Union rather than to end slavery. He rejects the idea that the war or the Union victory was inevitable. Individuals mattered: Lincoln and Grant made the victory, and Lee prolonged the war long enough to turn it into a war against slavery.

## Key points

- **Cause versus motivation.** Gallagher separates why the war happened (slavery) from why men enlisted. Northern volunteers saw the Union as a unique democratic gift from the founders. He puts true abolitionists at about 3% of white Americans.
- **Slavery was thriving.** Citing the 1860 census, he says slaves were valued at about $3 billion, more than all industry and railroads combined. Without the war, he thinks the US might have been the last country to end slavery.
- **The Confederacy could have won.** It only needed Northern voters to decide the war was not worth the cost. Gallagher compares this to Vietnam. Union morale hit bottom in July and August 1864, and it was Sherman's capture of Atlanta, not Gettysburg, that re-elected Lincoln.
- **Grant versus Lee.** Before the two met, Grant's battle casualties totaled 37,000 against Lee's 98,000. Citing Joe Glatthaar's study, Gallagher says a soldier in Lee's army had a 73% chance of becoming a casualty, so he calls the "Grant the Butcher" label unfair. McClellan was risk-averse and held back 20,000 men at Antietam.
- **Turning points.** Gallagher ranks Vicksburg above Gettysburg because Grant captured a whole army while Lee escaped. He traces Pickett's Charge back to Lee's faith in his infantry after Chancellorsville.
- **Memory and today.** He describes the Lost Cause myth: the war was about states' rights, it was always hopeless, and Lee was its hero. He thinks decisions on monuments should be made locally. On today's US, he sees 1850s-style rhetoric but no secession conventions, and he remains optimistic that the republic is resilient.
