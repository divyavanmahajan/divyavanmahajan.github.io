## Thesis

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang argues that AI has turned computers from retrieval systems into factories that generate tokens. Pre-training, post-training, test-time and agentic scaling now feed each other, so in his view intelligence scales with one thing: compute. NVIDIA's job is to co-design whole AI factories, and he sees nothing in physics that caps how large the company can become.

## Key points

- **Extreme co-design.** Huang says one GPU can no longer hold the problem, and Amdahl's law means every part (CPU, memory, networking, power, cooling) has to be optimised together. He runs this through about 60 direct reports and no one-on-ones; every problem is attacked as a group.
- **CUDA on GeForce.** Putting CUDA on consumer GPUs raised costs by about 50% at a 35% gross-margin company. The market cap fell to around $1.5 billion and recovery took a decade. Huang calls install base CUDA's main moat, ahead of its ecosystem across clouds, cars and space.
- **Scaling and agents.** Huang expects synthetic data to make training limited by compute, not data. He says inference is "thinking" and needs heavy compute, and he calls OpenClaw "the iPhone of tokens". NVIDIA's OpenShell lets an agent have only two of three rights: access to sensitive data, running code and talking to outside systems.
- **Power and supply chain.** Huang says the grid runs at about 60% of peak most of the time. He wants data centres that degrade gracefully and utilities that sell power with weaker guarantees, not six-nines contracts. He says he persuaded DRAM makers to invest in HBM and LPDDR for data centres, and that a Vera Rubin rack draws on about 200 suppliers.
- **China and TSMC.** Huang credits China's speed to its talent, competition between provinces and a culture of open sharing. He says TSMC's edge is orchestration, customer service and trust; NVIDIA has done business with it for three decades without a contract.
- **AGI and jobs.** Using Fridman's test of an AI building a billion-dollar company, Huang says AGI is here now, though agents building NVIDIA has zero odds. Citing radiology, he argues AI automates tasks rather than purposes, and expects coders to grow from 30 million to about 1 billion.
