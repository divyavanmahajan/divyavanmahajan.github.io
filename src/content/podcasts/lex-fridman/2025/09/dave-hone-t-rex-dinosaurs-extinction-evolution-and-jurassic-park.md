## Thesis

Dave Hone, a zoologist who works on fossils at Queen Mary University of London, argues that dinosaurs were real animals in real ecosystems, so modern ecology is the best guide to their behavior. With Lex Fridman he uses T. rex to show how much can be inferred from bones, bite marks and populations, while warning against reading too much into single specimens.

## Key points

- **T. rex scale.** Hone describes an animal about 12 meters long and roughly seven tons, with tennis-ball-sized eyes and a heavily boned skull. He puts its top speed at an upper estimate of about 40 kph, as a power walker, not a runner.
- **Hunting small.** Hone says carnivores mostly take prey far smaller than themselves, around 5 to 20% of their mass in his Microraptor work. Healed bite marks suggest T. rex targeted juveniles, while bite marks on top of erosion show it also scavenged.
- **Jurassic Park myths.** Hone says the idea that T. rex cannot see you if you stay still appears to be Crichton's invention, and that evidence for pack hunting in any dinosaur is very weak. Tracks heading the same way do not show group hunting.
- **Intelligence and display.** He supports the paper that cut T. rex neuron estimates to between 250 million and 1.7 billion, closer to crocodiles. Head crests in both sexes may point to mutual sexual selection and cooperative breeding.
- **Extinction.** Hone attributes the end-Cretaceous extinction to an asteroid off the Yucatan about 66 million years ago. Large, land-bound animals suffered most, though he would be surprised if some dinosaurs did not survive locally for a time.
- **What the field needs.** Hone notes 1,500 to 1,600 valid species, with 40 to 50 named a year, but argues population data matters more. Protoceratops, with over 100 good skeletons from embryos to adults, is the best case.

## Quotes

> "The evidence for pack hunting in any dinosaur at all is almost nonexistent. It basically doesn’t exist." — Dave Hone

> "another hundred Protoceratops will actually probably tell us a lot more than 50 new species, however cool 50 new species might be." — Dave Hone
