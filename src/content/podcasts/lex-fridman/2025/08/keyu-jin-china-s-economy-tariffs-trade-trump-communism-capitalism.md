## Thesis

LSE economist Keyu Jin, author of The New China Playbook, tells Lex Fridman that the West misreads China as a centrally run economy. She argues that political centralization combined with economic decentralization, through what she calls the "mayor economy", drove China's growth. In her view tariffs and export controls have hurt everyone and sped up Chinese self-sufficiency, while China's real challenges are weak consumption, the real estate downturn and eroding meritocracy.

## Key points

- **The mayor economy.** Jin explains that local officials competed on GDP growth for promotion, which drove industrialization and then a land-sale and real estate boom. When environmental protection became a penalizing factor, blue skies returned to Beijing within a few years. She suggests adding consumption to the yardstick.
- **State push, then retreat.** She defends early state mobilization in new sectors such as EVs, batteries and solar, while noting the waste of 80 cities each backing EVs; the market should then pick the last five survivors.
- **Export controls backfired.** Jin calls DeepSeek "crisis innovation" and says sanctions motivated a whole-of-nation push, as with Huawei. She warns that leverage such as rare earths loses force each time it is used.
- **Tariffs don't work.** She says China's principles in the trade talks are equivalence, reciprocity and realism, with Hong Kong and Taiwan off the table. She notes US trade deficits widened since Trump because the US saves less than it invests, and points to the 1980s US-Japan semiconductor contest as a better model of competing by strengthening yourself.
- **One-child policy effects.** In her class 98% of urban households had one child. Jin credits the policy with greater education investment in girls and a high saving rate, but also with intense competition and a demographic shift that was loosened too late.
- **No collapse.** Jin argues the West has predicted collapse several times since the 1980s; China's fundamentals remain, but at about $10,000 per capita income it is far below its potential while it weans itself off property.
