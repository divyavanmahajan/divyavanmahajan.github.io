## Thesis

Lex Fridman pushes for a quick three-way meeting between Zelenskyy, Putin and Trump to negotiate a ceasefire. Zelenskyy argues that a ceasefire alone is a trap: Putin broke earlier agreements, and without security guarantees backed by the United States he will rearm and attack again. Peace, in his view, comes from a strong Ukraine and pressure on Russia, with Ukraine and Trump agreeing a position first and only then talking to Moscow.

## Key points

- **The 2019 ceasefire failed.** Zelenskyy recounts the December 2019 Normandy-format talks in Paris with Macron, Merkel and Putin: the gas transit deal and the prisoner exchange happened, but Russian forces were killing Ukrainians again within about a month, and Moscow eventually stopped answering his calls.
- **Paper guarantees did not work.** He dismisses the Budapest Memorandum and the Minsk agreements as worthless paper, noting that Ukraine's letters asking the signatories for consultations got no reply. He wants NATO membership, even if at first it covers only the territory Kyiv controls, plus an arms package and continued sanctions.
- **The military balance.** Zelenskyy says Ukraine's army of about 980,000 is the largest in Europe, roughly four times France's. North Korea has sent 12,000 troops and given Russia 3.7 million artillery rounds, against 1 million from Europe.
- **Leverage and compromise.** Fridman proposes alternatives: both countries joining NATO, partnerships with India and Saudi Arabia, tech ties. Zelenskyy suggests using the roughly $300 billion in frozen Russian assets to buy US weapons, and rejects forgiving Putin as a compromise.
- **Corruption and aid.** Zelenskyy points to anti-corruption bodies and the jailing of oligarch Ihor Kolomoyskyi. He says Ukraine received less than half of the roughly $177 billion the US approved, and asks whether rules forcing Ukraine to pay for expensive US cargo flights, rather than use its own Antonov jets, count as corruption or lobbying.
- **Elections and the future.** He says elections are suspended under martial law but could be held about 90 days after it ends. For Ukraine's future he names digitalisation (Diia), tax reform and investment in energy, gold and uranium.
