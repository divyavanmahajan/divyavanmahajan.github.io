## Thesis

Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson, authors of the book Abundance, argue that the American left has become good at blocking rather than building. Blue states, they say, have failed on cost of living because rules they wrote choke the supply of housing and clean energy. They want Democrats to judge government by what it builds, not by what it spends. They see DOGE as a drive to centralise power under Trump rather than a real efficiency effort. Lex Fridman pushes back, suggesting the destruction may be a needed first step.

## Key points

- **Leaderless Democrats, attention politics.** Klein calls the Democratic Party more fragmented and leaderless than at any point in his lifetime. He says Democrats still chase money while the right understands that attention is what counts: Harris raised about a billion dollars, but would not take risks on social media herself.
- **Supply-side progressivism.** Thompson points to Chicago spending $11 billion on 10,000 affordable units, a $1.7 million public toilet in San Francisco, and $42 billion for rural broadband (BEAD) under a 14-stage process that only 3 of 56 states and jurisdictions had completed by November 2024.
- **Housing as opportunity.** Klein argues cities are where economic opportunity is, so pricing out a janitor or barber closes off a classic path to mobility. Thompson cites a study finding that a 10% rise in progressive vote share goes with a 30% drop in permitted homes.
- **Deregulate government itself.** Klein's "everything bagel liberalism" describes how affordable housing funded by LA's bond measure ended up costing $600,000 to $700,000 a unit once extra rules were attached. Thompson contrasts outcome rules like the Clean Air and Water Acts with process rules like NEPA, which make it easy to sue against new building.
- **DOGE without goals.** Thompson argues that cutting probationary staff at the FDA slows drug approvals, and says efficiency only means something when tied to a stated goal. Klein points to the shutdown of PEPFAR and to Musk threatening 50 to a hundred million dollars in primary spending against Republicans who defy Trump. Fridman defends Musk's engineering culture at Twitter.
- **AI and attention.** Klein worries AI will make people less capable by automating the hardest parts of thinking, like doing the reading and writing a first draft.
