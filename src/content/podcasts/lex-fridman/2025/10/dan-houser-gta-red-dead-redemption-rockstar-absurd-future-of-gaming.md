## Thesis

Dan Houser, who co-founded Rockstar Games and wrote the Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption stories, tells Lex Fridman that great open-world games come from combining a living, systemic world with a strong story and rounded characters. He believes games are still early as a medium, that AI will help with cheap, low-level work but not original ideas, and that his new company, Absurd Ventures, will stay focused on single-player open worlds.

## Key points

- **Story versus freedom.** Houser says people on the team pushed for less story and more procedural play, but he kept returning to story as the way to unlock features. GTA IV was criticised for too much story around Niko; he thinks Red Dead Redemption 2 reconciled the two best.
- **Building characters.** He describes games as the personality of the world multiplied or divided by the personality of the protagonist, and starts from one sentence ("a Serbian immigrant"). GTA V's three leads were built around ego, id and superego.
- **Killing the hero.** In a 2008-09 meeting with designer Christian Cantamessa, Houser proposed that John Marston must die, breaking Rockstar's rule that players stay free to finish side content. Arthur Morgan's tuberculosis drew on his grandfather, one of about three in 35 to survive a sanatorium.
- **Process and pressure.** He collects notes for months, then writes in bursts, such as a 30-page GTA IV synopsis written in an upstate cabin. Red Dead 2 was behind schedule and well over budget.
- **AI and writing.** Houser argues LLMs have done the first 90-95% of sounding human, and the last 5% will be most of the work. He expects AI to make life harder for beginners and to produce generic work if used in place of creativity.
- **Absurd Ventures.** He is developing A Better Paradise, about a superintelligent AI called Nigel Dave (game in Santa Monica), the Absurdiverse comedy open world (San Rafael, two to four years away), and the American Caper comic with artist David Lapham.
