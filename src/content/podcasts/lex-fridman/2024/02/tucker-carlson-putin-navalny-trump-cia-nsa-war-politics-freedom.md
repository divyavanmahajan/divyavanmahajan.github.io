## Thesis

Tucker Carlson tells Lex Fridman that he interviewed Vladimir Putin to bring more information to Americans, and that he judges every world event only by what it means for the United States. He argues that the US government, its media and its intelligence agencies have lied about the war in Ukraine, and that sanctions and surveillance damage American freedom more than they hurt Russia. Fridman pushes back throughout: he says Ukraine could still "win", that Zelensky showed courage by staying in Kyiv, and that Carlson could have been tougher on Putin.

## Key points

- **The Putin interview.** Carlson says Putin seemed nervous and over-prepared, and that he let him give his long history lesson rather than "get rolled". Before the trip, his lawyers sent a 10-page memo warning he could be arrested under US sanctions law.
- **War and sanctions.** Carlson claims Russia makes seven times as many artillery shells as all NATO countries combined and has 100 million more people than Ukraine. He says Boris Johnson was sent to stop an early peace deal, and that sanctions are pushing countries towards BRICS and away from the dollar.
- **Navalny.** Carlson says "we don't know" how Navalny died and opposes jailing opposition figures. He calls Russia not a free country, but says he saw no cult of personality in Moscow.
- **Surveillance.** Carlson says the NSA read his Signal messages and that intelligence leaks about his meeting with Edward Snowden reached Semafor. Two sources told him nothing is secure from state actors.
- **Press freedom.** Fridman cites the World Press Freedom Index: Norway first, the US at 71, Ukraine 61, Russia 35, North Korea 22. Carlson replies that you lose your job in the US for questioning the war, so the press is not truly free.
- **Technology and leaders.** Carlson questions whether any technology of the last 20 years has made life better. He points to South Korea's falling birth rate, calls nuclear weapons evil, and names Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed as the best leader he has met. Fridman defends Google search, Wikipedia and science.
