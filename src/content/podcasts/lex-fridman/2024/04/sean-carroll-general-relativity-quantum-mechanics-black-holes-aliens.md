## Thesis

Theoretical physicist Sean Carroll argues that physics progresses by trusting well-tested equations further than intuition wants to go. General relativity predicted black holes before anyone understood them, and the Schrodinger equation, taken literally, gives the many-worlds interpretation. He accepts dark matter and dark energy for the same reason: the data require them.

## Key points

- **Black holes.** Carroll notes that Schwarzschild found the black hole solution two years after 1915, but nobody recognized it until the 1950s. For a black hole of one solar mass, going from the event horizon to the singularity takes about a millionth of a second. He adds that the singularity lies in your future, not at the centre of space.
- **Holography and neutrinos.** A black hole's entropy scales with the area of its horizon, not its volume. In a paper led by postdoc Oliver Friedrich, Carroll predicted a cutoff for high-energy neutrinos. IceCube's data runs out right where that cutoff should appear.
- **Dark energy and dark matter.** Carroll's quintessence work predicts that dark energy rotates photon polarization by a couple of degrees, which could show up in the cosmic microwave background. He compares inferring dark matter to the discovery of Neptune. His own attempt to explain both with modified gravity helped with dark energy but did nothing for dark matter.
- **Many worlds and entropy.** Carroll says Everett's idea needs no change to the Schrodinger equation, only for observers to locate themselves correctly in the wave function. On entropy, the early universe held about 10^88 units, while the black hole at our galaxy's centre alone has 10^90.
- **Aliens and AI.** Carroll thinks the simplest reason we see no aliens is that they are not there. He says self-replicating von Neumann probes could fill the galaxy, so a quiet probe is more likely than a radio signal. On LLMs, he cites Melanie Mitchell's Othello work, which found that at least half of a model's apparent board representation came from the probe itself.
