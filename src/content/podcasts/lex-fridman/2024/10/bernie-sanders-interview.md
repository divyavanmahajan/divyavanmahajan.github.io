## Thesis

Bernie Sanders argues that the United States is drifting toward an oligarchic society in which billionaires and corporate lobbyists decide which issues are even discussed. He rejects Lex Fridman's description of his proposals as radical: in his view they are widely popular and blocked by money, and change comes from organised grassroots movements rather than from the top.

## Key points

- **Money in politics.** Sanders says outright bribery is rare; the real problem is candidates depending on wealthy donors. He counts 1,800 drug-company lobbyists in Washington, wants Citizens United overturned and calls for public funding of elections.
- **Medicare for All.** He proposes strengthening Medicare, adding dental, hearing and vision, and lowering the eligibility age step by step from 65 to 55, 45 and 35. He argues the US spends twice as much per person as other nations and that higher taxes would replace larger premiums and deductibles.
- **Inequality.** Citing the RAND Corporation, Sanders says about $50 trillion has shifted from the bottom 90% to the top 1% over 50 years while real wages stagnated. He calls for a $17 federal minimum wage.
- **Prophets and kings.** Fridman recounts Obama telling Sanders that prophets don't get to be king. Sanders answers with Eugene Debs, who lost every race but laid the groundwork for Roosevelt's reforms.
- **Capitalism.** Sanders favours Scandinavian-style safety nets with room for innovation, but says nobody needs $100 billion; he credits Elon Musk's work ethic while opposing his role in Trump's campaign.
- **2024 and the future.** He says he gave a 2024 run five minutes' thought, backs Kamala Harris to stop Trump, praises Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, and says that since his heart attack he fears infirmity more than death.
