## Thesis

Ed Barnhart, director of the Maya Exploration Center, argues that the ancient Americas deserve to stand alongside Egypt and Mesopotamia: people arrived far earlier than once believed, built pyramids on the Peruvian coast before Egypt did, and developed real science in astronomy, mathematics and engineering. He rejects Graham Hancock's single lost "mother" civilization in favour of independent cultures sharing a deep common ancestry, and says much of their knowledge was erased by the epidemics that followed European contact.

## Key points

- **Earlier arrivals.** Barnhart says DNA evidence pushes migration across the Bering land bridge from the old 12,500-year figure to around 30,000 years ago, possibly 60,000, with the oldest lineages isolated in South America.
- **Peru's pyramids.** Caral (about 3200 to 1800 BCE) traded farmed cotton nets for fish from coastal Aspero, and Huaca Prieta dates towards 6000 BCE; he suspects it began as a capped trash mound. Egypt has about 140 pyramids, Peru thousands.
- **A single fanged deity.** He argues Andean religion, from Chavin to the Inca's Viracocha, centres on one jaguar-like creator god that came out of the Amazon, rather than the pantheons early archaeologists assumed.
- **Maya science and collapse.** The 260-day Tzolk'in, 365-day Haab and base-20 Long Count interlock; the 2012 "end" was a misreading. He attributes the Classic collapse to overpopulation, seasonal starvation seen in Copan teeth, and a deliberate renewal at the end of the ninth bak'tun.
- **Aztec versus Inca.** The Aztecs ruled through fear, sacrifice and cannibalism and were widely hated; the Inca's road network, mit'a labour system and khipu accounting supported at least 10 million people, and Barnhart thinks the Inca would win a war between them.
- **Lost knowledge.** He cites estimates of about 150 million people in the Americas, 90% killed by disease within 50 years, and wants a "Before the Americas" K-12 curriculum. He is also testing a theory that the Inca fused stone walls using hydrofluoric acid.
