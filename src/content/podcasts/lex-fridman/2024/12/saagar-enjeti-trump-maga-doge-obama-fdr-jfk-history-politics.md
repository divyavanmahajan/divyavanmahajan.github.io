## Thesis

Political commentator Saagar Enjeti argues that Trump's 2024 win came from inflation, a global anti-incumbent mood, Biden's immigration record and, above all, a cultural backlash against elites, with education rather than race now dividing voters. Drawing on a long list of history books, he also argues that Washington's bureaucracy, the Senate and Congress's control of spending will limit what any president, Trump included, can actually change.

## Key points

- **A realignment on education.** Enjeti calls Trump "one of the most racially depolarizing" figures in US history, pointing to gains with Latino and young men, and cites Charles Murray's Coming Apart to argue that culture, not income, divides Americans.
- **Biden judged by his own goal.** He calls Biden the second-worst modern president (George W. Bush is the worst, over Iraq), blaming arrogance and a lack of FDR-style energy, and notes that Biden failed at his stated aim of keeping Trump out.
- **Immigration as a core issue.** Enjeti, himself a son of immigrants, supports mass deportation and a merit-based points system to replace family-based "chain migration", while doubting the public is ready. He suggests mandatory E-Verify as the most practical route and questions Kristi Noem's fitness for DHS.
- **DOGE meets Washington.** He describes DOGE as an unfunded, non-statutory "Blue Ribbon" commission that depends on Republican appropriators. With entitlements off the table, he argues real savings would have to come from overhauling Pentagon procurement, and that rewriting statutes would matter more than identifying cuts.
- **The bureaucracy shapes decisions.** Using Graham Allison's Essence of Decision, he argues whoever drafts the president's options holds the power, citing H.R. McMaster on Afghanistan and Obama's 2009 troop surge.
- **Low-, high- and "galaxy-brain" 2020 claims.** Enjeti rejects Trump's voting-machine fraud claims but considers arguments about COVID-era mail-in rule changes and media censorship of the Hunter Biden laptop story more credible.
