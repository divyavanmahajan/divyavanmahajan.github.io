## Thesis

Sam Altman treats the November 2023 board crisis as an early preview of the power struggle he expects on the road to AGI, and says it taught him OpenAI needs a board and structure that hold up under pressure. He argues that no single person, himself included, should control AGI, that governments should set rules, and that progress should be released iteratively so the world is not shocked by sudden jumps.

## Key points

- **The board saga.** Altman calls the firing the most painful professional experience of his life. He says the old board shrank from nine to six members, lacked experienced directors, and as a nonprofit board answered to no one. Brett and Larry Summers were chosen "on the battlefield"; new members will be picked in slates.
- **Ilya and Elon.** Altman says Ilya Sutskever "has not seen AGI" and that he hopes to keep working with him. On Musk's lawsuit, he says Musk wanted total control or a merger with Tesla, then chose to leave.
- **GPT-4 and what comes next.** Altman says GPT-4 "kind of sucks" relative to where models are heading, and expects the gap between GPT-5 and GPT-4 to match that between GPT-4 and GPT-3. He would not give a GPT-5 date but promised "an amazing new model this year".
- **Compute and energy.** Altman expects demand for compute to behave like energy, priced rather than capped, with energy the hardest constraint. He names Helion as doing the best fusion work and wants new fission reactors.
- **Tasks, not jobs.** On Sora and automation, he frames the question as what share of five-second to five-day tasks AI can do, and says artists deserve opt-outs and payment when their style is used.
- **Ads, safety and AGI.** Altman says he dislikes ads and believes OpenAI can pay for its compute without them. He proposes publishing a model's intended behaviour so failures like Gemini's image generation can be judged as bugs or policy. He defines AGI by a meaningful rise in the rate of scientific discovery.
