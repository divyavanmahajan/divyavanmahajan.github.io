## Thesis

Harvard historian Serhii Plokhy tells Lex Fridman that the Soviet collapse in 1991 was one stage in the long break-up of the Russian Empire, and that the current war continues that process. In his view, Putin's goal is to keep Ukraine in Russia's sphere of influence, and NATO and "denazification" are pretexts. Plokhy expects the war to end like other imperial collapses, with new states, but will not predict the near term.

## Key points

- **Ukraine ended the USSR.** Plokhy points out that the Soviet Union was dissolved one week after Ukraine's 1 December 1991 referendum. Yeltsin told Bush that without Ukraine, Russia would be outvoted by the Muslim republics. Bush had warned against independence in his "Chicken Kiev" speech that August.
- **Bandera as myth.** Plokhy separates the real Stepan Bandera from the propaganda version. Bandera worked with Nazi Germany, but he spent much of the war in Sachsenhausen and two of his brothers died in Auschwitz. Plokhy says the far right in Ukraine is marginal: it could not get more than 2% of the vote in 2019.
- **KGB culture in the Kremlin.** He tells the story of Bohdan Stashynsky, who killed Bandera in Munich with a spray pistol and later defected. Plokhy argues that Putin's circle brought the KGB mindset into Russian foreign policy.
- **NATO is not the cause.** Plokhy notes that Finland joining NATO roughly doubled Russia's border with the alliance without any matching troop shift. Putin's July 2021 essay was about Russians and Ukrainians, not about NATO.
- **No quick peace.** Once Russia wrote five Ukrainian oblasts into its constitution in 2022, he says, going back to the pre-2022 borders became impossible without political change in Moscow. He compares Zelensky's dismissal of General Zaluzhny to Truman firing MacArthur.
- **Nuclear risk is political.** Plokhy blames Chernobyl on secrecy and centralised decisions. None of the world's roughly 440 reactors was designed to survive a war like the one around Zaporizhzhia, and each major accident freezes reactor construction worldwide for about ten years.
