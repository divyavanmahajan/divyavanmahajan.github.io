## Thesis

Two sides argue throughout a five-hour, often hostile debate. Norman Finkelstein and Mouin Rabbani say that displacing Palestinians was built into Zionism and that Israel's current war in Gaza is genocidal. Benny Morris and Steven Bonnell (Destiny) say that the 1948 expulsions came out of a war the Arabs started, and that Palestinian rejection of offers, not Israeli design, explains why there is still no Palestinian state. By the end, none of the four expects peace any time soon.

## Key points

- **1948 and transfer.** Finkelstein keeps citing Morris's own phrase that transfer was "inevitable and inbuilt into Zionism". Morris answers that transfer was never adopted as policy and that 20% of Israel's population in 1949 was Arab and received citizenship. Finkelstein replies that those citizens lived under a military government until 1966.
- **Partition.** Rabbani, citing Walid Khalidi, says the 1947 plan gave about 55% of Palestine to a community that made up one third of the population. Bonnell argues the Arabs would have rejected any partition. Rabbani agrees they rejected it on principle.
- **October 7.** Morris cites about 1,200 killed and 250 abducted, and calls the 1988 Hamas charter genocidal. Finkelstein accepts that about 850 civilians were killed. Rabbani separates the attacks on military sites from the deliberate killing of civilians, which he calls illegitimate.
- **Genocide case.** Rabbani says the ICJ ruling of 26 January found South Africa's case plausible but did not decide it. Bonnell argues South Africa quoted Herzog and Smotrich out of context. Morris predicts the court will rule there was no genocide.
- **Peace talks.** Morris calls the Clinton parameters of December 2000 the best deal Palestinians will get and says Arafat was "constitutionally" unable to sign. Finkelstein argues that, measured against international law, every concession came from the Palestinian side. He cites Olmert's 2008 offer to let 5,000 refugees return.
- **Hope.** Morris says people dislike war. Finkelstein says there is no hope at the moment. Bonnell argues that only a Sadat-like Palestinian leader, not appeals to international law, would push Israel towards a deal.
