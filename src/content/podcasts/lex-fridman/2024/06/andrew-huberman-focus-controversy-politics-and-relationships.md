## Thesis

On his fifth appearance, Andrew Huberman tells Lex Fridman how he handles being a public health educator under constant online scrutiny. He argues for inviting critics into long-form conversation, correcting mistakes publicly as teaching moments, and looking at each issue on its merits instead of joining a camp. Fridman adds that scientists who are rude online erode trust in science, as he believes happened during Covid.

## Key points

- **Building a podcast company.** Huberman says SciCom Media, which he runs with Rob Moore, is launching Perform with Dr. Andy Galpin and scouting young science talent. He compares this to scientists such as Richard Axel, who are known for the researchers they mentor.
- **Turning a dogpile into a debate.** After a clip from his cannabis episode drew attacks on X, Huberman says he invited the most hostile critic on the show. That critic was hostile online but polite on the phone. Fridman calls the critic's tone elitist.
- **Owning the math gaffe.** Huberman admits a cumulative-probability slip ("20 to 120%") in a fertility episode. He says he posted the correct formula and now fixes errors in show notes and audio. He adds that AI now makes such fact-checking easier.
- **Deepfakes and sponsors.** He says a Jawzrsizer ad quoting him was AI-generated and that he never said it. His team lists its real sponsors on its website and has dropped some when products changed.
- **Politics and the centre.** Huberman calls for a "league of reasonable people" in health and politics. Fridman wants Trump and Biden to do two- to three-hour conversations, for example with Joe Rogan, instead of short TV-style interviews.
- **Deep work routines.** Fridman tracks 11 daily habits in Google Sheets, with scripts that turn cells green, and aims for four hours of morning deep focus. Huberman manages 90 minutes to three hours, uses NSDR, and says he and Matt Walker are planning a study on it.
