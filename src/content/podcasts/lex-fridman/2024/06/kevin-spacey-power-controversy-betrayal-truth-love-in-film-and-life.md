## Thesis

Spacey describes his craft as serving the writing: strip away mannerisms, say the words and mean them, and never judge the character. Fridman frames the second half around due process, pressing Spacey on the allegations that got him cut from House of Cards in 2017. Spacey admits to crossing boundaries, denies the most serious claims, and asks whether the entertainment industry offers a path to redemption.

## Key points

- **Directors who demand less.** Spacey says David Fincher, who Fridman notes averages 25 to 65 takes, repeats scenes to shred an actor's "pretense". He tells a George Cukor story in which Jack Lemmon is asked again and again to do "a little less".
- **Se7en and American Beauty.** Spacey replaced a fired actor on Se7en and asked for no billing so audiences wouldn't guess his role. On American Beauty, Sam Mendes used The Apartment to show him a character who evolves rather than changes. Three hairpieces and costume changes made that shift gradual, Mendes reshot the first two days, and the final narration came from a cassette guide track.
- **Jack Lemmon as mentor.** Lemmon told a 13-year-old Spacey to study acting in New York. Spacey later talked his way into playing opposite him in Long Day's Journey Into Night in 1985.
- **Allegations.** Fridman notes that Spacey was acquitted in the trials, including Anthony Rapp's $40 million suit. Spacey says he denies preventing anyone from leaving, ignoring a "no", or offering roles for sex. He admits he "hit on a lot of guys", crossed boundaries and has apologised privately to some people. He calls his 2017 statement, which included coming out, a mistake made while "backed into a corner".
- **House of Cards.** Spacey says Richard III taught him direct address, which turns the audience into co-conspirators. He often cut Frank Underwood's dialogue because a look was enough. He says he pushed back as Netflix moved from no creative control to notes on casting, writing and music.
- **Father and empathy.** Spacey describes a father who turned into a white supremacist, and says playing dark roles without judgment teaches empathy.
