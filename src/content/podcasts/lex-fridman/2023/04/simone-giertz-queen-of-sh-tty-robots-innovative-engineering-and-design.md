## Thesis

Simone Giertz, the inventor and YouTuber once known as the "Queen of Shitty Robots", tells Lex Fridman how building things that fail on purpose kept her perfectionism in check. She has since moved to designing products for everyday problems. She argues that a fragile influencer business needs diversifying, that manufacturing is a separate discipline from making one-offs, and that enthusiasm is a better guide than duty.

## Key points

- **From gifs to a career.** Giertz built a toothbrush helmet on a MeArm robot kit after a Swedish broadcaster ignored her pitch for a kids' electronics show. She posted it to Reddit's r/shittyrobots, where it drew about 50,000 views, and within six months building was her full-time job.
- **Self-deprecation as defence.** She calls the humour a way to avoid perfectionism, but also a survival tactic: being a woman in a male-dominated field meant downplaying her skill. Now a CEO of three businesses, she tries to use it only when it helps her.
- **One-off versus mass production.** The Every Day Calendar began with 365 toggle switches. For production it became a capacitive-touch circuit board front plate. Her Kickstarter shipped a year late. She says quality control requires "trust issues" and that a puzzle table she built would have to sell for $7,000.
- **A diversified business.** A brain tumour, treated with surgery and radiation, showed her how frail an ad-based YouTube business is. She now runs the self-funded Yetch store, which sells items such as a 499-piece puzzle (she keeps piece 500), and uses the channel for R&D and audience testing.
- **Bigger builds and wariness.** Truckla, a Tesla Model 3 turned into a pickup after about a year of planning, was her most complex project. She is uneasy about weaponised robots and drones, and worries that language models plus recommendation algorithms could manipulate people.
- **Advice.** Giertz recommends working out the context you want to work in, not only the task, and defining success for yourself. For her, that means pride in her projects and control over her time.

## Quotes

> "I think my main advice is enthusiasm is a much more potent fuel in life than duty." — Simone Giertz

> "I can turn my YouTube channel into an R&D department 'cause I have a reason to constantly be exploring things and turning out new products." — Simone Giertz
