## Thesis

In a joke-heavy Thanksgiving conversation, anarchist author Michael Malice argues that cynicism is a form of giving up: most movies, comedians and podcasts are bad, but the good ones are what change lives, so people should look for reasons to find joy. Lex Fridman shares the gratitude but pushes back on Malice's darker view of human nature, saying he sees mostly good in people.

## Key points

- **Gratitude list.** Malice names family (his six-year-old nephew Lucas), being an author who can live from his writing, friends in Austin after growing isolated in New York, and social media, which he credits with connecting niche hobbyists and with Twitter's community notes.
- **Human nature.** Malice says people are "basically animals" with a thin veneer of civilization, citing Hoarders and I Think You Should Leave. Fridman counters that cooperation and creation over the past century show good outweighing bad. Malice concedes humans are capable of profound goodness but calls it unusual.
- **Antisemitism.** Malice argues Jew hatred differs from other bigotry because it openly calls for killing, and that it treats Jews as the only actors capable of evil. His remedy is an "ambassador program": knowing members of a group makes bigotry harder, as with gay Americans since the 1980s.
- **Religion and soft power.** Malice rejects Marx's "opium of the people" line and what he calls internet atheism, saying faith has made people he knows better. Against John Mearsheimer's offensive realism, he argues American influence comes more from exported culture than military bases, closer to Brave New World than 1984.
- **Art and beauty.** Sculptor Jake Michael Singer sent Fridman a piece called Chance Murmur. Malice lists affordable contemporary galleries such as Corey Helford in LA, and describes buying an ibis mummy and a Patrick Nagel painting.
- **Habits and advice.** Malice keeps a rotation of 67 wet-shaving soaps with 86 candidates queued. His advice for happiness is to aim to be better, not great, track measurable progress, and accept getting hit in the face.
