## Thesis

Mohammed el-Kurd, a Palestinian poet and journalist from Sheikh Jarrah in East Jerusalem, argues that what is often reported as a real estate or religious dispute is a political fight over land, demographics and dispossession. Lex Fridman presents the conversation as a different perspective from his earlier episodes with Benjamin Netanyahu and Yuval Noah Harari. He questions el-Kurd on hate, violence, Israeli security concerns and what a peaceful future could look like.

## Key points

- **Sheikh Jarrah.** El-Kurd describes his family's expulsion in 1948 and the settlers who took over half of their home in 2009. He says the family received over 50 expulsion orders. He argues that Israeli courts would not consider Jordanian, UN or Ottoman documents. He describes a 2021 campaign that, he says, led the Israeli Supreme Court to suspend the evictions until a land registry is completed.
- **Law versus morality.** El-Kurd compares the legality of evictions and demolitions to Jim Crow. He cites a figure he attributes to an Israeli military spokesperson: 95% of Palestinian building permit applications in East Jerusalem and the West Bank are rejected.
- **Antisemitism and anti-Zionism.** El-Kurd rejects Netanyahu's claim that anti-Zionism is antisemitism. He criticises the ADL, which he says has labelled him antisemitic, and calls his own 2021 Hitler analogy a poor strategy.
- **Violence and security.** El-Kurd says violence alone will not bring change and argues for "fighting on all fronts". Fridman reads the Washington Post's account of the Jenin raid, questions the glorification of violence and asks about threats from Iran. El-Kurd argues that Israel faces no existential threat as things stand.
- **One state or two.** El-Kurd says settlements make a two-state solution geographically impossible. He calls for "recognition, return and redistribution". Asked about Israelis' future, he says he does not object to them having a state as long as it is not built at Palestinians' expense.
- **Writing.** El-Kurd says he writes in English to bridge the gap between Palestinian and Western discourse. He is critical of US media coverage and of $3.8 billion a year in US military aid, and he is working on a memoir, *A Million States In One*.
