## Thesis

Craig Jones, Nicky Rod and Nicky Ryan, who left John Danaher's team to found B-Team in Austin, talk with Lex Fridman about rivalry, training and running a gym. Jones refuses to take competition seriously, while Nicky Rod relies on visualisation and routine. All three see grappling moving towards wrestling, standing up and new teaching methods.

## Key points

- **The split.** Jones left Australia, where he trained mostly with Lachlan Giles, for the depth of Danaher's New York room. He won't say what happened in Puerto Rico and says he backed Nicky Ryan. Nicky Ryan calls the split justified and says his brother Gordon recently got in touch for the first time in almost two years.
- **Nicky Rod vs Gordon Ryan.** Nicky Rod lost their 20-minute EBI match in overtime, after a toe hold Gordon said broke his foot. He says he should have banked time instead of chasing the choke. Nicky Ryan argues EBI overtime can let a stalling, weaker grappler win.
- **Steroids.** Nicky Rod says he is natural and that he made his steroid claim about Gordon in reply to Gordon accusing him of greasing. Jones says that if a sport doesn't test, you should assume most athletes use. Nicky Ryan says the main benefit is faster recovery and more training sessions. Nicky Rod calls it cheating whether or not anyone tests.
- **Preparing Volkanovski.** Jones spent five to six weeks with Frank Hickman preparing Alexander Volkanovski for Islam Makhachev. They worked on cage wrestling, using turtle to get back up, and submission defence. Jones thought Volkanovski needed a finish going into the fifth round.
- **Teaching beginners.** Jones favours constraint-based games, such as holding side control with no instruction, so students meet the problem before the fix. B-Team's new white-belt classes split time roughly equally between top game, bottom game and wrestling.
- **Money and friendship.** Jones says some coaches think the pie is bigger than it is. He calls the departure of a long-time friend, Isaac, an unresolvable personality clash.

## Quotes

> "I think the best way to learn is through problem solving. And I think if you show the technique before they've discovered that problem, the learning is sort of held back." — Craig Jones

> "I'll visualize, I'll put myself in that competition. That way when it's game time, it's like, I've been there a thousand times already." — Nicky Rod
