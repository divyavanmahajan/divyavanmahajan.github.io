## Thesis

Sam Altman tells Lex Fridman that GPT-4 is an early, slow and buggy system that will still be seen as a milestone, and that OpenAI's strategy of shipping imperfect models early lets the outside world find strengths and flaws while the stakes are low. He argues that alignment and capability progress are closely linked, that no technique yet aligns a superpowerful system, and that decisions about the technology should become more democratic over time.

## Key points

- **RLHF made the difference.** Altman says ChatGPT's leap came less from the base model than from usability: reinforcement learning from human feedback, which he says needs remarkably little data, plus the chat interface.
- **Bias and steerability.** On a Jordan Peterson test of ChatGPT's answers about Biden and Trump, Altman admits the 3.5 launch was biased and says no single model will ever be seen as unbiased; the GPT-4 system message and per-user control are his proposed path.
- **Building in public.** Altman describes red-teaming GPT-4 from the end of training last summer, wanting alignment to improve faster than capability, and favouring "slow takeoff, short timelines" as the safest scenario.
- **Structure and power.** He explains the nonprofit-controlled capped-profit subsidiary (investor returns capped at 100x initially, much lower now) and says he holds no super-voting power or board control.
- **Jobs and economics.** Altman expects customer service roles to shrink, sees programmers becoming far more productive rather than redundant, and calls UBI a component, not a full solution, citing an OpenAI-sponsored study and Worldcoin.
- **Near-term risks.** His current worries are disinformation and economic shocks, given that capable open-source LLMs with few safety controls are coming; he also calls SVB's collapse a case of incentive misalignment and a preview of faster AGI-era change.

## Quotes
