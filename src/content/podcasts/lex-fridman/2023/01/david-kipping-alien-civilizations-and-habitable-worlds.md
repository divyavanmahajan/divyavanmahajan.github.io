## Thesis

David Kipping, who runs the Cool Worlds Lab at Columbia, tells Lex Fridman that the search for life must be guided by evidence, not hope. He stays consciously agnostic about whether we are alone, because wanting an answer biases how data is read. Fridman pushes back, saying he finds a lifeless galaxy hard to believe.

## Key points

- **Hunting exomoons.** Kipping describes the Kepler-1625b candidate: a Jupiter-like planet on a 287-day orbit whose transit hinted at a Neptune-sized moon. About 40 hours of Hubble data showed a roughly five-sigma dip and a transit about 20 minutes early. He refused Science's suggested "Discovery" title and called it "evidence" instead.
- **Why moons matter.** About half of Sun-like stars have a mini-Neptune, Neptune or Jupiter in the habitable zone, against perhaps 1% to 10% with Earth-like planets. Habitable moons could outnumber planets. A Titan-like moon blended into a planet's pixel could also fake a biosignature.
- **Telescope limits.** An Earth passing a Sun-like star dims it by 84 parts per million. Kipping estimates JWST would need about 200 transits of TRAPPIST-1e for a marginal biosignature hint. He hopes Starship makes it cheap to launch many telescopes built from existing ground mirrors.
- **Three traps in alien claims.** Kipping argues the alien hypothesis can explain anything, can dodge any future observation, and fills gaps in our physics. Examples include Boyajian's Star, whose chromatic dips pointed to dust, and Lowell's Martian canals.
- **AI and the Fermi paradox.** After trying ChatGPT, Kipping suggests civilisations may pass briefly from biological to artificial intelligence, which would make our moment unusual and lend weight to the zoo hypothesis. The two disagree on whether intelligence needs consciousness.
- **Simulation odds.** In his Bayesian analysis, a 50-50 prior on whether simulations are ever built leaves the chance that we live in one just under 50%. He also cites Sean Carroll's point that most simulated observers would sit in a bottom "sewer" layer unable to run simulations of their own.
