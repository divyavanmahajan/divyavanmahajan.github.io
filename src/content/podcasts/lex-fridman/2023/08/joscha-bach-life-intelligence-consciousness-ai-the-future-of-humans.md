## Thesis

Joscha Bach, a cognitive scientist and AI researcher, tells Lex Fridman that the mind is a self-built model, a "game engine" with a personal self running inside it, and that our worries about AI reflect the stage of development we are at. He sees large language models as powerful but inelegant, and argues that the more important task is building AI that is conscious and shares purposes with us, because life on earth is about complexity resisting entropy, not only about humans.

## Key points

- **Stages of lucidity.** Bach describes seven stages of self-awareness, adapted from psychologist Robert Kegan, from reactive survival to enlightenment and transcendence. Citing Kegan's figure that about 85% of people stay at stage three, he maps AI fears onto them: stage three fears biased opinions, stage four fears paperclip maximizers, stage five fears AI not becoming enlightened fast enough.
- **LLMs as brute force.** He calls transformer models "ugly and brutalist", comparing them to Claude Shannon's brute-force chess strategy. He thinks their gaps (no real-time world coupling or learning) are solvable with larger context windows, a database and nightly retraining, but finds them wasteful.
- **Golems, not players.** Bach says language models are golems with "nobody home". He proposes building small, controlled conscious systems from self-organization principles instead of starting from a language model, and argues a superhuman AI would not stay in a state of suffering.
- **Doom and e/acc.** He takes Eliezer Yudkowsky's warnings seriously but rejects "prepare for a short life" advice. He expects a singleton AI rather than competing AIs, so thinks effective accelerationism is probably wrong, and compares slowing AI to fungi trying to regulate photosynthesis.
- **Open source and corporations.** Bach argues that mandatory open source would cost good design, citing the Linux desktop, but that open source must never die. He warns against an FDA-like licensing process for training models and is not concerned by Meta open-sourcing Llama, since he thinks current models are not yet dangerous.
- **Be a creator.** Recalling East Germany after reunification, where friends ran a donation-based cafe and a community cinema, he advises young people to create rather than consume.
