## Thesis

Aella, a sex researcher, writer and sex worker, tells Lex Fridman that anyone curious enough can do science, and that her very large online surveys, with open methods and data, reveal more about sexuality than small academic studies. She traces this approach to a controlling, conservative childhood, a career in camming and escorting, and a habit of treating her own life as data. Fridman pushes back with a more romantic, less measurable view of love and relationships.

## Key points

- **Upbringing.** Aella describes a homeschooled evangelical childhood under a father she believes has narcissistic personality disorder. She memorised about 800 Bible verses and later worked about a year in an Idaho factory before a friend suggested camming.
- **Sex work as marketing.** She says she cammed on MyFreeCams for five or six years at around $200 an hour, where rankings depend on 60 days of earnings. A creative photo set reached Reddit's all-time top posts. OnlyFans has almost no internal discovery, so her existing Reddit and Twitter presence drove traffic.
- **Escorting economics.** She raised her rate from $800 to $1,400 an hour. Her survey of about 130 escorts put the median near $300. She argues that higher prices and longer bookings attract safer, kinder clients, a claim she says drew criticism as classist.
- **The fetish survey.** After a "kinky character" result went viral on TikTok, the survey grew from 40,000 to 500,000 respondents, about 70% female, covering roughly 850 fetishes. Aella reports that childhood experiences do not predict fetish preference for cis respondents but do affect age of onset, and that about 60% of women report being submissive while about 40% of men report being dominant.
- **Polyamory and jealousy.** She defines polyamory as not forbidding a partner from pursuing intimacy with others and recommends voicing even small jealousy early. Her relationship survey found men cheat about 30% more than women. Fridman says monogamy suits him personally.
- **Psychedelics and AI.** Aella credits one LSD trip with reframing her childhood trauma. She also says she is worried about existential risk from AI.
