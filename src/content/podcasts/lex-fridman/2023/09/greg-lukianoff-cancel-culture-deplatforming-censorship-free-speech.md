## Thesis

Greg Lukianoff, president of FIRE and co-author of The Canceling of the American Mind, argues that cancel culture has been a real, historic phenomenon since about 2014, coming from both left and right. He describes it as part of a lazy rhetorical habit of "winning arguments without winning arguments", which dismisses people by identity or label instead of engaging with their claims. His core defence of free speech is epistemic: censoring opinions hides what people really think and drives them towards polarised groups.

## Key points

- **Scale compared with the Red Scare.** Lukianoff counts well over 1,000 attempts to punish professors between 2014 and July 2023, with about two-thirds leading to punishment and around 190 firings. He compares this with roughly 100 professors fired during the Red Scare, and says 9% of professors self-censored then against 90% in FIRE's latest faculty survey on some measure.
- **Campus rankings.** FIRE's ranking put Michigan Technological University first and Auburn second, with the University of Chicago at 13 of 248. Harvard scored negative 10.69, rounded to zero. At the bottom five schools, 81% of deplatforming attempts succeeded.
- **Administrators' role.** Lukianoff argues that shout-downs, such as the one against Fifth Circuit Judge Kyle Duncan at Stanford, often involve administrators, and links this to the hyper-bureaucratisation of universities.
- **Rhetorical fortresses.** He describes a "perfect rhetorical fortress" on the left (dismissing people as conservative, white, male and so on) and an "efficient rhetorical fortress" on the right (dismissing liberals, experts, journalists and, for MAGA, anyone not pro-Trump), plus whataboutism and hypocrisy projection.
- **DEI statements and hate speech.** He calls DEI statements a political litmus test, citing Nate Honeycutt's experiment with almost 3,000 professors, and says hate speech codes give governments a weapon. FIRE won its lawsuit against Florida's Stop WOKE Act.
- **Printing press analogy.** Lukianoff compares social media to the printing press: a period of "epistemic anarchy" that top-down bans cannot fix. He also describes his 2007 suicide attempt and recovery through cognitive behavioural therapy.
