## Thesis

Lex Fridman and Mark Zuckerberg record from different states as photorealistic avatars in headsets. Zuckerberg uses the setup to argue that technology can now convey a sense of physical presence. He sees the "real world" becoming a mix of physical objects and holograms, with many specialized AIs rather than one superintelligence. Fridman, visibly moved, argues the realism crosses the uncanny valley.

## Key points

- **Codec Avatars.** Zuckerberg explains that the avatars come from detailed scans compressed into a model of each face. The headset sends only an encoded expression, which uses less bandwidth than video. Scans now take hours, as Fridman's did in Pittsburgh. The goal is a 2 to 3 minute phone scan, rolled out gradually over the next few years.
- **Quest 3.** He calls it the first mainstream mixed reality device. It costs $500, against $1,500 for last year's Quest Pro. It has passthrough cameras with several times the resolution, a custom Qualcomm chip stronger than the Pro's, and is 40% thinner.
- **Holograms everywhere.** Zuckerberg predicts that by about the end of the decade, rooms could hold as many holograms as physical objects. Remote colleagues and AI agents, such as "Bob, the AI" engineer, would join meetings as embodied avatars.
- **Many AIs, not one.** Meta is launching a plain Meta AI assistant, AIs for businesses, and character AIs, including Snoop Dogg as a dungeon master and personas of Jane Austen and Marcus Aurelius. AIs that represent real creators are harder and are expected next year. AI Studio would later let anyone build one.
- **Llama 2 and open source.** Zuckerberg says the value of open-sourcing Llama 2 is significantly greater than the risks, and downloads beat his expectations. He hopes to open-source the next model after a similar red-teaming process, but has no timeline.
- **Norms and harms.** On talking to deceased loved ones, he points to Meta's existing practice of memorializing accounts. He argues digital rules should follow actual harm, while noting that anonymity weakens the incentive to behave well.
