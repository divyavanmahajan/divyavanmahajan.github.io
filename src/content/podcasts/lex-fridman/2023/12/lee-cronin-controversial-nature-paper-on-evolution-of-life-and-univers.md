## Thesis

Lee Cronin, a chemist at the University of Glasgow, tells Lex Fridman that his Nature paper with Sara Walker gives a first quantitative handle on selection: an object's assembly index (the minimum steps to build it) combined with its copy number shows whether a process like evolution made it. He extends this into contested claims that time is fundamental, that the future cannot be predicted from initial conditions, and that today's AI mines the past rather than generating real novelty.

## Key points

- **Assembly index and copies.** Cronin argues a complex object alone could be random; finding many identical copies of a high-index object signals a "factory". He says mass spectrometry, infrared and NMR can measure the index.
- **Life detection test.** In blind tests with NASA samples, he reports biological samples produced molecules with an assembly index above 15 and abiotic ones stayed below. As a guide for Mars, he proposes molecules above 350 molecular weight with more than 15 fragments.
- **Backlash and review.** The paper, published on 4 October, drew 2.3 million engagements on Twitter, he says, and anger from evolutionary biologists. The preprint had 23 equations; he and Walker went through about 160 versions.
- **Time and free will.** Drawing on Nick Gisin and the Hilbert-Brouwer dispute, Cronin argues finite objects cannot store infinitely precise coordinates, so the universe is deterministic looking back but undetermined going forward. Fridman pushes back that the leap from determinism to free will is too large.
- **Sceptical of AI doom.** Cronin puts the chance of AGI doom at zero, calling agency in models a category error, but says fake data and fake users are real risks now. Fridman counters that capable systems could still cause harm through unintended consequences.
- **AI in his lab.** He describes a GPT-style model trained on electron densities that generates molecules to fit a binding site. Trained on up to nine heavy atoms, it produced plausible molecules of 12; he calls them new to his team but not truly novel.
