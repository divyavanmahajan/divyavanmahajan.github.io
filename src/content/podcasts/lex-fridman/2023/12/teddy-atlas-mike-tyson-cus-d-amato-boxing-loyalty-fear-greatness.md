## Thesis

Boxing trainer and ESPN commentator Teddy Atlas tells Lex Fridman that loyalty is what keeps people human, that fear is what makes bravery possible, and that a person is only known once tested. He applies this to his own break with mentor Cus D'Amato, who sided with the young Mike Tyson after Atlas confronted Tyson. Atlas says he has forgiven D'Amato, mainly because he is still trying to forgive himself.

## Key points

- **His father.** Atlas describes his father, a doctor who did house calls until 80 for $3, built two hospitals and admitted homeless alcoholics over the holidays so they would not be alone. He calls him his greatest teacher.
- **The scar.** As a violent teenager, Atlas was cut in a street fight while his best friend hid on a roof. He says it took around 400 stitches and missed his jugular by about a centimetre. D'Amato later spoke for him in court.
- **Finding Tyson.** Atlas built up the Catskill gym before Tyson arrived at 12, weighing 190 pounds. After two rounds against former pro Bobby Stewart, Atlas says, D'Amato predicted Tyson would be his first heavyweight champion.
- **The break.** Fridman summarises that Tyson, at 15, was sexually inappropriate with Atlas's 11-year-old niece, and Atlas held a gun to his head. Atlas says D'Amato feared Tyson, a ward of the state, would be taken away. He says D'Amato then offered him 5% of Tyson's future earnings to leave quietly, and he refused.
- **Greatness.** Atlas calls Tyson sensational but not great. He argues that Tyson faced only five real tests, including Buster Douglas and Evander Holyfield, and did not overcome them. He allows the Michael Spinks fight may have shown greatness.
- **The apology.** Atlas accepted Tyson's public apology during a broadcast, but says he is unsure whether it was genuine or part of 12-step recovery.
