## Thesis

Jeff Atwood, co-founder of Stack Overflow, author of the Coding Horror blog and founder of Discourse, tells Lex Fridman that good communities need clear rules and software they control rather than platforms like Facebook. He argues that strict systems produce better results, that programming is mostly about people, and that a software organisation's health is measured by how fast it can iterate.

## Key points

- **How Stack Overflow was built.** Atwood describes "frankensteining" voting from Digg and Reddit, wiki-style editing from Wikipedia and personal ownership from blogging, with peer recognition through reputation as the main motivator. Its goal was to replace Experts Exchange; the name was chosen by a poll on his blog.
- **Why it became strict.** He says open-ended questions such as favourite programming cartoons were later disallowed because they were not real problems and earned reputation for others' work. Poker and Lego Q&A sites failed because those topics are social, not answerable.
- **Discourse and community ownership.** Atwood argues forum software was still "very very bad" around 2012, that communities form around an interest graph rather than Facebook's relationship graph, and that every community should own its space. Discourse earns money from hosting, not ads, which he says rarely work for forums.
- **Blogging and decisions.** He started Coding Horror in 2004, naming it after an illustration in Steve McConnell's Code Complete. He turned down an offer of roughly $100,000 for the blog in 2007, following his rule of picking the slightly scarier option.
- **Programmers and people.** Atwood says effective programmers eventually stop writing code and work in "the highest level language", English, citing Peopleware and Patrick McKenzie. He suggests programmers can become terse because they work all day with an unforgiving computer.
- **Iteration as heartbeat.** He measures a software company by how quickly a single agreed word change reaches users. Fridman compares this with Tesla's over-the-air updates versus other automakers.
