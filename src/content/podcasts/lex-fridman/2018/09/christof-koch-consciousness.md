## Thesis

Christof Koch, President and Chief Scientific Officer of the Allen Institute for Brain Science, tells Lex Fridman that intelligence is about function while consciousness is about being. In evolved brains the two go together, but Koch argues that in digital computers they come apart: a machine could pass every Turing test and still feel nothing. He holds that only a theory such as integrated information theory (IIT), not behaviour, can settle which systems are conscious.

## Key points

- **Experience without function.** Koch points to vegetative and locked-in patients, dreaming under paralysis, and his own session in a flotation tank in Singapore as cases where experience exists with little or no behaviour or computation.
- **Measuring consciousness in humans.** He describes the "zap and zip" procedure: stimulate the brain with transcranial magnetic stimulation, record the EEG response and measure its complexity. Koch says it separates conscious from unconscious states with full accuracy in clear cases, but it cannot be applied to machines.
- **Simulation is not the real thing.** Koch argues that even a perfect brain simulation, of the kind the Blue Brain Project aims for, would not be conscious, just as simulating a black hole does not bend space-time. A conscious machine would need neuromorphic hardware with the causal power of a brain.
- **Panpsychism and IIT.** He favours a form of panpsychism in which consciousness is how physics feels from the inside, possibly extending to a paramecium or a bee, while things like a spoon are not conscious.
- **Empathy and AI safety.** Koch suggests that a powerful AGI with an empathic response may be in humanity's long-term interest, since empathy is why most people find animal abuse wrong.
- **The claustrum.** His near-term research target is this thin structure under the cortex, connected to every cortical region, which Francis Crick's final paper proposed as a conductor that binds experience. Koch's team is studying its neurons in mice with optogenetics.
