## Thesis

Stephen Kotkin, a historian and Stalin biographer, tells Lex Fridman that Russian rulers from the tsars to Putin keep trying to match a stronger West from a position of weakness, and that each attempt produces personalist rule that worsens Russia's position. He calls this "perpetual geopolitics" and treats the 2022 invasion as a choice, rejecting both the NATO-expansion explanation (which Fridman raises from his earlier Oliver Stone episode) and the idea of eternal Russian imperialism.

## Key points

- **Putin and Stalin.** Kotkin says Putin cannot be compared to Stalin in scale, but works in the same Kremlin buildings and faces the same dilemma of grandeur without capability. He describes Nikolai Patrushev, head of the Security Council, as holding a conspiracy view that the West wants to dismember Russia.
- **Against the NATO argument.** Kotkin cites the UN Charter, the 1975 Helsinki agreement, the 1990 Charter of Paris and the 1997 NATO-Russia Founding Act as Moscow-signed guarantees that countries choose their own alliances. Fridman questions whether his rape analogy fits, while accepting its spirit.
- **Why Putin invaded.** In Kotkin's view, weak Western responses to poisonings, Georgia, Crimea and the Donbas, plus Germany's "change through trade", made Putin think he could get away with it again.
- **Zelenskyy and the war.** Kotkin says Zelenskyy's approval rose from about 25% to over 90% after he refused to leave Kyiv. He notes Russia holds more territory than before February and that Ukraine needs $5-8 billion a month to keep going.
- **Nuclear risk and China.** Kotkin describes Russia's dual-key launch system and argues that tactical fallout would also hit Russia. He says Xi Jinping's support for Putin has destroyed China's strategy of driving a wedge between the US and Europe.
- **Regime survival.** Kotkin sees no evidence of a coup and says the Federal Protective Service (FSO) is the body to watch. He suggests Navalny may still be alive because he could be a card for getting sanctions lifted.

## Quotes
