## Thesis

Sarma Melngailis, founder of the New York raw vegan restaurant Pure Food and Wine and the One Lucky Duck brand, tells Lex Fridman that she was drawn into what she calls a "cult of one" by Anthony Strangis, who isolated, exhausted and frightened her until she handed over money and ended up on the run. She pleaded guilty to stealing over $2 million and served about four months at Rikers, but says she feels responsible rather than guilty, and that the Netflix documentary distorted her motives.

## Key points

- **The plea.** Melngailis says she pleaded guilty on her lawyers' advice because a trial would have been long, stressful and unaffordable, and that many defendants plead guilty for similar reasons.
- **Money went back into the restaurant.** She says she raised roughly $800,000 to $900,000 to reopen the restaurant, that about 90% was spent on it, and that she made two $10,000 sales tax payments before disappearing, which she argues contradicts criminal intent.
- **How the manipulation worked.** She describes weeks of messaging via Twitter and Words with Friends before meeting, a man implying a Navy SEAL or black-ops past and vague promises of repayment and even immortality for her dog Leon. She says he took over her phone and email and kept her overwhelmed, and that her own 2014 journal repeatedly records fear of him.
- **Sociopathy.** Melngailis, citing the book Confessions of a Sociopath, describes sociopaths as hollow, fast at spotting insecurities and unmoved by consequences. Fridman presses her on whether she could be one; she says no.
- **The documentary.** She objects that the film spliced audio to suggest she married Strangis for money, and that the closing phone call, which she says she recorded deliberately to capture his claims, implied a continuing friendly bond.
- **Rikers.** She describes 50-bed dorms, people held because they could not pay bail of as little as $500, and adapting with help from Michael Singer's The Untethered Soul.
