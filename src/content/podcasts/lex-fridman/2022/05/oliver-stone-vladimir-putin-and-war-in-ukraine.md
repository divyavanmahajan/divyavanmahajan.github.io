## Thesis

Oliver Stone, director of Platoon, JFK and a four-part series of interviews with Vladimir Putin, tells Lex Fridman that US hostility to Russia since the Cold War, NATO expansion and what he calls a Western "wall of propaganda" are essential context for the invasion of Ukraine. He repeats his written view that Russia was wrong to invade, but frames the conflict as a proxy war. Fridman, who is half Ukrainian and half Russian, challenges him on Russian propaganda, fear in Russian society and Zelensky's fight for sovereignty.

## Key points

- **Nuclear energy.** Stone describes his near-finished documentary, inspired by Joshua Goldstein's book A Bright Future, arguing that only nuclear power can close the electricity gap to 2050. He contrasts France's reactors with Germany's post-Fukushima return to coal.
- **Interviewing Putin.** Stone says Putin set no restrictions and asked to see nothing before release, and describes him as calm and reasonable. He criticises American interviews for aggressive questions and harsh Russian overdubs; Fridman agrees those interviews were shallow.
- **The invasion.** Stone claims Ukrainian forces were massing near Donbas before February 24 and that the 2014 Maidan events were a coup; these are his assertions. He speculates that Covid isolation or bad intelligence may have affected Putin's judgement, and lists Russia's miscalculations from his March essay.
- **Propaganda on all sides.** Stone criticises bans on RT and censorship in the West. Fridman counters that he hears fear and apathy when speaking to people in Russia, and asks Stone to steelman Zelensky; Stone declines, calling Ukraine's sovereignty "stolen".
- **Nuclear risk.** Stone warns that neoconservative policy and talk of nuclear and chemical weapons raise the danger of escalation, which is why he showed Putin Dr. Strangelove. His advice is that Biden should go and talk to Putin in Moscow.
- **Empathy and life.** Stone says interviewers and dramatists should seek to understand rather than show contempt, even with Hitler. He closes on love as the greatest virtue and the preciousness of life.
