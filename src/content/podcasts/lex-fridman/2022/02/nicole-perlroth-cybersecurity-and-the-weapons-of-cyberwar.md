## Thesis

Nicole Perlroth, a New York Times cybersecurity reporter and author of "This Is How They Tell Me the World Ends", tells Lex Fridman that governments have built an underground market for zero-day exploits that also leaves their own citizens vulnerable. She argues that every future geopolitical conflict will have a cyber element, that US critical infrastructure is a soft target, and that simple defences and more people working on defence would help most.

## Key points

- **The zero-day market.** Perlroth explains that a zero-click iOS exploit could sell to a broker for around two million dollars, and that Android exploits have recently fetched more. She traces the market to the 1980s and 90s, when vendors threatened hackers who reported bugs and government contractors began paying for silence. In Argentina, a veteran hacker told her sellers there would deal with whoever paid most.
- **Ransomware's real costs.** Fridman describes losing data on his QNAP storage to Deadbolt ransomware. Perlroth cites Baltimore refusing a roughly $76,000 ransom and then spending $18 million to rebuild, a Vermont hospital turning away chemotherapy patients, and a classified assessment that the US could have tolerated only two to three more days of the Colonial Pipeline shutdown.
- **Cyberwar and attribution.** She calls NotPetya a Russian attack on Ukraine that also hit Maersk, FedEx and Merck. She says over 80% of US critical infrastructure is privately owned, with no mandate to report breaches, and that unclear attribution weakens nuclear-style deterrence and a "digital Geneva convention".
- **Fix the basics.** Asked for one fix, Perlroth picks multi-factor authentication, noting Colonial Pipeline was breached through an old account without it. She recommends hardware FIDO keys and credits Apple's biometrics with making security easier.
- **Snowden and surveillance.** Perlroth calls Edward Snowden neither hero nor villain: she values the privacy debate but says the reporting lacked context about China and Russia. She describes Chinese surveillance of Uyghurs and the UAE's treatment of critic Ahmed Mansoor as abuses.
- **Advice and hope.** She urges young people to become hackers working on defence, citing 3.5 million unfilled cybersecurity roles worldwide, and says she believes people are basically good.
