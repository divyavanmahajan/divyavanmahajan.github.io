## Thesis

John Abramson, Harvard Medical School faculty member and author of Sickening, tells Lex Fridman that the biggest problem with the pharmaceutical industry is its control over the content and completeness of what doctors believe is the evidence. He argues that without independent "referees" the market optimises that knowledge for shareholder returns rather than health. Fridman frames the episode as a counterpoint to his earlier interview with Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla, and pushes back on censorship as a remedy.

## Key points

- **Culture, not villains.** Abramson says most people in the industry are good, but a culture built around investor returns makes questionable practices feel normal. He cites $38 billion in drug company fines between 1991 and 2017, which he says rarely exceed the profits made.
- **Advertising.** Using Trulicity as an example, Abramson says ads omit that 323 people must be treated to prevent one non-fatal event, and that no trial compared it with lifestyle change. He notes the federally funded Diabetes Prevention Program found intensive lifestyle counselling cut diabetes risk by 58%, against 31% for metformin.
- **Unverified evidence.** Abramson argues peer reviewers and guideline authors rarely see the underlying trial data. He describes Merck leaving three heart attacks out of a Vioxx paper in the New England Journal of Medicine, and calls for pre-publication access to clinical study reports.
- **Vaccine data and access.** Abramson says he is pro-vaccine, but criticises the FDA and Pfizer for resisting a faster release of trial documents, and argues that sending most early doses to wealthy countries allowed new variants to emerge.
- **Regulators.** He says the FDA approved Biogen's Aduhelm after its advisory committee voted 10 against with one abstention, and backs Fridman's proposed ban on staff moving between pharma and regulators.
- **Research priorities.** Abramson says 96% of US clinical research is about drugs and devices, while the US spends 17.7% of GDP on healthcare and ranks 68th in healthy life expectancy. He wants the NIH to fund more prevention research.
