## Thesis

In a short remote interview with Lex Fridman, Noam Chomsky argues that Putin's invasion of Ukraine is an act of aggression comparable to the US invasion of Iraq, but that Western policy focuses only on arming Ukraine and neglects diplomacy, which he sees as an escalation gamble. He extends the argument to China, saying cooperation between the US and China is a precondition for dealing with climate change, pandemics and nuclear risk.

## Key points

- **Putin's motives.** Chomsky says he cannot read Putin's mind but infers from 20-25 years of actions a goal of restoring Russia as a great power, enriching himself, and keeping Ukraine out of NATO. He traces Russian objections to NATO's eastward expansion under Clinton and Bush's 2008 invitation to Ukraine.
- **Blame and support.** Chomsky says any aggressor is to blame and that support for Ukraine's defence is legitimate, but argues the US and Britain discuss only weapons, not ways to end the war. He credits the Pentagon for vetoing a no-fly zone.
- **Nuclear risk.** He argues that pursuing Russia's total defeat assumes Putin will accept it quietly, which he calls a gamble with Ukraine's fate and possibly nuclear war.
- **Propaganda versus lying.** Citing his book Manufacturing Consent with Edward Herman, Chomsky says Western journalists are mostly accurate and courageous, but selection and framing create a propaganda system. He quotes former intelligence official Graham Fuller on unprecedented Russophobia.
- **China and the international order.** Chomsky describes US policy as encircling China through South Korea, Japan and Australia, calls Pelosi's Taiwan visit provocative, and cites former Australian prime minister Paul Keating's view that the China threat is that China exists. He contrasts a UN-based order with a US "rule-based" one.
- **Internal decline.** He points to falling US life expectancy, high health spending, a Republican Party he says has left the normal spectrum, and Supreme Court rulings as the larger threat to the United States.
