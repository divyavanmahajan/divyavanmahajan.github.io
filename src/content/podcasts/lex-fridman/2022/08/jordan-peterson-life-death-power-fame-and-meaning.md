## Thesis

Jordan Peterson tells Lex Fridman that people move forward only on faith in some highest value, and that growth comes from voluntarily facing suffering, death and one's own capacity for evil. He names love as the highest ideal, with truth as its handmaiden. Fridman repeatedly presses him on whether his own anger and resentment distort his public judgements.

## Key points

- **God, beauty and science.** Peterson defines God as "the spirit that you must emulate in order to thrive" and argues that science itself assumes a transcendent reality that corrects our theories. Fridman adds that AI and robotics lack the ethical tradition that medicine has.
- **Fame and criticism.** Peterson says fame has corrupted him "in some regard" and that he keeps critics around him because people stop pushing back on the famous.
- **Terrified tyrants.** Peterson argues that leaders who panic about a threat and use compulsion are not trustworthy guides. He applies this to climate policy, a Deloitte economic report and fertilizer cuts for Dutch and Canadian farmers, saying the poor pay first. Fridman asks whether Peterson's own fear of Marxism drives him the same way; Peterson says he is still working that out.
- **Trudeau and Ukraine.** Asked to steelman Justin Trudeau, Peterson credits his family life and marine protected areas, then criticises his cabinet choices and policies. On Ukraine, Peterson defends listing four causes of the war; Fridman, back from four weeks there, argues the experts miss the human hatred the war is creating.
- **Life on tour.** Peterson describes eating only steak, writing on flights, lecturing without notes to about 4,000 people, and his wife Tammy needing her own hotel room. He credits the diet with clearing his psoriasis and gum disease.
- **Depression and young people.** Peterson separates clinical depression from a "terrible life", recommends small achievable steps, his Self Authoring program, taking on responsibility and doing an entry-level job well.
