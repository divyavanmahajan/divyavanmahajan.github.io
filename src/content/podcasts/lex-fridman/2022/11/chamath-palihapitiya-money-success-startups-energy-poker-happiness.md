## Thesis

Chamath Palihapitiya, CEO of Social Capital and co-host of the All-In Podcast, tells Lex Fridman that life, business, investing and poker all reduce to "your mistakes minus my mistakes", so the way to succeed is to make and learn from many mistakes quickly. He links an abusive, poor childhood to a lifelong sense of being "less than", and argues money only amplifies who people already are. His biggest bet is that the marginal cost of energy and compute is heading to zero.

## Key points

- **Mistakes as learning.** Palihapitiya describes the poker edge as the difference between two players' mistakes, applies it to companies and investing, and says his weekly Facebook meeting celebrated running experiments, even failed ones.
- **Facebook years.** He says he coined the job title "data scientist" to recruit a particle-physics PhD, and calls People You May Know Facebook's first scaled machine learning. He argues lasting companies move down the stack, and that Facebook's real opportunity was an AI reasoning layer, not the metaverse.
- **Energy and compute to zero.** He argues rooftop solar plus batteries will let 100 million US homes compete with about 1,700 utilities, whose grid spending ($2 trillion for power lines over a decade, he says) drives up prices. He predicts the first trillionaire will come from the energy transition.
- **Social media and speech.** He argues toxicity is a psychological problem, and that networks need a social cost through real identity or an economic framework. Asked to steel-man both sides, he argued for and against Twitter's ban of Donald Trump, then said he would not have removed him.
- **War and interest rates.** He argues that interest payments of $455 billion against a $1.582 trillion discretionary budget make a long war hard to finance, and so make a negotiated settlement in Ukraine more likely.
- **Investing and teams.** He cites a 23-to-1 ratio of winners to losers over 11 years and near-zero correlation with top VC firms. He says founders must deal with three hiring mistakes: average but good people, people who hid their character, and high performers with poor ethics.

## Quotes
