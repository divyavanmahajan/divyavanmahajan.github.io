## Thesis

Skye Fitzgerald, a two-time Oscar-nominated documentary filmmaker (Hunger Ward, Lifeboat, 50 Feet from Syria), tells Lex Fridman that much of the world's hunger is the result of leaders using starvation as a weapon, despite international humanitarian law banning it. He sees his films as a way to show the civilian cost of these decisions to Western audiences and push for political change, and he describes how access, trust and a willingness to put the camera down shape his work.

## Key points

- **Starvation as a weapon.** Citing around 811 million hungry people, Fitzgerald argues the law against starvation in war goes unenforced in Yemen, Ethiopia and Ukraine. He holds Saudi crown prince Mohammed bin Salman responsible for the blockade of Yemen and criticises US support for it.
- **Hunger Ward.** The film follows a nurse, Makia, in the north and pediatrician Dr Aida al-Sadiq in the south. Fitzgerald recounts filming a child's death and a starving 10-year-old girl, Omama, giving water to a younger child, which he says left him inspired rather than crushed.
- **Accountability.** In Fitzgerald's view, individual, concrete sanctions, such as barring a leader from US airspace, carry special weight in authoritarian regimes. He says President Biden failed to make bin Salman a pariah after the CIA report on Khashoggi's murder.
- **Small crews and three creations.** He films with just two camera operators and long zoom lenses to avoid interrupting intimate moments, and treats consent as an ongoing relationship. His "three creations" are the pre-shoot vision, re-envisioning in the field, and a third vision after logging every frame, roughly 1,500 lines for Hunger Ward.
- **Lifeboat and intervention.** Off Libya with the NGO Sea Watch, his crew put down cameras to pull asylum seekers from the water. He says this moved him away from Frederick Wiseman's pure fly-on-the-wall ideal.
- **Fear and failure.** Fitzgerald traces his work to freezing as a young man at a fatal car crash, and resolving never again to watch without acting. He advises young people to step away from screens, read and learn to listen.
