## Thesis

Producer Rick Rubin tells Lex Fridman that he comes to every project blank, listens to understand rather than to judge, and treats making art as trial and error with no fixed rules. He describes his role as reducing a record to its essentials and shielding artists from business pressure, and says ideas pass through people like signals picked up by antennas.

## Key points

- **Try every idea.** Rubin says one agreement in his studio is that any suggestion gets demonstrated, because people imagine a described idea differently; about eight times out of ten, an idea he expected to sound bad turns out great.
- **Reduced by.** On his first album Rubin took a "reduced by" credit instead of "produced by". He argues that fewer elements give each one more space, that adding layers can make a record sound smaller, and he describes a "ruthless edit": from 25 recorded songs, pick the five or six you can't live without, then add back only what helps.
- **Johnny Cash's "Hurt".** Rubin sent Cash the Nine Inch Nails song on burned CDs twice before Cash took it up, choosing it purely for the lyrics: regret sounds different from a 70-year-old than from a 20-year-old songwriter.
- **Protecting the art.** Rubin calls keeping business voices away from artists one of his main jobs, saying art does not come on a quarterly schedule. He also warns that success can surround people with flatterers. Fridman compares this with administration pressure at MIT.
- **No performance.** For his Paul McCartney series, Rubin had the room blacked out, crew dressed in black and no shoes worn, so the conversation felt private. He tells of a Red Hot Chili Peppers member asking an engineer not to praise a solo, because praise breaks concentration.
- **Depression and health.** Rubin describes a depression triggered by a work comment, months of alternative therapies, and finally an antidepressant that worked. Separately, he went from about 318 pounds to running after reading about ultrarunner Stu Mittleman and contacting Phil Maffetone.
