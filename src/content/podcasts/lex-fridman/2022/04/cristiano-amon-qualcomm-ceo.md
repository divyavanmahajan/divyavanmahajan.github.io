## Thesis

Cristiano Amon, who joined Qualcomm as an engineer more than 26 years ago and is nearing his first year as CEO, tells Lex Fridman that 5G was designed for a society fully connected to the cloud, and that connectivity and on-device AI will grow side by side. He wants Qualcomm known not only for wireless but as the company bringing intelligence to the "connected intelligent edge": phones, PCs, cars, robots and industrial devices.

## Key points

- **What 5G is for.** Amon frames each generation by its problem: 2G gave everyone a phone, 3G added the internet, 4G broadband, and 5G aims at congestion, billions of connected things and guaranteed mission-critical links. He compares it to electricity. On safety, he says transmit power falls as networks densify and points to FCC testing.
- **Spectrum and coverage.** Sub-6 GHz 5G rolled out quickly on existing towers, while millimetre wave needs dense, Wi-Fi-like networks; Amon names site permits as the main obstacle and Verizon as a US leader.
- **Snapdragon and Android.** Amon describes Snapdragon as one chip combining modem, Wi-Fi, CPU, GPU, NPU and multimedia under tight thermal and battery limits. Qualcomm's mobile strategy is premium Android: about 75% of Samsung's Galaxy S22 uses Qualcomm, and Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo gained share as Huawei shrank under sanctions.
- **Beyond phones.** He cites a Lenovo ThinkPad on Snapdragon with 5G and 28 hours of battery life, a "Snapdragon digital chassis" for car makers, two Snapdragons in Amazon Astro, and the Mars Ingenuity helicopter.
- **Chip shortage.** Amon calls the pandemic an aggravation, not the cause; the root is long-term digital growth, with new car models carrying about 10x the chips of the prior one. He welcomes the US (about $52 billion) and European (about 43) chips acts.
- **Licensing and leadership.** He says the standard-essential-patent licensing model has been validated in every jurisdiction and the Apple dispute is behind them. His advice: make plans, learn where your competence ends, and surround yourself with people who are good at what you are not.
