## Thesis

Jonathan Haidt, an NYU social psychologist, responds to Lex Fridman's earlier interview with Mark Zuckerberg. He argues that the problem is not the internet or smartphones but a specific business model, in which users create viral content and are themselves the product sold to advertisers, and that it harms pre-teen girls and democratic institutions. Fridman agrees on much of the data but argues platforms could be built to maximise long-term well-being, and defends Elon Musk's authenticity.

## Key points

- **Teen mental health data.** Haidt says rates of teen depression, anxiety and self-harm were flat in the early 2000s, then rose 50 to 150 percent from around 2010 to 2013, hitting pre-teen girls hardest. He disputes Zuckerberg's claim that Instagram is generally positive for teens.
- **The "potato studies".** Haidt argues the widely cited tiny correlation covered all digital media; for girls and social media he puts it at about 0.15 to 0.2, against 0.09 for childhood lead exposure and adult IQ.
- **Raise the age.** He proposes keeping kids off these platforms until 16, with enforcement, and backs free play and phone-free schools. He frames this as a social dilemma that needs regulation, citing the Platform Accountability and Transparency Act and the UK's age-appropriate design code.
- **After Babel.** Using the Tower of Babel from his Atlantic article, Haidt argues that from around 2014 a small number of users could use social media to intimidate others, making universities, journalism and companies "stupid" by suppressing dissent.
- **Architecture over moderation.** Haidt favours user verification, compared to banks' know-your-customer rules, for posting privileges, plus an AI- or crowd-rated aggression score that hides the most hostile users by default.
- **Musk and Twitter.** Haidt criticises Musk's tweet declaring he would vote Republican as fuel on the culture war and a breach of CEO duties; Fridman sees it as authentic and light-hearted. Haidt praises Musk's "authenticate all humans" idea.
