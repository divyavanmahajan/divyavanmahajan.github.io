## Thesis

Lee Cronin, a chemist at the University of Glasgow, tells Lex Fridman that life is a general phenomenon driven by selection, which he sees as a directing force that existed before biology. He proposes assembly theory as a way to detect objects that could only have been made by evolution, and describes his "chemputer", a robot that turns written chemical recipes into executable code. Fridman pushes back on Cronin's claim that time is fundamental.

## Key points

- **Origin of life.** Cronin argues life on Earth appeared quickly from simple chemistry, and calls origin-of-life research a "scam" (tongue-in-cheek) because it chases today's biological molecules such as RNA. He notes that Craig Venter synthesised a genome but still needed an existing cell, and says he wants to go "from sand to cells" in his lab.
- **Aliens.** Cronin thinks the universe is full of life but that alien biology will be very different from ours. His answer to the Fermi paradox is that we cannot recognise or talk to aliens until we understand what life is. He finds the released UFO material poor; Fridman is more open to some sightings being significant.
- **Assembly theory.** An object's assembly index is the minimum number of steps needed to build it while reusing parts. Cronin says mass spectrometry, NMR and infrared can measure it, and that biological samples, including some NASA sent blinded, scored above a threshold of about 15.
- **Time and free will.** Cronin argues time is fundamental and novelty grows over time, and that assembly theory explains why Nietzsche saw "limited freedom" rather than Sartre's radical freedom. Fridman questions why time must be fundamental.
- **Chemputation.** Cronin reduces chemistry to reaction, workup, separation and purification, encoded in a language called χDL. He says his lab has 15 chemputers and made an antiviral in six steps that would take a person about a week. He proposes encrypted, licensed code and on-demand drug manufacture through his company Chemify, and discusses safety controls.
- **Consciousness.** Cronin argues chemical systems have far more states than silicon chips and suggests that any AGI will carry human consciousness in its causal chain.
