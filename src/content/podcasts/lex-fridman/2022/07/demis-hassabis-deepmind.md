## Thesis

Demis Hassabis tells Lex Fridman that DeepMind's mission (step one, solve intelligence; step two, use it to solve everything else) runs through games as a training ground towards general learning systems that speed up science. He presents AlphaFold as the first big proof point, says he would rather build AI as tools than as conscious agents for now, and says the people who build AI shape the values it carries.

## Key points

- **From games to generality.** Hassabis traces his path from junior chess and the ZX Spectrum to designing Theme Park, and argues that Deep Blue lacked the generality and learning he wanted. He describes the line from AlphaGo to AlphaGo Zero, AlphaZero and MuZero as steadily removing human knowledge, and sees Gato as pushing general sequence prediction.
- **AlphaFold.** He says it combined more than 30 component algorithms, trained on only about 150,000 known structures plus self-distillation, and went fully end to end in AlphaFold 2. DeepMind predicted all 20,000 human proteins and open-sourced the system after consulting about 30 bioethicists; he estimates over 500,000 researchers have used it.
- **Virtual cell ambition.** Hassabis hopes for a virtual cell within about ten years, discussed with Paul Nurse of the Crick Institute, which he thinks could cut the roughly ten years from drug target to candidate by an order of magnitude.
- **Fusion and quantum chemistry.** He describes reinforcement learning control of plasma shapes in EPFL's tokamak (a Nature paper) and learned density functionals for simulating electrons, both trained on simulator-generated data.
- **Consciousness and LaMDA.** Hassabis thinks intelligence and consciousness are "double dissociable" and that no current system has any semblance of sentience. He calls the Google engineer's claim about LaMDA a projection, and says large language models are not ready to deploy at scale without better interpretability and guardrails.
- **Power and values.** Asked by Fridman about power corrupting, he points to staying grounded and to ethical colleagues, and says AI should belong to humanity.
