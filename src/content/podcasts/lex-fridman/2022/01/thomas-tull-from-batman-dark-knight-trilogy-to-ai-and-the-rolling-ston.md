## Thesis

Thomas Tull, founder of Legendary Entertainment and the holding company Tulco, tells Lex Fridman that great work in any field needs patient capital, the right people and some constraint. He links his investing approach, his band Ghost Hounds and his part-ownership of the Pittsburgh Steelers to the same habits: intellectual honesty, accountability and a work ethic he traces to a poor childhood in upstate New York.

## Key points

- **Institutional capital for movies.** Tull says he started Legendary in 2004 because film was a $30 billion industry with no institutional capital, partnered with Warner Bros. under Alan Horn, and made five films with Christopher Nolan. He argues each movie is its own small startup and that too much money, like too little, hurts creativity.
- **Streaming and VR.** Tull recalls Hollywood calling Netflix "idiots" for releasing all of House of Cards at once; he says Netflix showed the studios had no moat. He expects immersive, social virtual reality to be the next storytelling medium.
- **Tulco's thesis.** Tulco buys whole companies or majority stakes in large, low-innovation industries and adds a labs team of data scientists and AI engineers. Tull cites Figs (healthcare workwear) and insurance broker Acrisure, led by Greg Williams. He says quarterly public-market pressure makes such reinvention hard for incumbents.
- **Manufacturing and AI.** Through Rebuild, with former Amazon executive Jeff Wilke, Tull backs American high-tech manufacturing for supply-chain self-reliance. He thinks AI is arriving slower than people assume; Fridman argues home AI that forms relationships is an underexplored opportunity.
- **Judging people.** Tull says investing starts with people because plans change; he values candour and warns that frauds like Theranos usually grow from small incremental compromises. He also led a $50 million seed round in Colossal, citing George Church and Ben Lamm.
- **Music, football and mortality.** Tull describes touring with the Rolling Stones, credits Mick Jagger's longevity to relentless work, and praises the Rooney family's stewardship of the Steelers. His advice: be honest with yourself, be accountable, avoid a victim mindset and practise memento mori.
