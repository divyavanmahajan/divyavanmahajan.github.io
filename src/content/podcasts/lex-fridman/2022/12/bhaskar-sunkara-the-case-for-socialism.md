## Thesis

Bhaskar Sunkara, founding editor of Jacobin and president of The Nation, argues that socialism should guarantee life's basic necessities and extend democracy from politics into the workplace. He describes his goal as going beyond Nordic-style social democracy to an economy of worker-controlled firms competing in markets, with a bedrock of civil rights and multi-party elections. Lex Fridman pushes back repeatedly on incentives, meritocracy, the risk of authoritarianism and the tax burden.

## Key points

- **Social democracy as a field goal.** Sunkara says social democracy is like settling for a field goal in the red zone, while socialism would be the touchdown. He argues that capitalists keep the ultimate power to withhold investment, as he says they did against Swedish workers in the late 1960s and 1970s.
- **Market socialism, not central planning.** In his picture, firms are worker cooperatives with elected management, publicly owned banks lend on risk and yield, and weak firms are allowed to fail. Healthcare, transit and natural monopolies would be planned.
- **Soviet and Chinese records.** Sunkara calls forced collectivisation a disaster that killed millions, while saying Stalin's industrialisation did change Russia's development path. He credits Maoist China with improvements in the status of women, primary education and healthcare, but says the Chinese Communist Party today has nothing to do with socialism.
- **Unions and wage bargaining.** He explains Scandinavian pattern bargaining, in which unions peg wages to the middle firm in a sector so that the most productive firm can expand and the least productive must adapt or close.
- **Speech and identity politics.** Sunkara describes himself as close to a free speech absolutist, drawing the line at direct incitement to violence, and criticises racialised rhetoric on parts of the centre left as both wrong and counterproductive.
- **Politicians.** He praises Bernie Sanders' clarity and message discipline, says AOC has not yet adapted her rhetoric to a national audience, and predicts Joe Biden will run in 2024.
