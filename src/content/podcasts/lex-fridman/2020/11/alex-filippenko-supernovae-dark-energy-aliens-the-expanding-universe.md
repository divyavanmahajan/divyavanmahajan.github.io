## Thesis

Alex Filippenko, a Berkeley astronomer who was on both teams behind the 1998 discovery of the accelerating universe, takes Lex Fridman from dark energy to cosmic threats and alien life. His recurring position is that of an evidence-first observer: dark energy is probably vacuum energy but unproven, UFO reports have not met the bar of extraordinary evidence, and the stuff we are made of came from exploding stars.

## Key points

- **Dark energy.** Filippenko expects the universe to expand forever because he thinks dark energy is most likely zero-point vacuum energy. Quintessence-type fields have not been ruled out, and dark energy and dark matter could even prove to be modern epicycles.
- **Cosmic threats.** He ranks human-made risks above celestial ones. Asteroids found 50 to 100 years ahead can be deflected; comets from the Oort cloud give months of warning; a giant solar flare could knock out transformers across North America unless power companies get a warning in time.
- **Travel and aliens.** At Earth's escape speed, reaching Sirius would take about a quarter of a million years, so he expects self-repairing machines rather than humans to travel between stars. He thinks humans are probably the only intelligent species in the Milky Way, citing the Fermi paradox, and calls Elon Musk's timeline for a Mars colony far too optimistic.
- **Standardizable candles.** His 1991 spectra of the unusual supernovae 1991bg and 1991T showed that Type Ia supernovae differ from one another. Mark Phillips' 1993 brightness-decline correlation made them calibratable. The two teams had 16 and 40 supernovae.
- **Nobel credit.** Filippenko says the three-person limit does not fit team science. He was second author on Adam Riess's paper and recounts Nobel committee members telling him that when four or more people are equally deserving, they wait or give no prize. He cites Jocelyn Bell and Vera Rubin as examples.
- **Feynman and meaning.** He took two courses from Richard Feynman, who taught that if you cannot explain something simply you do not understand it. He describes the origin of the elements in stars as perhaps the greatest story ever told.
