## Thesis

Charles Isbell, Dean of the College of Computing at Georgia Tech, and Michael Littman of Brown University join Lex Fridman together. Isbell argues that machine learning is more than computational statistics: the data and the questions it actually answers matter more than the algorithm. Both say good teaching means productive, hopeful struggle rather than suffering. Isbell also argues that students pay for the college experience as much as for the classes, which shapes how online education will change universities.

## Key points

- **Data over algorithms.** In Isbell's machine learning course, students may copy the code for algorithms such as SVMs, boosting and neural networks. They are graded on picking two "interesting" data sets and analysing the differences between algorithms. Isbell says students like this approach only later.
- **Struggle versus suffering.** Fridman argues that education is supposed to be hard. Littman separates hopeful struggle from hopeless struggle that breaks people's will. Isbell adds that learners must be able to see a way through.
- **Bell Labs and Siri.** Isbell calls Bell Labs, later AT&T Labs, the best job he has had, funded by what was in effect a tax on phone bills. After the layoffs, Littman says, their boss's boss Ron Brachman went to DARPA and started the CALO program, from which Siri grew.
- **Online master's degree.** Isbell says Georgia Tech's online master's reaches about 11,000 students, accepts about 60% of applicants and costs roughly $6,600-7,000, against $46,000 on campus. He says 80 to 100 graduates come back as TAs.
- **What AI to fear.** Isbell says Westworld-style stories miss the real risk: data-driven tools that make bad decisions more efficient. Fridman points to HAL 9000 and over-reliance on assistants.
- **Learning to program.** Isbell advises starting small and calls reading variables, writing variables and conditional branching the hard core of programming. He suggests Python; Littman jokes that Lisp is the only real language.
