## Thesis

Michael I. Jordan, a Berkeley professor of machine learning and statistics, tells Lex Fridman that what is happening now is not AI in John McCarthy's sense but the birth of a new engineering field, comparable to chemical or electrical engineering, built on statistics and computation. Its real job is making large collections of decisions under uncertainty and creating markets that connect producers and consumers. Fridman is more optimistic than Jordan about recommender systems and companies such as Facebook.

## Key points

- **AI as engineering, not intelligence.** Jordan says we have "no clue" how the brain computes, calls brain-computer interfaces such as Neuralink unscientific at this point, and says he disagrees with Elon Musk about timelines. He calls "AI" a bad name, coined because McCarthy wanted to differ from Norbert Wiener's cybernetics.
- **Prediction is not enough.** His main difference with Yann LeCun, Jordan says, is emphasis: prediction matters, but consequential decisions need error bars, risk, causal "what if" questions and awareness of other people's choices. He estimates about half the people at Amazon work on decision making.
- **Markets, not ads.** Jordan describes a missing music market where young creators could see where their songs are streamed and book shows. He cites United Masters, on whose board he sits, with 100,000 artists. He blames advertising and click-through-rate business models at Google and Facebook for many problems, including fake news, while saying some advertising is a useful signal.
- **Privacy and control.** Jordan finds it creepy for Facebook to infer his plans, says Microsoft under Satya Nadella is pivoting towards trust, and wants users to control what is known about them and when they are recommended to.
- **Optimization and statistics.** He explains why stochasticity helps gradient methods, praises Nesterov acceleration (one over k squared versus one over k for gradient descent), and frames Bayesian and frequentist thinking as averaging over different arguments of a loss function, with empirical Bayes and false discovery rate as bridges.
- **Markets as intelligence.** Jordan argues that markets that feed cities are robust and adaptive and form a kind of intelligence that the study of human minds would never reveal.
