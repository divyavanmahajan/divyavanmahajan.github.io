## Thesis

Michael Malice, author of Dear Reader and The New Right, makes the case to Lex Fridman for anarchism: political authority is illegitimate, decentralised voluntary relationships are safer than a state monopoly, and mocking the powerful is the most effective way to strip them of authority. Fridman repeatedly plays "angel's advocate", arguing that trolling deepens division and that most people can think deeply if approached with love. The two end largely in respectful disagreement.

## Key points

- **Mockery versus love.** Malice says social control is enforced through social pressure, so ridicule disarms it; he sides with "Batman" over "Superman" and says he wants divisiveness. Fridman worries mockery escalates conflict and hurts the chance of a cohesive society.
- **The case against the state.** Malice argues that no one signed the social contract, citing Lysander Spooner, and that geographic representation is outdated. He points to language as an example of order emerging without central control, and says a sociopath running Macy's is far less dangerous than one running the US government.
- **Lockdowns and consequences.** Malice says the New York lockdowns under Cuomo and de Blasio destroyed immigrant small businesses and isolated young people, and that politicians face no consequences for such decisions.
- **Elections and Trump.** Fridman says his one fear is a leader delaying elections; Malice answers that Trump declined to send troops into Portland without the mayor's request, which he reads as a sign of restraint.
- **Libertarian ideas going mainstream.** Malice says anti-war views, drug legalisation and scepticism of civil asset forfeiture began as libertarian positions. He argues third parties make little sense when outsiders like Trump and Bernie Sanders can take over existing parties.
- **Cancel culture and academia.** Malice calls cancel culture a form of Maoism and the universities "the real villains". Fridman defends MIT's science and engineering faculty as curious researchers, and raises the Richard Stallman case as a worrying example.
