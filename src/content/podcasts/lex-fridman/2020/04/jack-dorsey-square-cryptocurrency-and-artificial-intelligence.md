## Thesis

Jack Dorsey, CEO of Twitter and Square, tells Lex Fridman that Square's core idea is "access": letting people into the economy by trusting first and verifying with data, rather than screening them out. He argues that a native internet currency such as Bitcoin would remove much of the friction that holds back payments and entrepreneurship, and that the main AI risks are black-box decisions and data concentrated in a few large companies. The recording predates the pandemic and focuses on Square rather than Twitter.

## Key points

- **Risk modelling instead of credit checks.** Dorsey says early merchants were blocked by FICO-based checks; Square built data-science risk models that watch transactions, raising approval from roughly 30-40% of applicants to 99%. It earned the trust of its bank and Visa/MasterCard by scaling in tiers from 500 to 50,000 users.
- **Bitcoin as the internet's currency.** Dorsey calls the Bitcoin white paper one of the most important computer science works of recent decades and says a shared currency would let Square launch in every market without per-country banking partnerships. He cites an Ethiopian ride-hailing founder held back by payment problems.
- **Explainability.** Dorsey sees AI that cannot explain its decisions, in lending, content recommendation, driving and health, as one of the biggest risks. He also expects deepfake-style fake identities to outpace detection, which matters because payments come down to identity.
- **Self-awareness and recommenders.** Drawing on Yuval Noah Harari's 21 Lessons for the 21st Century, Dorsey worries that people hand self-awareness to algorithms and suggests meditation as a way to notice that.
- **Automation and UBI.** Dorsey says Andrew Yang was right about job displacement, pointing to cashiers being replaced by phone ordering, kiosks and Amazon Go, and backs a $1,000-a-month floor. He wants regulation focused on who owns and can use data.
- **Personal habits.** Dorsey eats one meal a day, inspired by Wim Hof, and links it to childhood speech therapy and joining a speech club: deliberately seeking discomfort. He thinks about death daily as a way to prioritise.
