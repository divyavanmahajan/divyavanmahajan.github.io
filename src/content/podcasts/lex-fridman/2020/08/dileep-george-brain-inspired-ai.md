## Thesis

Dileep George, co-founder of Vicarious and earlier of Numenta, tells Lex Fridman that you cannot build a brain without a theory of how it works. His approach is to turn neuroscience findings into functional computational models, test them on real tasks and feed the results back to biology. He argues that perception, cognition and language must be designed together, and that large text-only models lack the world model needed for real understanding.

## Key points

- **Theory before simulation.** George says the Blue Brain project's bottom-up neuron simulation cannot be debugged without a theory, comparing it to modelling one transistor well and hoping to get a working microprocessor.
- **Feedback and inference.** He notes the visual cortex has more feedback than feed-forward connections, and describes perception as inference: the brain projects its world model onto the input and settles on the best explanation, as with the Kanizsa triangle illusion.
- **Recursive Cortical Network.** George's RCN, a non-differentiable graphical model published in Science, adds lateral connections and generative feedback to a convolutional-style hierarchy. It broke text CAPTCHAs and reached about 95% accuracy on MNIST with tens to hundreds of examples. He says Science's press office still described it as a "new deep learning model".
- **Language as simulation control.** With the "Sally pounded a nail on the ceiling" example, George argues that answers come from simulating the scene in the visual and motor systems. He says he disagrees with Chomsky "completely".
- **GPT-3's limits.** George calls the 175-billion-parameter model intriguing but says it models the "text world", not the world, and that scaling alone will not deliver AGI. Fridman plays devil's advocate on how far large networks might surprise us.
- **Memory, BCI and the Wright brothers.** George describes episodic memory as a hippocampal index into the cortex's learned model, calls Neuralink's intermediate steps reasonable, and notes the Wright brothers learned from watching birds that control, not propulsion, was the problem to solve.
