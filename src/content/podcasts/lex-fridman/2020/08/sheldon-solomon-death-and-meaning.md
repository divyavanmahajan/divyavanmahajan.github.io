## Thesis

Sheldon Solomon, co-developer of Terror Management Theory and co-author of The Worm at the Core, tells Lex Fridman that, following Ernest Becker, the uniquely human awareness of death is the main motive behind almost everything people do. Culture, self-esteem and belief systems serve to manage that terror. He argues that repressed death anxiety produces the worst human behaviour, while consciously coming to terms with mortality, as Heidegger describes it, can lead to resolve, care for others and joy.

## Key points

- **Experiments.** Solomon describes simple studies: asking people to write about dying, stopping them near a funeral home, or flashing "death" for 28 milliseconds. He reports that mortality reminders raised support for George W. Bush over John Kerry before the 2004 election, and change what people buy, eat and whom they sit near.
- **Becker and Heidegger.** Becker, a Pulitzer winner for The Denial of Death, ended up near Kierkegaard's leap into religious faith. Solomon now prefers Heidegger's secular route: accept that death can come at any moment, take responsibility for your choices, and turn towards "anticipatory resoluteness".
- **Politics and economics.** Solomon argues that both left and right rest on false views of human nature, criticises John Locke's premise of autonomous individuals, and cites studies showing that 14-month-old babies reciprocate. He says he disagrees with Jordan Peterson on Christianity and free markets. Fridman pushes back that Marxism should be discussed seriously rather than used as a slur.
- **Economic pain and demagogues.** Fridman worries about anger after the COVID economic shock. Solomon cites Hannah Arendt and John Gray on populists who redirect existing fears towards scapegoats.
- **AI and mortality.** Fridman argues that a sense of mortality may be needed for AI that humans can love, and for autonomous vehicles dealing with pedestrians. Solomon mentions a screenplay about an AI that learns it is mortal.
- **Academia and advice.** Solomon says Skidmore College gave him tenure while his first terror management paper was rejected for eight years. He advises young people not to define themselves by a role that may disappear.
