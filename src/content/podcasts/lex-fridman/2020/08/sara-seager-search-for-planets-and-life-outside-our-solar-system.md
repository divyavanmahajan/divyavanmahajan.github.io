## Thesis

Sara Seager, a planetary scientist at MIT, tells Lex Fridman that she believes life exists somewhere given the sheer number of stars, but that science will answer the question only through "hints": gases in an exoplanet's atmosphere that are hard to explain without life. She sees that answer coming within one or two decades if the right space telescopes are built. The second half turns to her memoir, The Smallest Lights in the Universe, and how her husband Mike's death made her devote her career to finding another Earth.

## Key points

- **The Seager equation.** With Frank Drake's blessing, Seager recast the Drake equation for telescope searches: the number of stars a survey such as NASA's TESS can access (about 30,000 red dwarfs), the fraction that are quiet, the fraction with transiting planets, the fraction in the habitable zone, and two unknowns: whether life exists and whether it emits a detectable gas.
- **Biosignature gases.** Oxygen, which makes up about 20% of Earth's atmosphere because of photosynthesis, is the classic indicator. Seager notes many gases are destroyed by ultraviolet light or oxygen, and that water vapour on a small rocky planet implies a liquid water reservoir.
- **What has been found.** The first exoplanet around a sun-like star came in the mid-1990s. The most common type, two to three times Earth's size, has no counterpart in our solar system. Proxima Centauri appears to host an Earth-mass planet in its habitable zone.
- **Starshade.** A roughly 30-metre, flower-shaped screen first conceived in the 1960s would fly in formation with a telescope at tens of thousands of kilometres to block starlight so a planet 10 billion times fainter can be seen. Seager says most "tall pole" engineering problems are nearly solved. She also describes a solar gravitational lens telescope and Breakthrough Starshot's laser-driven "star chips", and welcomes SpaceX's cheaper reusable rockets.
- **Grief and purpose.** Seager describes doctors dismissing Mike's symptoms, and how his illness led her to ask people what they would change with a year to live. She recommends The Giver, the film Picture a Scientist and P.G. Downes' Sleeping Island.
