## Thesis

Ryan Hall, a jiu jitsu black belt who was undefeated in the UFC at the time, tells Lex Fridman that martial arts are a physical way of learning humility, persistence and self-expression. He links that to a wider view: violence sits just beneath civilised life, power corrupts whoever holds it, and people should offer each other grace rather than demand justice or silence views they dislike.

## Key points

- **Nature and power.** Hall cites Ender's Game: if you cannot cause harm, you are forever subject to those who can. He argues that trying to change human nature is futile, and compares wielding power to Gandalf refusing the One Ring.
- **Grace over cancel culture.** Hall says punishing every past mistake discourages people from changing for the better, and that most people want mercy rather than justice. He draws on reading the Bible and the Quran side by side while on The Ultimate Fighter.
- **Jiu jitsu as a language.** Hall compares techniques to vocabulary and principles to grammar. He says students who only collect moves never learn to "speak" in their own style, and that skill is studied as a science but expressed as an art.
- **Coaches and getting started.** Hall advises newcomers to manage their expectations and trust their coach. He separates an instructor, who passes on information, from a coach, who builds a relationship. Fridman recalls a coach who pushed him hard and another who told him "win or lose we always love you."
- **Platforming and free speech.** Asked whether he would talk to Alex Jones, Hall says yes, arguing the cure of silencing people is worse than the disease and that refusing to engage is cowardly. Fridman worries about conspiracy theories, such as those aimed at Bill Gates, spreading through anonymous trolls.
- **Fighting and fear.** Hall describes waiting two years for an opponent before beating BJ Penn, ranks Fedor as the greatest MMA fighter, and says Conor McGregor gets too little credit. Jen Hall, his wife, describes a head injury that left her with headaches for nearly two years.
