## Thesis

Lee Smolin, co-inventor of loop quantum gravity, tells Lex Fridman that Einstein's revolution is unfinished on two fronts: quantum mechanics is consistent but incomplete, and it has not been joined with general relativity. As a realist, Smolin argues that the way forward is to find the right principles first, treating causality and time as fundamental and space as emergent, and for the field's competing communities to stop fighting each other.

## Key points

- **Science as an ethical community.** Influenced by Paul Feyerabend, Smolin says there is no single scientific method. Science works instead as a community bound by rules such as not lying, reporting all results and constant checking; a PhD certifies that you can defend a case against your colleagues.
- **Realism and the measurement problem.** Smolin believes in a real world independent of observers. He says quantum mechanics has two contradictory ways of evolving a system, with and without measurement, and that it needs extra degrees of freedom to become complete.
- **Time is real, space emerges.** In work with Marina Cortês, and earlier with Roberto Mangabeira Unger, Smolin treats events and their causal relations as fundamental. Time is the continual creation of new events, the future does not exist, and three-dimensional space emerges in sufficiently complex universes. He notes that experiments testing John Bell's inequality show that one form of locality is false.
- **Many worlds.** Smolin says the Everett interpretation struggles to explain where probabilities come from. He describes the Oxford decision-theory approach (Simon Saunders, David Wallace) as still unsettled, and says he has not studied Sean Carroll's own approach closely.
- **The Trouble with Physics.** Smolin says Andrew Strominger's finding of countless string solutions with torsion worried him and led to his idea of cosmological natural selection. He says his 2006 diagnosis of string theory has largely held up. His regret is that the same critique also applies to other communities, including his own.
- **A hopeful sign.** Smolin sees younger researchers organising conferences that bring loops, strings, causal sets and causal dynamical triangulations together.
