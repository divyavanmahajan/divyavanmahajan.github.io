## Thesis

Tyler Cowen, economist at George Mason University and author of The Great Stagnation and Big Business, tells Lex Fridman that America's strength comes from being a country of outsiders and "weirdos", backed by business that mostly innovates better than government. He has become more optimistic about growth since writing about stagnation, but he sees conformity, especially in second-tier universities, as a drag on new ideas.

## Key points

- **Growth is back.** Cowen says he long agreed with Eric Weinstein, Peter Thiel and Robert Gordon that growth had slowed. Over the past two years he has turned optimistic, pointing to green energy, batteries, mRNA vaccines, a malaria vaccine in phase 3 trials and CRISPR, much of it rooted in cheaper computation.
- **The case for big business.** Cowen argues that Amazon, Zoom, delivery services and the NBA performed well during the pandemic. He says business is oriented toward consumers and the biggest firms tend to pay the highest wages, though he notes American capitalism's record on race and Native Americans.
- **Russia, China and UBI.** Cowen says Putin's Russia lacks a consistent spread of economic, social and political power and depends too much on fossil fuels. He expects China to muddle through rather than overtake the US. He supports something like UBI for children but thinks a generous general UBI would make loneliness worse.
- **Crypto and WallStreetBets.** Cowen sees Bitcoin taking over some of gold's role, says he is only "40 60" optimistic about transaction uses such as DeFi and prediction markets, and expects regulation to erode DeFi's advantages. He calls WallStreetBets a new kind of esport, not a macroeconomic shift.
- **Conformity in academia.** Fridman says junior faculty at MIT are pushed to be safe. Cowen calls academia only partly bankrupt and says the worst enforced mediocrity is at schools just below the top dozen. He argues that podcasts are where the humanities now innovate.
- **Mentors, small groups, UFOs.** Cowen advises young people to find mentors and build small peer groups. He also says UFO reports deserve serious attention, though he doubts they are alien.
