## Thesis

Josh Barnett, a catch wrestler and former UFC heavyweight champion, tells Lex Fridman that violence is part of every person and every human interaction, and that the fight or the mat is one of the most honest places a person can be. Drawing on Nietzsche, Heidegger and Jung, he argues for authenticity: knowing your own flaws and temperament and working with them. Fridman offers a more hopeful view, that abundant resources and technology can let people's cooperative side win out.

## Key points

- **Nietzsche and authenticity.** Barnett reads the Übermensch as something to strive towards rather than a state anyone holds for long, tied to Heidegger's idea of being authentic. He believes in some moral universals, rooted in biology and visible across religions, as Joseph Campbell argued.
- **Scale, resources and the state.** Fridman suggests that with enough resources people treat strangers well. Barnett answers that behaviour changes with scale beyond small groups, that a state will always emerge as groups grow, and that anarchism ignores how people really behave.
- **Catch wrestling.** Barnett describes catch as essentially collegiate wrestling plus submissions, credits Karl Gotch and Billy Robinson with keeping it alive, and says it is more honest about pain and aggression than jiu-jitsu's image as a gentle, purely technical art.
- **Finding your state.** Citing a Japanese boxer who used hypnosis to cure pre-fight nerves and then lost, Barnett says each fighter must find whatever state works for them, whether fear, anger or calm. He describes combat as the most meaningful moments of his life and says he accepts that he could die in the ring.
- **Greatness and the sport.** He argues that Alistair Overeem's career record matches up to Daniel Cormier's and Stipe Miocic's, and says the business side of MMA has grown while the athletic side has become repetitive as fighters learn to game the rules.
- **Advice and losing.** Barnett tells young fighters to compete only out of love, since most will never win a title or make money. On losing as the best teacher, Barnett says he is self-critical after wins and losses alike, and still disputes his decision loss to Nogueira.
