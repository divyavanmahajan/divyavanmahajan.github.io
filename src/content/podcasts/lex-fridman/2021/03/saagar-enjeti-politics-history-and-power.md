## Thesis

Saagar Enjeti, co-host of Rising and the Realignment podcast, tells Lex Fridman that power is best understood through systems and incentives rather than personalities. In his view a president can change the government only with a focused agenda and the right people around him, while today's voting is driven mostly by cultural identity and dislike of the other side. Fridman, who says he has no political affiliation, keeps asking how bolder leaders could break through.

## Key points

- **Trump up close.** Enjeti, who interviewed Donald Trump four times, describes him as living in the moment, with little interest in the history of the Oval Office, and as very aware of when a camera or microphone is on.
- **Personnel is policy.** Enjeti argues the bureaucracy runs on autopilot unless a president is "hyper-intentional". He gives the progressive case against Obama for hiring Clinton-era staff such as Larry Summers, and the nationalist case against Trump for hiring Gary Cohn and Steve Mnuchin.
- **Culture over left and right.** Enjeti says voters now split mainly by whether they hold a four-year degree. He admits he was wrong about why Trump won in 2016, pointing to Trump gaining votes in 2020 despite, in his view, governing largely as a conventional Republican.
- **The popular-policy path.** He argues that clean, popular measures such as $2,000 stimulus checks (which he says polled around 80 percent) and fast vaccination would win broad support. He cites the FDR and LBJ eras, via Robert Caro's books, as proof that government can be remade.
- **Future of media.** Enjeti predicts journalism will look more like the partisan press of the 1800s, with many people famous to small audiences. He says the New York Times will survive as a subscription product rather than a mass outlet.
- **History and exploration.** The two discuss World War I, the Bolshevik revolution and Putin. Enjeti recommends Caro, Ron Chernow's Grant, Shackleton's Incredible Journey, Sapiens and Born to Run.
