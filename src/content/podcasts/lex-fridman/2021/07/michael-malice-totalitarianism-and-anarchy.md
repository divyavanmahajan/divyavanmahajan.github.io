## Thesis

Author Michael Malice, promoting his collection The Anarchist Handbook, argues that much of the evil in totalitarian regimes comes from ordinary, mediocre people enjoying small amounts of power, and that the state differs from a business only in its claimed legitimacy. Lex Fridman, for his 200th episode, pushes back: he sees government as humanity's best attempt so far at large-scale representation and a safety net that can be improved rather than abolished. Both reject cynicism as a worldview.

## Key points

- **Heroes on the left.** Malice names Emma Goldman, Albert Camus and George Orwell as left-wing figures who opposed totalitarian communism, citing Goldman confronting Lenin over free speech and Camus's essay on the guillotine.
- **The banality of power.** Drawing on the Soviet Union, North Korea's three-generation punishment and Romania's Ceausescu, Malice argues that petty officials exploiting the powerless are a more common form of evil than overt killers.
- **Can most people resist?** Malice says many people simply follow orders and lack that defiance; Fridman counters that almost everyone has the capacity for small acts of courage but may not yet have had the chance.
- **State versus market.** Malice, citing Murray Rothbard and Ludwig von Mises's calculation problem, says the state is the only agency that gets its income by force and that markets trend towards more choice. Fridman argues any successful private provider at US scale would come to resemble government.
- **Voting and drugs.** Malice explains why he does not vote, preferring referendums on issues to electing representatives; Fridman agrees issue-based votes would be an improvement. Both criticise the war on drugs, and Malice argues for decriminalising sex work.
- **Personal threads.** The two discuss their Russian-Jewish family histories during the 1941 Nazi invasion, Malice's planned trip to Ukraine and Russia with Chris Williamson, his move from New York to Austin, and his recent interview with Alex Jones.
