## Thesis

Cumrun Vafa, a Harvard theoretical physicist and 2017 Breakthrough Prize winner, walks Lex Fridman through the history of physics as a story of symmetry, beauty and replaced intuitions, ending at string theory. He concedes there is no experimental evidence for strings, but argues that the theory's unplanned links between gravity, particle physics and mathematics amount to strong theoretical evidence. He holds that no current law of physics is exactly correct.

## Key points

- **Math and physics.** Vafa says physics cares about describing reality and distrusts strict deduction, because a derived idea often turns out to be the deeper principle. He cites Maxwell's added term predicting light, Dirac's minus sign predicting the positron, and Galileo's three-brick symmetry argument.
- **What a string is.** Strings are loops of energy with zero thickness, perhaps around 10^-30 cm against 10^-8 cm for an atom; each vibration mode is a particle. Vafa notes the theory was discovered by accident, in Veneziano's formula for hadron collisions, and forced a graviton on physicists who did not want one.
- **Extra dimensions as a feature.** Superstrings need 10 dimensions. Vafa explains how physicists build intuition for them through lower-dimensional analogies, and says work with Strominger traced black hole entropy, as predicted by Hawking, to strings wrapping tiny extra dimensions.
- **Skepticism.** Vafa calls criticism healthy and the lack of experiments a valid worry, but says string theory makes predictions that today's colliders cannot reach. He also suggested, with Brandenberger, a model where winding strings only meet and unwind in three large spatial dimensions.
- **Landscape and swampland.** Most quantum field theories cannot be coupled to gravity; Vafa gives the example of maximally supersymmetric 4D theories, where only gauge groups below rank 23 survive. The weak gravity conjecture, backed by black hole evaporation, places the electron mass between 10^-30 and 1 in Planck units; it is about 10^-20.
- **Credit and Witten.** Vafa says his PhD advisor Edward Witten taught him that interconnected ideas can replace mathematical rigour, which later led him to conjecture mirror symmetry against Yau's doubts. He stresses collective work, acknowledging about 180 collaborators for his prize.
