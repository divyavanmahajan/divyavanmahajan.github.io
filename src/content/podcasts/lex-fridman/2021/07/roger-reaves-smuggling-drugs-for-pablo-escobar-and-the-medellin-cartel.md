## Thesis

Roger Reaves, author of the memoir Smuggler, tells Lex Fridman how a Redondo Beach firefighter became a pilot flying loads for Jorge Ochoa and Pablo Escobar, then spent about 33 years in prisons around the world. Reaves says money was his motive and that it was not worth losing decades with his family. Fridman frames the conversation as a question of who society labels a bad man, and opens with his own criticism of the war on drugs.

## Key points

- **Working for the cartel.** Reaves says Ochoa and Escobar paid $5,000 a kilo to fly 300 to 500 kilos per trip, and describes the cartel as an "insurance company" that replaced lost loads. He calls them honest in business, but says he regretted shaking Escobar's hand after the airliner bombing.
- **Barry Seal.** Reaves says he met Seal on a flight from Honduras and paid him about a million dollars a trip. He claims Seal insisted on paying $50,000 per landing at Mena, Arkansas, and links this to CIA agents and Iran-Contra; Fridman pushes back that a popular conspiracy theory is not evidence.
- **Refusing to testify.** When Seal became an informant and named him, Reaves says he would not testify against his partners and fled to Brazil. He says Seal was later killed in a hit he attributes to the cartel, and calls the film American Made "disgustingly wrong".
- **Shot down and escapes.** Reaves describes being shot down in Mexico and Colombia, torture in a Mexican prison, and escapes including a jump from a Spanish courtroom window and a climb out of a prison in Lübeck, Germany.
- **Prison and drug policy.** Reaves calls US prisons places where basic humanity is gone, contrasts this with self-care units in Australia, and argues drugs should be treated as a health issue, comparing tobacco deaths with overdose figures.
- **Marie and family.** His wife Marie joins to describe meeting him in the 1960s and waiting through his imprisonment, crediting her faith and their letters.
