## Thesis

Konstantin Batygin, a planetary astrophysicist at Caltech, tells Lex Fridman that several independent oddities in the outer solar system are best explained by one hypothesis: Planet Nine, an unseen body of about five Earth masses on a roughly 10,000-year orbit. He places this within a wider argument that our solar system is an unusual product of planet formation, and that theory should aim for compressed understanding rather than ever-larger simulations.

## Key points

- **Evidence for Planet Nine.** Batygin says Kuiper belt objects with orbital periods beyond about 4,000 years point in the same direction and share a tilt of about 20 degrees. He adds that objects tilted by around 90 degrees are another sign, since planet formation alone does not produce them. Proof will come when a telescope images it, and he expects the Vera Rubin Observatory to grow the census of distant objects by a factor of 100.
- **A rare solar system.** Batygin estimates that a system able to host an Earth is perhaps a one percent outcome. Jupiter and Saturn analogues exist around only about 10 percent of Sun-like stars. In his models, Jupiter's early inward-then-outward migration destroyed a set of close-in planets of the kind most other stars have.
- **Simulation versus understanding.** Batygin notes that three gravitating bodies are already chaotic, so full-history simulations give statistical answers. He describes deriving the Schrödinger equation as a model for waves in astrophysical disks.
- **Black hole or planet.** On the idea that Planet Nine is a primordial black hole, Batygin says his calculations give only orbit and mass. He would prefer a planet, as the galaxy's most common planet size has no local example. Fridman mentions Ed Witten's proposal to find it with a fleet of probes.
- **Oumuamua and new missions.** Batygin favours a falsifiable explanation from Darryl Seligman and Greg Laughlin: Oumuamua as a hydrogen iceberg. He welcomes CubeSats and commercial launch but says Starlink interferes with observing, and calls Mars no safe haven.
- **Music and advice.** Batygin says his creativity drops when he stops playing music, and advises young people to follow passion over checkbox CVs.
