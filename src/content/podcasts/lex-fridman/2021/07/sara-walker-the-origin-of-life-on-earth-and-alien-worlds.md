## Thesis

Sara Walker, an astrobiologist and theoretical physicist at Arizona State University and the Santa Fe Institute, tells Lex Fridman that "what is life?" is best answered not with a checklist definition but with new physics of information and causation. In her view, living things are objects that need a long causal history to exist, so life is "the physics of existence" and can be detected by how hard an object is for the universe to make.

## Key points

- **Against the hard RNA world.** Walker accepts that RNA was probably life's first genetic material, but rejects the idea that a single RNA molecule spontaneously arose and started evolving. She adds that lab origin-of-life experiments carry hidden information from the scientists who design them.
- **Laws that change with state.** She argues cellular automata and classical physics share a flawed frame: a fixed law plus an initial condition. In biology, she says, the rules and the state change together, and she treats time, as the ordering of causes, as fundamental.
- **Assembly theory.** With Lee Cronin's lab, her group measures a molecule's "assembly number", the shortest path to build it from parts, using mass spectrometry. Walker says non-biological processes do not produce objects above a threshold, which offers an agnostic biosignature instead of looking for single molecules such as oxygen or phosphine.
- **Life as a planetary phenomenon.** Walker sees life as tied to planetary cycles, so settling Mars means reproducing Earth's conditions there. For the same reason she is sceptical of a "shadow biosphere" (a second, independent origin of life on Earth), though she calls it a hypothesis worth testing.
- **Free will and imagination.** She defines free will as being a causal agent and points to imagination, such as rockets imagined centuries before they were built, as a physical cause.
- **AI and the future.** Walker expects AGI to emerge as a planetary-scale phenomenon that includes humans, and argues that life is driven by creativity rather than only by survival of the fittest.
