## Thesis

Ray Dalio, founder of Bridgewater Associates, tells Lex Fridman that the rise and decline of reserve-currency empires (Dutch, British, American, Chinese) follows a repeating "big cycle" of debt, internal order and external order, which he tracks with 18 objective measures. He says the US is losing ground on most of them and that its main war is with itself, while he describes China, Russia and other systems without imposing his own values.

## Key points

- **How the cycle works.** Dalio says a war settles the world order, as in 1945 when the US, holding 80% of the world's gold and half the world economy, built the Bretton Woods system. Peace and prosperity follow, then wealth gaps, rising debt and lost competitiveness, until internal or external conflict resets the order.
- **US versus China scorecard.** Dalio points to heavy US borrowing and money printing, which forces the Fed to choose between higher rates and debt monetization. He says China has become the largest trading country and is improving faster in education and technology, producing about eight times as many computer engineers.
- **Five kinds of war.** Dalio lists trade, technology, geopolitical, capital and military wars, and says a military war is likely only over Taiwan. He expects a high chance that neither side will accept losing the 2024 US elections and urges a bipartisan cabinet.
- **Different systems.** Dalio describes China's Confucian, hierarchical focus on the common good versus American individualism, citing a 26-fold rise in real per capita income since 1984. He sees internal disorder as democracy's weakness and inflexibility as autocracy's.
- **Bitcoin and gold.** Dalio says Bitcoin has proven itself in about 11 years but prefers gold because it cannot be traced and central banks hold it. With Bitcoin near $1 trillion against roughly $5 trillion of non-jewelry, non-central-bank gold, he thinks about 20% of gold's value seems about right.
- **Principles and advice.** Dalio recommends writing down the principle behind each decision, because events repeat, and offers a five-step loop: goals, problems, diagnosis, design, execution.
