## Thesis

Bryan Johnson, founder of Kernel and previously Braintree, tells Lex Fridman that the human mind is the one major input to society that is still unmeasured. He frames Kernel's headsets as a measurement system rather than a control interface, and argues that cheap, wearable brain data would let people stop relying on their unreliable conscious minds, the same way he lets biomarker data set his diet. Fridman, who wears a Kernel Flow headset at the start, largely agrees but pushes back on over-optimising life.

## Key points

- **Kernel Flow.** Johnson explains that Flow uses light to measure blood oxygenation in the cortex: 52 modules, each with one laser and six sensors, giving over a thousand channels, without the noise of fMRI or the discomfort of EEG.
- **No obvious killer app.** Johnson says he funded the first $53 million himself and spoke to 228 investors for the first round, with one saying yes. His go-to-market is a "Drake equation" bet: put good-enough devices in many hands and let others discover the valuable uses.
- **Invasive versus non-invasive.** Johnson says he and Elon Musk discussed working together before Musk started Neuralink. Kernel began with implants but switched to non-invasive in a "bet the company move"; he calls it too early to know which approach wins and compares Flow, Flux and Neuralink to different screen resolutions.
- **Data privacy.** Johnson argues the internet started wrongly with companies taking data without consent; brain data needs individual control, transparency and the ability to delete.
- **Braintree and Venmo.** After becoming the top door-to-door card-processing salesperson out of 400, Johnson built Braintree around developer-friendly tools, signed merchants such as Uber, Airbnb and GitHub, acquired Venmo to copy PayPal's two-sided model, then sold to PayPal.
- **Firing the conscious mind.** Johnson tracks over 200 biomarkers every 90 days, eats once a day around 8:30 a.m. and ties willpower to deep sleep. Fridman says he values passion and occasional chaos over strict optimisation.
