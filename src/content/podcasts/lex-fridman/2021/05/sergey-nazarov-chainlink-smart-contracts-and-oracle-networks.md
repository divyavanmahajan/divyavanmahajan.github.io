## Thesis

Sergey Nazarov, co-founder of Chainlink, tells Lex Fridman that blockchains alone are secure but isolated: on-chain code cannot see the outside world. He argues that "hybrid smart contracts", which pair on-chain code with decentralized oracle networks that reach consensus on what happened, will let contracts settle automatically on "definitive truth" agreed in advance. In his framing, nearly every use case comes down to solving a trust issue, and mathematically guaranteed agreements will gradually displace the "brand-based, paper guarantee" model of banks and insurers.

## Key points

- **Definitive truth.** Nazarov places it between objective truth and unilateral claims: parties pre-agree that, for example, three weather stations or a threshold of 20-30 nodes reaching consensus settles a contract.
- **DeFi's three properties.** He cites transparency, user control (pointing to the Robinhood trading restrictions) and global yield, claiming roughly 7-8% in DeFi versus 1% or less at a bank. He links opaque private agreements to the 2008 mortgage crisis.
- **Oracle networks as services.** Nazarov compares oracles to the Google Maps, Twilio and Stripe APIs Uber relied on. Chainlink has no blockchain of its own; networks scale from seven nodes and a few data sources to 31 or more as secured value grows, and also provide verifiable randomness and privacy via the Mixicles paper and trusted execution environments.
- **Use cases.** He highlights parametric crop insurance for farmers in emerging markets using a cheap Android phone, insurers using drones to check hard-hat compliance, data marketplaces, and an early contract releasing bitcoin when a site's search ranking improved. Fridman raises AI agents and accountable politicians; Nazarov suggests blockchains could set guard rails on AI.
- **Bitcoin and Ethereum.** Nazarov calls Bitcoin the first true non-governmental fiat money, says he is not Satoshi Nakamoto, and describes Chainlink as complementary to Ethereum and other chains.
- **Advice.** He tells young people to do what they would most regret not doing, including starting a company before others depend on their income, and cites Plato's dialogues and a Vanderbilt biography as influences.
