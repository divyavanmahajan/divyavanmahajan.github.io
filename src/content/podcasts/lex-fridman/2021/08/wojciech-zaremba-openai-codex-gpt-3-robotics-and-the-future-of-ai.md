## Thesis

Wojciech Zaremba, OpenAI co-founder and head of its language and Codex teams, tells Lex Fridman that simple methods (stochastic gradient descent, next-word prediction) become powerful at scale, and that intelligence may be closely tied to compression. He sees Codex as the next step in making computers programmable in natural language, and argues for deploying AI gradually and spreading control of it widely. Much of the conversation also covers consciousness, love as shared reward functions, and meditation.

## Key points

- **Compute, algorithms, data.** Zaremba calls these three multiplicative levers. He says internal OpenAI studies found more of the gains came from compute than from algorithms, but spending cannot keep growing exponentially.
- **How GPT-3 works and fails.** He describes GPT-3 as a large network trained on the internet to predict the next word, with tasks such as translation or role-play set up as text completion. Because it never trains on its own mistakes, longer outputs drift and errors compound.
- **Codex and Copilot.** Codex is an API for models trained on both language and code; GitHub Copilot is the first product built on it. Zaremba compares it to the move from punch cards to assembly, C and Python, and notes that code, unlike prose, can be run and unit-tested automatically.
- **Robotics.** He led the OpenAI team that trained a single robot hand to solve a Rubik's cube using randomised simulations. Today he would build a robotics company on teleoperation data and supervised learning, and he expects video models to supply common sense later.
- **Safety and power.** Zaremba backs iterative deployment to avoid a capability overhang and wants AGI controlled by a broad collective. He defers to Sam Altman on governance, citing Altman's proposal to tax company equity. Fridman raises his worry about national AI races and autonomous weapons.
- **Working habits.** Zaremba takes meetings only on Mondays and Tuesdays, keeps a voice recorder by his bed and a separate phone with no apps, and advises newcomers to implement models from scratch.
