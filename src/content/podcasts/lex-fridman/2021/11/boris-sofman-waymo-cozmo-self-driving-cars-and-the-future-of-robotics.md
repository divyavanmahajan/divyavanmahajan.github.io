## Thesis

Boris Sofman, co-founder and former CEO of Anki and head of trucking at Waymo, tells Lex Fridman that robotics works best when you pick tight constraints and play to your strengths. At Anki that meant a cheap toy robot with a deep personality; at Waymo it means narrowly scoped L4 trucking that expands step by step. He also argues that market and timing often matter more than team or execution.

## Key points

- **Character over hardware.** Sofman says Cozmo has only four degrees of freedom, cost under $50 to build and sold for under $200. Its appeal came from a Pixar-trained animation team and a behaviour engine; doubling eye contact raised play time by about 40%.
- **Why Anki failed.** Near $100 million in annual revenue, about 85% of volume landed in Q4. Sofman describes cash swings, several stretches with days of runway, and a financing round that collapsed at the last moment in 2019.
- **Trucking first steps.** Waymo Via targets L4 Class 8 trucks moving between transfer hubs, with a purpose-built hub in the Dallas area. Sofman cites a shortage of over 80,000 drivers and a $900 billion US industry.
- **Sensors and data.** Trucks carry roughly twice the car's sensors, with pods at the mirror positions to see past the trailer. Sofman favours early fusion of lidar, camera and radar, and says 85% of one behaviour-prediction model's data comes from cars.
- **Evaluation is the hard part.** Waymo simulates about 1,000 miles per real mile. Serious truck incidents occur roughly once per 1.3 million miles, so validation draws on aerospace-style structured testing.
- **Waymo versus Tesla.** Sofman credits Tesla's data as its main strength but calls camera-only perception an added risk. Fridman argues that people may accept some risk from robots that communicate intent; Sofman holds that the safety bar must be beyond reproach.
