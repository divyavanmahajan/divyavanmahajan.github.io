## Thesis

Carl Hart, department chair and professor of psychology at Columbia University and author of "Drug Use for Grown-Ups", tells Lex Fridman that the effects of drugs depend heavily on setting and on the user's state, and that most people who use any drug never become addicted. He argues that addiction is driven mainly by co-occurring psychiatric conditions and social factors, and that drugs people seek should be legally regulated and available to adults. Fridman introduces the ideas as controversial and pushes back on several points.

## Key points

- **Set and setting.** Hart, who says he uses heroin, cocaine and MDMA himself, argues that biology alone does not determine a drug's effects; sleep, diet, trusted company, knowing dose and purity, and low anxiety shape whether an experience is good or bad.
- **Overlooked harms.** Hart names contaminated street drugs and mixing sedatives as the main overdose risks, says alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can kill while cocaine withdrawal is limited, and singles out constipation from regular heroin use and sleep disruption from amphetamines.
- **Addiction as environment.** Hart links addiction to depression, anxiety, immaturity, unrealistic expectations and job loss. When Fridman suggests some drugs pull people in harder, Hart says there is no evidence for that.
- **Legalisation.** Hart would end drug arrests, expunge records, and regulate dose and route of administration (oral or intranasal, no intravenous products) as is done with alcohol, alongside drug education.
- **Who benefits from the war on drugs.** Hart lists law enforcement, media, treatment providers, drug testers, prisons and politicians of both parties. He criticises "The Sopranos", "The Wire" and "Scarface" for portraying drug users as worse than murderers.
- **Treatment and AA.** Hart values Alcoholics Anonymous for social support but says it knows little about drugs, and objects to the DSM's "once an addict, always an addict" framing.
