## Thesis

RZA, the producer behind Wu-Tang Clan, tells Lex Fridman that losing his mother broke his youthful belief that he was a god and taught him both mortality and a new joy in living. He argues that creativity comes from absorbing life and studying those who mastered a craft before you, and that the point of life is to build on what you were given and give back.

## Key points

- **Mortality and mentors.** RZA says his mother's death showed him he could not control life and death. He recalls Quincy Jones telling Ol' Dirty Bastard "when it rains, get wet", and says he asked Quentin Tarantino, whom he calls an "encyclopedia" of film, to mentor him around 2001-2002.
- **Kung fu as a way of thinking.** RZA names The 36th Chamber of Shaolin as his favourite kung fu film and explains how the Hung Gar five-animal styles teach a mindset of adapting instinct, not just technique. He calls Bruce Lee a "minor prophet" for shifting how people think.
- **Hip-hop greats.** RZA praises Biggie's voice, says Tupac could both express love and stir rebellion, and calls Nas a master lyricist already at 15, comparing him to Bobby Fischer.
- **God and Allah.** RZA separates God, which he links to wisdom, strength and beauty, from Allah, whom he sees as the source; he calls the Quran the strongest book he has read.
- **Veganism and AI.** RZA says no animal needs to die for him to live. Fridman asks whether we should give AI systems the capacity to suffer; RZA asks whether there could be an algorithm for emotion between machines.
- **Film and creativity.** From American Gangster, RZA credits Ridley Scott with "multi-vision", tracking many cameras at once. He advises young people to read The Tao of Wu and study the masters.
