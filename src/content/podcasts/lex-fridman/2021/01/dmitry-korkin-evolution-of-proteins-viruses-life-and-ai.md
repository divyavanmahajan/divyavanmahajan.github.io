## Thesis

Dmitry Korkin, a professor of bioinformatics and computational biology at WPI, tells Lex Fridman that proteins are best understood as modular assemblies of domains, and that this modularity runs through evolution, structure and function. He calls DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 one of the top AI achievements for its scientific impact, but argues that "solved" applies mainly to compact proteins, not large multi-domain proteins or protein complexes.

## Key points

- **Domains as building blocks.** Korkin describes an average protein as a string of beads, each bead a domain. Domains get shuffled during evolution but are rarely split, because splitting tends to destroy function.
- **Faster science in 2020.** Korkin says structural work on the coronavirus that took several years after SARS now takes months, helped by advances in cryo-electron microscopy and unprecedented sequencing volumes linked to geography.
- **AlphaFold and CASP.** He explains CASP as the "olympic games" of bioinformatics, where teams predict structures held back by experimentalists. The 2018 AlphaFold was a shock wave because machine learning beat the biophysics-based leaders; AlphaFold 2 widened the gap again. Fridman notes that at the time only a blog post, not a paper, was available.
- **Expert systems never really died.** Korkin recounts how Nobel laureate Joshua Lederberg answered his forum post about Dendral, a 1960s expert system for analysing mass spec data. He argues expert systems evolved into modern methods that build domain knowledge into models.
- **Ranking AI milestones.** Fridman lists AlphaGo, Deep Blue, AlexNet, GPT-3, Boston Dynamics and self-driving cars. Korkin puts AlphaFold in his top three for impact and calls Deep Blue's 1997 win over Kasparov his number one.
- **Risk and regulation.** Asked whether machine learning could be misused to design viruses, Korkin says data is limited, lab work is a separate hurdle, and regulation such as the earlier moratorium on gain-of-function research can follow. He remains more concerned about natural pandemics.
