## Thesis

Max Tegmark, MIT physicist and co-founder of the Future of Life Institute, tells Lex Fridman that AI should be held to a physics standard: systems that affect people's lives should be understood and, where safety-critical, provably correct. He argues that harm from automation so far has come from over-trust and poor alignment rather than malice, and that the same alignment problem applies to corporations, governments and social media algorithms. Fridman is more optimistic that humanity will rally at the last minute; Tegmark says relying on luck is a poor strategy.

## Key points

- **Intelligible intelligence.** Tegmark cites the Boeing 737 MAX and Knight Capital, which he says lost 10 million dollars a minute for 44 minutes, as cases of trusting systems nobody fully understood. His group trains a neural network first, then distils it into something understandable; the AI Feynman project rediscovered all 100 equations it was tested on from the Feynman Lectures.
- **Two routes to AGI.** Tegmark calls simply scaling ever bigger black boxes reckless and puts the chance of extinction at about 50 percent if humanity takes that route. He prefers combining neural networks with symbolic methods and formal verification.
- **Alignment beyond machines.** Using genes, corporations and Teddy Roosevelt's trust-busting as examples, Tegmark argues the key is getting incentives right. He says planes and cars should carry basic "kindergarten ethics", such as refusing to fly into mountains.
- **News and filter bubbles.** Tegmark says engagement-optimising algorithms deepened polarisation, and that omissions matter more than fake news. His ad-free Improve the News aggregator offers left-right, nuance and establishment sliders.
- **Autonomous weapons.** Tegmark calls 2020, with drones in Libya and Nagorno-Karabakh, a turning point. He wants a stigma-backed treaty modelled on Matthew Meselson's bioweapons ban, and recounts Vasili Arkhipov and Stanislav Petrov averting nuclear war.
- **Consciousness and rare life.** Tegmark defines consciousness as the way information feels when processed, wants a scientific consciousness detector, and argues we may be the only technological civilisation in the observable universe.
