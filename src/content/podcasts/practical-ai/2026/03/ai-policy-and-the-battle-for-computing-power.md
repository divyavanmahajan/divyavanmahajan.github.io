## Thesis

Ben Buchanan, a Johns Hopkins professor and former White House special adviser on AI under Biden, tells Chris Benson that AI is the first revolutionary technology in about a century to come from the private sector, which leaves governments short of knowledge and control. Because compute drives progress and democracies control the chip supply chain, he argues they should use export controls to build a lead, and then spend that lead on safety and trust rather than racing China.

## Key points

- **Compute over data.** Buchanan cites OpenAI's January 2020 scaling-laws paper as showing that compute is the usual limiting factor. That turns AI into something physical, chips and power, which governments can control. He calls chipmaking the hardest thing humanity does and says about 97% of advanced chips are made by TSMC in Taiwan, using tools from ASML, the US and Japan.
- **Export controls and the policy shift.** He credits Trump's first administration for pressing the Dutch in 2018 not to sell chipmaking equipment to China. He says Biden's controls went much further, and that today's willingness to sell advanced chips to China is a bigger break from Trump's first term than Biden was.
- **DeepSeek doesn't disprove the thesis.** Buchanan argues DeepSeek's models are trained on smuggled or stockpiled US chips, and that its V3.2 paper admits it is held back by limited compute.
- **Safety enables speed.** Using early railroads, with derailments, no air brakes and no standard track gauge, he argues that safety rules eventually made trains faster too. He contrasts this with J.D. Vance saying he was "not here to talk about AI safety".
- **Norms and cyber.** He cites the Political Declaration on military autonomy (about 58 countries), a unanimous UN resolution co-sponsored by China, and the G7 Hiroshima process. On cyber, Anthropic, which he advises, reported its model found about 500 high-severity open-source vulnerabilities.
- **Three tests for democracies.** Buchanan measures success by invention, which he thinks democracies are winning; adoption, which he thinks is doable; and the "harvest", meaning using AI in line with democratic values on jobs, surveillance and concentration of power.
