## Thesis

In their start-of-year episode, hosts Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson argue that 2025 was the year AI agents moved from hype to real productivity, especially for coding after late-2025 models. They also argue that models are now commoditised, so value in 2026 lies in people who can connect tools, data and models into orchestrated systems with domain expertise.

## Key points

- **Coding crossed a threshold.** Benson says Opus 4.5 and OpenAI's 5.2 now write senior-level code, even in Rust, and describes a carefully built prompt producing in six minutes what he estimates as six weeks of work, after many hundreds of earlier prompts. Whitenack says his board noticed Prediction Guard shipping faster without growing the team.
- **Why pilots fail.** Whitenack cites Gartner figures (11% of organisations with agentic AI in production, 40% of projects expected to fail by 2027) and attributes failures to missing integration expertise and to automating processes that were bad to begin with.
- **Multimodal in, text out.** Whitenack sees businesses feeding video, audio and images into models, but output is still mostly text, structured data or tool calls, with synthesised speech the exception.
- **Reasoning has a cost.** Reasoning models help with dynamic workflows, but the hosts note the latency and expense of reasoning tokens, and Benson says power, not GPUs, is now the constraint, with energy and chips shaping geopolitics.
- **Predictive models and orchestration.** Whitenack argues predictive models keep improving and that an LLM orchestrating SQL, forecasting tools such as Prophet and other non-generative models is where the multiplier lies. Benson links this to Yann LeCun leaving Meta to pursue world models.
- **2026 predictions.** Benson expects an "AI maker era" as cheap chips reach consumers. Whitenack expects continued fragmentation: mapping NIST guidance to Azure, he needed nine services to reach 39% compliance, and he sees winners in vendors that consolidate this complexity.
