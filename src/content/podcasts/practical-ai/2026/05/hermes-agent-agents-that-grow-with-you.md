## Thesis

Jeffrey Quesnelle, co-founder and CTO of Nous Research, argues that the harness matters as much as the model and that the best harness stays out of the model's way. Hermes Agent ships few hard-coded features and instead nudges the model to build its own skills and memory, so it improves with use and with each new model release.

## Key points

- **From research tool to product.** Quesnelle says Hermes Agent began about six months earlier as an internal tool to automate Nous's model-research work (a "recursive self-improvement" bet). Once open-sourced it found strong product-market fit; he says it was first built by Technium, who is not a traditional developer.
- **Brain and body.** His analogy: the model is the brain, the harness the body. He claims a better harness with a worse model can beat a better model with a worse harness, and expects customers to pay for outcomes rather than for either layer.
- **Emergent skills.** Example: on a flight, the agent spent 30-45 minutes working past bot defences to book a Las Vegas restaurant, then saved a skill; the next booking was instant. Internally, a read-only agent connected via MCP to Nous's back end built up enough skills over a month that support staff now use it for account reports and root-cause analysis.
- **What to automate.** Quesnelle frames agents as having infinite patience but little creativity, and warns against one-to-one mappings such as "CEO agent" or "CFO agent". He says models lack human aesthetic judgement, so users should state outcomes and evaluation criteria explicitly, as if explaining to an alien.
- **Open-source landscape.** He traces open models from Llama through Llama 4's costly misstep to a near-frontier open model from China that made open source geopolitical, and sees Nvidia's reported commitment of about $20 billion to Western open models as aligned with its business interest.
- **Human-centric AI.** Having had a baby five days before recording, he worries users could stop thinking critically, and says Nous aims for AI that makes people better each day.
