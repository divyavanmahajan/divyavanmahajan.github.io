## Thesis

Mike Lewis, Chief AI Architect at TiER1 Performance, argues that companies waste energy mandating AI use for everyone. Using an L0-L3 proficiency framework he adopted from a Peter Yang Substack article, he says the measurable value comes from "L2" non-technical builders: people who know the company's work in an uncanny way and can align agents to reproduce it, backed by an L3 who makes their tools durable.

## Key points

- **Against threat framing.** Lewis disagrees with the Ramp approach he read about, where staying L0 could be grounds for dismissal. His research reading suggests threats slow learning, and not every role needs AI; his wife, a nurse, has no use for it at work.
- **Five kinds of L0.** Performative users, the disinterested, the too busy, the job-fearful and the quality-disappointed. Lewis says about six in ten resisters are quality-disappointed and executives should listen to them, citing a marketing employee whose copy was better than the AI's.
- **L1 value takes care of itself.** Everyday users will not give up their tools, but their gains (faster email, reading documentation) are hard to measure, so Lewis does not focus there.
- **Picking L2s.** TiER1 screens L2 candidates first for "company DNA" rather than technical aptitude, and warns that the "AI excited" are often the wrong builders. Lewis wants one L2 per team, with an L3 within arm's reach for scale and governance, which also limits licence spend.
- **Tacit knowledge into process.** Lewis says an aligned agent documents tacit knowledge. His example: at a top-four pharma company, an L2 built a Claude skill in three hours for a document-conversion job budgeted at $4,000,000.
- **Push against the brick wall.** Lewis encourages staff to keep finding creative ways to use their governed, imperfect corporate AI tools, and says faster or cheaper is not always the goal; new kinds of work are.
