## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack argue that schools and universities are lagging behind an AI-saturated world. Education has two jobs: teaching students to use AI, and protecting the foundational learning that AI can short-circuit. Whitenack stresses that growth comes from discomfort and struggle, so the aim is to move that struggle to the right places rather than ban the tools.

## Key points

- **Two sides of the problem.** Whitenack separates teaching people how to use AI at work from protecting skills such as basic maths that a child could outsource to a chatbot before ever learning them. He asks whether skills like spelling still matter if they no longer train useful thinking.
- **Teachers lack authority.** Benson recalls teachers telling him after an earlier episode that classroom AI policy comes "from on high". He proposes publishing a whole year's curriculum on day one so students can learn ahead with AI tutors, with teachers acting as facilitators of personalised learning.
- **AI as an object of critique.** Whitenack describes teachers who have students generate an essay with AI and then critique what is inaccurate or badly framed, so students stop treating the output as a gold standard.
- **Mixed evidence.** Whitenack cites a 2025 field experiment with nearly a thousand high-school maths students in Turkey: a general chatbot helped assisted practice but cut later unassisted exam scores by 17%. A Harvard physics study of about 200 students found larger gains from a carefully structured AI tutor. Purdue now has an AI competency requirement across all undergraduate degrees.
- **Graduates and the job market.** Whitenack says AI use is an expectation at Prediction Guard, but students may associate AI with cheating because of detection tools in their courses. Benson suggests that schools may be measuring the wrong thing.
- **The value of a PhD.** Whitenack says his PhD grew his confidence and problem solving far more than earlier schooling, because novel research forces you to defend ideas under expert scrutiny. Entrepreneurship and creative work build the same comfort with ambiguity.
