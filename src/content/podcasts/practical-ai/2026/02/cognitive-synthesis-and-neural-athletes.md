## Thesis

Deborah Golden, Chief Innovation Officer at Deloitte, tells Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson that adopting AI is mostly about unlearning. Organisations built on deterministic "if-then" systems and playbooks have to adjust to probabilistic systems that keep learning. That shift puts a new kind of cognitive load on people. She argues that leaders should answer it with vulnerability, empathy and deliberate management of attention, not just speed.

## Key points

- **Deterministic habits break AI projects.** Golden says cyber, digital and cloud transformations relied on predictable if-then logic. An AI model built on that playbook is "bound to fail". She warns that speed has become the new adoption metric even though most firms never measured their baseline. The real gains will come from new business models, not from automating existing processes.
- **Vulnerability as an asset.** People feel invisible despite constant connection, and hybrid work has not changed what an office means. Golden argues vulnerability is currently something AI cannot simulate. Empathy then works as a diagnostic tool that exposes the conflicts between legacy systems and new tools, which dashboards miss.
- **Cognitive synthesis and neural athletes.** Responding to Whitenack's account of juggling agents, Golden says work has moved from hours and output to constant judgement between model output, known facts and organisational needs. An hour of prompting can feel like a day of executive judgement. She calls people "neural athletes" who must manage cognitive energy and sometimes pause or discard work.
- **Learning through everyday use.** She suggests building intuition with low-stakes tasks, such as photographing a pantry to get recipes for four people with different needs. Doing so shows how misspellings, prompting detail and bias change answers.
- **Systems, not single models.** Agreeing with Whitenack that successful deployments are distributed systems, Golden argues for a "multi model hive" with continuous orchestration, where one model can flag another's hallucinations. She describes "anti fragility" as expecting about 20% of efforts to fail and learning from it.
- **Train for the edge case.** Drawing on her work raising service dogs for veterans and first responders, she argues that the future depends on designing for edge scenarios and building trust.
