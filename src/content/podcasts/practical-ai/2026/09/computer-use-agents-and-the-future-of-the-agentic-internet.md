## Thesis

Demetrios Brinkmann, now at the Agentic AI Foundation, argues that computer-use agents have become good enough to handle almost any personal web task, so users no longer need to wait for APIs or MCP servers. He expects that consumer experience to put pressure on clunky enterprise software, while he and host Chris Benson debate how agent-driven shopping will reshape payments, marketplaces and the merchant relationship with customers.

## Key points

- **MLOps community joins the Agentic AI Foundation.** Brinkmann says MCP was donated to the Linux Foundation, which formed the Agentic AI Foundation; it now has over 250 member companies, more than a thousand ambassador applications and chapters in over 80 cities. The MLOps community is rebranding under it.
- **Computer use in daily life.** Since OpenAI's 5.6 release, Brinkmann says the Computer-Use skill rarely fails him: it books flights (he still clicks pay), checks in automatically from his inbox, booked a German driver's licence appointment and built a 500-person outreach list from his exported LinkedIn data.
- **Any web form is a candidate.** His rule of thumb: whenever you meet a form online, ask an agent to do the whole task. He concedes B2B use is harder and that company-wide computer use raises serious security risks.
- **Harnesses blur into models.** Brinkmann says he has mostly stopped using Claude Code after the latest Anthropic model, arguing that RL-ing system-prompt behaviour into the model makes it fight third-party harnesses such as Pi or Hermes. He recommends community "bro" and "show me" skills to curb verbose output.
- **Agentic commerce.** Brinkmann asks how retailers like Amazon will cope when agents skip upsells and first-party data collection, and whether labs could become payment processors and marketplaces. Benson expects agent-to-agent negotiation interfaces beyond MCP, with personal agents that learn household preferences.
