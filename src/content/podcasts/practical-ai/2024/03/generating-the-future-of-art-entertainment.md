## Thesis

Anastasis Germanidis, co-founder and CTO of Runway, tells Chris Benson how a company that began in an NYU art and technology programme became a maker of text-to-video models. He argues that generative models accelerate creators' ideas rather than supply them, and that the hard, lasting work is bridging the gap from research demo to controllable tools artists actually use. He sees video models as general world models that will soon be common, which makes closeness to artists the real differentiator.

## Key points

- **Unplanned origins.** Germanidis worked as a machine learning and distributed systems engineer before art school. Runway gives every employee the book "Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned", reflecting a preference for next stepping stones over fixed goals.
- **Fresh eyes on old models.** An early project wrapped an NVIDIA street-layout model built for self-driving research in a drawing tool; artists used it to draw giant pedestrians and street signs falling from the sky.
- **Useful at each stage.** Runway first packaged open-source models, then built an in-house research team and an AI-assisted video editor for VFX artists and editors, long before Gen-1 and Gen-2.
- **Control matters most.** With text-only prompts in Gen-2, users generated tens or hundreds of outputs per result. Germanidis says adding camera and object motion controls, the way a film director thinks, always opened new uses.
- **Running their own race.** He says film studios, streaming companies and ad agencies use Runway, which also runs an AI film festival. An in-house creative team tests models daily with researchers, feedback he says academic and big-lab researchers lack.
- **Advice.** For young technical artists: follow curiosity, tinker, publish open-source projects and build a community rather than planning five years ahead.

## Quotes
