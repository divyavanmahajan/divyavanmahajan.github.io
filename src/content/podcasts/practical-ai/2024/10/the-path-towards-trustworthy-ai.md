## Thesis

Elham Tabassi, Chief AI Advisor at NIST, argues that trust in AI is built the way NIST has always built trust in technology: by defining what to measure, developing ways to measure it, and turning that into voluntary, consensus-based standards. Since risk depends on context, she says organisations should use the AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) selectively and keep monitoring, while the field still lacks reliable ways to test AI systems.

## Key points

- **Seven characteristics.** The AI RMF, released in January 2023 under a congressional mandate, describes trustworthy AI as valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed. Tabassi stresses that no single one is enough.
- **Context decides risk.** Her example: face recognition to unlock a phone, to board a plane and in law enforcement is the same technology with very different risks. She also uses tumour detection in brain imaging to explain each characteristic.
- **Govern, map, measure, manage.** The four functions have no fixed order, but Tabassi recommends starting with govern and map, picking a few of the roughly 70 subcategories, and setting a cadence for reassessment because of data and model drift.
- **Executive order deliverables.** After the October 30, 2023 executive order, NIST met its July 26 deadline, including NIST AI 600-1, a generative AI profile drawing on a public working group of more than 2,000 volunteers. It lists risks that generative AI introduces or worsens, such as confabulation, information integrity and intellectual property.
- **Getting started.** She points to the AI Resource Center (airc.nist.gov), with a filterable playbook and sector profiles such as the Department of Labor's on inclusive hiring and the Department of State's on human rights.
- **Measurement is the gap.** Quoting Lord Kelvin, Tabassi says benchmarks saturate too quickly and calls for better evaluations, then globally interoperable standards for evaluation, assurance and governance.
