## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson share their AI learning goals for 2019 and the reasoning behind them. Their advice: first decide whether you want research or applied work, pick resources that stretch your weaker side (maths or coding), then lock in the learning with a side project you care about and time spent with other practitioners.

## Key points

- **Research or application.** Whitenack says you do not need a PhD to work in AI, and should only do one if you want to do research. For industry research he mentions the OpenAI Fellows Program, the Google AI Residency and an Allen AI Institute pre-doctoral programme.
- **Whitenack's NLP plan.** He plans to work through O'Reilly's NLP with PyTorch book, Udacity's free Deep Learning with PyTorch course, and a more TensorFlow-based Pearson video series so he can work in both frameworks.
- **Benson's picks.** Benson keeps returning to the Deep Learning textbook by Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville as a test of his understanding. He plans to take Amazon's Machine Learning with AWS and Google's Machine Learning Crash Course, and recommends Whitenack's book Machine Learning with Go.
- **Fill your gaps.** Whitenack suggests developers choose resources that build their maths, while scientists choose ones with hands-on coding. Benson says he saw the same split in a former employer's AI team.
- **Side projects.** Whitenack wants to try Facebook's unsupervised machine translation code, possibly with BERT embeddings, on low-resource languages. Benson wants to use computer vision on maps to detect dogfighting operations and to build small robotics projects with his daughter. They point to DrivenData, DataKind and Google Dataset Search for data.
- **Community.** Benson says his Atlanta Deep Learning Meetup has about 2,200 members and often draws 100 people, and the conversations matter more than the talks. Whitenack hopes to attend or submit to a research conference such as NeurIPS or EMNLP.
