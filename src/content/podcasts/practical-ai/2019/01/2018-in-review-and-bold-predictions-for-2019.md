## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson look back on the show's first 25 episodes and argue that 2018 opened people's eyes to how data and AI are used. They expect 2019 to be a year of practical reckoning: more work on trust, bias and interpretability, and a shift from research towards building and running AI products.

## Key points

- **Beyond supervised learning.** Whitenack saw a move to semi-supervised learning, generative models and reinforcement learning, citing the episode with OpenAI's Wojciech Zaremba. Benson notes that GANs went from curiosities to many real use cases.
- **NLP returns.** Whitenack points to BERT (episode 22) as part of a shift from image work to natural language. Benson describes a former employer moving its NLP work from legacy techniques to deep learning during the year.
- **GDPR and Cambridge Analytica.** Both name these as the year's two big shocks. Benson says the congressional hearings showed lawmakers had much to learn; Whitenack says practitioners should not assume users know how their data is used.
- **Trust, bias and regulation.** Whitenack expects government use of facial recognition and biased datasets in hiring and advertising to stay controversial, and GDPR to push interpretability. Benson wants ethics discussed alongside technical solutions, plus more AI-for-good stories.
- **Productisation over research.** Whitenack predicts pressure on data scientists to build scalable, versioned systems with tools such as Pachyderm and Kubeflow. Benson expects inference APIs to become standard in software stacks and to reach developers who are not data scientists.
- **AutoML versus transfer learning.** Benson is a fan of Google's AutoML; Whitenack is sceptical of its widespread use and bets on transfer learning instead. Benson also sees new leadership roles such as chief data officers and chief AI officers, and AI that augments rather than replaces workers.
