## Thesis

Ben Lorica, O'Reilly's chief data scientist, draws on O'Reilly's "AI Adoption in the Enterprise" survey to argue that companies with mature machine learning practices plan to invest far more than those still exploring, so the gap between leaders and laggards may widen. He says lasting adoption depends less on algorithms than on educating the whole organisation and building unglamorous foundations: data integration, governance, lineage, and tooling to manage models as assets.

## Key points

- **Different bottlenecks by maturity.** Lorica reports that exploring companies cite difficulty identifying use cases and company culture, while mature ones cite lack of data and lack of skilled people. Successful early adopters tended to layer machine learning on existing analytics infrastructure and familiar KPIs.
- **Leading edge moves to deep learning and RL.** Lorica says advanced firms are testing deep learning on structured data such as recommenders and forecasting, and some are moving to reinforcement learning, citing Netflix's contextual bandits and Ant Financial using UC Berkeley RISELab's Ray (with Modin and RLlib) in production.
- **Tooling for ML development and governance.** Lorica, an advisor to Databricks, notes MLflow reached over 200 user companies in 10 months. He expects model catalogues, monitoring and persona-specific dashboards to become as necessary as data governance.
- **Roles are splitting.** Lorica describes the machine learning engineer role and a Twitter poll in which over a third of former data scientists now used that title. Benson argues the catch-all "data scientist" is fragmenting into specific roles.
- **Domain knowledge and education.** Lorica says pre-trained models often need tuning for domains such as healthcare, and that front-line workers should help pick automation targets, as with robotic process automation.
- **Risk beyond accuracy.** Lorica lists fairness, privacy, security, safety and explainability, and urges explainability vendors to serve end users, not just model builders. Benson notes the FAA will not currently certify neural networks.
