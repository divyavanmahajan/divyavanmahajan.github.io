## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack review the White House "Executive Order on Maintaining American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence" of 11 February 2019. Both say it names the right areas but adds no new federal funding and few concrete steps, so they doubt it will change much on its own. Benson stresses that his views are personal and do not represent Lockheed Martin.

## Key points

- **Five areas of action.** Whitenack lists them: more federal AI R&D, more access to federal data and computing, standards for safe and trustworthy AI, training an AI workforce, and engaging international allies while protecting the technology from adversaries.
- **Late, and light on detail.** Whitenack notes that Canada, China, France, Australia, Korea and others published national strategies earlier. Benson speculates that Dr. Lynn Parker of the Office of Science and Technology Policy drafted it, and calls it well written by someone with limited authority.
- **Data access.** Whitenack says federal data is often already available but slow and hard to parse, and he doubts the order will fix that quickly.
- **Where is the funding?** The order asks agencies to reallocate existing budgets. Both hosts say agencies already do this. Whitenack contrasts it with China's stated $150 billion AI spend through 2030; Benson wanted a Kennedy-style "moonshot".
- **Talent and immigration.** Whitenack argues visa difficulties push foreign AI PhD graduates out of the US, and Benson agrees the country loses that expertise. Benson recommends Kai-Fu Lee's "AI Superpowers".
- **Defense already moved.** Benson cites DARPA's roughly $2 billion of AI research funding and the new Joint AI Center at $1.7 billion over five years. He says the Department of Defense "far outran" the White House. Resources: Coursera's "AI for Everyone", Intel's ethics toolkit list, data.gov and Chicago's data portal.
