## Thesis

Recorded at EMNLP 2022 in Abu Dhabi, Daniel Whitenack talks with four researchers building NLP for their own language communities. They argue that "massively multilingual" models built for English-like languages work poorly for languages with little data or different structures. Their answer is collaboration between linguists and NLP practitioners, plus the slow work of building data sets themselves.

## Key points

- **Linguists and engineers apart.** Andiswa Bukula, an isiXhosa researcher at SADiLaR, says linguists and NLP practitioners in South Africa work separately. Models trained on her agglutinative language get poor accuracy because they were not designed for its structure.
- **Access to text.** Rooweither Mabuya, an isiZulu researcher, wants isiZulu to become a language of teaching and learning. She has to scan isiZulu novels by hand while Shakespeare is freely online, and she asks researchers to share data rather than keep it to themselves.
- **Sranan Tongo translation.** Just Zwennicker built a Dutch–Sranan Tongo system for his master's thesis after his son asked why he never learned the language. He describes the creole's stigma, then explains his data: the JW300 Bible corpus, about 3,000 sentences scraped from an SIL dictionary, and OCR'd texts he aligned by hand. A pilot web app asks speakers to rate and correct translations.
- **Masakhane.** Bonaventure Dossou describes Masakhane as a grassroots movement building NLP for African languages, by Africans. He argues transfer learning from large pre-trained models does not reliably carry over to low-resource settings.
- **Just start.** Dossou began work on Fon by collecting about 25,000 sentences through Google Forms. He says the work was cited in Google Translate's expansion to more African languages and in Meta's No Language Left Behind. He also promotes Lanfrica, a catalogue that makes African language resources easier to find.
- **Preservation.** Bukula recommends recording speech in undocumented dialects before they disappear, and digitising out-of-print literature.
