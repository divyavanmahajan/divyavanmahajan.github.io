## Thesis

Kate Bradley Chernis, CEO of Lately.ai, tells Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson that her company built AI before realising it. Lately learns a brand's "voice" from its own social analytics and turns long content into many short posts. She argues that human judgement stays central to marketing, and that AI is still at an early stage that needs guidance.

## Key points

- **From radio to Walmart.** Bradley Chernis was a rock DJ who broadcast to 20 million listeners a day on XM Satellite Radio. She says she used ideas from radio about "theater of the mind" in a spreadsheet system for Walmart that delivered 130% ROI year over year for three years. Lately started out as marketing resource management software (Cloud MRM).
- **Accidental AI.** Its most-used feature turned a blog URL into dozens of social posts. A mentor pointed out this was AI and helped secure a 2018 grant with IBM Watson.
- **Long in, short out.** She contrasts Lately with ChatGPT: you give it long text, audio or video, and it produces short posts and clips. She describes Lately as her own engine, with IBM Watson, Google Pegasus, MeaningCloud and ChatGPT as small add-ons. She says it uses only private customer data and a feedback loop from connected social analytics.
- **Click or share.** She says social media has only two goals, getting a click or a share, and that sharing is driven by ego. She claims that repurposing podcast appearances across brand and employee channels gives Lately a 98% sales conversion rate.
- **Be the magnet.** She recommends "thank you marketing": tag other people and send traffic back to them. She says nonprofits face the same click-or-share logic, citing a United Way and Walmart free tax-prep project.
- **Calm down.** She tells listeners not to panic. AI is not sentient, people need prompting skills, and she thinks companies that ban generative AI are repeating earlier bans on Facebook. Coming up for Lately: sentiment controls, cleaner transcripts and paid ads.
