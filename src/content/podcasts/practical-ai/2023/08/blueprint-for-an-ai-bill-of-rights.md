## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson look at how governments are trying to govern AI, from the EU AI Act to the White House's Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights. Benson argues that the principles sound reasonable but are vague and hard to implement. Whitenack expects that practical pressure will come from enterprise compliance frameworks, similar to SOC 2 or HIPAA, rather than from laws.

## Key points

- **SDXL 1.0 is out.** Whitenack covers Stability AI's general release: a 3.5-billion-parameter base model plus a 6.6-billion-parameter refiner, released under an OpenRAIL licence. It runs on consumer GPUs with 8 GB of VRAM and supports LoRA fine-tuning. He also notes the flood of Llama 2 fine-tunes on Hugging Face.
- **Training on GPT output is a legal grey area.** Whitenack raises a puzzle: fine-tuning open models on GPT-4 output seems to break OpenAI's terms, yet machine-generated content is not copyrightable. Benson doubts provenance-based licence terms, such as Meta's restriction on competitors, can be enforced.
- **Open-source pushback on the EU AI Act.** GitHub, Hugging Face, Creative Commons and others signed an open letter arguing that the Act regulates upstream open-source projects as if they were commercial products. Whitenack cites Jeremy Howard's concern about large fines for unlicensed models made available in the EU.
- **The Blueprint is principles without practice.** Whitenack walks through safe and effective systems and algorithmic discrimination protections, and notes that much of it overlaps with existing compliance work. Benson, who worked on AI ethics in earlier roles, says principles are the easy part and implementation is where the difficulty lies.
- **The human fallback is questioned.** Benson objects to the right to opt out and reach a human, arguing that future automation will run at speeds and complexity beyond human oversight. Whitenack sees the risk of automated systems handing users off to other automated systems.
- **Compliance as a business.** Whitenack expects frameworks like the NIST AI risk management framework to turn into vendor audits between enterprises. He recommends Eugene Yan's article "Patterns for Building LLM-based Systems & Products" as a learning resource.
