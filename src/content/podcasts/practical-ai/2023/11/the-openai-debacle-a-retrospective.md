## Thesis

Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson look back at OpenAI's history to explain the week in November 2023 when Sam Altman was fired and then reinstated. They argue the crisis came from two cultures colliding: a nonprofit mission to build safe AGI for humanity, and a startup drive to raise money and ship products quickly. Their practical lesson is that companies need to diversify their models and manage AI risk formally.

## Key points

- **A convoluted structure.** Benson describes OpenAI Inc., the nonprofit founded in December 2015, controlling OpenAI Global LLC, a for-profit with returns capped at 100 times investment. Whitenack notes that board members could not hold a financial stake, so Microsoft, with more than $10 billion invested, had no seat.
- **From robots to ChatGPT.** Whitenack recalls episode 14 with co-founder Wojciech Zaremba, which covered robotic arms and reinforcement learning. He then traces the path through the 2016 DGX-1 gift from NVIDIA, the open GPT-2 release, GPT-3 as a gated API, DALL-E and ChatGPT in late 2022.
- **The timeline.** The hosts walk through the week: Altman fired a day after his Dev Day keynote, Microsoft told "a minute before the world", Greg Brockman quitting, Microsoft's job offer, reports that up to 95% of staff would leave, and Altman's return with a new board chaired by Bret Taylor.
- **Single-supplier risk.** Whitenack, who runs Prediction Guard, says he spent the week answering customers who were reconsidering their dependence on OpenAI. He points to alternatives such as Mistral, MosaicML and Meta's Llama 2.
- **Q* and regulation.** The hosts treat the Q* reports cautiously, citing Yann LeCun's view that it is probably OpenAI's work on planning. Whitenack suggests the episode, together with Meta disbanding its responsible AI team, tells regulators that self-regulation may not hold up.
- **Employee voice and AGI.** Benson sees the staff revolt as a lasting precedent. He also says AGI has become a mainstream worry, noting that Q* came up at his family's Thanksgiving dinner.
