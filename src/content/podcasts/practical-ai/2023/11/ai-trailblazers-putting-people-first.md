## Thesis

Solana Larsen, editor of Mozilla's IRL podcast, walks Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson through season seven, its second season on AI, themed "people over profit". She argues that "AI" covers too many contexts to debate as one thing. She also says openness is only good when it serves a purpose such as transparency, accountability or local communities, and she looks for builders showing that AI can be made differently.

## Key points

- **Open, with nuance.** The first episode included David Evan Harris (formerly of Meta's responsible AI team), Abeba Birhane on dataset audits, Sasha Luccioni of Hugging Face on carbon footprint, and Nomic's GPT4All for offline use. Larsen says Mozilla leans towards open source as "our safety net", because companies have repeatedly shown they cannot be entirely trusted.
- **Invisible data workers.** Larsen describes content moderators in Kenya who were badly paid and treated, and contrasts them with Karya in India. Karya pays voice donors again each time it resells a voice dataset.
- **Data as land.** Keoni Mahelona, working on Māori language voice recognition, helped write an indigenous data sovereignty license. Under it, nobody can use the data without permission. Larsen says such communities face VC funders asking why they don't just use OpenAI.
- **Crash test dummies.** Larsen says city streets have become testing labs for autonomous vehicles. Cruise had its driverless licence pulled while the episode was being made. She asks whether some problems need sidewalks or street lighting rather than AI.
- **Governance as seatbelts.** She points to the White House AI blueprint and to Navrina Singh's Credo AI, which builds dashboards that ask companies how they measure their values and possible harms. Whitenack adds that the Llama 2 paper's language breakdown helped a Hindi-speaking engineer at Prediction Guard understand weak results.
- **Reasons for optimism.** Larsen sees the public, regulators and journalists becoming more literate about AI, and social movements taking up AI issues.
