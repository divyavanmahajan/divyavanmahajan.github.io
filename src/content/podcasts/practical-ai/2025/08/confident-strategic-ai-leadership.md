## Thesis

Allegra Guinan, co-founder and CTO of Lumiera, argues that most missed AI returns come from leadership and communication gaps rather than technology. Leaders do not need to track every model release; they need confidence, clear principles and a focus on their own business challenges, and they must build trust into what they ship, both internally and for customers.

## Key points

- **Leadership over silver bullets.** Guinan describes Lumiera's core product as an eight-week executive education programme built on three foundations, starting with confidence and then action (risk, industry radar and a personal AI vision), instead of telling clients what to build or buy.
- **Noise exhaustion and FOMO.** She says leaders either freeze or make ungrounded, reactive decisions from narrow information sources; it does not matter if 10 new models came out last week if you know which numbers you are trying to move.
- **Top-down mandates backfire.** Guinan cites a report that employees use AI three times more than their leaders think, and recommends open sharing sessions, AI champions and a fail-forward culture. At Cloudflare she ran a largely qualitative pilot comparing AI coding assistants for a specific group of engineers.
- **Trust needs documentation.** She warns that teams have abandoned product thinking, testing and documentation for AI; without system cards, documented guardrails, tracking and system prompt versioning, systems will not pass risk and compliance or keep customer trust.
- **Framing responsible AI.** Guinan says there is no standard definition and many leaders care mainly about the bottom line, so she ties responsible practice to financial results, EU compliance fines and the organisation's existing values such as privacy and access management.
- **Literacy starts in-house.** Responding to Whitenack's story of executives who think an API key means they run their own model, she advises leaders to first understand what is in production and who owns it. Walking through a user-experience wireframe often shows that an "agentic" request is really deterministic automation, which Whitenack says usually turns out to be a RAG chatbot or an automated workflow.
