## Thesis

Mark Daniel Ward and Katie Sanders of The Data Mine at Purdue University argue that data skills now matter in every career, and that students need real-world, team-based project experience well before the traditional junior-year internship. Their model combines interdisciplinary teams, shared student housing and long-term corporate partnerships, and they want other universities to adopt it.

## Key points

- **Data is pervasive.** Ward says careers from aerospace to manufacturing floors are being remade by data, so it is not only for computer scientists. Sanders, who calls herself non-technical, notes that an engineer sees data differently from a data scientist, and the program spans over 160 majors.
- **Growth from a grant.** Ward explains The Data Mine grew out of a grant for 20 sophomores a year; when opened to the whole campus it went from 100 students to 600 and then 800, with no one required to join.
- **Program structure.** Sanders describes a one-credit seminar teaching R, Python, SQL and Bash, plus a three-credit corporate partner program where teams of eight to 12 students meet a company mentor weekly and work in agile labs led by a TA.
- **Living-learning community.** Ward says the sophomore year is a "murky middle" when students question their careers; living and working together in a residence hall, with a second hall being built, helps them through it. Cummins staff drove up from Columbus, Indiana to meet students there.
- **Corporate value.** Sanders reports 88 projects with about 60 companies last year, usually back-burner work rather than mission-critical projects. Ward says relationships have deepened into paid or sponsored research, simplified by five-year agreements; Beck's Hybrids grew from around 300 to 1,000 employees during the partnership.
- **Spreading the model.** Sanders lists Youngstown State University, Data Mine of the Rockies, an Indianapolis site and work with over 60 institutions. Whitenack says Practical AI has submitted its own project for the coming year.
