## Thesis

Jason Beutler, CEO of RoboSource, argues that business is still a people enterprise and that AI adoption is mainly a change-management problem. Bolting AI onto existing steps made his own team less efficient. He says real gains come from rethinking workflows from scratch, then bringing employees along gradually with agents that run plain-English standard operating procedures through tools they already use.

## Key points

- **Fear is about how leaders see you.** Beutler says most people he talks to fear that leadership will stop seeing their work as meaningful. He stresses that relationships and context still drive business.
- **Rethink the workflow.** RoboSource first tried automating each step of its SDLC and became less efficient. Asking whether a product requirements document was needed at all, and going straight from a recorded conversation to an AI project plan, cut work from weeks to days.
- **Play before use cases.** Comparing it to jamming Beatles covers with his son to beat writer's block, Beutler encourages hands-on experimentation. He says most people still treat AI like Google, and that automating an existing task is often something "you could have done that ten years ago."
- **Adoption is shallower than claimed.** Whitenack and Beutler agree that few companies use AI productively. Beutler says CEOs mostly cite marketing content, and clients complain about spending fifteen minutes giving background for every question.
- **Process Coach and "plays".** The product lets AI carry context between the steps and people in an SOP, working through Teams, Slack, email and text. Agent mode comes later, once people trust it. Version two was drag-and-drop and executives handed it to IT, so the third version uses plain English, with A/B testing of processes on the roadmap.
- **Voice next.** Beutler expects business leaders to manage work mostly by talking to an "external team member" on their phone.
