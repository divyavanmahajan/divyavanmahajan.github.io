## Thesis

Igor Nikitin, CEO and co-founder of NICE Technologies, argues that actuarial modeling is still trapped in Excel workbooks and proprietary software that lack basic software-engineering controls. His company's source-available Python calc engine, combined with AI coding assistants, aims to bring version control, testing and automation to insurance pricing. Host Chris Benson explores what that changes for working actuaries.

## Key points

- **Regulation drove modernisation.** Nikitin says programming was only added to actuarial exams in 2018, and that more demanding regulation, such as stochastic runs with thousands of scenarios, forced insurers off Excel more than technology did.
- **AI adoption is cautious.** He sees little AI use so far because models do not reliably give the truth, which compliance requires. The useful areas are summarising data, assisting model development and giving a second opinion on analysis.
- **The game-engine analogy.** Nikitin modeled the calc engine on Unreal Engine and Unity: a shared core that lowers development costs. Clients get the source code, but the public does not. The product launched publicly in February.
- **Replayable pricing.** The data platform came from late nights pricing reinsurance deals, when a new input could mean four or five hours of rework. The system remembers the pricing steps and reprices in about ten to fifteen minutes.
- **Workflow changes.** Modelers use Git merges, unit tests, performance benchmarks and AI assistants that explain or speed up code. Pricing and valuation actuaries need no Python and can bring their own GUI, such as Excel or a web interface.
- **Portable skills.** Nikitin expects common tooling such as Python, Visual Studio and DevOps to replace proprietary systems, so skills transfer between companies and into data science.
