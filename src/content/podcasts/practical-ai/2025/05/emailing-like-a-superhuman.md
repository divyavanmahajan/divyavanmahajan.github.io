## Thesis

Loïc Houssier, head of engineering at Superhuman, argues that LLMs have moved email clients from making users faster to making them smarter. The same shift lowers the barrier to entry and makes quality harder to control. Because so much now depends on how users prompt and what they perceive, he says the edge lies in product taste, user education and fast reprioritisation, not in the model itself.

## Key points

- **Perceived quality.** Houssier says Superhuman once ran on a zero-bug standard. Now output from user-written prompts, such as custom auto labels, can feel low quality even when the engineering is sound. The response is system prompts around user input, prompt guidance and shared prompt libraries.
- **Auto draft.** The feature users talk about most writes ready-to-send replies and follow-ups in the user's tone. He says it is simple LLM use but addictive for a user base of CEOs, executives, sales teams and consultants.
- **Best model first, optimise later.** New features start on the most expensive model so weak feedback isn't caused by the model. Once a feature has traction, the team moves to cheaper or fine-tuned models, such as BERT-type classifiers for labelling. That requires easy model switching, A/B tests and golden-dataset evals.
- **Reprioritise every two weeks.** Citing Geoffrey Moore's Zone to Win, he says disruptive changes like reasoning models and MCP now arrive every few weeks. A product and engineering group of about 40-50 people can change its roadmap quickly.
- **Enterprise buyers have flipped.** Fortune 500 executives now push their security teams to approve AI tools from Series A-C startups, because the cost of missing out outweighs the compliance risk.
- **Humans and the future.** AI meeting notes cut his interview debriefs from 20-30 minutes to three. He treats coding assistants as interns whose work must be reviewed. He expects a unified, focus-oriented inbox across email, Slack, WhatsApp and documents.
