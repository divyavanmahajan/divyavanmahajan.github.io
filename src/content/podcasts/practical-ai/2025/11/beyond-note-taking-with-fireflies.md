## Thesis

Krish Ramineni, co-founder and CEO of Fireflies.ai, argues that off-the-shelf models now get an AI note-taker about 80% of the way, but the last 15-20% (integrations, security, compliance, search) is what builds a lasting business. He also argues that SaaS is not dead and that Fireflies' next step is helping people during meetings, not just after them.

## Key points

- **Validate before coding.** Ramineni says the company started in 2016 as a human-in-the-loop AI secretary for about $100 a month, tested with around 10 friends. He decided it could not scale and built software instead, launching in 2020.
- **LLMs as the turning point.** Early access to GPT-3.5 in 2022 (investor Vinod Khosla also backed OpenAI) transformed the product. Ramineni says revenue then passed seven and then eight figures, and the company has been profitable since 2023 without touching its Series A funding.
- **Cost engineering.** Transcription costs fell about 10x, and Fireflies runs more traffic on its own bare-metal servers than on Google or AWS, which Ramineni says makes a $10-a-month plan viable.
- **The moat is the boring part.** Ramineni lists 95+ integrations, SOC 2 and HIPAA compliance, private storage, audit logs and custom data retention (some finance customers want data wiped every seven days). He cites ElevenLabs as another company that went deep on a "generic" problem.
- **Live Assist.** The new real-time feature offers meeting prep, live suggestions, a "catch me up" button and web answers via a Perplexity partnership, delivered through a desktop app that works without a bot. Ramineni says manual Ask Fred queries rose because suggestions prompt follow-up questions.
- **What comes next.** Ramineni hints at a hardware partnership that could reach 10,000,000 devices next year and at products beyond meetings, built by a company of about 100 people.
