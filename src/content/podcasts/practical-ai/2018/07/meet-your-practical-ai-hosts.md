## Thesis

Changelog's Adam Stacoviak and Jerod Santo interview the new hosts, Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack, about their backgrounds and plans. Both hosts argue that compute, data and tools are now widely available, so AI practice is open to ordinary developers, and the show should be a welcoming community that helps them get started.

## Key points

- **Benson's route in.** Benson's parents were Lockheed engineers on the F-22 programme. After a YF-22 prototype crashed in 1992, his father worked on the problem with neural networks, which Benson heard about at home. He later came back to deep learning as a hobby and moved his career into it.
- **Why then and now.** Benson says his father was unusual in 1992 because he had compute, large sensor data and algorithm expertise together, and that this combination is now available to everyone.
- **Whitenack's route in.** Whitenack did a physics PhD, calculating atomic and molecular properties from first principles, while a Berkeley group used machine learning for the same problem. He then helped lawyers understand maths at an IP firm, became a data scientist at a startup and was working on Pachyderm.
- **Defining AI.** Benson treats AI as a moving label: symbolic AI in his mother's 1980s Georgia Tech studies, mostly deep learning in 2018. He cites NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's line that "AI is the new source code". Whitenack sees AI as filling the gap between data and insights.
- **Planned content.** Whitenack expects application interviews (finding planets, detecting cancer), learning episodes and topics such as AI ethics, infrastructure and AI on phones. Benson adds helping newcomers find resources, and discussion of responsible use and societal impact.
- **Community.** The hosts invite listeners to the Changelog Slack and the GitHub "ping" repo, and Whitenack encourages them to get hands-on and share side projects.
