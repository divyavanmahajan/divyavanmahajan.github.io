## Thesis

Susan Etlinger, an industry analyst at Altimeter, presents her report "The AI Maturity Playbook: Five Pillars of Enterprise Success". She argues that AI changes how organisations interact, decide, innovate and lead, and that getting value from it needs engineering for scale plus practical controls for bias and explainability, not abstract debates about killer robots.

## Key points

- **Four trends.** Etlinger describes shifts from screens to senses, from business rules to probabilities, towards data engineering, and from hierarchical to data-informed leadership. She calls the move from if-then rules to probabilistic decisions the biggest.
- **From data science to engineering.** Whitenack notes job titles shifting to machine learning engineer. Etlinger hears that enterprises struggle to scale models into products; Benson says team members with prior product experience outperformed fresh graduates once deployment mattered.
- **Uncertainty in the interface.** Using Google Translate, Etlinger shows "she is a doctor" round-tripping through gender-neutral Turkish as "he is a doctor", and argues interfaces should signal when output is low-confidence.
- **Controls.** She cites AI Now's algorithmic impact assessment, IBM's supplier declaration of conformity and bias dashboards, and Rachel Bellamy's line that explainability is the new user interface for AI.
- **Bias trade-offs.** Etlinger cites the ACLU finding that Amazon's Rekognition matched John Lewis and other members of Congress to faces in a criminal database, then notes that improving accuracy for people of color needs face data many will not give. She and Whitenack warn that proxies such as zip code can stand in for removed sensitive fields.
- **Regulation and the playbook.** Etlinger supports GDPR's philosophy but says most people experienced it as opt-in emails. Her five pillars cover strategy, data science, product and service development, organisation and culture, and ethics.
