## Thesis

Absci founder and CEO Sean McClain and lead AI scientist Joshua Meier argue that earlier attempts to apply AI in biotech failed because biological data was scarce, messy and low quality. Absci spent about ten years as a synthetic biology company building ways to generate high-throughput lab data, and they say this now lets models design drug candidates rather than finding them by trial and error.

## Key points

- **A slow, costly baseline.** McClain describes traditional drug discovery as taking years and billions of dollars, with only a 4% success rate for drugs entering the clinic.
- **Data is the bottleneck.** Meier, formerly at Facebook AI Research, says progress was limited by data and by the lack of lab testing. Absci's data is molecular, so patient privacy is not an issue, and academic metrics often fail once designs are tested in the lab.
- **An end-to-end pipeline.** Meier outlines the goal of going from patient to drug fully on the computer: identify targets from blood samples, design candidates from scratch, optimise the lead, then manufacture in Absci's own SoluPro cell line.
- **Models as digital twins.** Meier says the models agree with lab measurements almost as closely as different lab techniques agree with each other. In a case study shown at NVIDIA GTC, they made the breast cancer drug trastuzumab bind two orders of magnitude more tightly.
- **Optimising several properties at once.** McClain says firms often drop the best candidates because they are hard to manufacture. Designing for function, manufacturability and "naturalness" together could raise success rates, and could produce COVID antibodies that keep working as the virus evolves.
- **Long-term vision.** McClain predicts success rates could reach 50%, enabling personalised medicine sold like a lifetime subscription. He also says regulators need to be educated now.
