## Thesis

Bonaventure Dossou, a researcher from Benin, argues that AI can improve life in Africa through three routes: healthcare, education and access to language technology. Chris Benson, co-hosting with Natalie Pistunovich, draws out the common thread in his work, drug discovery and NLP for under-resourced languages, and encourages him to own the "AI superhero" label as a role model.

## Key points

- **An unplanned path.** Dossou trained for medicine, then took a scholarship to study mathematics in Russia, taught entirely in Russian. He credits a high-school teacher who focused on his strengths for turning him towards maths.
- **From maths to machine translation.** With his friend Chris, he looked for a research area that linked theory to applications. Translating between French and his mother's native language became the starting project, because few people worked on such languages.
- **Drug discovery at Mila.** Advised by Yoshua Bengio, Dossou works on antimicrobial peptides as a response to antimicrobial resistance. He describes an active-learning framework with a graph-based method to generate candidates that are effective but diverse, and says generative models are needed because discovery means creating things that are similar but not equal to known data.
- **Languages and identity.** Through Masakhane and, from January 2022, Google, he builds NLP for sub-Saharan languages. He says a keyboard and translator his team released are already used by musicians, which creates more content and online representation, and he links language technology to cultural identity.
- **Healthcare and education goals.** Dossou calls access to healthcare a luxury in Africa and mentions a sister he never knew, born early, who did not survive for lack of care. His long-term aim is a Mila-style research institute in Africa that attracts investors and world-class scientists.
