## Thesis

Theresa Kushner, Data and Analytics Practice Lead at NTT DATA Services, tells Daniel Whitenack that emotion AI is still early, but reading signals from voice, face and body language can make digital humans more helpful. She argues that the value depends on picking a specific problem, having fast access to good data, and being transparent about how personal emotional data is used.

## Key points

- **From sentiment to emotion.** Kushner traces the field back to sentiment analysis she ran at Cisco to measure press relations, and says the right modality depends on the problem: a contact centre may get more from voice than from visuals.
- **Data is the hard part.** She says an avatar that resolves a store customer's billing problem needs that customer's billing data delivered quickly, plus streaming data from other sources, and that data quality matters most.
- **Ethics and permission.** Kushner points to cultural differences (eye contact) and the risk of deepfakes, and argues that consent checkboxes must be understandable. Responding to aggregate emotions is different from responding to one individual.
- **Reading buddy for children.** An avatar built with the MIT Media Lab and NTT's Denmark unit watches a child read, notices stumbles or fatigue, and prompts them. It has won awards but remains experimental because the compute is still expensive.
- **Digital humans in the wild.** A kiosk avatar greets visitors at NTT's lobby in Japan; the avatar was first built for Kia to explain electric cars at an airport and then in showrooms. Kushner's long-term vision is one avatar that remembers a customer from airport to showroom to car.
- **Open problems.** Directed microphones are needed for one-to-one interaction, crowds cannot yet be handled, and adapting the avatar's appearance to the person in front of it is still being developed in Denmark.
