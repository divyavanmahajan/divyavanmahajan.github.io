## Thesis

Hilary Mason, co-founder of Hidden Door and earlier founder of Fast Forward Labs, argues that useful data products start from a clearly understood human problem, not from a model. She applies this to Hidden Door, a storytelling tool for kids built on language generation, which she says is powerful enough to help but unsafe and tasteless unless constrained, filtered and steered by people.

## Key points

- **Builder over academic.** Mason says she preferred building to proofs and has a short attention span, traits she found a poor fit for long academic research but useful in data science and entrepreneurship.
- **Product lesson.** Her first startup modelled career paths from millions of crawled resumes in 2007-2008 and failed in nine months: nobody wanted the polished models, and the assumed audience of college students had no career data.
- **Community under COVID.** Mason calls remote data science community an unsolved problem: Zoom loses the informal brainstorming and even the physical cues she relies on to remember conversations. Whitenack describes a standing "ML water cooler" call with a peer.
- **Why NLP now.** Fast Forward Labs' first research in 2014 was template-based generation, including real estate ads from 60,000 New York listings. Mason says transformers and large pretrained models brought a step change, though they tend toward "mediocre drivel".
- **Safety and taste.** She says raw GPT-3 output cannot be put in front of people without constraining the domain, adding classifiers and human review, and that Hidden Door builds on fine-tuned GPT-2 models. She stresses the work was at an early stage.
- **The kid's experience.** Answering questions from Benson's daughter, Mason describes an AI co-writer the child stays in control of, plus a writing coach that nudges them when stuck, more like using an Echo than playing with a peer.
