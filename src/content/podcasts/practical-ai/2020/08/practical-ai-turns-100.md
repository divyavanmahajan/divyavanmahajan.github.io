## Thesis

Changelog's Adam Stacoviak and Jerod Santo, who launched the show in 2018, crash the 100th episode to question Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson. Whitenack argues that AI is now another layer of the software stack that every developer will touch. Both hosts say the "practical" focus, on deployment, ethics and real uses, keeps the show grounded despite AGI talk.

## Key points

- **Where AI is applied.** Whitenack lists commoditised vision and language services on cloud platforms, and good uses of GANs such as synthetic tumour images. He notes about 98% of the world's languages lack support and points to agriculture outside the US and Europe.
- **Ethics and governance.** Benson says GDPR is the main national-level law and, after a World Economic Forum call, suggests organisations adopt an existing set of AI principles rather than write another. Whitenack argues tracking tools like Allegro AI's Trains and Pachyderm make governance improve development.
- **Explainability and research trends.** Whitenack recommends Christoph Molnar's "Interpretable Machine Learning" and cites Sasha Rush's ICLR data showing graph neural networks as the fastest-growing topic from 2018 to 2020.
- **Beyond deep learning.** Benson sees progress becoming evolutionary and separates intelligence from consciousness. Whitenack calls a Terminator-style singularity inconceivable, citing a study that training one state-of-the-art NLP model emits as much carbon as five cars over their lifetimes.
- **GPT-3 and power.** Whitenack sees GPT-3 as scale, not a paradigm shift, and finds OpenAI's API-only access the bigger change. He argues state surveillance with computer vision shows how unequal access to compute and data is.
- **Highlights and struggles.** The hosts recall guests such as Stuart Russell and Bill Dally, AI-for-good episodes like the cassava disease app, cold-emailing guests early on, and first meeting in person in January 2020.
