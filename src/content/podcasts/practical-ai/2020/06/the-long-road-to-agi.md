## Thesis

Chris Benson argues that deep learning has reached a mature, incremental phase (new variants of CNNs, BERT and GPT-2) and that research attention is shifting to what comes next. Using Yoshua Bengio's NeurIPS 2019 keynote and DARPA's "three waves of AI", he and Daniel Whitenack discuss the small evolutionary steps between today's narrow models and artificial general intelligence. Whitenack comes in as a self-declared cynic about AGI talk but finds the System 2 framing grounded in real research.

## Key points

- **What AGI means.** Whitenack cites OpenAI's definition, highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work, and contrasts it with today's task-specific models such as an English-to-French translator. Benson labels the narrow approach "weak AI" and AGI "strong".
- **System 1 versus System 2.** Drawing on Kahneman's "Thinking, Fast and Slow", Whitenack explains fast, automatic tasks versus slow, logical, sequential reasoning. Benson summarises Bengio's case that attention and a computationally defined consciousness are the ingredients for System 2 deep learning. Whitenack adds that, in his own view, such quantifiable consciousness differs from what makes a human human.
- **Attention already moved the field.** Whitenack recalls SIL colleague Gary Simons saying GPT-2-style text generation was something he thought he would not see in his lifetime.
- **DARPA's three waves.** Benson describes DARPA's 2018 framework: handcrafted knowledge (expert systems), statistical learning (today's deep learning, strong at perception and learning, weak at abstracting and reasoning) and contextual adaptation. He notes DARPA's $3.5 billion budget and rotating managers meant to encourage risk-taking.
- **Reinforcement learning.** Whitenack asks why reinforcement learning, around since the 1950s, is not standard practice; Benson calls deep reinforcement learning early and transitional. Whitenack mentions a marketing team using it to sequence seven campaigns.
- **Regulation lags.** On GDPR's second anniversary, Benson argues law and ethics trail even current AI. He recommends DoD Directive 3000.09 on autonomy in weapon systems; Whitenack suggests OpenAI Gym with TF-Agents or PyTorch for hands-on learning.
