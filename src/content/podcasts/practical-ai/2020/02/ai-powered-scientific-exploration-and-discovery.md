## Thesis

Doug Raymond, general manager of Semantic Scholar at the Allen Institute for AI (AI2), argues that science suffers from information overload and that AI should "read" papers in advance so researchers only spend time on what is relevant. Because AI2 is a non-profit, he says it can tackle this general discovery problem where commercial tools have little business incentive.

## Key points

- **The scale of the problem.** Raymond cites about 3.5 million new publications a year, growing 5-6% annually, while the average scholar reads around 250 papers a year at 30-45 minutes each, up to 15 hours a week. Whitenack calculates that reading one year's output would take about 300 years.
- **Why a non-profit.** Raymond says Google Scholar innovates slowly and ResearchGate is pulled towards ad-driven social features, while domain-specific tools are point solutions. AI2, founded by Paul Allen with a mission of AI for the common good, can pursue the problem broadly.
- **Knowledge graph plus discovery.** Semantic Scholar has indexed over 180 million papers into a knowledge graph covering citations, full text, influence, social media discussion, datasets and GitHub repositories, which supports filtered search.
- **Beyond citation counts.** To counter a "rich get richer" effect, AI2 built SciBERT, trained on 3 billion words of scientific text. Combined with a citation-graph feature in a similarity model, it powers a personalised feed ("a Spotify for research") that Raymond says becomes relevant after rating three to five papers.
- **Meta-research and community uses.** The ACL conference used the graph to avoid reviewer conflicts of interest, and AI2 has published studies on open access growth, gender equality in computer science publishing and bias in clinical studies, with climate change research funding a planned focus.
- **Open resources and roadmap.** Parts of the knowledge graph, SciBERT (on GitHub) and a public API are open. Raymond says upcoming work targets paper highlights, intent and succinct summaries.
