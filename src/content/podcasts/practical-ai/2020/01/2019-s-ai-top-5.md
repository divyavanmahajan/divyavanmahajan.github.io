## Thesis

Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack pick the notable AI developments of 2019, assess the state of the field and make predictions for 2020. Their shared view is that large pre-trained transformer models made 2019 "NLP's ImageNet moment", while the field also became more aware of misuse, energy cost and the limits of deep learning.

## Key points

- **Transformers.** Whitenack calls 2019 the year of large language models such as BERT and GPT-2, noting Google integrated BERT into Search. Benson recalls OpenAI's staged GPT-2 release, trained on over 8 million documents (40 GB of text), and the debate over withholding the model. Whitenack credits Hugging Face's Write with Transformer app and Transformers library, and its $15 million funding.
- **Robotics and deepfakes.** Benson highlights OpenAI's Rubik's Cube robot hand, where reinforcement learning drove dexterity; Whitenack points to domain randomization for robustness. Benson also flags GAN-powered deepfakes, which prompted congressional hearings, while Whitenack notes growing work on detection and positive uses such as synthetic tumour imagery.
- **Tooling and compute.** Whitenack, a self-described PyTorch user, praises TensorFlow 2.0 (with Keras as the default API) for usability. He also cites a study that training one large model can emit as much carbon as five cars over their lifetimes, and sees model distillation and smaller architectures as responses.
- **State of AI.** Whitenack sees software engineering practices entering AI through tools like TensorBoard, Pachyderm and Kubeflow, and argues for including researchers from around the world, citing NeurIPS visa denials. Benson notes maturing AI ethics frameworks and references to China's social credit system.
- **Predictions.** Benson predicts tooling for ethical AI, neural network development folded into software lifecycles, simpler tools, and a possible "post deep learning" shift. Whitenack bets that at least one of multimodal learning, mobile AI or federated learning will take off.
