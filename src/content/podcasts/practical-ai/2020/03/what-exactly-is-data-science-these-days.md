## Thesis

Matt Brems, global lead data science instructor at General Assembly and managing partner at the consultancy BetaVector, argues for a broad, practical definition of data science: using data to make better decisions than you would without it. He says practitioners are paid to solve problems, so basic techniques, clean data and an ethical lens matter more than the latest neural network.

## Key points

- **Union, not intersection.** Brems builds on Drew Conway's Venn diagram of maths/statistics, programming and subject expertise, but treats data science as the union of the three. He counts analysis done in Excel as data science, while saying professionals need more sophisticated tools.
- **Misconceptions.** Brems says about 80% of project time is commonly put on gathering, cleaning and exploring data, and that linear and logistic regression often solve the problem. Benson agrees, advising people to start with the cheapest method rather than a neural network.
- **The bootcamp toolkit.** Brems describes a 12-week, full-time immersive built on pandas, scikit-learn and statsmodels, with a week-six project classifying posts from two subreddits using NLP. Deep learning content has grown from a single two-hour lesson.
- **Who adapts well.** Brems notes that some of his strongest students came from chemistry, journalism, English and law. He agrees with a host's suggestion that companies can often upskill engineers or reskill domain experts such as financial analysts.
- **What executives need.** Brems says managers need to understand data provenance and bias rather than programming, using the example of a Yelp-based customer analysis that is not a random sample.
- **SQL and deepfakes.** Brems calls SQL the most common requirement in data science roles but "table stakes" rather than a differentiator. He names deepfakes as the biggest coming challenge and argues for an ethical lens on everything taught and practised.
