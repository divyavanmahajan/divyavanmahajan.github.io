## Thesis

Madhurima Khandelwal, vice president and head of American Express AI Labs, describes how the company applies machine learning across risk, credit, marketing and servicing at very large scale. She argues that the starting point is always the business process, not the technique: teams should first ask whether a problem needs an ML model at all, then be precise about data, features and how decisions are explained and governed.

## Key points

- **A lab for horizontal needs.** Khandelwal says AI Labs, with teams in Bangalore and New York, does research and builds tools and platforms for modellers, while business units keep their own ML teams. She joined Amex in 2005 and moved into ML through digital marketing personalisation.
- **Fraud at trillion-dollar scale.** Fraud prevention was one of the first ML deployments, around 2010. Khandelwal cites more than $1.2 trillion in annual transactions that need a fraud decision within milliseconds; Whitenack estimates that as a couple of million inferences a minute.
- **Earn the complexity.** Asked by Benson how to choose a technique, Khandelwal says to build the best segmentation and the best ML model and only accept the extra cost if the model clearly wins, re-evaluating as data changes.
- **Bias, explainability and oversight.** She describes fair lending training for all modellers, fair lending reviews, a separate oversight team, platform tools for interpreting model scores at scale, and internal ethical AI principles in development with risk and compliance.
- **Good governance improves the models.** Responding to Whitenack's reference to an O'Reilly article by DJ Patil and Hilary Mason, she says knowing what drives an approval or decline also helps detect data drift and anomalies.
- **Internal NLP and automation.** Examples include a duplicate-invoice detector for vendor management and a natural-language interface for querying complex reports, complicated by multiple languages across global markets.
