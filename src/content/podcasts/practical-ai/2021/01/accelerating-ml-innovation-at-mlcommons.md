## Thesis

David Kanter, executive director of MLCommons, compares machine learning today to aeronautics at the turn of the 20th century: custom and handcrafted. He argues that reaching "analog" companies requires standard components, and MLCommons, launched in December 2020, pursues this through three pillars: benchmarks and metrics, datasets and models, and best practices.

## Key points

- **From MLPerf to MLCommons.** Kanter says MLPerf, a collaborative benchmark started around 2017-2018 and dreamed up by Google's Peter Mattson, drew 70 or 80 companies. MLCommons is now its non-profit home, which Benson likens to Linux inside the Linux Foundation.
- **Why performance benchmarks.** Kanter says MLPerf measures how fast systems train or run inference across software, hardware and cloud, giving sales, engineering, academia and government a common language. He cites Greg Diamos's work showing accuracy grows as compute and data scale up.
- **People's Speech.** Kanter calls it "ImageNet for speech": about 87,000 hours and 10 terabytes, roughly 30-40x larger than any public set. He hopes it extends speech technology to languages like Portuguese, which has little public data.
- **Durable dataset expertise.** Kanter argues a funded organisation can retain know-how that volunteer projects lose to turnover, and can tap members' cloud compute. Membership is free for individuals and academics, paid for companies.
- **MLCube.** Kanter describes it as conventions, distinct from Docker containers, that let models such as ResNet or BERT run across clouds and restricted supercomputers. He hopes researchers will package published models with it.
- **What comes next.** Kanter says the board picks future datasets based on member interest and vision, naming medicine and self-driving vehicles as high-impact areas.
