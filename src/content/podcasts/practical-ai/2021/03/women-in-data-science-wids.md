## Thesis

Stanford professor Margot Gerritsen, co-founder of the Women in Data Science (WiDS) initiative, tells Chris Benson that the gender gap in STEM will not close by itself. She argues that two myths do the damage: that success needs strong innate ability, and that girls have less of it. In her view, fixing this is the job of the majority in the field and of parents and teachers, starting in elementary school.

## Key points

- **Background.** Gerritsen grew up in Zeeland, studied mathematics at Delft (free tuition, which she calls lucky), went to Colorado on a Rotary scholarship and then did a PhD at Stanford. She says she was usually the first or only woman in every job.
- **Why WiDS started.** She describes a Stanford data science conference with only male speakers, whose organiser said they could not find any women. WiDS began as a conference in 2015 to put outstanding women on stage.
- **Progress is not automatic.** Gerritsen disputes the "pipeline is growing" view. Below roughly 25-30% representation, she says, a group stays seen as different and change does not happen organically.
- **Better metrics.** Rather than mirroring the general population, she suggests checking whether anyone who wants to work in the field feels unwelcome, and tracking attrition: who leaves and who never comes in. Benson notes that people who look like him have a responsibility not to hire in their own image.
- **What WiDS does.** It aims to inspire through role models, support through local networks with over 600 ambassadors, and educate through outreach to middle and high schools that shows data science as interdisciplinary.
- **Advice for parents and teachers.** Do not let the father always explain the maths; avoid saying "math is hard"; ask daughters why they drop maths. She recommends Carol Dweck's "Mindset". Benson admits his own homeschooling falls into this pattern.

## Quotes

> "If you are below a certain threshold, and that's maybe 25, 30%, that's been my experience, and you're seen as different, it is exceedingly hard for that to change naturally." — Margot Gerritsen

> "I always think that a culture change needs to really be spearheaded and supported by the majority. It doesn't have to be the task of the minority." — Margot Gerritsen
