## Thesis

Prompted by Erik Bernhardsson's article on building a data team at a mid-stage startup, Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack swap experiences of starting AI and data teams. Their shared argument is that the hard part is rarely modelling: it is hiring for the full workflow, getting the right data, changing a non-data-driven culture and showing value through working prototypes.

## Key points

- **Hire for the whole workflow.** Benson recalls building the first AI team at a large, well-known company by hiring only data scientists who were strong at deep learning maths but knew nothing of SQL, containers or deployment. He now catalogues every stage from problem framing to DevSecOps and hires against the effort each needs.
- **First needs are often basic.** Whitenack notes, following Bernhardsson, that the immediate demand is usually getting metrics out of scattered sources, which can frustrate hires who expect advanced ML work.
- **Data exists, but not the right data.** At the same employer, Benson found product telemetry useful for diagnosing failures but useless for training models. Whitenack adds that systems built for looking up single transactions often cannot answer "give me all transactions of this type".
- **Culture over expertise.** Benson says teams that do not use data rely on self-proclaimed experts whose decisions are inconsistent. Whitenack describes his wife's roughly $10 million business, where marketing must now unify sources such as Facebook pixel, Google Analytics and UTM codes.
- **Prototype to win support.** Whitenack argues data teams should build demonstrable prototypes, for example in Streamlit, rather than presenting 90% accuracy from a GPU server with no path to shipping. Benson ties this to agile-style spikes.
- **Decentralise as you grow.** Whitenack sees himself becoming a bottleneck when all project communication flows through him; Benson says data management then becomes people management. They recommend Mike Bugembe's "Cracking the Data Code" and "Data Driven" by DJ Patil and Hilary Mason.
