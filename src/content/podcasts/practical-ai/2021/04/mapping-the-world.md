## Thesis

Ro Gupta, co-founder and CEO of CARMERA, says that for self-driving cars maps work as priors in a Bayesian sense. As onboard perception improves, he expects the old split between standard and high-definition maps to merge into a "medium definition" layer. That layer needs far more coverage and far more frequent updates, so cheap change management becomes the main problem.

## Key points

- **SD versus HD.** Gupta explains that consumer SD maps can be off by tens of meters, while HD maps aim for accuracy in the tens of centimeters and much more feature detail, down to which lane each traffic-signal bulb controls.
- **Convergence on MD.** CARMERA keeps change data at medium definition and upgrades it to HD only where needed, such as dense urban robotaxi areas. He argues Tesla already uses enhanced "MD-like" data, even while it rejects HD maps.
- **"Pro-sourcing."** Beyond automotive partners like Toyota, CARMERA supplies dash cams with on-device vision to delivery fleets, whose drivers cover 10 to 100 times more miles than commuters.
- **Nature versus nurture.** Drawing on the Yann LeCun and Gary Marcus debate at NYU, Gupta presents the pure-AI versus prior-based argument. He expects maps to move from safety towards comfort and economics, much as weather data did in aviation.
- **Trust and "boring" technology.** CARMERA sits on the board of PAVE. Gupta credits Tesla with pushing the industry forward, but worries that incidents with "full self-driving" could damage public trust.
- **What maps need.** His rule of thumb: improve ubiquity and freshness by 100x each, two orders of magnitude rather than one. He also hopes cheaper maps will help developing countries leapfrog, as they did with mobile.
