## Thesis

Michael Malyuk of Label Studio, returning two years after his 2019 appearance, argues that as ML infrastructure becomes a commodity, the labeling process is where companies build strategic advantage. He says firms are moving from "just get it labeled" to investing in labeling processes, metrics and tools that capture domain experts' knowledge.

## Key points

- **From chore to asset.** Malyuk describes labeling as turning raw data into a "liquid asset" and says model accuracy tracks label quality. Benson compares it with GPUs, which offer no edge because everyone can buy them.
- **Quality versus quantity.** Malyuk says early R&D projects can favour quantity, while uses such as trading tie quality directly to dollar impact; over time he expects quality to win.
- **Consensus is not correctness.** With subjective data, three labelers can agree and still all be wrong, so a verification step is needed. He also sees teams in large organisations naming the same label differently across projects. His advice to leaders: validate the use case with as little labeling as possible, then design the process.
- **Selling the investment.** He compares a labeling-driven feedback loop (better model, more competitive product, more data) to how Google came to dominate search.
- **Label Studio itself.** The open-source tool lets users build interfaces with a configuration language, often around 10 lines; one prospect used 400. Whitenack, a user, built a question-answering task with nine contexts. It supports ML models in the loop for iterative retraining and has close to 2,500 people in its Slack.
- **Roadmap and open source.** Malyuk sees multimodal labeling rising, notes Label Studio is used by most people doing time-series labeling, and plans video object tracking to cover all major data types. He says community feedback and testing outweigh the difficulty of deciding what goes into the paid product.

## Quotes

> "I think over time, quality will dominate over the quantity." — Michael Malyuk

> "Basically, over time, I think those companies that are investing into the data labeling now would dominate their specific markets." — Michael Malyuk
