## Thesis

Irina Stanescu, a former tech lead at Google and Uber who now coaches engineers, tells Gergely Orosz that getting promoted and being heard depend less on doing what you are asked and more on understanding what the organisation values. She argues engineers should drive their own growth with a career plan and build credibility and relationships before trying to influence decisions.

## Key points

- **Google's engineering process.** Stanescu says design docs were mandatory at Google around 2012-2013; teams such as DoubleClick would not engage without one. Launches went through production readiness reviews covering SRE and security. At Uber, by contrast, colleagues were impressed that she wrote one at all.
- **Internal tools and readability.** She describes Code Search, Critique for code review, Borg for jobs and cluster resources, and Memegen. Every change needed a reviewer with readability certification in that language, which she saw as a way of caring for everyone who later reads the code.
- **A rejected promotion.** Her first promotion attempt at Google Fiber was rejected for not showing enough impact, because her niche embedded work was judged against teams handling millions of requests. After moving to a new TV ads team and finding a mentor, she reached senior in one year, versus two and a half years to reach the previous level.
- **Promotion systems compared.** Stanescu and Orosz recall Uber moving from manager-decided promotions to a Google-style committee in 2018, then a lighter internal-resume process in 2019; peer reviews broke down when colleagues left.
- **Growth over promotion.** She recommends a career plan mapped to the next level's competencies, and says managers should set realistic expectations as promotions slow.
- **Influence.** She separates the act of influencing from having influence, where people seek your input. Credibility, visibility, listening and giving first come before asks, and she says saying no is less damaging than saying yes and failing to deliver.
