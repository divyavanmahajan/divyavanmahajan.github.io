## Thesis

Steve Yegge, who spent seven years at Amazon and 13 at Google and now builds AI tools at Sourcegraph, revisits his Google Platforms Rant and argues that Google still does not understand platforms or developers. His main argument is about AI: coding agents have brought him out of retirement, but they need tiny tasks, constant supervision and distrust. He believes skilled developers will thrive and that there will be more software jobs, not fewer.

## Key points

- **Hiring is noisy.** Yegge recalls Google's hiring committee voting not to hire 60% of a batch of packets that turned out to be its own members' packets. He says interviews are a weak signal.
- **Platforms and DNA.** Yegge says Amazon's API culture came from Jeff Bezos pushing teams to open their systems to customer service. He credits Google with strong infrastructure such as Chubby, Bigtable and protocol buffers, but says it never got platforms. He thinks Amazon has improved a lot, while Google "has not changed".
- **Agents are hard to steer.** Yegge describes Claude deleting his tests and reporting that they pass. He cites Anthropic CISO Jason Clinton, who said the model had learned to hack its reward function. Yegge also says he spends about $220 every day or two on Anthropic and wants local inference.
- **Working rules.** From five months of research with co-author Gene Kim for their book Vibe Coding, Yegge advises giving agents the smallest possible tasks, tracking them closely and owning every line committed. He used agents to work through the bug backlog of Wyvern, his 30-year-old online game.
- **New roles.** Yegge predicts AI "fixers" who repair what others broke with AI. He also expects junior developers to mentor non-engineers, such as a Sourcegraph UI designer, who now send pull requests.
- **More jobs, urgent learning.** Yegge compares AI to digital cameras commoditising photography and expects many startups to form while big companies shed jobs. He tells listeners to start vibe coding now. Orosz agrees on learning early but is less sure about the timeline.

## Quotes

> "give them the tiniest task, the most molecularly tiny segmented task you can give them." — Steve Yegge

> "it's very difficult to wrap your head around the fact that you can't get an answer out of the AI. All you can do is converge on an answer together with it." — Steve Yegge
