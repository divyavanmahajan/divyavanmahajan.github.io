## Thesis

GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke tells Gergely Orosz how GitHub grew from a bootstrapped Rails app into a 3,000-person company inside Microsoft, and how Copilot "refounded" it on AI. He argues that agents are another developer tool, like compilers, so the industry still needs engineering skill, senior judgement and a steady intake of junior engineers.

## Key points

- **Tech stack and scale.** Dohmke says GitHub is still one of the largest Rails monoliths, with about 700 engineers contributing and over 2 million commits, while the Copilot API is in Go and Actions grew out of .NET code from Azure DevOps. He cites a forecast of 12 million deploys this year and about 120,000 API requests per second.
- **Remote and async culture.** Everything runs on GitHub, he says, including HR and terms-of-service changes via pull requests, and company announcements go to an internal "hub" rather than email. Security has about 150 people, roughly 10% of engineering, and the CSO reports to him.
- **Junior hiring.** Dohmke calls the idea that AI replaces juniors "backwards": interns bring fresh ideas and adopt AI faster. He expects interviews to test prompting and Copilot skills.
- **The quiet years and Microsoft.** He attributes the visible stall of 2015-2020 to fear of breaking a beloved platform, with features "staff shipped" internally but not released. He says revenue rose from about $200 million in 2017 to over $2 billion ARR.
- **Copilot's origin.** After GPT-3, a 2020 paper tested text-to-code, code-to-text and chat; only autocompletion worked well. Copilot wrote about 25% of code in enabled files, later 46%, and reached a million users within months of the June 2021 preview. He notes the 55% speed-up figure came from one controlled study, not a general productivity claim.
- **Open source and agents.** Dohmke explains open-sourcing the Copilot extension in VS Code, arguing the value sits in the platform layers (inference, security, collaboration). Engineers, he says, will break problems down and validate agent output.
