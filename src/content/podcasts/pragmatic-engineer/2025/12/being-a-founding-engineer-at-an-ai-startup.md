## Thesis

Michelle Lim, Warp's first engineer and now founder of Flint, tells Gergely Orosz that being a successful founding engineer starts with choosing the right founder and continues with taking on whatever the business most needs. She argues that early-stage hiring works differently from big-company hiring: equity, references on the founder, and genuine commitment matter more than standard negotiation tactics.

## Key points

- **Smaller companies, more ownership.** Lim interned at Facebook (about 12,000 people), Slack (1,200) and Robinhood (300), and says each step down gave her more ownership. She joined Warp, then called Denver, when it had two people, design mocks and no code, turning down Series A offers at companies with $10 million ARR.
- **Negotiate equity, check the founder.** She was given a spreadsheet with three salary/equity options and pushed for the most equity. She also asked Zach Lloyd for references from engineers he had managed; two former juniors had risen to director level at Google Sheets.
- **Hell yes references.** As a founder, Lim asks referees whether they would work with a candidate again and only accepts an emphatic yes. She suggests engineers ask whether founders would share secondaries or bring the team along in an acquisition, and judge whether the founder has thought about it before.
- **Product-first versus code-first.** Lim prefers this split to front-end versus back-end. Warp rebuilt its TypeScript prototype in Rust after two to three months for performance and appeal to developers, with Nathan Sobo pair programming with her.
- **Volunteer for unwanted work.** Lim ran Warp's Hacker News presence, Twitter, YouTube and Discord, became head of growth at 22, and later led enterprise sales after picking up security questionnaires. She stresses the core engineering job must come first.
- **Flint's autonomous websites.** Lim describes websites that generate competitor comparison pages overnight and adapt to visitors, including AI agents. Flint uses Claude Code in Cursor and Vim.

## Quotes
