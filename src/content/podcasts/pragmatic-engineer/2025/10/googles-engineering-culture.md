## Thesis

Gergely Orosz and researcher Elin Nilsson, a former Google intern, argue that most of what makes Google unusual comes from its history. Planet-scale search forced it to build every layer of its stack, and strong ad revenue paid for top pay, perks and light on-call. They say today's Google is a different company: layoffs since 2023, flat headcount and mature products make it a good fit for some engineers and a poor one for others.

## Key points

- **A "tech island".** Nilsson and Orosz walk through Borg, B4 networking, Colossus, Bigtable, Spanner, Piper, Blaze, Critique, Code Search and Cider. She cites 2015 figures for the monorepo: a billion files, 2 billion lines and 40,000 commits a day.
- **Pay and on-call.** Orosz estimates a Silicon Valley senior engineer at $400,000-500,000 in total compensation. He describes tiered on-call: tier one pages must be acknowledged within five minutes, on-call is paid, it is capped at two weeks per quarter, and SRE "toil SLOs" make teams fix noisy systems.
- **Performance and promotion.** Nilsson explains that GRAD replaced Perf in 2022, with "outstanding impact" going to roughly 8% of people. Orosz describes promotion committees made up of people who do not know the candidate, and Michael Lynch's four years stuck at L4. He argues these incentives reward launches over maintenance, which helps explain Killed by Google.
- **Design docs and OKRs.** Orosz says even small projects need a design doc. Nilsson traces OKRs to Intel and John Doerr, who brought them to Google in 1999. Orosz doubts OKRs explain Google's success.
- **Google Cloud stays third.** Orosz points out that, unlike Amazon and Microsoft, Google does not run mostly on its own public cloud. Nilsson notes that Kubernetes is deliberately not Borg.
- **Culture shift and fit.** Orosz describes 6% layoffs in 2023 after years of near-total job security. Nilsson says Google suits engineers who handle ambiguity and frequent reorgs, not those who want to keep up with industry frameworks. Orosz adds that the Google name helps a CV for years.
