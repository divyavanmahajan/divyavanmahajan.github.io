## Thesis

Janvi Kalra, who went from Google and Microsoft internships to Coda and then to OpenAI's safety team, tells Gergely Orosz that she got into AI engineering by teaching herself and building on nights and weekends after first being turned down. She argues that AI engineering is still mostly software engineering, and that engineers who join startups should do the same due diligence as investors.

## Key points

- **Self-taught route in.** When Coda started a two-engineer AI team after ChatGPT launched, Kalra got a polite no. She studied deep learning basics, blogged about it and entered hackathons, including a six-week Buildspace contest judged on users. Five months later the team said yes.
- **Workspace Q&A became Coda Brain.** Kalra glued Coda's new search index, LLM infrastructure and chat feature into a RAG chatbot in a couple of days. CEO Shishir Mehrotra saw an enterprise search product; it grew to 20 people and was shown at Snowflake Dev Day.
- **A startup rubric.** Kalra looks for high and steeply growing revenue, a large market, loyal customers and a reason to beat competitors. Orosz cites the collapse of Fast as what happens when engineers skip this.
- **Three kinds of AI company.** Kalra, crediting mentor Yash Kumar, splits the field into product companies (Cursor, Codeium, Hebbia), infrastructure (Modal, Pinecone, Braintrust) and model companies (OpenAI, Anthropic). Across 46 interviews she found a mix of LeetCode, system design and build-in-a-day projects.
- **Speed at scale.** At OpenAI, Kalra says her service handles 60,000 requests per second yet ships with one reviewer, which she attributes to trust in engineers.
- **Scrap and rebuild.** Kalra describes Coda moving from parsing JSON to function calling to MCP, and says AI engineering often means throwing work away as models improve.

## Quotes

> "all engineers that take a pay cut to go to a startup should have an informed thesis on why they think that company is going to grow during their tenure." — Janvi Kalra

> "Don't wait for someone to give you the opportunity to go work on something. Go work on it." — Janvi Kalra
