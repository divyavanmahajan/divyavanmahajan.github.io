## Thesis

Ebi Atawodi, now Director of Product Management at YouTube Studio and formerly at Uber and Netflix, was Gergely Orosz's first product manager when he became an engineering manager in Uber's Amsterdam office. Both argue that product and engineering work best as one team when engineers see the business numbers, when partners know the person behind the role, and when everyone acts as a product leader, much as they would in a startup.

## Key points

- **Earn trust before directness.** Orosz recalls Atawodi's first team meeting, where she relayed a bad product review and warned about headcount, leaving him asking whether they were on the same team. Atawodi says she has since refined her onboarding: trust is given, then earned.
- **Business scorecard and State of the Union.** Atawodi introduced a scorecard of P0 metrics (conversion rate, failed payments, cancellations, share of cash) and a State of the Union session. Orosz says engineers began proposing ideas, and the team had no attrition over his four-plus years there.
- **Bootstrap, then ask for headcount.** Orosz describes two engineers building web payments without approved headcount and returning six months later with customers and results. Atawodi says UberPay grew out of a single partner integration in Latin America and now processes over a billion dollars.
- **Know the human.** Atawodi argues for regular one-on-ones that are never cancelled, dinners outside the office, and knowing colleagues' birthdays. She says junior staff should talk to a recruiter about every six months.
- **Traits of standout engineers.** Atawodi lists constant learning, getting your hands dirty, conviction combined with willingness to be wrong, and explaining things simply. She says genius jerks drag whole teams down.
- **Careers as projects.** Atawodi defines product as business impact, feasibility and customer experience. She prefers sponsorship to mentorship, recommends regular check-ins on your own career, and says she has never got a job by applying for it.
