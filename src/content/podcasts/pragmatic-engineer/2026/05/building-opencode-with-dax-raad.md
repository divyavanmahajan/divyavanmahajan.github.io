## Thesis

OpenCode co-founder Dax Raad argues that AI coding tools have made much of the job objectively easier without making the hard part, deciding what to build, any easier. He tells Gergely Orosz that OpenCode won through positioning as the open source, multi-model coding agent, and that his team now has to slow down, ship fewer features and clean up the hacks that agents make too easy to accept.

## Key points

- **Positioning over harness quality.** Raad says no coding agent had claimed the open source spot, so OpenCode took it first. Its harness was only "good enough" for about five months; once it had market share, the team went back to improve it.
- **Growth and the Anthropic block.** OpenCode went from 650,000 monthly actives in December to 2.5 million in January and close to 8 million now. When Anthropic blocked Claude subscriptions in OpenCode, Raad contacted OpenAI that night and announced official OpenAI support the next day.
- **Business model.** Raad describes OpenCode Zen, an inference service he says reached a $15 million run rate within five or six months, plus an enterprise control plane. He claims some models carry around 80% margin and that GPU supply is a bottleneck even at OpenCode's size.
- **Gains get cashed in as time.** Raad says most engineers use AI to do the same work with less effort, while the few who care about quality get buried in slop PRs and burn out.
- **The muted hack.** In his memo to the team, Raad wrote that they shipped features they shouldn't have and absorbed too many hacks because agents hide the discomfort, without actually moving faster than competitors.
- **Old patterns return.** Raad says OpenCode now leans harder on domain-driven design because agents need guardrails. His career advice is to pair engineering with deep expertise in one industry.

## Quotes
