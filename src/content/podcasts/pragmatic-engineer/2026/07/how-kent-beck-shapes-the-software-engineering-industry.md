## Thesis

Kent Beck tells Gergely Orosz that coding is a small part of software engineering; the rest is building understanding and trust with people, which cannot be automated. Looking back on SUnit, JUnit, TDD, XP and the Agile Manifesto, he argues AI has wiped the playbook clean, so everyone is back to exploring cheap, "stupid" ideas.

## Key points

- **Reply to Anthropic's CEO.** Beck calls Dario's viral claim that coding will go away first a statement by someone who does not understand software engineering. He says empathy and communication gate a career, skills he long thought he did not need.
- **Smalltalk and Ward Cunningham.** At Tektronix Beck paired with Cunningham on HotDraw, kept a physical thesaurus for naming, and drew on Christopher Alexander for the first software patterns. At Apple he says he was fired from Alan Kay's Playground project for being in "punk mode".
- **TDD as rediscovery.** A childhood book advised typing the expected output tape before writing a program; Beck mapped that onto SUnit, laughed at the idea, tried it on a stack and found his anxiety gone. He calls TDD a practical choice, not a moral one.
- **XP and Agile.** On the Chrysler C3 payroll project he restarted the code on three-week cycles and picked "extreme" so Grady Booch would not adopt the name. He objected to "agile" because nobody will say they are not agile; his one word in the principles is "daily".
- **Lost decade and Facebook.** After 9/11 cancelled eight months of booked work, Beck burned out. At Facebook in 2011 nobody signed up for his TDD class, so he relearned how layered feedback replaced unit tests, and his Good to Great coaching doubled promotion rates.
- **Explore, expand, extract.** Beck says AI accelerates development but not business, citing a client that vibe coded a replacement for a $2 million-a-year SaaS product, and warns about vibe coding only the tip of the iceberg, as with payroll compliance. It is "too soon" for an AI manifesto.
