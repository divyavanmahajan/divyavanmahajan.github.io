## Thesis

In a reversed-format AMA, with Wordsmith CTO Volodymyr Giginiak reading out subscriber questions, Gergely Orosz argues that AI is changing what companies hire for and how teams work, but not the fundamentals. He holds that engineers who get hands-on AI experience inside their current jobs are best placed, and that professionals who care about the craft will stay in demand.

## Key points

- **From Uber to writing.** Orosz says the 2020 Uber layoffs, which cut about a quarter of his team, left him demotivated. Asking what he would do with startup money, he realised he would write anyway. The newsletter now has more than 10,000 paying subscribers.
- **Hiring in the AI era.** Giginiak describes Wordsmith's process: an AI-assisted take-home followed by a long discussion that tests whether the candidate can reason about and correct the AI's choices. Orosz expects LeetCode-style interviews to survive because they select for people who tolerate corporate process.
- **Who is in demand.** According to Orosz, engineers who have actually built AI systems and can advise on RAG versus fine-tuning, model hosting and inference costs are hard to hire. Those with no AI exposure at work struggle.
- **Big Tech scorecard.** Orosz rates Google as trying hardest, with Gemini the only product eating into ChatGPT's share. He sees Meta's morale falling after its reassignments, Microsoft as political, and Apple as a possible winner in local AI. He calls Anthropic too unusual to copy, since its product is the model itself.
- **Quality, standards and managers.** Orosz uses Uber's pre-2016 polling hack to argue that speed-versus-quality trade-offs predate AI. He thinks MCP spread because Anthropic was not seen as a threat when it launched, and that the industry's swing towards coding managers will eventually reverse.
- **Personal AI use.** Orosz uses no AI in his writing and has Grammarly turned off, but codes with Codex, Claude Code and Cursor. He accepts his hand-coding skill will decline. His favourite products are Granola and Perplexity's deep research.
