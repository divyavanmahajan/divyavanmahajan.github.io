## Thesis

Maggie Appleton, a staff research engineer at GitHub Next, argues that product design is software design: the same problem solving as engineering, done with space, colour, motion and a product's "nouns and verbs". Now that agents can handle much of the implementation, she says the hard work sits upstream, in sketching, planning and team decisions, and that agents still lack the visual judgement and taste that design needs. Gergely Orosz relates her notebook habit to engineers working at a whiteboard.

## Key points

- **Design engineers.** Appleton defines a design engineer as a designer who understands the technical architecture and often builds the front end. She does not count micro-interaction polish, which she says an agent can now produce, as design engineering.
- **Familiar primitives win.** At Elicit, she says months of experiments with infinite canvases, card stacks and Notion-like documents ended with scientists asking for a table. Her lesson is to start from familiar primitives.
- **Paper first, then jigs.** She sketches in notebooks because agents are weak at spatial reasoning. She then asks agents to build "jigs": live prototypes with sliders and colour pickers (built with Dial Kit) that act as a personal Figma. She demoed one for a GitHub Next homepage.
- **Planning is the bottleneck.** Appleton says agent planning that asks dozens of A/B/C questions wears people out. She is prototyping multiplayer decision cards with diagrams and a named owner for each decision. She also describes GitHub Next's Ace prototype, Slack plus cloud sandboxes, whose sandboxes later shipped in the GitHub desktop app.
- **Capability gaslighting.** Her term, similar to Ethan Mollick's "jagged frontier", for models that impress on one task and fail on another, or on the same task the next day.
- **Craft and AI tells.** She expects agent-made aesthetics, such as Claude's cream backgrounds and the Linear look, to become tells that people reject. She also wonders whether design would lose its satisfaction if agents designed exactly to her taste. Elsewhere she argues for local-first "barefoot developers" who build home-cooked software for communities.
