## Thesis

Jean Lee tells Gergely Orosz that WhatsApp served 450 million monthly users with under 30 engineers because it stayed small, trusted people and ruthlessly limited scope. She suggests that much of the efficiency now credited to AI may simply come from staying lean. Later, as a Facebook manager, she saw that larger organisations reward visible work.

## Key points

- **Eight native platforms.** Lee lists iPhone, Android, BlackBerry, Windows Phone, Nokia S40 and S60, KaiOS and web, with an Erlang backend. She says the choice followed Jan Koum's goal of a lightweight app a grandmother in the countryside could use on any phone.
- **Almost no process.** Brian Acton reviewed only her first commit, in detail; after that there were no formal code reviews. Releases relied on dogfooding, and Koum, titled "chief QA officer", hunted bugs himself. An office display counted days since the last outage.
- **Saying no.** Lee recalls Koum rejecting about 99% of feature requests. Video calling was tested with employees' families for a long time before launch. Orosz contrasts this with Skype's 1,000 engineers and Scrum training.
- **The $1 fee.** Lee says the yearly fee, charged only in some countries to slow growth, covered servers, salaries and SMS codes; the roughly $8 million from Sequoia was never touched.
- **Inside Facebook.** After the $19 billion acquisition she was levelled L3, later became a manager and helped open the London office. In calibration she saw herself as a lawyer for her reports, and people who posted often on Workplace reached consensus most easily.
- **AI and advice.** Lee expects AI to remove OKR and documentation work but not the people side of management, and tells new graduates to invest in foundations.
