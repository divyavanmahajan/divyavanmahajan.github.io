## Thesis

Will Larson, CTO of Imprint, argues that the 2010s low-interest-rate era produced management norms that no longer hold: managers stayed out of technical decisions and focused on keeping engineers happy. With AI coding tools, leaders are expected to be in the details again, and Larson tells middle managers to reskill or step back into line management.

## Key points

- **ZIRP shaped management.** Larson says that when hiring and retention set companies apart, leverage moved to engineers. Companies sold career development and comfort, sometimes including no on-call duty. Managers barred from technical decisions had only process, scheduling and headcount left as tools. He now calls hiring "the least interesting way to solve every problem".
- **Staff engineers serve the business.** At Stripe, where his org grew to about 250, Larson pushed back on senior engineers who wanted more interesting problems. He says staff-level work is about making trade-offs under ambiguity, and that agents often miss those trade-offs because the context is not in the window.
- **Hands-on CTO.** Larson says he has shipped over 500 pull requests in about 18 months at Imprint. He moved Imprint from Jira to Linear in under four weeks, with only about three of 230 people in favour, so agents could pull tasks from one place.
- **Technologist plus bureaucrat.** Larson says an executive has to be good at both. The old route through middle management trained the bureaucrat side and let the technical side lapse, so he now sees line managers as the better future executives.
- **Durable teams and TLMs.** Larson says long-lived, high-ownership teams that build up domain context are the only way to build skilled organisations, and that moving extra people onto a late project still fails. He describes the tech lead manager role as "still a trap, but it's a highly demanded trap". He says EMs now often manage 12 to 14 people.
- **Taking a step back.** Larson went from a team of about 250 at Carta to about 25 at Imprint so he could relearn how software gets built today.
