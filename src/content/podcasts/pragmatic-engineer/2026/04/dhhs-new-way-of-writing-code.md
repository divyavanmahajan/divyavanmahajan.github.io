## Thesis

David Heinemeier Hansson tells Gergely Orosz that his opinions on AI have not changed; the circumstances have. Tab-style autocomplete annoyed him, but terminal agent harnesses combined with Opus 4.5 (late November) produced code he was willing to merge, so he now starts every project agent-first. He argues this amplifies senior engineers and designer-builders, keeps taste and quality central, and that the industry may have seen "peak programmer".

## Key points

- **Workflow.** Hansson uses OpenCode as his main harness and some Claude Code. In tmux he runs Neovim on the left, OpenCode with Kimi K2.5 and Opus in Claude Code on the right, and reviews diffs with Lazygit before committing. He reviewed 100 pending Omarchy PRs in about 90 minutes with Claude.
- **Bigger ambitions.** Hansson says the pie is "just exploding": engineer Jeremy optimised the P1 (fastest 1%) of requests from about four milliseconds to under half a millisecond in a few days, and Opus and Codex passed a dual-boot plan for Omarchy back and forth.
- **CLIs over MCP.** After an OpenClaw agent signed itself up for HEY and Fizzy and joined a Basecamp project, 37signals began building CLIs for all its products, which Hansson sees as a vindication of the Unix philosophy.
- **Seniors versus juniors.** Hansson says the most senior people get the most acceleration because they can judge whether agent output is fit for production, and cites Amazon requiring review of juniors' agent-generated code after outages.
- **Designers who build.** 37signals has about 20 programmers and 10 designers in a 60-person company; Hansson says designers act as product managers and implementers, and that agents push the industry towards this model. He adds that Shape Up's two-month cycles no longer make sense.
- **Careers and balance.** Hansson argues developers in cost centres will face price pressure, that warm referrals beat open calls in hiring, and that he protects eight hours of sleep despite the "intoxicating" dopamine of shipping with agents.
