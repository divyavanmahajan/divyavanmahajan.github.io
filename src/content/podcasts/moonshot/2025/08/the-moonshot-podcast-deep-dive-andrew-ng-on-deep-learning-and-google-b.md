## Thesis

Andrew Ng tells Astro Teller that Google Brain grew from two unpopular ideas: that bigger neural networks keep getting better, and that one learning algorithm could handle many kinds of data. Data from his Stanford students gave him the confidence to push on. Looking ahead, Ng argues that foundation models are like electricity and that most value will come from the applications built on them.

## Key points

- **Scale was heresy.** Around 2008, Ng says, senior researchers told him to invent new algorithms instead, and Yoshua Bengio warned it was bad for his career. A chart by his student Adam Coates showed every model improving with size.
- **Founding at X.** Sebastian Thrun arranged a 2010 dinner with Larry Page at a Japanese restaurant, where Ng pitched without slides. Jeff Dean became the systems partner: training started on MapReduce and the stack later evolved into TensorFlow.
- **Hardware regrets.** Ng says Google was slow to adopt GPUs because a mixed fleet worried the data-centre teams; TPUs came after Brain left X. He credits the transformer's success to its authors designing every step to run in parallel on that hardware.
- **Early wins.** A class for about 100 Googlers created allies. The first projects were speech recognition, reading house numbers in Street View (a bigger impact at the time), advertising, and YouTube tagging with Jay Yagnik's team. The "cat paper", from an unlabelled-YouTube experiment with Quoc Le, was Brain's public debut.
- **AI Fund and education.** Ng's venture studio launches about one startup a month, around six months from idea, and roughly 75% of companies graduate. He notes that only four US states require computing education for a high school diploma, and wants everyone to learn AI-assisted coding.
- **Speed and sandboxes.** Ng sees 10x to 100x differences in how fast people execute. He credits X with letting the team move fast with no risk of taking down Google search. Teller's version is to shorten the time from hypothesis to result.
