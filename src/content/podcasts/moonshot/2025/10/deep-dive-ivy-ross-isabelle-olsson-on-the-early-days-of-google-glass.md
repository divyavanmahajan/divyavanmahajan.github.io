## Thesis

Astro Teller and the two designers who shaped Google Glass, Isabelle Olsson and Ivy Ross, look back on it as a prototype and learning platform that the public treated as a finished product. Both guests say the technology was too early for a mass-market success, though not too early to be a moonshot. They point to human factors, social acceptance and a clear purpose as the problems that held it back.

## Key points

- **The problem Glass addressed.** Olsson had a drawing made of humans standing upright, then hunching back over phones; Glass aimed to keep people present while informed. Teller saw it as merging physical and digital experience.
- **The Explorer program.** Olsson recalls internal prototypes named after animals, which went from roughly a backpack to about 500 grams and finally under 50 grams. Sebastian Thrun then decided to open the prototype to about 10,000 public "explorers" so the team could learn what people did with it.
- **Grounding the unfamiliar.** Both designers argue that new technology should take a familiar form. Olsson pushed a modular design that fitted into ordinary eyeglass frames, and she once sanded down an off-the-shelf camera part to show that a curved custom component was possible.
- **Unplanned uses.** Ross describes uses no one had planned for: an emotion-reading "happy glasses" app for children with Asperger's, surgeons streaming their view to students, and assembly-line workers looking up parts. The industrial use led to an enterprise version that X later rebuilt for industry.
- **Timing and pace.** Ross says the problems of weight, heat and the display could not be solved in the short term. Olsson credits Glass with starting the "Okay Google" voice work and the public debate about cameras. Teller wonders whether growing at half the speed, and stating more clearly that Glass was not a product, would have kept public opinion on side.
- **Exploration versus scaling.** Teller says X is the right place to explore but the wrong place to scale, which is why Glass moved to Tony Fadell's group. Ross says that team built audio-only versions similar to products that came to market years later.

## Quotes

> "the consumer will accept new actions if they are getting something big in return." — Ivy Ross

> "If this is the right place to do the exploration, it is the wrong place to do the scaling." — Astro Teller
