## Thesis

Emily Ma, who led X's food waste project Delta, argues that food waste is a lossy system rather than a single broken step: surplus appears at every stage from farm to retailer, and the main barrier to rerouting edible food is slow, phone-based coordination. Her team's answer was an "air traffic control" system for surplus food, later released as open data and communication standards. Astro Teller and Ma also trace how the sensor project Chorus grew out of the same work.

## Key points

- **Scale of the problem.** Ma cites an estimate that 40% of food in the US food system is never eaten, that households account for 40% of US waste, and that a US household could save $1,500 a year by cutting food waste 10-20%. She also points to landfill methane from food waste.
- **Waste at every stage.** Using strawberries as an example, Ma describes growers planting extra as insurance, processors trimming edible fruit, and whole truckloads rejected for early mould, which means consumers pay for the waste.
- **A human "air traffic controller".** The idea came from Dana Yost in Nogales, Arizona, who matched rejected produce trucks from Mexico with food banks by phone. Delta piloted a digital version with Kroger (2,800 stores) and Feeding America.
- **Open Product Recovery.** Ma says the prototype became open standards in which organisations run their own nodes. Adopters include the Arizona Food Banking Network's seven food banks and the FarmLink Project, and there is interest from Australia, Japan and the Bahamas.
- **Chorus.** Chorus aimed to give objects a voice through sensors that report location and temperature. Trials in Japan found that fish left on airport tarmacs arrived in worse condition than fish sent by truck. The team later moved into healthcare, where Ma says 30% of vaccines were discarded because of temperature problems.
- **Graduation and play.** Delta moved to Google, which Ma says enabled partnerships with Kroger and Walmart and philanthropy through Google.org. Both speakers argue that a playful team is what gets a project through the "pit of despair".
