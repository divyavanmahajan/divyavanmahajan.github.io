## Thesis

Astro Teller uses three X graduates to argue that health technology should integrate seamlessly into daily life, like anti-lock brakes in a car. Skip's founders, Iris engineer Will Biederman and Verily CEO Stephen Gillett each describe how their projects reached real-world use through pivots: falling in love with the problem rather than the first solution.

## Key points

- **An e-bike for walking.** Kathryn Zealand says Skip's MO/GO, built with Arc'teryx, came from watching her grandmother's life shrink as her mobility declined. Requirements from user research: hands free, not embarrassing like a medical device, and useful every day. Anna Roumiantseva says a knee motor provides roughly 30 pounds of assistance on a steep hill.
- **AI controls are the hard part.** Zealand compares intent detection with cheap on-body sensors to the self-driving problem, for example telling a squat to lift a box from a squat to tie a shoelace. An early prototype got a tester within 10 seconds of the Salesforce Tower stair-race record.
- **Rewriting kill criteria.** X kills projects that miss agreed criteria; Skip's demand for washable fabric actuators failed, but Zealand says the real criterion was comfort, not cloth. Skip spun out in 2023 and also builds a hip device for Parkinson's patients on the same software layer.
- **From contact lens to arm.** Biederman describes Iris's lens with an antenna, chip, glucose sensor and a one-by-one-millimetre battery. When the platform matured, a trial pairing it with an off-the-shelf sensor gave results indistinguishable from a commercial CGM, which Teller says became the Dexcom 7.
- **Wearables beyond glucose.** Biederman expects CGMs to spread into general metabolic health and to sense other biomarkers, part of a shift to proactive at-home care.
- **Verily's medical-grade cloud.** Gillett says the Verily Me app retrieves full medical histories because Verily requests records as a clinical provider, and its AI assistant Violet answers questions on that data privately. He describes Verily Pre as a purpose-built hyperscaler for health data and expects eventual autonomous care.
