## Thesis

Kathy Hannun, founder, president and CTO of Dandelion Energy, which was spun out of X, argues that ground-source heat pumps beat every other way of heating and cooling a home on running cost and efficiency. Their weakness has always been the upfront cost of drilling. Her answer, reached after years of failed experiments, is to need less drilling, standardise every installation, and sell through homebuilders.

## Key points

- **Why it matters.** Hannun says buildings account for 40% of US energy use, mostly heating and cooling. The grid is sized for peak demand, usually the hottest day when every air conditioner runs, and geothermal cuts demand most on exactly those days.
- **How it works.** A 5-inch borehole 300 to 500 feet deep holds a looped HDPE pipe. Water circulates between the heat pump and ground that stays at roughly 55 degrees, absorbing heat in summer and releasing it in winter. Pumping energy is small, Hannun says, because water goes down and comes up at the same time. Unlike an air conditioner, the system does not have to fight the outside temperature.
- **The barrier.** Running costs are the lowest of any option, but installation has historically been the most expensive. Hannun says payback was three to five years in high-cost regions, while elsewhere it did not pay off.
- **Failed drills.** X tried a percussive, jackhammer-like mole, which turned out messy, and a miniaturised conventional drill that tipped over. Hannun says the lesson was that ease of hiring, training, moving and repairing matters more than drilling speed.
- **Spinout and scale.** Hannun describes leaving X before any spinout process existed. Dandelion now sells to homeowners in the Northeast and to top-10 US homebuilders, installing hundreds of systems at a time in single-family and multifamily housing.
