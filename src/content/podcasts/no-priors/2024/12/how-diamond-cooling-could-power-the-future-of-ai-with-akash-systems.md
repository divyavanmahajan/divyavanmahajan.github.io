## Thesis

Felix Ejeckam and Ty Mitchell, who founded Akash Systems in 2017, tell Sarah Guo that heat lies behind most of the problems in AI data centres: reliability, throttling, power and cost. They argue that fans and liquid cooling cannot fix it alone. Akash bonds lab-grown diamond directly to semiconductors, applying technology first proven in satellites to AI servers.

## Key points

- **From space to servers.** Ejeckam says Akash cools 4,000 to 5,000 watts per square centimetre in space. AI chips run at a couple of hundred, which gave the founders confidence the technology would transfer. He cites temperature reductions of 80 to 90 degrees in space, about 10 degrees in servers now, and a target of 20 to 40 degrees over the next 12 months.
- **Build the system, not just the material.** Mitchell, drawing on his silicon carbide work at Cree/Wolfspeed, argues that a materials company must build the full product to convince customers. Akash therefore ships complete servers that use bought-in GPUs.
- **Heat as the blocker.** Mitchell points to Nvidia's heat-related delays with Samsung HBM and Blackwell. Both founders say each layer of the chip "sandwich" adds a thermal barrier, which external cooling cannot reach. Ejeckam warns that without physics-based solutions, AI demand could "crash the grid".
- **Faster than Moore's law.** Ejeckam claims that removing thermal cross-talk could deliver in two years the performance gains that used to take six or seven.
- **Sovereign cloud customer.** Their first major customer is NextGen Data Center and Cloud Technologies, which they describe as India's largest sovereign cloud provider. The order is due to ship within a quarter.
- **US manufacturing and AI for research.** As a CHIPS Act recipient that works with Raytheon, Akash wants more US investment across the supply chain. Mitchell says he uses AI as a research assistant to find overlooked older work. Ejeckam expects AI-powered manufacturing to multiply output per person.
