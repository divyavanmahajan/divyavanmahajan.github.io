## Thesis

Zapier co-founder Mike Knoop tells Elad Gil that AGI progress has stalled because the field measures the wrong thing. Following François Chollet, he defines general intelligence as efficiently acquiring new skills, which large language models, as memorization systems, do not do. He has launched the ARC Prize, a nonprofit challenge worth more than $1 million, to pull outsiders and open research towards new approaches.

## Key points

- **A different AGI definition.** Knoop rejects the popular "most economically useful work" definition, which he credits to Vinod. He argues that systems that win at poker or chess cannot be taught to drive, while a person learns new skills from a few examples.
- **ARC resists scale.** On Chollet's ARC-AGI eval, the state of the art rose only from 20% to 34% in four years of Lab42's annual contest. Knoop argues that language models fail when the reasoning chain they need does not appear in their training data.
- **Where new ideas may come from.** He points to program synthesis, which reached the mid-20% range, and to revisiting neural architecture search now that compute is cheap. Because puzzles fit on grids of at most about 15 by 15, he thinks a solution might need under 10,000 lines of code.
- **Why a prize.** About 300 mostly one- or two-person teams entered the 2023 contest, many outside the Bay Area. Knoop expects an outsider to win and wants prize money to compete with the pull of LLM startups.
- **Zapier's AI work.** After Chain of Thought, Knoop and CTO Bryan gave up executive roles to prototype for six months. Zapier connected models to its 6,000 integrations as a ChatGPT plugin launch partner, has run over 50 million AI tasks, and lets users add "clamps" on Zapier Central bots as risk rises.
- **Open research and regulation.** Knoop says closed frontier publishing slows progress. He wants today's AI regulated through existing frameworks, and nothing written for AGI until there is empirical evidence of what it can do.
