## Thesis

Glen Coates, VP of core product at Shopify and founder of the B2B platform Handshake, argues that AI can act as a driver who knows all of Shopify's switches. That would let more would-be entrepreneurs get "over the hump" and launch a store. For now, Shopify's AI only suggests changes and a human commits them. Coates says the slow work of fixing core data models and duplicated platform layers is what makes the AI features useful.

## Key points

- **Founder-led organization.** Coates says many of the leaders who report to him are former founders, each running an R&D organization of a couple of hundred people. He credits Tobi Lütke's return to strong, opinionated decision-making. He ran a 2020 "code red" on checkout with roughly 200-300 people, after which Shopify consolidated 12-15 business units into three or four.
- **Duplicated layers.** Coates warns about building a second "negotiation engine" by accident, for example through order-editing or invoicing tools. That can turn a one-month feature into a six-month one, and customers end up paying the cost too.
- **Propose, don't commit.** Shopify Magic features generate text and replies, but the merchant has to save or send them. Coates says this gives a three-part signal: suggestions accepted as-is, accepted with edits, or rejected. He imagines offering a full auto mode once acceptance without edits reaches about 99%.
- **Product data model.** Shopify is adding support for far more variants, which forces a breaking move to paginated APIs. It is also adding a standard product taxonomy, where AI guesses the category and attributes so products become more discoverable on Google, Facebook and Amazon.
- **Semantic storefront search.** Coates describes searches for "LBD" or "Christmas themed shoes" that now return the right products, using fine-tuned embeddings and multimodal models.
- **The last 20%.** Coates says an agent like Sidekick reaches 75% quality quickly but is hard to push to 95%. He also points to Rabbit's GUI-driven agents and the Perplexity, Google and ChatGPT race over LLM-native search.
