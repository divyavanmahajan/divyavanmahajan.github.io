## Thesis

Howie Liu, co-founder and CEO of Airtable, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that Airtable was always an app platform disguised as an easy spreadsheet, and that the same platform approach is how the long tail of enterprise workflows will get AI. He argues the gap today is imagination and know-how rather than model capability, and that code generation will not replace no-code, because non-developers need output they can read and edit.

## Key points

- **Platform, not app.** Liu says the idea came from working at Salesforce after it acquired his first startup: Salesforce won CRM through customisation. Airtable launched in 2015 after two and a half years of building and now serves half a million organisations.
- **Low floor, rising ceiling.** A 2012 vision deck planned to start simpler than low-code incumbents and then add scale (hundreds of thousands of rows, soon millions) and code extensibility. Liu says organic adoption showed which enterprise use cases, such as media content production, to sell into.
- **Three product hats.** Citing Brian Chesky, Liu splits product management into product marketing, program management and complex UX design, and says Airtable makes sure someone covers each.
- **AI adoption is hands-on.** Airtable's AI beta had a thousand customers, including a top-five law firm. Liu says customers lack imagination about use cases, so Airtable runs day-long AI workshops (about 60 people in LA) and teaches patterns such as a translate, self-critique, then review pipeline.
- **Beyond chat.** Liu sees chat and RAG over internal data as a small slice of the value. The bigger prize is structured recurring processes with humans in the loop, plus features like many-shot prompting from a customer's own records.
- **No-code survives code generation.** Liu argues that complex apps, such as a content-production ERP for Netflix or NBCU, need output a non-technical owner can inspect and directly change, so generated apps should be no-code.
