## Thesis

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang argues that computing has been reinvented for the first time in 60 years. In his view the new unit of computing is the whole data center, which he calls an "AI factory" that produces tokens rather than storing files. He believes co-design across chips, networking and software can keep performance at scale doubling or tripling every year, and that every company and country will need this capacity.

## Key points

- **A "hyper Moore's law".** Huang says Dennard scaling and Carver Mead's VLSI scaling have run out of steam. He wants to replace them with full-stack co-design, for example dropping precision from FP64 down to FP8 and FP4, and with treating the network as a compute fabric, which is why NVIDIA bought Mellanox and built out NVLink.
- **Software compatibility as a principle.** Since Llama first came out, Hopper performance has improved five-fold without changes to the model layer above, Huang says. He credits CUDA's stable foundation and says NVIDIA never strands users, such as its 300 million GeForce gamers. Gil adds that the cost of a million tokens for a GPT-4-class model has fallen roughly 240x in about 18 months.
- **Data center as a product.** NVIDIA builds every configuration in-house, with five supercomputers today and "easily five more" planned next year, then sells the parts so they fit into GCP, AWS, Azure and OCI. Huang says that, given space, power and cooling, NVIDIA can stand up a data center in about 30 days.
- **xAI's 100,000-GPU cluster.** Huang credits Elon Musk's willpower and says NVIDIA pre-staged everything as a digital twin, so the cluster came up within weeks rather than the usual year.
- **AI chip designers and agents.** Huang says NVIDIA could not have built Hopper without AI design tools. He expects to "rent" AI engineers from Synopsys and Cadence, and argues SaaS platforms such as SAP and ServiceNow sit on a "gold mine" of specialised agents.
- **Science and personal use.** Huang predicts that within two to three years generative AI will underpin nearly every breakthrough in science and engineering. He says he uses ChatGPT or Perplexity as a tutor before learning anything.
