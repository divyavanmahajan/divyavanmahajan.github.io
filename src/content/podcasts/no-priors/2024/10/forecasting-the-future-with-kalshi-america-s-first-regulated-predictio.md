## Thesis

Tarek Mansour, co-founder and CEO of Kalshi, tells Sarah Guo that prediction markets, where people trade yes/no contracts on future events, can become a major asset class. He argues they will only grow large if they are regulated from the start. In his view they serve the same purposes as futures markets, transferring risk and revealing prices, and they turn "wisdom of the crowd" plus financial stakes into forecasts that beat the alternatives.

## Key points

- **Regulation first.** Mansour says Kalshi spent close to three years writing rules with the CFTC before launching, then sued the regulator to list US election contracts and won. He calls it the first time in 100 years that election trading has been legal in the US.
- **Trading versus gambling.** Mansour cites the 1905 Supreme Court decision on grain futures, which were once called gambling too. He argues that election outcomes are real risks with economic consequences (for example for climate tech or crypto companies) that people may want to hedge, unlike a roulette spin.
- **Polls versus prices.** He explains that a 55% Trump price is a probability, a biased coin flip, not a 10-point lead in the polls, and that markets move more sharply than poll margins.
- **Track record.** Mansour claims Kalshi has beaten Bloomberg and the Economist survey on inflation, and has outperformed alternatives on Fed decisions, TikTok, the Omicron wave and daily weather. The top inflation forecaster is "a random dude from Kansas".
- **Market structure.** Kalshi owns a clearinghouse, pays 4.1% interest on positions, and hopes to add leverage within 12 to 18 months while managing credit risk. Listing a market now takes 24 hours (the first took 18 months), and half of all markets come from users.
- **Live trade.** Guo puts $150 on Trump at 55% on air and asks for conditional markets. Mansour says these are coming within months, and that he personally thinks Elon Musk leaving Tesla is underpriced at 2%.
