## Thesis

Hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil cover recent AI news: NotebookLM, AI in games, AI companionship, OpenAI's o1 and the first Nobel Prizes for AI work. They then ask which businesses are most at risk. Gil argues that the main opportunity is in changing how people do "email jobs" rather than replacing existing SaaS. Guo adds that thin vertical software and data stores with ecosystems built around them are exposed too.

## Key points

- **NotebookLM and consumer AI.** Gil likes NotebookLM's AI-generated podcasts. Guo mainly uses it as a ready-made RAG interface over her own data. She argues that products built around users' existing artifacts get past the cold-start problem.
- **Games.** Gil expects intelligent NPCs and generated levels, citing a project from a Stanford cohort he sponsored. Guo says agentic NPC actions at game-ready latency are finally reaching large games, and that AI could extend modding.
- **AI companions.** Both discuss a researcher who expects his son to have an AI partner. Gil worries that people are drifting away from human contact, and he raises artificial wombs as a related concern.
- **o1 and test-time compute.** Guo sees o1 as opening a new scaling dimension for tasks that need iterative reasoning, even though it is not better on every axis. She spent three and a half hours failing to guide it through a brain teaser.
- **Nobel Prizes.** Guo questions whether the physics prize advanced physics. Both see the chemistry prize for AI-driven protein folding as clearly deserved.
- **Who is at risk.** Gil points to customer support (Sierra, Decagon) and multilingual, 24/7 voice agents. Guo points to vertical CRUD software of the kind Constellation Software rolls up, and to web hosting. Gil calls Rippling "AI durable".
