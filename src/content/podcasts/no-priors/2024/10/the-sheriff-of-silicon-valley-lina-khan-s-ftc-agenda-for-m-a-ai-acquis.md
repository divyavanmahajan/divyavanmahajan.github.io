## Thesis

Lina Khan, chair of the Federal Trade Commission, argues that two decades of hands-off enforcement in tech rested on a wrong assumption: that dominance in fast-moving digital markets would quickly be undone by new entrants. Instead, she says, network effects and data advantages let these markets tip, so merger review matters most at inflection points such as AI. Her pitch to Sarah Guo and Elad Gil is that tougher enforcement ultimately gives founders more buyers, more capital and a fairer shot at competing.

## Key points

- **From reporter to regulator.** Khan started as a business journalist documenting consolidation in airlines, book publishing, chicken farming and aluminium. Her Yale paper on Amazon argued antitrust had narrowed to short-term price effects and missed structural dominance.
- **Merger review is predictive.** She says enforcers must judge whether a deal could snuff out a future competitor, citing Microsoft versus Netscape as the case whose enforcement let Google, Amazon and Facebook grow.
- **Most deals still clear.** Fewer than 3% of deals reported to the FTC and DOJ are investigated. Her example: the FTC sued to block a big pharma monopolist buying Maze, which was developing an oral alternative for Pompe disease; Maze then partnered with another firm on terms she says were as good or better.
- **Advice for founders.** Selling to the existing monopolist in a market is the most likely to raise issues; she points to the new merger guidelines and argues a market with eight to ten potential buyers is better for startups than one with one or two.
- **AI and open weights.** Khan has spoken in favour of open-weight models as a way to lower startup costs, and relays concerns from authors, designers and models whose work was ingested without consent or compensation.
- **Simple rules over complex ones.** She warns that complex regulation favours incumbents with armies of lawyers, and offers the FTC's rule banning most non-competes, modelled on California's long-standing practice, as an example of a clear rule.
