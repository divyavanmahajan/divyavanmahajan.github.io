## Thesis

Ramp co-founders Eric Glyman (CEO) and Karim Atiyeh (CTO) tell Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that Ramp is really a productivity company: it sells finance teams time by automating receipts, bookkeeping, bill pay and procurement. They argue that AI carries little risk in finance when problems are tightly constrained, and that engineers should treat sales and marketing as systems to be rebuilt with better tooling.

## Key points

- **From Paribus to self-driving money.** Glyman's previous company scanned inboxes for receipts and claimed price-drop refunds; it reached almost a million members and was bought by Capital One. Ramp, incorporated in March 2019, applies the same savings idea to company cards and now serves 25,000 businesses.
- **An "Iron Man suit" for sales.** Glyman describes engineers shadowing Ramp's best sales rep, then automating his lead lists, email guessing and A/B-tested copy. He says Ramp's SDRs now book multiples of the meetings booked at close competitors.
- **Constrain the problem.** Atiyeh argues AI in finance is low-risk when the model picks from known categories, pre-fills editable forms and asks only the one missing question. Ramp stopped fine-tuning and invested in evals instead, moving about 90% of tasks to GPT-4o mini within a day.
- **Buying is a skill.** Atiyeh notes that bad vendor choices are rarely measured; Ramp interviews vendors' engineering teams and weighs their rate of progress over current gaps.
- **Marketing as a production system.** Atiyeh wants AI review of outgoing copy, like a CI/CD test suite. Glyman uses Andy Warhol's Factory as the model: automate production so people focus on taste and striking ideas.
- **Jobs and internal podcasts.** Glyman cites a fall of about a million bookkeepers alongside a rise in accountants and advisers as evidence that jobs shift rather than vanish. Ramp generates five-minute internal podcasts from tens of thousands of hours of customer calls; other founders most often ask for the angry-customer version.
