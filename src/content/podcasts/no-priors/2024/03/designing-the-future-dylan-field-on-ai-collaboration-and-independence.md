## Thesis

Dylan Field, founder and CEO of Figma, argues that AI will first augment design work, giving designers efficiency and letting more people take part, rather than replace designers. He expects more and better-designed software, and more UI rather than less. After the failed Adobe merger, he sees Figma growing into a platform for the full path from idea to shipped code.

## Key points

- **After Adobe.** Field says regulators blocked the deal after 16 months, and he told the team in late December. Figma came out independent with a billion dollars. A host compares this with Microsoft's blocked purchase of Intuit, now a $178 billion company.
- **Three products.** Field describes FigJam for ideation, Figma Design, and Dev Mode for developers, who make up almost a third of weekly users and had lower NPS. FigJam went from project start to beta in about six to eight months, with "fun" as its differentiator.
- **Following use cases.** Field says Figma expands by watching what users already do in the product, such as whiteboarding during the pandemic.
- **Designing with AI.** Field defines design as art applied to problem solving, which points to a hybrid of diffusion models and LLMs. He says the fast loop between human and model matters most, through direct manipulation such as parametric 3D sliders, not memorised prompt phrases. He also says a prompt cannot capture culture, context and emotional state.
- **Interfaces.** Field doubts that chat or voice will replace traditional UI, saying new media types do not remove old ones. He does not want Minority Report-style interaction on the Vision Pro. A host says they are bullish on intelligent cameras as an input mode.
- **Leadership.** Field says his current focus is the value "run with it" while Figma sprints, with play set aside for now.
