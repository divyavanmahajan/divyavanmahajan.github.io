## Thesis

In a hosts-only episode, Elad Gil argues that investors are confusing market size with speed. Few companies can reach the $50-100 billion in revenue a trillion-dollar valuation needs within five years. Sarah Guo pushes back that many investors still underestimate how far AI companies can grow by charging for outcomes. Both worry about founder ambition, and both say safety-first regulation has cost the US dearly in other industries.

## Key points

- **Punctuated equilibrium.** Gil notes that Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX went roughly from near zero to a trillion dollars in about five years, where such arcs usually take 15-20 years. He sees tech moving in waves followed by consolidation. He expects many more $100 billion companies but few new trillion-dollar ones soon.
- **Meek founders.** Gil sees some of the best founders choosing niche markets or hardware out of fear of the AI labs. Guo agrees that founders are often less ambitious than they could be, though she says some portfolio companies take on the labs directly.
- **When to sell.** Gil suggests a pre-scheduled board discussion every six months on whether to exit, because a year of AI time equals three to four normal years. He says the biggest cost is the founder's time. Guo adds that founders should ask whether they capture value as costs fall and capabilities rise.
- **RSI and burnout.** Gil reports "manic energy" at the labs: code solved within months and light recursive self-improvement by the end of next year. Guo notes that researchers have predicted this 18 months out every 18 months for five years. She finds some researchers' reactions tragic.
- **Return on invested tokens.** Gil argues that a few dozen researchers drive most results, so compute goes to them. Companies will start rationing token budgets by return, which he thinks is why the death of SaaS is overstated.
- **Regulation.** Guo says California's billionaire tax is the fastest way to drive the ecosystem out, and Gil warns of a possible exit tax. They point to Texas as an energy and hardware hub. Gil contrasts France's 70% nuclear power with the US at 18% after a 1970s safety lobby.
