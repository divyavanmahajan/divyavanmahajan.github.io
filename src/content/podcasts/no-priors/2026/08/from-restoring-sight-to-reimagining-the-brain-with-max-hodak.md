## Thesis

Max Hodak, co-founder and CEO of Science Corporation and formerly of Neuralink, tells Sarah Guo that the brain is literally a computer, and that working with it as one gives effects drug discovery rarely achieves. Science's PRIMA retinal implant, newly approved in Europe, is his proof of concept. His long-term goal is to make humans less fragile by repairing, replacing and eventually upgrading parts of the body, up to substrate independence for the mind.

## Key points

- **How PRIMA works.** A tiny chip sits under the retina of patients who have lost their light-sensitive cells, for example through macular degeneration. Glasses with a laser projector beam an image onto it, and the chip stimulates the retina directly. Science acquired the technology by buying Pixium, a French company that licensed it from a Stanford inventor. It received European marketing approval in July.
- **Trial results and limits.** Hodak says trial patients filled in Sudoku and crossword puzzles and read books. Vision is still narrow and black and white; Science sees a path to greyscale and red and green.
- **Market and pricing.** Hodak says one in two people have early age-related macular degeneration by 80. He cites Second Sight being paid about $150,000 per patient and a gene therapy reimbursed at almost half a million dollars per eye. He puts the current addressable market at hundreds of thousands of patients in the US and Europe, rising to millions with the next version.
- **BCI is a category, not a bet.** Hodak compares BCI to pharma. Silent-speech and "brain keyboard" devices are hand substitutes, he argues, while vision, hearing, balance and a kilobit per second of motor control get you "halfway to the Matrix".
- **AI as neuroscience.** He backs the platonic representation hypothesis: Science gets alignments between animal neural recordings and AI model representations. He jokes that neuroscientist friends at OpenAI and Anthropic find neuroscience easier on the models.
- **Identity and continuity.** Hodak argues that people accept drift in identity as long as experience is continuous, and that a non-continuous software copy would not satisfy them. He says connectomics, starting with a mouse connectome, is a key missing piece.
