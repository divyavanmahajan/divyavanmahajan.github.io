## Thesis

Simon Last, co-founder of Notion, tells Sarah Guo that Notion's goal has changed from building the best tool for humans to do their work to building the best tool for humans to manage agents that do it. He says the old primitives (documents, databases, boards) still matter, and that he now works as an "agent manager" rather than a coder.

## Key points

- **From writer to agent.** After trying GPT-4 in 2022, Notion shipped AI Writer in February 2023 and workspace Q&A in October 2023. Last says the team tried to build a general agent three or four times before shipping a personal agent last August or September, followed by custom agents that run in the background.
- **Indexing is craft.** Notion now also indexes Slack and Google Drive. Last says most companies are bad at their own indexes, and good retrieval comes from empirical tuning per data source. With embeddings, how users organise their workspace matters less.
- **Rewrite every six months.** Notion rewrites its AI harness about every six months to match current model capabilities. Last calls sticking with one design a common mistake.
- **Coding agents inside Notion.** Last began using Claude Code around April last year. All pull requests are now written by agents. They are larger but better tested, and the gap between ordinary and top engineers has grown. Designers built a "design playground" repo for high-fidelity prototypes.
- **APIs for a new customer.** Because the verbose block JSON worked badly for agents, Notion built a markdown dialect for pages and uses SQLite for databases. Notion positions itself as the "Switzerland" for models and has added Chinese open-source models.
- **Personal workflows.** Last once kept a coding agent running for 13 days straight. His custom agents include an email triage agent that learns rules in a memory page and now archives mail without approval, plus a feedback router that has built up hundreds of rules.
