## Thesis

Melisa Tokmak, founder and CEO of Netic and formerly at Scale AI and Meta, tells Elad Gil that Netic is building an "autonomous enterprise". AI agents sit between essential-service companies and their customers and handle everything except the services and labor themselves. She argues that these complex, high-stakes workflows need a product that works across companies, rather than an AI roll-up, and that frontier labs are partners rather than competitors.

## Key points

- **What Netic does.** Netic serves large companies in HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, wellness, hospitality, automotive and pet care. When a customer calls or texts, Netic's agents work out the need, check the business's operating rules and decide who to send and when. Over 70% of its customers are "Netic first", meaning the end customer's first contact is with Netic agents.
- **Why not a roll-up.** Tokmak gives three reasons. She wants to build a compounding product, her skills are engineering and product rather than M&A, and roll-up software only serves the companies you bought. She grew up in a small town in Turkey and did not own a computer before her Stanford scholarship.
- **Robots are a later chapter.** Robotics will matter eventually, she says, but dexterity, varied buildings and the human side of customers' worst days put it far off for these industries. Every Netic engineer visits customers on site.
- **Labs are not the threat.** She says enterprises do not want products that launch and die quickly. She calls waiting for AGI to solve essential services "a bit lazy". Winning requires models, harnesses and orchestration, and product.
- **Not slow adopters.** One half-million-dollar contract closed in 14 days. For roofing customers, Netic feeds satellite data on hurricane damage into its agents. She says Netic has generated over $600 million for customers from AI-handled interactions, and she pitches private equity on new revenue rather than cost cutting.
- **Hiring for agency.** Tokmak asks candidates about the hardest thing they have done and digs into whether they followed through. She warns against a Gen Z "permanent underclass" mindset and short-term exit thinking among founders.
