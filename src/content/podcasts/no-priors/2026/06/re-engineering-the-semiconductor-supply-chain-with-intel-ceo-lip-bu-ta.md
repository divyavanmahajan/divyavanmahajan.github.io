## Thesis

Lip-Bu Tan, former Walden investor and Cadence CEO, describes his first 14 months running Intel as "crawl, walk, run": fix the culture and the balance sheet, simplify the product line, then build a full-stack company. He argues that agentic AI is making CPUs important again and that a domestic foundry is critical for a resilient US supply chain, even if catching up with TSMC will take until the early 2030s.

## Key points

- **Balance sheet first.** Tan says the US government became a large shareholder, as Taiwan's government was for TSMC. Nvidia's $5 billion investment is now worth about $25 billion, and SoftBank also helped. He recounts President Trump asking him to resign, and then being given a chance to explain himself.
- **CPUs and agents.** Tan says the CPU-to-GPU ratio, once 1 to 8 in training, is moving towards 1 to 4 or even 1 to 1, because reinforcement learning and agent orchestration run better on CPUs. Millions of agents will need compute, he argues.
- **Foundry as a trust business.** He says success depends on IP, yield, defect density and cycle time. Intel is moving 14A into production, sees a path to 10 and 7, and is working on EMIB-T advanced packaging. He calls Elon Musk's Terafab a collaboration in which Intel shares technology and process.
- **New materials.** Tan has invested in gallium nitride, silicon carbide, indium phosphide, glass substrates and artificial diamond (Diamond Foundry). He names memory, power and helium as AI bottlenecks.
- **Investing framework.** He cites 159 IPOs and 126 M&A exits. He backs bottlenecks such as interconnect (Credo) and optics (Celestial AI), wants a hyperscaler as the first customer, and notes that nine of ten portfolio companies changed their plan halfway.
- **Returns and culture.** Tan says shareholders have earned six times in 14 months, and his target is 10x over 5-10 years. He is bringing in younger software and AI talent to move away from a spreadsheet culture.
