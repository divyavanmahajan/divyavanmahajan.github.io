## Thesis

Jake Stauch, co-founder and CEO of Serval, argues that ServiceNow is at heart a workflow automation platform, and that code is a better way to express automation than drag-and-drop builders. Now that AI lets anyone write code, he wants to give IT departments coding agents. Serval's value lies in the guardrails around them: approvals, deterministic workflows, staging and audit trails that let large enterprises adopt agents safely.

## Key points

- **Why ServiceNow is a target.** When Stauch was at Verkada, IT teams often named ServiceNow as both their largest cost and the tool they hated most. Building on it needed consultants and ran 6 to 12 months behind demand. From day one, he and co-founder Alex aimed to beat it directly instead of building a layer on top or going down-market.
- **The bet on coding agents.** Serval started when coding tools were mostly tab-complete. The bet was that verifiable, procedural scripts such as password resets would improve quickly. Early demos seeded the model with example workflows, until models made it hard to find a workflow they could not automate if the APIs existed.
- **Least privilege plus escalation.** Stauch expects enterprises to shrink everyone's permissions as agents make mistakes more costly. Serval grants exceptions through just-in-time access requests and tickets. He sees the same incremental approval process as the only safe path to background agents, which he calls "the ultimate moonshot in enterprise software".
- **Deployment.** Deployment strategists and deployed engineers go on site. A ProServal team of automation engineers handles the long tail of requests. An admin agent called Catalyst translates requirements into platform primitives. Fox adopted Serval company-wide.
- **Economics and the "SaaS-pocalypse".** Serval prices on workflows run rather than tokens. Stauch expects point solutions and middleware to consolidate, while systems of record such as ERP, HRIS, ITSM and CRM survive under new incumbents.
- **Team.** Serval is two and a half years old and has 200 people. It has no product function: engineers sit in customer Slack channels and make product decisions themselves.
