## Thesis

Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong tells Elad Gil that crypto rails fit an economy where AI agents transact. Card fees do not work for micropayments, and agents cannot pass KYC. Coinbase is building an "everything exchange" for tokenized assets, stablecoin payments and "agentic finance". Armstrong also describes New Limit, his epigenetic reprogramming company, which plans its first clinical trial next year.

## Key points

- **Banking the agents.** Armstrong says a card payment has a fixed fee of about 30 cents, while roughly 76% of the agentic commerce transactions Coinbase sees are below 30 cents. Coinbase offers self-custodial agent wallets and is adding segregated agent accounts. It incubated the X402 payment protocol, which now sits with the Linux Foundation, and publishes stats at agentic.market.
- **AI inside Coinbase.** Each team and repository has a markdown "brain" holding incident history, financial controls, A/B tests and PR history. When reviewers correct an agent, the fix goes back into the brain, so one-shot PR acceptance rises over time. Armstrong uses the internal harness, Toshi, to send his team finished PRs instead of feature requests.
- **Headcount.** Armstrong argues that AI removes tasks rather than people. Existing teams will ship faster, while some new companies will be very small.
- **Tokenization.** He says 88% of Coinbase revenue now comes from sources other than Bitcoin trading. Its tokenized stocks, currently offered outside the US, are backed one-to-one by real securities. He estimates that about 4 billion people lack access to a brokerage account.
- **Prediction markets.** He says they reached a $100 million revenue run rate within months of launch. He sees uses well beyond sports, such as forecasting the effects of policy and "opinion markets" on big questions.
- **New Limit and beyond.** The company has 50-60 people in South San Francisco and uses AI to pick transcription-factor screens. Its first targets are liver cells, vascular cells and T cells, starting with alcohol-related liver disease. Armstrong also backs special economic zones such as Prospera and US "freedom cities".
