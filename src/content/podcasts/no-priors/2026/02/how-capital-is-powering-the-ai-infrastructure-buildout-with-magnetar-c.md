## Thesis

Neil Tiwari, who leads AI infrastructure at the $22 billion alternative asset manager Magnetar Capital, tells Sarah Guo that the AI buildout cannot be paid for with equity alone and needs structured debt. He argues that fears about GPU depreciation and circular financing miss how these deals are built, and that the real near-term constraints are physical: power distribution, equipment and skilled labour.

## Key points

- **The CoreWeave origin.** Magnetar met CoreWeave in 2021, when it was moving from Ethereum mining to high-performance compute such as visual effects. Tiwari credits CoreWeave's early win to scale and reliability, plus founders with a background in energy asset management.
- **Contracts, not used cars.** Hyperscaler capex for 2026 is projected at $660-690 billion. Tiwari says the SPV debt structures used are secured mainly by five-year take-or-pay contracts with investment-grade buyers, with GPUs as secondary collateral. The debt fully amortises over four to five years against a two-to-three-year payback, so the cloud operator keeps the residual value.
- **Deals are maturing.** Early SPVs held only investment-grade counterparties. They now mix in AI labs and startups. Tiwari cites SemiAnalysis data showing Blackwell at 90-100 times Hopper's inference efficiency, against a claimed 30 times.
- **Circularity and inference.** Tiwari sees no "dark GPUs" as in the dark-fibre era, and puts enterprise AI at about $37 billion last year. He says inference is harder than training: variable demand, memory throughput across prefill and decode, and distributed clusters of four or five megawatts. Application companies want to own compute because it is their largest cost of goods.
- **Power and steel.** Tiwari argues there is stranded power held for peak demand, so storage and distribution matter more than generation in the short term. Magnetar invested in Taurus for this. Structural steel, electricians, transformers and chillers are the 6-12 month bottleneck.
- **Sovereigns, physical AI, SaaS.** Sovereigns treat compute as national security but lack builders and cyber security. Physical AI will need the same flexible capital. Tiwari calls the sell-off in SaaS overdone, since free-cash-flow margins have risen while multiples fell.
