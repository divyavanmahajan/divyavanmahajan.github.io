## Thesis

In No Priors' first live recording, at MongoDB.local SF, MongoDB CEO CJ Desai tells Sarah Guo that claims software's terminal value is going to zero are overblown. He argues that platforms, not products, last. The LLM layer and the data layer will stay in the stack, and incumbents must strengthen their moat with AI and show that it reaccelerates sales.

## Key points

- **Platforms are rare.** Desai notes that only a single-digit number of companies have more than $10 billion in pure-play software revenue. A disruptive wedge that is easy to adopt is also easy to remove. A platform has at least two products working together plus deep integrations with customer systems.
- **Stickiness in practice.** One London bank's CTO told him it runs 300 critical applications on MongoDB, out of 9,000. Vibe coding speeds up building apps, Desai says, but enterprise buyers still need regulatory checks, multi-cloud resilience, air-gapped deployment and security audits.
- **Why MongoDB.** After roles at Oracle, ServiceNow and Cloudflare, Desai looked for a durable market. Databases have existed for about 50 years, and he calls MongoDB, founded in 2007, the only disruptive force to pass $2 billion. AI applications produce messy, unstructured data, which suits it.
- **What Fortune 500 buyers report.** Office copilots get weak feedback on value. Coding assistants got very positive feedback, and he calls 2025 the breakthrough year. Customer support is still being tested. He says he would replace a system of record outright if an AI-native vendor were cheaper, faster and better.
- **Customer intimacy.** Desai credits John Thompson, his CEO at Symantec, with teaching him to ask customers about their other pain points. He aims to talk to at least 10 customers a week. Change management, not technology, decides transitions, as Nokia and BlackBerry showed. He told investors MongoDB's Q3 results came from its core business, not AI.
