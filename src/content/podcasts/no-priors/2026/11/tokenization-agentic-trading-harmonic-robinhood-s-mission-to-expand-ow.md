## Thesis

Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev tells Sarah Guo that Robinhood is "the ownership company". He argues that a society without broad ownership is fragile, and that the AI boom is worrying because most of its gains go to venture and institutional investors. His answer is to let individuals own private companies early, trade globally through tokens, and use agents with hedge-fund-like abilities.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Tenev grew up in Bulgaria, where inflation was about 170% in 1991 and about 2,000% later on. His grandfather stored his savings as copper cookware. He links this to his view of money and to staying calm under pressure.
- **Harmonic and maths.** His startup Harmonic built Aristotle, one of three models that won gold at the International Math Olympiad, and the public has used it on Erdős problems. Tenev objects that AI maths is too centralised in labs. He thinks breakthroughs will be accepted more readily if individuals using these tools make them.
- **Tokenization.** Stock tokens work like stablecoins: the assets stay with a custodian and the tokens trade freely on DeFi. They now run on Robinhood Chain, which he says is the top chain by developer activity. He sees them as the default route to US equities for people in South America or Africa.
- **Prediction markets.** Tenev describes these as a way to profit from what you know, with KPI contracts on earnings prints coming next. In their purest form he sees them as a new kind of news.
- **Agentic trading.** More than 150,000 customers have opened agentic accounts, and tool calls have reached about 30 million a day. These accounts are sandboxed from main balances. He does not expect agent-driven trades to strain exchanges or market makers soon.
- **Private markets and Trump accounts.** Robinhood Ventures Fund One holds OpenAI and other late-stage companies, and Fund Two partners with YC for seed exposure. Tenev wants accreditation rules relaxed. Robinhood is the sole initial trustee for Trump accounts, which Treasury seeds with $1,000 per newborn. The Dells have added more than $6 billion.
