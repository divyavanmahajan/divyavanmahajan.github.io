## Thesis

Max Jaderberg, president and founding team member of Isomorphic Labs, tells Elad Gil that drug discovery has stalled because it is an empirical, trial-and-error process in which each drug is a bespoke project. Isomorphic's bet is that general AI models accurate enough to replace experiments will let learnings compound across programmes, so fewer molecules fail in the clinic.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Jaderberg sold a computer vision company to DeepMind around 2014 after asking Demis Hassabis for angel money, then worked on AlphaGo and AlphaStar. AlphaFold was the proof that deep learning could generalise in biology, which led to founding Isomorphic in about 2021.
- **Why pharma stagnated.** Gil notes that new drug approvals stay flat while costs rose to about $1.5 billion and timelines to 15 years. Jaderberg says the empirical process has picked the low-hanging fruit, and that little learning carries over from one drug to the next.
- **Experimental accuracy as the target.** Every internal leaderboard has a line for experimental accuracy. Once a model like AlphaFold matches it, months of X-ray crystallography can be skipped, and the lab is used to validate rather than to iterate.
- **Data and toxicity.** Public and pharma datasets are biased by-products of past work, so Isomorphic generates its own data and reports strong scaling laws. Rat studies are about 30% predictive of human toxicity, Jaderberg says; he would combine in vitro assays, organoids and organs-on-a-chip with AI to model human toxicity directly.
- **Business model.** The models are built in house and not licensed; they power an internal pipeline in oncology, immunology and inflammation, plus partnerships with pharma. The engine is agnostic to disease, target class and modality.
- **Cocktails and longevity.** Better-engineered molecules with fewer liver effects and drug-drug interactions could make multi-drug combinations safe. Jaderberg gives no date for a first clinical trial but expects a step change in predictive biology within five years.
