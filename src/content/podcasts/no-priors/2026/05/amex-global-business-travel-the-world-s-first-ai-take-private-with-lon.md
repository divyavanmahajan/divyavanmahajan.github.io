## Thesis

Alexander Taubman, co-founder and CEO of Long Lake, tells Elad Gil why his firm buys services businesses instead of selling them software. Owning the company aligns incentives and makes change management possible. Long Lake's shared AI platform then frees staff to serve more customers. Its latest deal is an agreement to acquire American Express Global Business Travel for $6.3 billion, which Gil calls possibly the world's first AI take-private.

## Key points

- **The Nexus platform.** Roughly 80% of Long Lake's horizontal, model-agnostic infrastructure is shared across verticals including HOA management, architecture, HR services and specialty tax. The early acquisitions took over a year to show AI results. Now the platform has impact within days.
- **Growth, not cost cuts.** Taubman says acquired HOA businesses grew 0-5% a year in volume and now grow 20%-plus. In labor-intensive firms, growth used to mean hiring 20% more people and keeping 20 cents per incremental dollar. Making teams 30-40% more efficient gives software-like incremental margins.
- **Talent flywheel.** He says staff are spared the mundane 25-30% of their day, retention is very high, and Long Lake can pay the most. Engineers sit with field teams in about 20 states.
- **Building the team.** All of the first 20 hires came through his network, from places like Palantir, Ramp and Robinhood. M&A co-founder Manny came from GTCR, and others came from Blackstone, TPG and HIG.
- **Why Amex GBT.** The company was founded in 1915 to help American Express customers get out of Europe during World War I. Travel was on Long Lake's whiteboard of 15-20 target industries because it is mission-critical and failures are costly. The plan is to double down on its existing AI strategy: "your travel counselor with AI superpowers."
- **Permanent capital.** Gil compares the model to Berkshire Hathaway. Taubman points to Danaher and TransDigm and encourages founders to roll over equity. He estimates enterprise AI is around 1% penetrated.
