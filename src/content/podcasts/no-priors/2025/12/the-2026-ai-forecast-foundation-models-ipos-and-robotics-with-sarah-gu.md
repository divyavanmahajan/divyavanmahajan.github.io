## Thesis

In their year-end episode Sarah Guo and Elad Gil argue that the "AI bubble" debate will get louder in 2026 while real adoption keeps accelerating, even in conservative professions. They expect market structure, capital needs and incumbents' distribution to decide who wins in robotics, models and consumer AI. A closing segment collects 2026 predictions from industry guests.

## Key points

- **Adoption outruns the sceptics.** Gil expects more overhyped-bubble claims, citing the MIT report as noise, and predicts the next set of verticals will consolidate as coding, medical scribing and legal (Harvey) did. Guo points to a report from Offcall showing broad physician adoption of tools such as Abridge and OpenEvidence.
- **Science models.** Gil predicts one or two showcase results in physics, materials or maths that get overhyped in the short run while the long-run trend is underrated.
- **Robotics.** Guo predicts small-scale humanoid deployments and a collapse in sentiment around some robotics companies when timelines slip. Gil notes that Waymo and Tesla won self-driving over dozens of startups and expects Tesla's Optimus and Chinese firms to be strong in robotics. Guo counters that a self-driving model alone cannot handle manipulation.
- **IPOs and research labs.** Gil expects many more IPOs, driven by retail appetite for AI beyond Nvidia. Guo relays a hedge fund manager's view that funds must buy a lab IPO whatever they think of it. They discuss the funding of new "neolabs" and Ilya Sutskever's "age of research" framing.
- **Consumer agents.** Guo is seeing new consumer agent software she wants to use. Gil asks why consumer AI has lagged and warns that labs and Google can copy breakout apps.
- **Beyond AI and guest calls.** Gil predicts faster growth in drone-based defence tech; Guo calls GLP-1 drugs still underrated. Guests include Aaron Levie on enterprise agents and harnesses, Misha Laskin on US open models, Joshua Meier on AI drug discovery and Ben and Asher Spector on energy-efficient AI.
