## Thesis

Gabe Pereyra, co-founder and president of Harvey, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that the first phase of legal AI was an "IDE for lawyers", and that the next is organisational: orchestrating teams and firms across thousands of client matters. He argues the hard problems are now orchestration, governance and secure collaboration rather than raw model intelligence, and that Harvey should equip every law firm instead of building its own.

## Key points

- **Scale.** Harvey has almost 1,000 customers and 500 employees after about three and a half years. It began with large law firms and now sells to in-house teams at Walmart, AT&T, PwC, private equity firms and the Global 2000.
- **Associates as agents.** On the day Harvey got GPT-4, co-founder Winston spent 14 hours redoing his associate tasks as chained model calls. Pereyra, a former DeepMind RL researcher, says the legal equivalent of a coding RL environment is a client matter.
- **Verifying legal work.** Some tasks, such as finding change-of-control clauses in a data room, are verifiable. Drafting a merger agreement is not, and the only real reward signal is partner judgement. Pereyra compares partners such as Gordon Moodie, who worked on Dell's take-private, to distinguished systems engineers whose reasoning never reaches public data.
- **Forward-deployed engineers.** Harvey's engineers work more like Sierra's agent engineers than Palantir's. Large banks ask for things like a document management system, and customers such as Blue Owl want help mapping operations onto generative AI.
- **No Harvey law firm.** After talking to about 30 former Atrium staff, Pereyra concluded that running a law firm and a tech company means building two companies. Harvey would also be conflicted out. He sizes legal at about a trillion dollars and professional services at 3 to 5 trillion.
- **Organisational productivity.** Pereyra predicts models will keep improving faster than most people expect. He says the open problem is how humans and AI work together at organisational scale, much as law firms grew tenfold with computers.
