## Thesis

For their 100th episode, Sarah Guo and Elad Gil argue that DeepSeek was real technical work but largely on the existing curve of falling costs, and that the market overreacted. They see frontier models converging in capability while big clusters still matter. Their 2025 outlook centres on vertical AI apps, agents, reasoning and early signs of progress in robotics.

## Key points

- **DeepSeek's real cost.** Gil credits DeepSeek with novel reinforcement-learning techniques, but says a final training run of $5-10 million is normal and hundreds of millions were likely spent on compute beforehand; he calls the roughly 20% Nvidia drop unwarranted. Guo notes the V3 base model came out in December without a market reaction; the R1 reasoning model was the moment that broke the narrative.
- **Cost collapse.** The hosts cite a 180x fall in cost per token for GPT-4-level models over 18 months, and Gil points to Artificial Analysis benchmarks showing models drawing closer together.
- **Frontier value.** Gil lists market share and using a top model to build the next one; Guo adds that strong base models used for synthetic data generation can level the playing field.
- **Deep Research.** Guo calls OpenAI's Deep Research a novel product that raises the bar for analyst work, but in areas she knows well she distrusts its sense of which sources are authoritative. Gil compares this to Gell-Mann amnesia and warns about control of AI outputs, which is why he values open source and a multi-company market.
- **Stargate.** Guo frames scaling as uncertainty, not risk, and says any lab would take the biggest cluster it could get.
- **2025 predictions.** Gil expects foundation-model consolidation, vertical apps (Harvey, Decagon, Sierra), self-driving traction (Tesla, Waymo, Applied Intuition), consumer experiments and early agents. Guo expects agents in security, support and SRE, capable small local models, technical proofs in robotics, and smarter domain-specific data generation.
