## Thesis

Winston Weinberg, co-founder and CEO of Harvey, tells Sarah Guo that domain-specific AI can transform whole professional-services industries if a company keeps betting on rising model capability. His product strategy is to "expand and collapse": build many specialised workflows, then fold them back into a simple interface. He argues AI removes repetitive tasks, not lawyers, and pulls strategic work earlier into a career.

## Key points

- **Founding test.** Weinberg and co-founder Gabe ran about 100 landlord-tenant questions from r/legaladvice through GPT-3 with chain-of-thought prompts; three attorneys said 86 of 100 answers could be sent unedited, which got them a meeting with OpenAI's leadership.
- **AI patterns and domain evals.** Harvey builds 30-50 reusable "AI patterns" (such as case-law research) that other teams wire into the product. Weinberg says public benchmarks are "completely useless" for Harvey, so senior lawyers do evaluation; his target end-to-end task for the year is filing an S-4.
- **Two quality bars.** Productivity features can be partially correct if they show their work with inline citations, mirroring associate review; specialised workflows must hit a much higher bar but are easier to evaluate step by step.
- **Start with the hardest customers.** Harvey began with firms like A&O and PwC and is partnering with LexisNexis, because winning the most prestigious players earns trust across the industry. Guo cites more than 250 clients and over $50M in ARR.
- **Law firm economics.** Weinberg expects automated tasks to move to fixed fees while senior advisory work stays on the billable hour, possibly at higher rates. Some firms are turning their expertise into Harvey systems they sell to clients.
- **Hiring and scaling.** He hires for agency over experience, says his biggest mistake was learning to scale himself too slowly (Harvey had 40 people at the start of the previous year), and promotes first-time managers who ask what to improve.
