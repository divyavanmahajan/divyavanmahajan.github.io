## Thesis

Kyle Vogt, co-founder of Twitch and Cruise, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil why he started The Bot Company to build home robots. He argues that end-to-end and imitation learning now make messy home environments tractable, and that a home robot, unlike a robotaxi, can ship before it is perfect. He draws on Cruise to argue for smaller teams, no acquisitions and phased regulation.

## Key points

- **Founding Cruise.** Vogt says he pitched about 120 investors, started with a retrofit kit for regular cars, then moved to robotaxis once Uber and Lyft created demand. GM acquired Cruise for about a billion dollars.
- **Tesla versus Waymo.** Vogt says Elon Musk "nailed it" on the business model by earning profits while developing self-driving. In 2025, he argues, the right approach uses commodity cameras rather than expensive lidar, and remote assistance at a one-to-four ratio already makes the economics work.
- **Why home robots.** Chores like making beds and folding laundry use up people's free time. Vogt predicts that in five to ten years a home without several robots will seem as odd as one without a sink, though he says the timeline could be one year or twenty.
- **Robots versus robotaxis.** A driverless car has no product until it beats human safety; a home robot can launch with tighter constraints. On design, he cites Rodney Brooks: a humanlike form raises expectations, so he would rather under-promise and surprise.
- **China and regulation.** Vogt thinks US hardware firms can compete on complex software and taste. He calls for an FAA-style body for AVs, cybersecurity rules for home robots with cameras, and phased approval paths like Boom Supersonic's stepped test flights.
- **Lessons from Cruise.** GM's car business clashed with urban robotaxis and it abandoned Cruise. Vogt says he will never sell a company again and will keep the team small, using coding assistants to cover specialist roles.
