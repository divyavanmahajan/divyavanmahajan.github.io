## Thesis

Fei-Fei Li, creator of ImageNet and co-founder of World Labs, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that spatial intelligence is as fundamental and as hard as language, and that AI will stay incomplete until models can understand, reason about and generate 3D worlds. She describes World Labs as the first company she knows of working on a 3D generation foundation model, and puts her research record down to being fearless about big problems.

## Key points

- **World models must be plausible.** Li says generated worlds can be fantastical, but their geometry and physics have to be plausible. She sees 3D as a problem on the scale of language, with emotional intelligence as another large unsolved area.
- **Robots in many shapes.** Li expects humans to live alongside robots that take many forms, not only humanoids (a fish shape suits underwater work, for example). She thinks simulation is underrated and haptics data is the most underappreciated input for manipulation.
- **Creativity first.** For near-term commercial use she points to designers, 3D artists, VFX artists and game developers working alongside AI, much as coders use Cursor and Windsurf, and to content creation for AR/VR.
- **Data is the bottleneck.** Unlike LLM researchers, 3D modellers lack abundant internet data, so World Labs needs more sophisticated data acquisition, processing and synthesis. Productization is also hard because people do not passively "watch 3D".
- **ImageNet's origins.** Li recalls picking 101 categories from a dictionary for her PhD dataset after her advisor Pietro asked for 100, and getting her mother to help clean images. ImageNet grew to 15 million labeled images and fed AlexNet; she also cites Andrej Karpathy's and Justin Johnson's image-captioning work as a highlight.
- **Hiring for diversity of thinking.** World Labs hires graphics, vision, data, generative AI, ML infrastructure and optimization experts, and Li screens for fearlessness.
