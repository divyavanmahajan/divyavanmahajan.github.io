## Thesis

Applied Intuition CEO Qasar Younis and CTO Peter Ludwig tell Elad Gil that the industry now agrees on how to build autonomy. They argue the open question is who makes money from it, and that their answer is to supply manufacturers of cars, trucks, mining machines and military vehicles with the tools, operating system and autonomy software that Tesla builds in-house.

## Key points

- **The company.** Gil describes Applied Intuition as having more than 1,000 people and hundreds of millions in revenue, profitable throughout, after raising at a $15 billion valuation. Younis compares its path to Microsoft's: tools first, then an operating system, then applications. He calls it "a Tesla minus the hardware".
- **Vehicle OS.** Ludwig, who worked on Android at Google, describes a stack running from bootloaders through safety-critical middleware to applications. Android's compatibility test suite is a model for supporting many chipsets. Younis says pulling scattered "embedded non-intelligence" onto a central computer removes wiring harnesses and thousands of dollars of hardware. Porsche, which he says makes $30,000-40,000 profit per car, is the public reference customer.
- **China and Europe.** Younis calls Chinese manufacturers such as BYD, Xiaomi and Huawei impressive, with autonomy better than Tesla's, but subsidised as national assets. He says Europe is "asleep at the wheel", and that automated factories erode the labour cost advantage that used to favour China.
- **Timing and data.** The company waited for techniques to converge on end-to-end, camera-heavy systems. Ludwig cites L4 trucking work in Japan, aerial and maritime autonomy, and synthetic data built with Gaussian splats and diffusion models.
- **Safety and adoption.** Younis argues that Waymo already beats human drivers and that liability, not capability, is the real issue. He expects FSD-like features to be common within five years, with prices falling towards free by 2030-2035. That puts at risk any company that spends $12-15 billion and then sees the technology commoditised.
- **Defence and hiring.** Younis says defence is moving from one person per machine to one person controlling many machines. The company has over 100 open roles, and 82% of staff are software engineers.
