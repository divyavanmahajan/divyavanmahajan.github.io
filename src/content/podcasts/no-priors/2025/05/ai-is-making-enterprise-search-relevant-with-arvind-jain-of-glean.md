## Thesis

Glean founder and CEO Arvind Jain tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that enterprise search, long a graveyard of failed companies, became solvable once SaaS APIs, cloud infrastructure and transformers arrived together. He argues that the hard part is not the model but picking correct, current, authoritative information and respecting permissions, and that this is what makes safe workplace AI possible.

## Key points

- **Embeddings from day one.** Jain says Glean started in 2019 with transformer-based "embedding search", building custom embeddings on each customer's content from Google's BERT model, before terms like vector search or RAG were in use.
- **Why earlier attempts failed.** In the pre-SaaS world, Jain says, nobody could reach data scattered across data centres. SaaS made content reachable through APIs; one Glean customer has more than 1 billion documents, about the size of the whole web in 2004.
- **Models still need organised input.** Jain doubts that huge context windows remove the need for retrieval: models, like people, reason poorly over a million unordered documents, so signals like freshness and authority still matter.
- **From search to assistant to agents.** Glean moved from search to Glean Assistant and then to curated "apps", such as an HR experience limited to approved content, which customers now call agents.
- **Good search exposed governance gaps.** Customers found colleagues' salaries and an unannounced M&A document, so Glean built checks beyond source permissions. Jain says many companies now buy it partly to become "AI ready".
- **PLG was not an option.** Jain wanted product-led growth, but Glean has to index a whole company's data to work, so it sells company-wide. Given a choice, he would run PLG and enterprise sales from the start.
