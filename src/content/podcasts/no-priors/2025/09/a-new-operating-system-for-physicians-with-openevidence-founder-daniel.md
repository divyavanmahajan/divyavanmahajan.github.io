## Thesis

Daniel Nadler argues that OpenEvidence became the default clinical knowledge tool for American doctors in about 18 months for two reasons. It treats high-stakes clinical decision support as a semantic search problem that routes doctors to cited sources rather than handing them answers. And it sells to doctors directly as consumers instead of going through health systems. He expects medical education to shift towards continuous learning as medical knowledge grows faster than any one physician can read.

## Key points

- **Search, not answers.** Nadler says a clinical query is a paragraph-long case, such as a 44-year-old dermatology patient with psoriasis and MS, where IL-17 inhibitors can make MS worse and IL-23 inhibitors are well tolerated. Once the query is understood semantically, the system finds the exact snippets across about 35 million publications, including phase 3 trials in the New England Journal of Medicine.
- **Adoption and citations.** He claims daily use by 40% of US doctors and about 20 times the usage of the next clinical decision support platform. He credits citations as a first-class feature, shipped 6-9 months before ChatGPT had them, and says OpenEvidence is one of NEJM's largest sources of referral traffic.
- **Doctors as consumers.** Nadler says doctors had been treated as extensions of health systems and were not the gatekeepers of their own tools. Letting them download a free app made the difference. Patients get information through doctor-generated handouts.
- **Medical education inverts.** Citing estimates that medical knowledge now doubles every 73 days, or every 5 years by OpenEvidence's more conservative count, Nadler expects continuing education to become most of a doctor's training. He cites a rural Georgia oncologist who uses OpenEvidence as his curbside consult.
- **Humans stay in the loop.** Using autopilot as an analogy, Nadler says people personify trust, so doctors will stay in the loop for the rest of our lifetimes.
- **Will over ideas.** Nadler says success comes from compulsive motivation rather than clever ideas. He recruits people with their own drive so that management becomes unnecessary.
