## Thesis

Joe Liemandt, founder of Trilogy and now principal of Alpha School, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that the teacher-in-front-of-a-classroom model is the main obstacle to better schooling. He argues that AI tutors grounded in learning science, combined with deliberate motivation design, let children learn up to ten times faster. He wants to bring that model to a billion children through for-profit schools and his Timeback platform.

## Key points

- **Two hours, then workshops.** Alpha students spend two hours a day with an AI tutor and the afternoon on project-based workshops covering leadership, financial literacy, public speaking and similar skills. Liemandt says 96% of students say they love school, and 40-60% say they prefer it to vacation.
- **Mastery, not seat time.** In Liemandt's account, one grade level of one subject takes 20-30 hours to master. The tutor keeps students at 80-85% accuracy, the zone of proximal development, because engagement drops off at 66%. He says transfers from expensive private schools often arrive several grade levels behind.
- **Guides instead of teachers.** Adults act as motivational coaches, paid at least $100,000, and high schoolers interview them before they are hired. Guides are held to three commitments: students love school, learn twice as fast, and build life skills.
- **Paying for effort.** Liemandt defends extrinsic rewards: Alpha bucks, a $1,000 reward for reaching the top 1% in a subject, and a "100 for 100" scheme that pays $100 for a perfect score on a state test at any grade, so students go back and fill gaps. He argues that $400-500 per student nationally could fix the gaps behind falling scores.
- **AI and cheating.** He calls ChatGPT a cheating tool for 90% of students and allows no chat in the academic block. He argues that kids still need memorised facts, such as multiplication tables, to reason.
- **Economics.** Alpha is the high-end brand; sports and gifted-and-talented schools run at $15,000-25,000. Liemandt points to $1 billion in new Texas vouchers and notes that the US private school market already exceeds $50 billion.
