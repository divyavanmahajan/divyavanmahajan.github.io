## Thesis

Zach Lloyd, co-founder and CEO of Warp, tells Elad Gil that today's models distil intelligence without consciousness, and that the terminal is a good base for an "agentic development environment". He expects software work to move from developing by hand to developing by prompt and then to automated development, with senior engineering judgement gaining value along the way.

## Key points

- **Intelligence without consciousness.** Lloyd, who holds a master's in the philosophy of science, says the Turing test has been passed with little notice, but that models are mechanistic. Gil points to the novel Blindsight. Lloyd thinks people would credit consciousness sooner to systems with live sensory input, such as robots.
- **What Warp is.** Warp grew out of the terminal: users type commands or English, and English launches an agent for coding, project setup or debugging. Lloyd contrasts this with VS Code clones and text-only terminal apps such as Claude Code. Gil says Warp adds about a million dollars in new revenue every seven to 10 days. Lloyd credits the coding agent launched three to four months earlier.
- **Pro developers, not vibe coders.** Lloyd, who worked on Google Sheets, targets professional developers building heavily used apps rather than the long tail of vibe-coded apps.
- **Three phases.** He describes develop by hand, develop by prompt and automated development. He treats agents as junior engineers that create bugs, security issues and unmaintainable code without senior review.
- **Bundling and platforms.** Lloyd expects agentic code review, security checks and CI to fold into agent platforms built on a shared harness and context. He sees Anthropic and OpenAI moving into coding, but says the coding front door is still a native app or GitHub, and hopes model competition makes tokens a commodity.
- **Slowing model gains.** Moving from Sonnet 4 to 4.5 gave Warp a few percentage points on SWE-bench, against a much bigger jump from 3.7 to 4. Lloyd is most excited about headless agents in CI, such as keeping documentation up to date.
