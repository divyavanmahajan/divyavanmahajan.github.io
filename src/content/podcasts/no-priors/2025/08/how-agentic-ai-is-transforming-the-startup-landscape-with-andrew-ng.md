## Thesis

Andrew Ng argues that AI progress now runs along several vectors beyond scale, and that the biggest barrier to agentic workflows is talent, not technology. Because AI-assisted coding has made building software fast and cheap, the bottleneck in startups has moved to deciding what to build, which favours technical, AI-literate founders and small, highly enabled teams.

## Key points

- **Agentic as a spectrum.** Ng says he coined "agentic AI" to stop debates over what counts as an agent and to treat agency as a matter of degree; marketers then put the label on everything.
- **Talent over components.** Computer use and evals still need work, but Ng sees the main differentiator as whether a team can run systematic error analysis with evals, rather than trying things at random. Coding agents such as Claude Code and question answering are the two clear sources of economic value today.
- **Rapid engineering, not vibe coding.** Ng rejects "vibe coding": a day of AI-assisted coding leaves him mentally exhausted. Work that took six engineers three months can now be built over a weekend, so his teams lean more on gut and customer empathy to make product calls quickly.
- **Technical founders win again.** Ng says tech-oriented product leaders who understand what AI can and cannot do are more likely to succeed. He hired a fresh graduate fluent in AI tools over an engineer with 10 years of experience, and says everyone at AI Fund, including the CFO and front desk operator, knows how to code.
- **Small teams, with caveats.** Ng finds very small, AI-enabled, co-located teams among the most productive. The hosts push back that staying lean can be a trap against incumbents, citing Sketch versus Figma, while noting that Minecraft sold to Microsoft with a tiny team.
- **What humans keep.** Deep company research and LP reporting look ripe for automation, but Ng and the hosts see durable human advantages in private context, such as reference checks and meeting founders, and in trust-based relationships.
