## Thesis

Mustafa Suleyman, DeepMind co-founder and now CEO of Inflection, argues that most computing will become a conversation and that each person will want an AI on their own side, as opposed to the business, brand and political AIs that will try to persuade them. Inflection's first product, Pi, is his bet on that future, starting with companionship rather than information retrieval.

## Key points

- **From conflict resolution to AI.** Suleyman describes running a youth helpline and facilitating part of the 2009 Copenhagen climate talks, which convinced him that governance could not keep pace with technology. He co-founded DeepMind in 2010 with Demis and Shane Legg; Google acquired it in 2014 for $650 million.
- **Intelligence as routing.** He now thinks the field over-rotated on generality. He expects the key unlock to be a "router" that sends each request to the right specialised model, tool or database, which he calls more an engineering problem than an AI one.
- **Search is a bad conversation.** Suleyman says Google is already a dialogue, conducted through ten blue links, and that ad incentives have pushed web content into SEO-shaped formats. He says that if he were Google he would be worried.
- **Platforms are not neutral.** Asked by Elad Gil about filter bubbles, Suleyman says reinforcing existing beliefs is the default without intervention, and argues that AI and social media companies should be transparent about curation and accountable to democratic oversight, with Silicon Valley more open to the European approach.
- **How Pi is built.** Inflection measures emotional intelligence, fluidity, respectfulness and even-handedness; memory is about a hundred messages. The team of about 30 ships every six weeks, followed by an in-person hackathon week.
- **Compounding compute.** He contrasts the two petaFLOPs used for DeepMind's 2013 Atari DQN paper with 10 billion petaFLOPs for Inflection's biggest models, and notes that Chinchilla lead author Jordan Hoffmann is on his team. He also previews his book, The Coming Wave.
