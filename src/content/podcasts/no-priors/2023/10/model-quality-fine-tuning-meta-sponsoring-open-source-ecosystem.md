## Thesis

In this host-only discussion, Elad Gil argues that AI systems 10x or 100x more useful can be built on today's models, such as GPT-4 or GPT-3.5, by adding customization and orchestration around them. Sarah Guo adds that much of the value lies in tuning models for specific tasks with high-quality data. Both see Meta's open-source Llama models and generative content as openings for new products, including social apps.

## Key points

- **Six levers.** Gil lists multimodality, long context windows (for example, Magic's work on code), model customization, memory, recursion, and small specialized models orchestrated by a central router.
- **Fine-tuning matters.** Guo discusses OpenAI's expanded fine-tuning for models such as 3.5 and asks whether this reverses a research view that only the general model counts. Gil points to ChatGPT, which was GPT-3.5 fine-tuned with RLHF, as proof that fine-tuning can change how useful a model is.
- **Why RAG.** Guo describes retrieval over a chosen corpus, such as legal cases or medical records. She cites trustworthiness, cost and freshness as the drivers, and Gil adds fewer hallucinations. Gil cites a Google paper showing that AI feedback (RLAIF) can match human feedback for some tasks, as well as Med-PaLM 2.
- **Meta and open source.** Guo compares Meta's support for Llama with Facebook hardening MySQL to avoid relying on database vendors. Gil counters with IBM, which spent up to a billion dollars a year on Linux as a counterweight to Microsoft. Guo expects Meta to use the models in data centers, ad serving and consumer products, such as the Instagram chatbots it is testing.
- **Social is open again.** Gil notes that TikTok, launched seven years ago, was the last large new social network. He sees generative AI as the first real opening since then and points to Can of Soup, Midjourney, Pika and Character. Guo says Toutiao and TikTok solved the cold start for relevance, while generation could solve the cold start for content.
- **Take the easy markets.** Gil advises founders to chase the easy opportunities early in a technology shift. Guo agrees.
