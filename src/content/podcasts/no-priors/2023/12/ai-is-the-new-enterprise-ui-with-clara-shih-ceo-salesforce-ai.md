## Thesis

Clara Shih, CEO of Salesforce AI, tells Sarah Guo and Elad Gil that AI is becoming the new user interface for enterprise software. She argues that most companies are still early, held back mainly by scattered data, and that a common platform of models, prompts, actions and trust controls lets a large vendor ship AI across every product. Over time she expects software teams to describe goals and leave the AI to work out the steps, except where workflows must stay deterministic.

## Key points

- **Open model architecture.** Shih says Salesforce mixes in-house models such as CodeGen (behind its developer GPT), customer fine-tuned models via Einstein Studio, and third-party models from Anthropic, Cohere, OpenAI and Google Vertex, with bring-your-own-key options.
- **Copilot Studio.** She describes three parts: Prompt Builder (pilot launched the week before), Action Builder, which turns existing workflows and MuleSoft integrations into agent actions, and Einstein Studio for bring-your-own models.
- **Adoption stage.** Asked by Gil, Shih puts enterprise adoption at roughly the second or third inning. Most customers are still sorting out data; she says Data Cloud is the fastest organically developed product in Salesforce history, helped by zero-ETL sharing with BigQuery, Databricks and Snowflake.
- **Gucci example.** Shih says retrieval-augmented service replies cut average handle time for Gucci service advisors and let new hires move into sales and brand conversations.
- **Unstructured data.** She distinguishes documents written to inform, such as knowledge articles, from call and Slack transcripts that need pre-processing, and from which structured fields can be extracted.
- **Pricing and ROI.** Shih calls AI pricing difficult: costs must be covered without forcing customers to predict token usage. Guo is optimistic because model costs keep falling. Shih cites Runway's claim that a film's effects needed seven editors instead of 700.
