## Thesis

Aravind Srinivas (CEO) and Denis Yarats (CTO) of Perplexity argue that "answer engines" will become a separate market from link-based search engines. In their view, these products answer directly, cite only what they can source, and encourage follow-up questions. They say Perplexity can compete with Google and Microsoft only by moving faster: a small team, carefully vetted hires and a narrow focus.

## Key points

- **Winding path to search.** Srinivas says the team, with co-founder Andy Konwinski, tried a visual search engine, text-to-SQL and a notebook copilot before settling on search. He credits Elad Gil, their first investor, with warning that search depends on distribution.
- **Hiring for drive, not pedigree.** Yarats prefers candidates with a "burning desire" to work on LLMs over experienced but less motivated ones, and uses trial work periods to keep the team small. Srinivas notes that OpenAI and Anthropic hired physicists and Dropbox and Stripe engineers, and that co-founder Johnny Ho came from competitive programming and trading.
- **Citation first.** Srinivas compares Perplexity to writing a research paper: say only what you can cite. Even personal questions go to the search engine. He admits that merging sources can mix up people who share a name, and says users can remove irrelevant sources.
- **Ranking techniques.** Srinivas describes RLHF using user feedback and contractors. Yarats says simpler steps, such as rejection sampling with a model that ranks several outputs, already improve quality a lot.
- **Fewer clicks, pricier clicks.** Srinivas expects more follow-up questions, more actions taken on results and less traffic to publishers, since Perplexity deliberately shows only three to four links. Yarats argues Google is slow to ship answers because ad clicks fund its business. Srinivas thinks citation will reward high-quality publishers.
- **Monetisation options.** Srinivas lists an API, a prosumer tier built around the Chrome extension (nearly 100,000 users) and eventually ads kept separate from the core product. He is wary of subscriptions if Google or Bing offer something similar for free.
