## Thesis

Ilya Sutskever traces OpenAI's path from the bet on large neural networks to scaling transformers, and argues that the next gains will come mostly from reliability and deeper insight. If progress continues, he says, data centres smarter than people are plausible within 5 to 10 years, and the superalignment project aims to make sure such systems are prosocial towards humanity.

## Key points

- **The AlexNet bet.** Sutskever says neural networks were marginalised because you could not prove theorems about them. AlexNet worked because of early GPU use, his realisation that networks were simply too small, and Alex Krizhevsky's skill at writing fast GPU code.
- **Capped profit.** The goal, AGI that benefits all of humanity, never changed, but the tactic did: a non-profit could not build the large compute clusters needed, so OpenAI became a capped-profit company. He traces the research path from the Dota 2 project to GPT-1, GPT-2 and GPT-3.
- **Reliability over size.** Smaller models mainly lose reliability, he argues: if a model answers 20 questions correctly, the 21st should not contain a gross mistake. Small models will serve niches; larger ones will justify their cost for tasks such as legal advice.
- **Open source has a boundary.** Sutskever supports open models at current capability levels but says models that could autonomously build a company or do science are a different case, and finding that boundary is an urgent research project.
- **Architecture and data.** He sees data as the most near-term limit to scaling but says it can be overcome. On architecture, he frames the question as compute efficiency rather than whether transformers can get there, citing the uniformity of the cortex and a ferret experiment.
- **Digital life.** He defines digital life by autonomy rather than reproduction, since technology already reproduces through people copying ideas, and calls autonomously reproducing capable AI a scary prospect.

## Quotes
