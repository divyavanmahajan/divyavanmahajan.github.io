## Thesis

Emad Mostaque, founder of Stability AI, argues that base foundation models should be open infrastructure available to everyone, nations included, while private companies compete on instruction tuning, fine-tuning and human-in-the-loop deployment. He bets on many smaller, customisable models over a single giant model, and calls for some regulation of the largest models.

## Key points

- **Background.** Mostaque describes moving from hedge funds to AI after using AI-based literature review to study his son's autism, then running a COVID AI initiative. He funded EleutherAI and early image-generation developers before founding Stability.
- **Models as infrastructure.** He calls a 2 GB Stable Diffusion model a new programming primitive and says its GitHub stars overtook Ethereum and Bitcoin in three to four months. He claims Stability has more compute access than Microsoft or Google through national supercomputers, including a seven-million-hour grant on Summit.
- **Where Stability competes.** Mostaque names media (image, video, audio, soon 3D) and private or regulated data, which he says enterprises are more likely to run on-premises with open models. Examples include Bollywood video models trained with Eros and a partnership with Amazon SageMaker.
- **Optimisation over scale.** He cites Chinchilla, InstructGPT's 1.3B model and Flan-T5 outperforming larger GPT-3, and Stable Diffusion's drop from 5.6 to 0.9 seconds per image on an A100, as signs that language models will also shrink through optimisation.
- **Emerging markets.** He says Stability is helping deploy four million tablets to children in Malawi and expects Asian and African systems to leapfrog to generative AI, as East Asia did with mobile.
- **Safety and regulation.** Mostaque supports registering models above a certain compute threshold, labelling AI-generated advertising and offering dataset opt-outs. He opposes the EU approach of making creators responsible for outputs and favours provenance metadata, pointing to content-authenticity work with Adobe.
