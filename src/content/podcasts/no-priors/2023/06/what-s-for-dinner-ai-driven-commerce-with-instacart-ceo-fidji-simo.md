## Thesis

Instacart CEO Fidji Simo calls herself a "pragmatic technologist". She argues that generative AI matters because it finally lets software answer shoppers the way they think, in natural language and around meals and budgets, rather than in keywords. In her view the advantage goes to companies that pair large language models with unique data and a physical network that turns intent into delivered goods.

## Key points

- **AI was already core.** Simo says Instacart has always relied on AI. It runs a billion searches a quarter, has suggested a billion replacements for out-of-stock items, and uses AI to match shoppers to stores.
- **How the company is organised for AI.** Instacart has a centralized team under its Chief Architect and a federated approach in which every team uses AI. It also ran a hackathon and built an internal assistant that gives engineers access to GPT-4. Simo says marketing has produced 200+ AI-generated characters, and the legal team uses AI to track custom retailer terms.
- **Being AI-native.** Simo compares this shift to Facebook's move to mobile ads, where News Feed ads replaced shrunken desktop ads. Following the same logic, Ask Instacart was put into the main search box instead of a separate feature.
- **Bits to atoms.** She says the ChatGPT plugin reaches customers earlier, at moments like "I have a birthday party". She wants Instacart's 80,000-store network to become a platform that turns intent into a delivery within two hours.
- **Robotics economics.** Simo argues that automated warehouses have failed because of cost and distance, not technology. She sees Caper smart carts, rolled out at Kroger, Sobeys and Wakefern, as the more practical in-store technology.
- **Ads, agents and health.** Simo expects AI to bring more personalised ads, many more creative variations and new sponsored placements in conversational answers. Speaking about Metrodora, the institute she co-founded, she pushes back on AI doomers and says it takes 14 years for a research discovery to reach the clinic. Gil adds that, in his view, calls for regulation are premature.
