## Thesis

In a listener Q&A, Elad Gil argues that the market misreads generative AI as a continuation of the last decade of machine learning, when it is a new set of capabilities only about six months into enterprise planning. Both hosts see the field as early. They think heavy-handed regulation could do more harm than good, and that incumbents' AI announcements leave real room for startups.

## Key points

- **Misconceptions.** Gil says diffusion models and LLMs differ in kind from earlier CNN and RNN work. He notes that six months is a single enterprise planning cycle, so slow adoption is expected. He predicts more hype, not less.
- **Regulation and nuclear.** Gil, calling himself a long-term doomer, argues that industry calls for regulation are naive. He points to the nuclear regulator, after which no new reactor designs were approved for 50 years. Guo adds that newer, safer reactor generations go unbuilt because licensing costs too much.
- **Code generation and context.** Guo sees Copilot as early. She imagines an AI junior developer that turns Jira or Linear tickets into pull requests. She says context will expand to fill whatever window exists, and that structuring what goes into context is the real product problem. Gil expects window size to be reasonably solved within two years.
- **China.** Gil expects China to build local champions, citing MiniMax's $250 million raise at a $1.2 billion valuation and Baidu's $145 million AI fund. Guo argues that hardware sanctions will push domestic chip investment from firms such as Huawei.
- **Incumbents.** Gil expects most large companies to need 12 to 24 months to ship real AI products, and singles out Adobe for moving fast. Guo notes Salesforce's record of announcing products that see little deployment.
- **Startup ideas.** Gil argues that AI-generated integrations could make Salesforce and ERP vendors vulnerable for the first time. Guo points to SIEM triage, root-cause analysis and synthetic data, and to systems that write real-world economic events straight into financial databases.
