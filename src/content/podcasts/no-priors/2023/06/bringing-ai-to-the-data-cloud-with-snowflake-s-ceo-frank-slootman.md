## Thesis

Snowflake CEO Frank Slootman argues that natural language is the "last mile" in how people relate to data. He also argues that real enterprise value lies in structured, proprietary data that is well governed and kept in one place rather than siloed. Separately, he says the leadership lesson from his three CEO roles is simple but hard to practise: push urgency and standards in every interaction.

## Key points

- **Career choices.** Slootman tells graduates to avoid consulting and take real jobs in the economy. He agrees with Elad Gil that the industry and the company matter more than the role. He says Data Domain had 15 people, no revenue and no customers when he joined, and that it did $3 million in its first year.
- **Amp It Up.** He says people naturally slow down unless leaders set the tempo. If people leave because of higher standards, he argues, culture is sorting and sifting as it should. He says he killed customer success at every company he has run.
- **Architecture.** He says Snowflake's founders never saw it as a data warehouse company. He credits separating storage and compute and making clusters stateless with releasing pent-up demand. The aim is full-spectrum workloads, multi-cloud and applications running inside the governance perimeter through Snowpark, with Blue Yonder re-platforming its supply chain software on Snowflake.
- **LLMs and structured data.** Slootman says a language model will not explain an insurer's unusual bodily injury claims in Florida. He expects business- and industry-specific models, and sees Neeva's search expertise adding context to queries. He predicts that BI and dashboards will be "severely affected" by natural-language querying.
- **Streamlit and governance.** He says Streamlit is being brought inside Snowflake, and that it took two years to make Python "non-porous" enough for banks to accept it near core data.
- **Macro.** He defends consumption pricing as fair to customers, even when a bank growing 3% cuts back on Snowflake usage growing 22%. He says Snowflake avoids layoffs by "always pruning the tree".
