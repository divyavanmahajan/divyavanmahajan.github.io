## Thesis

Brian Schimpf, co-founder and CEO of Anduril, argues that the future of defense lies in large numbers of lower-cost systems, and that software is what makes them work. The US cannot out-build China in expensive crewed platforms, he says. Lessons from Ukraine show that those platforms are too vulnerable, so autonomy, sensing and speed of iteration now decide outcomes.

## Key points

- **Why cheap and many.** Schimpf points to pilot and recruiting shortfalls and to nuclear submarine output of about 1.7 per year. He says Ukraine showed that air defenses and pervasive commercial intelligence make large, fixed or expensive assets easy targets, which pushes forces toward small, dispersed units.
- **One software core.** Anduril's Lattice platform fuses sensor data to track objects. Schimpf says there are only about five sensor types, so detection software generalizes from base protection and border security to counter-drone systems, small drones and a school-bus-sized autonomous submarine.
- **Bounded autonomy.** Schimpf says the US will not field robots that make lethal decisions on their own, because accountability stays with the person who employs a weapon. He argues for a crawl-walk-run approach in which systems are predictable within known bounds. He sees LLMs as useful for synthesizing intelligence (where hallucination is unacceptable), for cyber work and for drafting mission plans that a human then edits.
- **Scaling the business.** Anduril has about 1,600 people. Schimpf cites a contract of roughly $250 million with Customs and Border Protection and a $1 billion counter-drone contract with SOCOM. He describes demand that jumps abruptly in scale, and a lobbying presence from day one.
- **Next shifts.** Schimpf expects cheap launch and satellite mega-constellations (which he estimates are about five percent played out) and lower-cost hypersonics to change how the US fights. He wants more investment in Pacific sensing and allied defense.
- **Why the climate changed.** Schimpf credits the invasion of Ukraine and China's stated posture for the tech industry's new openness to defense work. Gil recalls getting hate texts for investing at the seed round.
