## Thesis

Charles C. Mann, author of 1491, 1493 and The Wizard and the Prophet, tells Dwarkesh Patel that the meeting of Eurasia and the Americas, and the epidemics that followed, were close to inevitable, but how it played out depended on chance and human choices. On the future, Mann says the "prophets" are good at diagnosing problems while the "wizards" more often supply workable solutions, and that research effort is concentrated in the wrong places.

## Key points

- **Empires fall at weak points.** Mann says Cortés exploited resentment inside the Triple Alliance and Pizarro arrived after an Inca civil war and epidemic; he compares this to Bengal elites inviting in the East India Company.
- **Slavery's end is still debated.** Mann notes that about a third of the Code of Hammurabi concerns slaves, then lists explanations for abolition: machinery, industrialisation, the decline of colonialism and movements led by figures like Wilberforce and Las Casas.
- **"Collapse" is overstated.** Mann rejects collapse narratives such as Jared Diamond's, pointing out that 30 million Maya still live in the region; he attributes Native losses mainly to disease, a shock he likens to something 30 or 40 times worse than COVID.
- **New book on the West.** Mann describes a coming history of the American West framed around its likely future: hotter, drier, ethnically mixed, an energy centre and home to 294 federally recognised Native nations regaining sovereignty.
- **Silver, Bitcoin and hubris.** Responding to Patel's analogy, Mann agrees that Ming China, like Bitcoin miners, spent real resources to obtain a medium of exchange; he compares the Virginia Company's rosy pitches to Uber's cash burn.
- **Regulation and research.** Mann sees regulators as trapped by edge cases, contrasting the poisoning deaths that, in his account, created the FDA's review process with the fast COVID vaccine approvals; he expects geoengineering trials within 10 years and calls for research on cassava, tree crops and silvopastoral farming rather than wheat.
