## Thesis

Marc Andreessen, co-founder of a16z, tells Dwarkesh Patel that, following James Burnham, most of the economy is run by managers who cannot build new things, and that venture capital exists to fund founder-owners who can. He argues the world has too much capital and too few great entrepreneurs, so the binding question is how to get more founders like Elon Musk.

## Key points

- **AI as the next turn of software.** Andreessen says AI may upend every application category within five years, replacing forms-and-database apps with a dialogue between human and machine; a16z treats AI as part of its core software fund rather than a separate fund.
- **Managerial vs bourgeois capitalism.** He calls the economy about 99% managerial and says founders hand off to the "long suffering number two" at scale, which pushes ambitious people out to start new companies.
- **Money and time.** Andreessen doubts 100-year funds would help because projects need contact with reality, but finds bigger cheques more interesting, citing Tesla and SpaceX. He cites Bill Janeway's view that only computing and biotech have paid off for VC, because both drew on decades of federal research.
- **Crypto and NFTs.** He says a16z applies a buy-and-hold venture playbook to crypto rather than trading like a hedge fund, and defends NFTs as digital art, using the Mona Lisa to separate production cost from cultural value.
- **Risks to a16z.** Andreessen names bad picks, too little underlying technological change, or regulation outlawing innovation, pointing to the NRC not approving a new nuclear design in nearly 50 years.
- **Twitter, big tech and VC size.** He sees the public follow graph as underused, expects tech to move into education, healthcare and law, criticises universities as a cartel, and cites Andy Rachleff's estimate that VC is about 5x overfunded.
