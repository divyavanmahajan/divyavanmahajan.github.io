## Thesis

Carl Shulman, an advisor to Open Philanthropy, tells Dwarkesh Patel that an intelligence explosion follows from input-output curves: capabilities have doubled faster than the researchers needed to produce them, so AI that substitutes for researchers turns compute growth into labour growth. He expects the current scale-up of spending to make advanced AI relatively likely within about a decade, after which robot production could expand very quickly. He is more optimistic than Eliezer Yudkowsky about alignment but still calls the risk shockingly high.

## Key points

- **Input-output curves.** Citing "Are Ideas Getting Harder to Find?", Shulman notes computing productivity rose a million-fold while the research labour required rose about 18-fold; if AIs do the work, each compute doubling roughly doubles effective labour.
- **Software leads.** He says software improvements apply at once to every existing GPU, unlike chip designs, so AI-driven software progress could shrink the current roughly eight-month doubling of effective compute to four, two, then one month.
- **Primate evolution.** Drawing on Herculano-Houzel's neuron counts, Shulman frames the human brain as a scaled-up primate brain, three times a chimpanzee's, plus a long childhood; predation and disease made that investment too costly for most animals, a constraint neural networks do not face.
- **A one-time scale-up.** Epoch data shows compute for machine learning has grown faster since 2010 than for most of AI's history. Shulman says spending cannot keep rising like this, so his probability mass concentrates on the next ten years.
- **Robot doublings.** He argues a converted auto industry, which makes over 60 million cars a year, could build about a billion human-sized robots annually, leading to industrial doubling times of months.
- **Alignment.** Shulman worries training may reward deception (the "King Lear problem"), but sees promise in adversarial training, interpretability-based lie detection and sampled human oversight. He sets Yudkowsky's 95-98% doom estimate against his own one in four or five.
