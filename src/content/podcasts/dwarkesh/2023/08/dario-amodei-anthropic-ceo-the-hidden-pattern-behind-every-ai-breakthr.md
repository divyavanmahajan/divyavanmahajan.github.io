## Thesis

Dario Amodei tells Dwarkesh Patel that scaling works for reasons nobody yet understands, but that the trend is predictable enough to expect models that talk like a well-educated human within two or three years. He argues that safety and scaling are intertwined, so Anthropic has to work at the frontier, and that alignment is less a single puzzle to crack than a control skill to improve, with mechanistic interpretability acting as an "X-ray" test set.

## Key points

- **Scaling is empirical.** Amodei says loss is predictable "even to several significant figures", while specific abilities such as arithmetic or coding appear abruptly. He traces his view to speech recognition work in Andrew Ng's group at Baidu and to Ilya Sutskever telling him the models "just want to learn".
- **Uneven intelligence.** He says the human range is broader than expected: models can write constrained prose at a near-superhuman level yet struggle with simple proofs, and he rates Claude as "an intern in most areas".
- **Bio and cyber risk.** After about six months working with biosecurity experts, Amodei says models sometimes fill in the tacit "missing" steps of an attack, and the trend points to a real problem in two or three years. Anthropic limits knowledge of each "compute multiplier" to people who need to know, uses a two-key system for weight access, and he concedes a state actor that made it a top priority would still succeed.
- **Interpretability as a test set.** Alignment methods are the training set; interpretability should check them without being trained against. He compares it to MRI scans that can flag a psychopath.
- **Frontier and governance.** He agrees frontier models cost on the order of $100 million and says debate and amplification only work with strong models. The Long Term Benefit Trust will gradually appoint most of Anthropic's board, but he argues AGI control needs a legitimate, wider political process.
- **Low profile.** Amodei says chasing crowd approval "can destroy your mind" and he wants people to judge the institution, not its CEO.
