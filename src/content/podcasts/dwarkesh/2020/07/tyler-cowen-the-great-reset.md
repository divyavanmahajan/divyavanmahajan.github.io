## Thesis

Economist Tyler Cowen tells Dwarkesh Patel that COVID-19 is the "great reset" foreseen in his 2017 book The Complacent Class, arriving sooner than he expected. He argues growth can continue for centuries under liberal institutions, with war and weapons of mass destruction as the main threat, and that the pandemic's silver lining will be much better biomedicine.

## Key points

- **Institutions under stress.** Cowen says Congress, the executive branch and the regulatory state failed on masks and test-and-trace, while the Fed and Supreme Court worked. He expects strong biomedical dynamism but is unsure about other sectors.
- **Growth and time horizons.** Cowen reconciles Stubborn Attachments with a cyclical view: growth slows in periods (such as since 1973) but persists over centuries. He is not a "space optimist" and guesses humanity has 700-800 good years left.
- **War as the binding risk.** Cowen reads Steven Pinker's data as consistent with a "ratchet" of rarer but worse wars, and puts the chance of a nuke killing people in Patel's lifetime above 70%, most likely via terrorism.
- **Big business and stagnation.** Cowen says the startup decline is concentrated in retail and praises Alphabet and Amazon as innovators; stagnation sits in education, healthcare, non-tech services and government.
- **Education and talent.** Cowen sees Marginal Revolution University, built with Alex Tabarrok, as a replacement for the worst 20% of an education, not for universities. His advice: find one to three good mentors and a small group of friends. He is writing a book on spotting talent with Daniel Gross.
- **Outlook.** Cowen expects a vaccine in early 2021 of uncertain quality. His pessimistic case is years of scarring or a war alongside the pandemic; his optimistic case is a V-shaped recovery by March and reopened borders.
