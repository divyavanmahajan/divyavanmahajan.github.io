## Thesis

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang tells Dwarkesh Patel that Nvidia sits between electrons and tokens, doing "as much as needed, as little as possible", and that its advantage comes from CUDA's programmability, a huge installed base and the ability to convince the whole supply chain to invest ahead of demand. In a long, heated exchange he argues that export controls on AI chips hurt US technology leadership without denying China the compute it needs.

## Key points

- **Supply chain.** Patel cites about $100 billion in purchase commitments, possibly rising to $250 billion. Huang says upstream partners invest because Nvidia's downstream demand is so large, and that no bottleneck (CoWoS, HBM, EUV) lasts more than two or three years. He sees energy policy and trades such as plumbers and electricians as the harder constraints.
- **TPUs and ASICs.** Huang says AI needs more than matrix multiplies, and that programmability enabled Blackwell's 50x gain over Hopper despite Moore's law delivering about 25% a year. He calls Anthropic "a unique instance" behind all TPU and Trainium growth, and invites rivals to prove their cost claims on InferenceMAX or MLPerf.
- **Regret on Anthropic.** He admits he did not understand early that labs needed multi-billion-dollar investments from suppliers, which Google and AWS provided. Nvidia now invests in OpenAI, Anthropic and others, but declines to become a hyperscaler or to pick winners.
- **Allocation.** GPUs are allocated first in, first out once a purchase order is placed, never to the highest bidder. Huang says Nvidia and TSMC have worked together for nearly 30 years without a legal contract.
- **China.** Patel presses with Anthropic's Mythos cyber capabilities and Dario Amodei's export-control arguments. Huang says China has abundant energy, most mainstream chip capacity, half of the world's AI researchers, and Huawei shipping millions of chips. He argues conceding the market will push open models to run best on a non-US stack, and calls for researcher dialogue instead.
- **Groq.** Nvidia is folding Groq into CUDA to serve a new market for premium, fast-response tokens.
