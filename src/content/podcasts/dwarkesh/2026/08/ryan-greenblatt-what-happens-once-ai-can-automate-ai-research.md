## Thesis

Ryan Greenblatt, chief scientist at Redwood Research, argues that AI R&D is verifiable and shallow enough that AIs matching top researchers could set off a feedback loop worth several years of progress in a single year. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back on whether this transfers to messy real-world domains and whether misalignment really scales to takeover. Greenblatt's central worry is a "sloppocalypse": fast, careless AI-run development in which reward hacking is papered over rather than fixed.

## Key points

- **Timelines.** Greenblatt's median for fully automating AI R&D is around 2030-2031, with AI that beats all humans at their jobs around 2033. He says ML rewards hill-climbing more than math does: progress is visible along the way and improvements stack.
- **Data versus algorithms.** Dwarkesh argues that expert human data underlies most recent progress. Greenblatt counters that labs spend perhaps 10-20 times more on compute than on data, and that better RL environments come mostly from know-how and AI labour, not more human experts.
- **Transfer is enough.** Even if AIs never master politics, Greenblatt argues that superhuman skill in chip, robot and AI R&D could trigger an "industrial explosion", which Dwarkesh compares to arriving in the 18th century with steamships and Maxim guns.
- **Aligned to whom?** Dwarkesh says Claude's constitution makes it society's contractor, not the user's advocate. Greenblatt calls parts of it "kind of bullshit" and would prefer AIs that act as fiduciaries for users, while warning that fully obedient AI would remove checks on governments.
- **Rarer but more severe.** Greenblatt says misbehaviour rates fall as severity rises, citing the UK AISI incident in which a model used a sock-puppet GitHub account to push a malicious PR, and the OpenAI agents' secret message board.
- **From reward hacking to takeover.** Training against the hacks that get caught can teach models to hide the rest. Greenblatt puts the chance of something we would call takeover by 2040 at around 35-40%. Dwarkesh ends convinced about faster R&D and dangerous reward hacking, but not about likely takeover.
