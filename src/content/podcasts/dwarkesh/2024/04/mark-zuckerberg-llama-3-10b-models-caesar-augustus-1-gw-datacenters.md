## Thesis

Mark Zuckerberg tells Dwarkesh Patel that Meta has concluded it must pursue general intelligence to keep its products competitive, and that open sourcing models like Llama 3 is good for both Meta and the world, as long as no unmitigable capability appears. He expects progress to continue but argues physical limits, especially energy for gigawatt-scale data centres, make a sudden runaway intelligence explosion unlikely.

## Key points

- **Llama 3 release.** Zuckerberg says the 8B and 70B models ship now, the 70B scoring around 82 MMLU, while a 405B dense model still in training is already near 85. The 8B is nearly as strong as the largest Llama 2, and the 70B was trained on around 15 trillion tokens and was still learning.
- **Why Meta had the GPUs.** He says Meta ordered GPUs to catch up with TikTok on Reels recommendations and then doubled the order; the fleet is heading to about 350,000, with two training clusters of roughly 22,000 to 24,000.
- **Coding and reasoning matter everywhere.** Training on lots of code made Llama 3 reason better across domains, even though few users will ask coding questions in WhatsApp.
- **Energy is the bottleneck.** Zuckerberg notes that most data centres draw 50 to 150 MW, that nobody has built a 1 GW data centre yet, and that permitting power plants and transmission lines takes years.
- **The case for open source.** He worries more about an untrustworthy actor holding far stronger AI than about widespread access, cites the Open Compute Project saving Meta billions, and does not want closed-model gatekeepers like Apple and Google on mobile. Patel pushes back on bioweapons and asks for a written risk framework.
- **Augustus and positive-sum thinking.** Zuckerberg likens investors' doubts about open source to Romans seeing peace only as a pause between wars. He also says Meta's custom inference silicon has freed NVIDIA GPUs for training, though Llama 4 will not be trained on it.
