## Thesis

Tyler Cowen, discussing his book GOAT: Who is the Greatest Economist of all Time, argues that the classic economists still help explain AI, risk and growth. His main frame is Hayekian: humanity chose a decentralized world long ago, so AI cannot simply be legislated into safety. In his view, relative safety has come from benevolent hegemons. He expects AI to end the great stagnation and return growth to early 20th-century rates, but not to anything like science fiction.

## Key points

- **Animal spirits.** Asked about Keynes's view that investment is often irrational, Cowen says returns on private investment are highly skewed and only a few percent make anything. He estimates that innovators keep about 2-5% of the social value they create. He puts finance at a fairly constant 2% of wealth, not GDP.
- **Firms are markets.** Cowen rejects Coase's view that firms are islands of central planning. He calls the later Soviet Union a badly run decentralized system and credits most Soviet and Chinese growth to urbanization.
- **AI agents.** Cowen predicts that AI agents will form their own markets, using Bitcoin and NFT-like property rights at first. He expects a productivity split between people who delegate well to AI and people who don't.
- **Growth limits.** Cowen does not find decades of 10% growth plausible. He points to bottlenecks from law, regulation and human behaviour, and suggests the universe may not be "legible" enough for more intelligence to solve problems such as unifying general relativity and quantum mechanics.
- **Risk and anarchy.** Cowen's bigger long-run worry is cheap destruction, such as a $50,000 nuke, which could push civilization back to a "medieval Balkans" existence. He wants the US and UK to lead in AI. He argues that any anarchy that survives ends up recreating collusion, and he names payment networks as the real risk of network-based collusion.
- **Internet writing.** Cowen argues that multidisciplinary internet writing, such as Scott Alexander's and Scott Aaronson's, is a new field. He expects no new GOAT contender unless it is something AI-related.
