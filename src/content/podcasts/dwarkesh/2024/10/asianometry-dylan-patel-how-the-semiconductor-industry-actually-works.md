## Thesis

Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis) argues that AI compute will keep scaling fast as long as capital flows in, and that China could match any single US lab if it decided to centralize its compute. Jon Y (Asianometry) agrees the chip industry can deliver, but stresses that semiconductor know-how is siloed and that the whole investment wave depends on the next model generation being impressive.

## Key points

- **China could centralize.** Patel estimates more than a million NVIDIA H20s still reach China legally each year, plus around 600,000 Huawei Ascend 910Bs. Power is not China's constraint, so a 10-gigawatt site could be built quickly and hidden among heavy industry.
- **Export controls are piecemeal.** Patel says China was about 48% of ASML's and 45% of Applied Materials' recent revenue, and the equipment allowed in can make chips better than those NVIDIA may legally sell there. Jon Y thinks China's chip industry cannot be stopped.
- **A siloed, master-apprentice industry.** Both describe each layer (etch, lithography, packaging) as held by one to three firms with undocumented craft knowledge. Jon Y argues that without AI demand, nodes like N2 would be hard to justify economically.
- **Taiwan concentration.** Patel says about 80% of the world's 28nm output is in Taiwan, which covers display drivers and microcontrollers, not only AI chips. Cars carry 2,000+ chips.
- **Where efficiency comes from.** Patel says most H100 power goes to moving data, not computing, so architecture alone could give 100x gains. On cost of ownership he puts GPUs at about 80%, the data centre at 10% and power at 10%.
- **Scaling path and financing.** Patel expects 300-500K-GPU clusters next year after today's 100K, and about 1e30 FLOPs by 2028-29. He frames big-tech spending as Pascal's wager and expects Sam Altman to raise $50-100 billion. Jon Y counters that it all depends on GPT-5 being good.
