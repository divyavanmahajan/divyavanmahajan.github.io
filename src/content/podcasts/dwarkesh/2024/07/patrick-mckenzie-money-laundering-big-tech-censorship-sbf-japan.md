## Thesis

Patrick McKenzie (patio11) tells Dwarkesh Patel that VaccinateCA, a volunteer effort that began on a Discord server, became the de facto US clearinghouse for COVID vaccine locations because no institution saw the job as its own. He blames incentives rather than malice: governments avoided software risk, big tech avoided political risk, and funders followed their own processes. He argues that tech must engage with government more skilfully, and he carries the same lens into crypto, anti-money-laundering rules and censorship.

## Key points

- **Nobody owned the problem.** McKenzie says county health departments, governors and two administrations each saw only a narrow role. Large tech firms had public-health teams ready to help, but after January 6th their policy staff told them to stay out of the news.
- **Politics over throughput.** He describes California's 1A/1B tiers as shaped by lobbying (veterinarians, teachers' unions), a 57-page New York eligibility web app, and bans on giving out expiring end-of-day doses. California reported injecting only 25% of its allocation.
- **Funding a lifesaving project.** VaccinateCA's total budget was $1.2 million. One CEO wired $100,000 overnight, other funders took six weeks, and the last $100K came from McKenzie's daughter's college fund. He argues that waste is acceptable when the alternative is losing.
- **Crypto and bank surveillance.** McKenzie judges crypto by realised value against the tens or hundreds of billions of dollars invested, and offers to check back in 2030. He agrees with crypto advocates that banks act as a policy arm of the state, running analyst teams that file memos to FinCEN that few people read.
- **Censorship by proxy.** Citing Missouri v. Biden, he says government officials used tech platforms to suppress constitutionally protected speech, and that tech should push back publicly.
- **SBF and laundering.** He calls SBF an extremely sophisticated money launderer whose Alameda arbitrage relied on a co-operative Japanese bank. He adds that reporters could have checked Mt. Gox's insolvency simply by asking Mizuho whether it was still sending wires.
