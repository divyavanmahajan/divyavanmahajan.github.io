## Thesis

Leopold Aschenbrenner, formerly on OpenAI's superalignment team, argues that straight-line trends in compute and algorithms, combined with "unhobbling" models into agents, point to a drop-in remote worker around 2027 and a fast intelligence explosion after that. Because superintelligence would be decisive militarily, he expects the US national security state to take over, and he argues that locking down lab secrets and building clusters in the US matters more than most people in AI realise. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back on the speed of takeoff and on handing the technology to the government.

## Key points

- **The trillion-dollar cluster.** Aschenbrenner extrapolates training compute growing about 0.5 OOMs a year: from GPT-4's roughly 25,000 A100s (about $500 million, 10 MW) to a gigawatt cluster by 2026, 10 GW by 2028, and by 2030 a 100 GW cluster using over 20% of US electricity.
- **Unhobbling and the data wall.** He expects agents once models can use millions of tokens of test-time compute instead of a few hundred. The main risk to his timeline is data: Common Crawl is about 30 trillion tokens and Llama-3 already trained on 15 trillion.
- **Security and secrets.** He says labs are close to trivial to steal from, citing DeepMind rating itself level zero in its Frontier Safety Framework. Putting clusters in Middle Eastern states would, in his view, let them steal weights or seize compute.
- **Government project vs. private labs.** Aschenbrenner predicts a joint venture between labs, cloud providers and government, run like Operation Warp Speed. Patel counters that a government-held ASI is no safer than a private one and that nationalisation cannot be undone.
- **Leaving OpenAI.** Aschenbrenner says he was fired after sharing a security memo with board members, and over a brainstorming document he had shared with three external researchers. He turned down equity worth close to a million dollars rather than sign the NDA.
- **Research speed.** Patel doubts that adding AI researchers scales output, noting 100x more researchers than a century ago without 100x faster progress. Aschenbrenner replies that inputs still matter and that AI researchers can be copied without training costs.
