## Thesis

Stripe CEO Patrick Collison tells Dwarkesh Patel that many people in their 20s might do better to leave San Francisco and spend years building deep expertise, because its founder culture undervalues that. Across science, payments and software, he argues that results depend on culture, structure and taking goals seriously rather than on spending or defensibility. Stripe, which he says handles about a trillion dollars a year, is his main example.

## Key points

- **Depth over founding.** Collison cites Genentech co-founder Herb Boyer, who needed decades of expertise first, and the book Apprentice to Genius on how mentors pass on problem selection and high standards.
- **Science funding is not just dollars.** The US had roughly 1% as many scientists before World War II, yet output did not scale with spending. In a Fast Grants survey, 79% of recipients said their research would change a lot with flexible funding. Arc funds scientists rather than projects, shares infrastructure, and offers careers outside the principal investigator track.
- **Payments for AI agents.** He expects a smooth slide from usage-based billing to agent-made purchases, raising questions on liability and KYC/AML, where crypto might play a role.
- **Moats and markets.** Collison calls moats overrated and cites Conquest's third law on organisations drifting from their goals. Stripe's Frontier commitment raised a billion dollars for carbon removal with Shopify, Alphabet, Meta and JP Morgan; 74% of its 40-50 suppliers said it helped cause their founding.
- **Multi-decade abstractions.** Pointing to iOS classes still prefixed NS from NeXT, he says Stripe asks whether it can stand behind an API in 2044. He defends card interchange at roughly 2-3% and sees cross-border sales taxes as a bigger change.
- **Reliability and LLMs.** Stripe deploys core services about 1,000 times a day at 99.9995% availability, and its internal LLM tooling now handles millions of calls a day, for example optimising SQL queries.
