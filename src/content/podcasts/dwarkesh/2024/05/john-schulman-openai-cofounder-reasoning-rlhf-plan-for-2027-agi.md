## Thesis

John Schulman, OpenAI cofounder and head of post-training, argues that much of the recent progress comes from post-training, and that training models on long-horizon tasks could unlock assistants that carry out whole projects. Pressed by Dwarkesh Patel on what happens if AGI arrives within two or three years, he says it would call for caution and coordination among labs, but says he has no full answer.

## Key points

- **Pre- vs post-training.** Schulman says pre-training imitates the web and produces calibrated base models, while post-training narrows them to a helpful assistant persona. He attributes most of GPT-4's roughly hundred-point Elo gain since launch to post-training.
- **Longer tasks.** He expects that within one or two years, models will be able to run whole coding projects, helped by RL on long tasks and better recovery from errors. On generalization, he says about 30 examples taught early ChatGPT about its own limitations across capabilities it was not trained on.
- **If AGI comes early.** Schulman would slow training and deployment, and says the small number of labs able to train the largest models makes coordination possible. He prefers releasing models a little at a time, each improving safety along with capability, backed by evals, red teaming and monitoring.
- **How ChatGPT came about.** It grew out of WebGPT and instruction-following work and was built on GPT-3.5. He says chat was easier to label than "complete this text helpfully", which gave the model a more coherent personality.
- **Model Spec and raters.** The Model Spec weighs four stakeholders: end user, developer, platform and the rest of humanity. Raters are an international group, with STEM labelers more often in India and writing labelers more often U.S.-based.
- **Moats and his own job.** Post-training is "something of a moat" because of tacit knowledge, though distillation erodes it. He guesses AI could replace his job in maybe five years.
