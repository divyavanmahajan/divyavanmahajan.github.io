## Thesis

Naval War College historian Sarah Paine argues that geography sets what a country can do strategically. Britain, behind its sea moat, won World War II with blockade, convoys, peripheral theatres and alliances, after learning hard lessons from World War I. Dwarkesh Patel pushes back that Allied industrial output, not strategy, decided the war. Paine replies that the outcome depended on many factors, none of which could have been removed without changing it.

## Key points

- **Lessons from World War I.** Paine says Britain had twice as many deaths in World War I, when it fought on the main front, as in World War II, when it fought on the periphery. It also convoyed merchant ships from the start and coordinated civil and military leaders with its allies.
- **Battle of the Atlantic.** German U-boats working from French ports nearly sank a "terminal" amount of shipping, and Britain depended on imports for about half its food. Enigma decrypts, radar, hedgehogs, escort carriers and US shipbuilding turned the battle. In May 1943 Germany lost 41 U-boats.
- **Peripheral theatres.** Between two-thirds and three-quarters of German ground forces fought Russia. At Normandy, Russia was tying down 228 Axis divisions against 58 in the west, which made the landings possible.
- **Russia's and China's geographic prisons.** Paine argues that narrow seas hem both countries in. She points to Russia's only aircraft carrier, which has been under repair since 2018, and Ukraine showing that drones and shore weapons can wreck a navy. She says China has 20 neighbours and no allies.
- **Industry vs strategy.** Patel says the Axis had about a quarter of the Allies' combined GDP and that China is now the leading manufacturer. Paine counters with the idea of limited versus unlimited war aims, citing Bismarck's limited wars. She expects a Taiwan war to destroy TSMC and bring sanctions that slow China's growth.
