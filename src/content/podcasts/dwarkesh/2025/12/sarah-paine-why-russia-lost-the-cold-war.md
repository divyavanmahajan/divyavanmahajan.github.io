## Thesis

In the final lecture of her series with Dwarkesh Patel, historian Sarah Paine gives a "tour of the counter-arguments" to the claim that Ronald Reagan single-handedly defeated the Soviet Union. She weighs external explanations (US presidents, the China card, submarines) against internal ones (empire, economy, Gorbachev's mistakes), and argues the West may have barely won, helped by statesmen like George Bush Sr. and Helmut Kohl. She closes with lessons for what she calls a second Cold War.

## Key points

- **Arms race asymmetry.** Paine notes US, NATO and Japanese GNP combined was seven times the Soviet figure; the CIA estimated Soviet defence spending at 20% of GNP, but later data put it at 40-50% or more.
- **External pressures.** She credits the Helsinki Accords' human-rights clauses, Carter's human-rights campaign, the 1969 Sino-Soviet border war that let the US side with China, and US submarines threatening Soviet second-strike forces.
- **Internal rot.** Oil was up to 55% of the Soviet budget when prices collapsed; by 1990 there were about 76 ethnic rebellions; 20-40% of crops rotted. Paine cites Gorbachev telling the Central Committee that the threat was superior economies, not invincible armies.
- **Gorbachev's false assumptions.** He expected history to move only towards communism, Eastern Europe to thank him, and NATO to dissolve with the Warsaw Pact.
- **Buying unification.** Kohl and Bush fast-tracked German unification, with West Germany paying billions of Deutschmarks, including 15 billion for housing returning Soviet soldiers. Paine says the Gulf War ground fighting stopped after 100 hours to protect Cold War termination, and Bush was not re-elected despite his role.
- **Q&A.** Dwarkesh asks how central planning lasted 74 years; Paine points to a war economy, unreliable data (heaviest TVs), her 1988-89 Moscow year with a 77-item supermarket, and Russia's lack of legal and commercial traditions.
