## Thesis

Arthur Kroeber of Gavekal Dragonomics argues that China's industrial success comes less from picking winners than from an export-driven, highly competitive ecosystem backed by a state willing to lose money for years. He sees China as too large and too integrated to contain, and argues the US should invite Chinese investment and rebuild communication channels rather than replay the Cold War.

## Key points

- **The state as VC fund.** Kroeber compares China to a giant VC fund that backs many bets and lets successes pay for failures. EV subsidies are estimated at $200-300 billion; the contrast he draws is the US reaction to one failed loan to Solyndra.
- **Tesla as catalyst.** Allowing a wholly-owned Tesla Gigafactory in Shanghai in 2018 showed BYD and rivals how to design appealing cars; by 2022 they competed with Tesla on price and quality, Kroeber says.
- **Not Japan.** China bans the bank-industry cross shareholdings that turned Japan's land crash into debt deflation. Kroeber puts total debt at about 300% of GDP and disputes Victor Shih's 200% estimate for government debt, citing double counting.
- **Effectiveness over efficiency.** Kroeber defends China's wasteful growth as the price of 20 years of 10% growth, but says Xi Jinping's focus on technology and a supply-side strategy has left demand weak and growth below potential now.
- **Electrification bet.** China's generating capacity is more than double that of the US, and electricity is about 30% of its energy consumption. Kroeber sees this as an enabling advantage for AI, though he gives the US an edge in AI applications because China is a walled garden.
- **Broken communication.** Kroeber notes Trump inherited about 100 working-level US-China dialogues in 2017 and cut them to one. He links the loss of CDC ties to the early COVID response and argues 140 countries trading more with China makes an anti-China bloc unworkable.
