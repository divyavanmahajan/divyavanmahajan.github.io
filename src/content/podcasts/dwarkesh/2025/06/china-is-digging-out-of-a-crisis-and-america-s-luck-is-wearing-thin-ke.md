## Thesis

Ken Rogoff, Harvard professor, former IMF chief economist and author of Our Dollar, Your Problem, argues that China's overbuilt, investment-heavy economy is in a crisis it will take years to dig out of, while the US has relied on luck as much as dynamism. He expects high US debt and an inflexible political system to end in an inflation crisis rather than a default, and the dollar's privilege to decline gradually.

## Key points

- **China is overbuilt.** Rogoff says China is overbuilt in housing and infrastructure, with real estate a third of the economy by some measures and consumption around 45% versus close to 70% in the US. He traces the problem to the 2010 stimulus and says Xi Jinping replaced technocrats with loyalists.
- **Bearish on catch-up.** He expects China to gain only about a percent a year on the US in nominal GDP, and questions official growth figures in the Xi Jinping era.
- **Japan's lesson.** Rogoff now thinks US pressure after the 1985 Plaza Accord pushed Japan to liberalise too fast, leading to a crisis he and Carmen Reinhart date to 1992. He estimates Japan might be 50% wealthier per person without it, then softens that to 25% or 30%.
- **Inflation, not default.** Because the US prints its own currency, Rogoff expects a shock to produce 10-20% inflation rather than a Greek-style crisis. He warns markets place too much faith in Federal Reserve independence.
- **AGI and rates.** Rogoff sees AI as upward pressure on real interest rates, through energy needs and capital investment, but says fast AGI would dwarf today's populism and that fiscal crises stem from politics, not arithmetic.
- **Dollar dominance.** He calls the exorbitant privilege worth hundreds of billions of dollars on $37 trillion of debt, notes China is building alternative payment rails and buying gold, and expects foreign equities, especially European, to outperform.
