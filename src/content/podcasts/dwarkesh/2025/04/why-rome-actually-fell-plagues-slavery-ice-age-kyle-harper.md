## Thesis

Kyle Harper, historian and author of The Fate of Rome, argues that disease and natural climate change played a decisive part in turning Rome from the centre of a Mediterranean empire into a shrunken city. Without the sixth-century plague and cooling, he thinks a pan-Mediterranean Roman Empire could plausibly have survived. He also tells Dwarkesh Patel that Rome's advanced markets and finance could not produce an industrial takeoff because it had no self-sustaining science.

## Key points

- **A double shock.** Harper says the Plague of Justinian hit just as Justinian had retaken Africa and most of Italy. At the same time a series of volcanic eruptions cooled the climate by one to two degrees for decades, bringing famine. By the end of the period the city of Rome had only 50,000 to 100,000 people.
- **Why no industrial revolution.** Rome had strong property rights, contract enforcement and banks, which Harper calls the most advanced financial markets before the 17th or 18th century. What it lacked was the 17th-century mix of basic science, Baconian empiricism and "useful knowledge" that linked people like Newton and Denis Papin.
- **A slave society.** Dwarkesh cites estimates of 10 to 20% of the population enslaved, and Harper puts Pompeii near 30%. Harper says Roman slavery rested on property law rather than race. He rejects Aldo Schiavone's argument that cheap slave labour blocked mechanisation.
- **Agriculture and disease.** Harper argues that farming raised the infectious disease burden over thousands of years. He adds that a heavy disease load in childhood probably left pre-industrial populations both shorter and, on average, lower in cognitive ability.
- **Plague's route and the next pandemic.** Harper thinks the sixth-century plague left its rodent reservoir in the Tian Shan mountains, passed through India and crossed the Indian Ocean by ship. On future pandemics, he says COVID-19 spread because of its latency, unlike SARS-CoV-1 in 2003, and that oddball pathogens deserve more attention.
- **AI and de-extinction.** Harper now uses AI such as Deep Research as a constant research partner, but says it cannot yet match historians at synthesis. He doubts de-extinction is a reason to worry less about species loss, because a mammoth has no mammoth steppe to return to.
