## Thesis

Mark Zuckerberg tells Dwarkesh Patel that AI is a large space where no single lab will win everything, and Meta is betting on fast, personal, multimodal assistants built on its own open Llama models. He finds the case for automated AI research compelling, but argues compute buildout, energy, testing capacity and people learning to use AI will keep progress from being a sudden takeoff.

## Key points

- **Llama 4 and benchmarks.** Meta released Scout and Maverick, with "Little Llama" and the 2-trillion-plus-parameter Behemoth to follow. Zuckerberg says Meta AI has almost a billion monthly users and that tuning Maverick to top LM Arena was easy, but the released model was not tuned for it because Meta measures user value instead.
- **AI coding.** He predicts that within 12 to 18 months most code for Meta's Llama efforts will be written by AI agents that set goals, run tests and fix issues, not autocomplete.
- **Bottlenecks.** Gigawatt clusters need chips, permits and energy. An ads-team experiment showed Meta already has more good hypotheses than compute or user cohorts to test them.
- **DeepSeek and China.** Zuckerberg credits DeepSeek's low-level optimisations but says export controls forced that work, leaving it text-only. He argues Llama 4 matches it on text with a smaller model and leads on multimodal.
- **Open source and distillation.** He defends the 700-million-user licence clause as a way to make large cloud companies talk to Meta first. He says distillation can keep 90-95% of a model's intelligence at 10% of the size, but warns that models encode values and that coding models from other countries could plant vulnerabilities.
- **Relationships and jobs.** Citing the stat that the average American has fewer than three friends, he expects AI companionship to become accepted. He also expects AI to make phone support viable for Meta's three and a half billion daily users and to lead to more support hires, not fewer.
