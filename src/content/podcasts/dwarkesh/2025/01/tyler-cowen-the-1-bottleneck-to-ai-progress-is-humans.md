## Thesis

Recorded live at the Progress Conference 2024, Tyler Cowen tells Dwarkesh Patel that AI will not produce 20%-plus economic growth. When intelligence becomes abundant, he argues, the other factors of production become more binding, so he expects AI to raise growth by roughly half a percentage point a year: transformative over 30 to 40 years, barely noticeable in any single year. Patel pushes back repeatedly with population-driven growth models and China's catch-up decades.

## Key points

- **Cost disease and slow sectors.** Cowen estimates that government consumption, healthcare (almost 20%), education and nonprofits make up about half the US economy, and these will adopt AI slowly; replacing them could take 30 years.
- **Trust both sets of experts.** He says he trusts AI researchers on AI capability but diffusion researchers on adoption, and that combining the two gives "amazing in the long run" but a "tough slog" in the short run. He rejects Chad Jones's population-driven model as too one-factor, citing Renaissance Florence's 60,000 people.
- **Humans are the bottleneck.** Asked for specifics, Cowen points at the audience: regulation, energy supply and opposition from people who do not want their world changed will slow AI. He thinks Bay Area people overvalue intelligence, while classical economists such as Ricardo and Malthus understood diminishing returns.
- **Writing for the AIs.** Cowen says he wrote his book Goat: Who's the Greatest Economist of All Time? mostly for AI readers and that his next book is aimed even more at them. Because AI raises the value of other inputs, he sees himself becoming less a producer of content and more a connector of people.
- **Variance, not decline.** Disagreeing with fellow conference speaker Patrick's "competency crisis", Cowen argues the top of the youth distribution (chess, NBA, podcasting) and the very bottom are improving while a thick middle is getting worse. He also says he called peak EA just before SBF's collapse.
- **War as the main risk.** His biggest worry about progress is that new technologies become instruments of war, with wars becoming rarer but more destructive.
