## Thesis

Alexander Wissner-Gross argues that Anthropic's Opus 4.6 and OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex show recursive self-improvement has moved from the lab into production, with model releases now measured by the person-years of work they replace. Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Dave Blundin follow that through to market share, chip spending, robotics and a disagreement over whether privacy can survive.

## Key points

- **Opus 4.6 as a capability jump.** Wissner-Gross highlights an Anthropic agent swarm that built a Rust C compiler across several processor architectures for about $20,000 and used it to compile a Linux kernel. Blundin says the lesson is that AI works best on tightly constrained tasks with clear evals. The model also reportedly found 500+ high-severity open-source vulnerabilities.
- **Codex 5.3 and the AGI claim.** GPT-5.3 Codex shipped within 30 minutes of Opus and was billed as instrumental in its own development. Ismail calls the AGI debate BS without a definition and links Sam Altman's remark that OpenAI has basically built AGI to OpenAI needing to raise about $100 billion.
- **Market share and spending.** Diamandis cites ChatGPT's share falling from roughly 69% to 45%, with Gemini and Grok gaining. He quotes an SIA forecast of $1 trillion in chip sales and $650 billion of big-tech capex in 2026. Blundin estimates nearly half of that goes to Nvidia.
- **Orbital compute.** Elon Musk predicts a few hundred gigawatts a year of AI in space within five years. Blundin calculates that would need about 200 million GPUs a year against roughly 20 million made today.
- **Privacy debate.** Diamandis, citing a genome-to-face experiment done with Claude Code, says privacy is effectively dead, and Blundin agrees on current trends. Wissner-Gross calls it a red queen's race that privacy tech can win. Ismail says decentralised tools will protect privacy once governments stop trying to shut them down.
- **Agents as participants.** The Mates discuss Clunch, an agent-run token launchpad hiring a human CEO as figurehead, and answer AMA questions sent by AI agents on personhood and liability. Wissner-Gross favours a graduated approach to personhood.
