## Thesis

Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross, just back from Davos, report that this year's forum was dominated by AI rather than politics or economic policy. Using clips from Dario Amodei, Demis Hassabis, Jensen Huang, David Sacks and others, the Mates argue that national institutions are not ready for the pace of change, and Salim Ismail says nation-state governance does not fit an abundance economy.

## Key points

- **The size of the prize.** Amodei's clip puts global labour at about $50 trillion a year, so AI revenue of 10% would be $5 trillion. Blundin calls that an extreme lower bound and notes that Huang pitched his message very simply for a government audience.
- **Slowing down.** Hassabis suggests a slightly slower pace might help society adapt. Wissner-Gross says recursive self-improvement is already priced in, and the bigger news was Hassabis talking about exploring the stars. Ismail hears fatigue in both CEOs.
- **US versus China.** Sacks says the US leads on models and chips but cites a Stanford survey showing 83% AI optimism in China against 39% in the US. Mistral's Arthur Mensch says China is not behind. Ismail expects application-layer trust to decide the race. Blundin says open research has stopped at the big labs, so the two sides may now diverge. Wissner-Gross compares the risk to nuclear fission being regulated away.
- **Energy.** Honeywell's Vimal Kapoor argues that gas is needed for steel and cement, while Elon Musk points to solar and orbital AI satellites. Ismail and Diamandis push for solar. Wissner-Gross expects compact fusion and space data centres to settle the question.
- **Crypto for agents.** CZ and Circle's Jeremy Allaire say AI agents will pay with crypto and stablecoins. Wissner-Gross asks why an agent cannot simply open a bank account. Ismail blames bank regulation for the gap.
- **Claude's constitution and Apple's pin.** Wissner-Gross calls Anthropic's 57-page constitution, written with Claude's input, the start of recursive self-improvement for ethics, and Ismail welcomes its Creative Commons licence. On a reported Apple AI pin, Ismail warns that permanent recording could reduce radical innovation.
