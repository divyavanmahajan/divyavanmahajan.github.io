## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross, recording from CES follow-ups and Davos, argue that Claude Code with Opus 4.5 marks an inflection point in agent autonomy. They disagree on what that means for incumbents: some see SaaS and the web under threat, while Wissner-Gross expects markets to rebalance because everyone has the same tools. He also argues that AI solving open math problems is the first step towards solving problems in other sciences.

## Key points

- **Robots at CES.** Diamandis counted about 38 humanoid and 12 robot-hand companies at CES and compares them to the 253 US car makers of 1908, expecting a shakeout. Wissner-Gross reads Nvidia's Cosmos, Alpamayo and Vera Rubin as Nvidia commoditising its complement and becoming a vertically integrated data-centre vendor.
- **Agents in consulting.** A clip has McKinsey's Bob Sternfels describing 40,000 humans and 20,000 agents. Ismail expects consultancies to do well but says one agent per person is far too low.
- **SaaS after "Clopus".** Wissner-Gross says Claude Code plus Opus 4.5, and GPT-5.2 Codex, push autonomy horizons at a possibly hyper-exponential rate. One Mate reports a Claude bill of $100 to $1,000 a day. Blundin says rigid incumbents are doomed but flexible ones survive. Ismail predicts an AI-native enterprise stack will grow up alongside systems of record.
- **Siri and the web.** Diamandis reads a post claiming Gemini on iPhone and the Universal Commerce Protocol could make websites obsolete. Wissner-Gross and Ismail disagree, calling UCP a checkout standard and pointing to how slowly user habits change.
- **Capex and frontier labs.** Wissner-Gross argues trillions in capex need consumers and enterprises to buy far more costly reasoning compute. Diamandis expects Google or Amazon to try to buy Anthropic. Wissner-Gross considers big mergers unlikely under current antitrust policy.
- **Math, chips and energy.** Wissner-Gross reports GPT-5.2 Pro, with Harmonic's Aristotle checking the proofs, solving Erdős problems several times a week. The Mates link OpenAI's Cerebras deal to SRAM-based inference. Diamandis warns that China generates far more electricity than the US.
