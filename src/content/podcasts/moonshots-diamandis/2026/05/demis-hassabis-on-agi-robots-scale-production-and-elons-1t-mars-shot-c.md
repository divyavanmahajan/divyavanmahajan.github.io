## Thesis

Recorded live at MIT, this news roundtable pits the Mates' exponential optimism against co-author Steven Kotler, who says that in his daily work as a writer and neuroscientist AI is far more limited than the hosts claim. Alexander Wissner-Gross argues that moonshots are now achievable, so companies should be structured, and their leaders paid, for those goals from day one. Demis Hassabis features only through a news item about his view on AGI.

## Key points

- **Moonshot compensation.** Peter Diamandis describes a reported SpaceX package that pays out when Elon Musk reaches a million-person Mars colony and a $7.5 trillion valuation. Wissner-Gross sees a new kind of corporation that exists to achieve moonshots; Salim Ismail calls it an exponential-organization pay package.
- **Musk v. OpenAI.** The hosts cover the trial, including Greg Brockman's diary. Wissner-Gross says OpenAI and Anthropic show that idealistic non-profits end up needing capital, while Dave Blundin argues Musk "doesn't need to win to win" if the case slows OpenAI down.
- **Robots at scale.** Diamandis reports Figure AI going from one robot a day to one an hour and 1X targeting 10,000 Neo robots this year. Ismail questions the humanoid form factor; Wissner-Gross expects humanoids to outnumber humans by the end of the 2030s and nanorobots to follow.
- **AGI debate.** On Hassabis's 50/50 estimate that a breakthrough (perhaps world models) is still needed, Wissner-Gross argues AGI arrived with GPT-3 in 2020. Kotler calls AI a convergent engine that has got worse at writing; the hosts tell him to build a benchmark.
- **AI and employment.** A Hangzhou court reportedly ruled that a company could not demote a worker because AI automated his role. Wissner-Gross notes the irony that China is setting standards for AI disemployment.
- **Health and 2028.** Diamandis points to GLP-1 drug revenue and Eli Lilly's upcoming retatrutide; Wissner-Gross reads Lilly's near-trillion valuation as the market backing longevity drugs alongside AI. On how AI will feel by mid-2028, Blundin predicts a compute shortfall and Ismail expects perfect memory.
