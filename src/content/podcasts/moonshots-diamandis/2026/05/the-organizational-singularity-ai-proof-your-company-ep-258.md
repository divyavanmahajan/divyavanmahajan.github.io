## Thesis

Salim Ismail presents his "organizational singularity" framework (EXO 3.0) to Peter Diamandis. He argues that AI has made coordination more expensive than execution, which breaks Ronald Coase's 1937 theory of why firms exist. Companies should be rebuilt around agentic workflows that improve themselves rather than around hierarchy, and Ismail says the only approach that works is a separate AI-native digital twin built at the edge of the existing business.

## Key points

- **Coase breaks.** Ismail says building a website with Vercel at home beats internal approvals. A firm still matters as a legal, fiduciary and liability container, which he calls the "fiduciary wedge".
- **Intelligence stack.** The proposed architecture turns the massive transformative purpose (MTP) into a protocol. It adds OODA-style layers for sensing, interpretation, decision, orchestration and learning, wrapped in "govern and assure": searchable agent logs, rollback, human review queues and agent "passports" that set permissions.
- **Why AI projects fail.** Ismail attributes the 80%-plus failure rate he cites to automating human-centric legacy bottlenecks. His test for CEOs is whether two people with OpenClaw or Hermes could copy a high-margin line of business in 60 to 90 days.
- **Workforce shape.** He predicts middle-management coordination work falls by about 90% and that companies will run with 10-25% of today's staff. He cites a Fermi America power plant estimate of 80 people instead of 800, Cognition Labs' 73x ARR growth, and a claim that 44% of Gen Z workers sabotage AI. Both hosts expect more startups, not just job losses.
- **Edge, not core.** Using Nespresso as the main example, Ismail describes a methodology he calls "rewrite": backcast, score organizational drag, map workflows, then copy workflows such as invoice processing into the twin and run them in parallel. Diamandis adds that the edge unit must report to the CEO and have board support. Ismail expects gains of 100x or more and a 5-7 year transition.
- **Moats and next steps.** Ismail lists proprietary data, regulation, learning speed, purpose and brand as moats. He plans to release the book as a Claude skill and run pilot batches of 10-20 companies.
