## Thesis

With Peter Diamandis away, Salim Ismail hosts Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross to discuss US midterm results in which data centers became a campaign issue. They argue that political and community resistance may now constrain AI infrastructure more than chips or capital. Wissner-Gross claims AI research is already so far into recursive self-improvement (RSI) that software gains will continue regardless.

## Key points

- **Data centers on the ballot.** Ismail cites 56% opposition to Big Tech and data centers and a Goldman Sachs estimate that 45% of planned US data center buildout could face restrictions. Blundin says he has never seen a technical topic dominate an election like this. He expects building to shift to welcoming states such as Tennessee, and to Canada for its power supply. Wissner-Gross suggests municipalities bargain with hyperscalers for local benefits instead of banning them.
- **Markets and orbit.** Ismail cites a 5.5% Nasdaq drop and a 9.3% fall in Nvidia. Blundin says much of this was priced in weeks before the vote. Wissner-Gross argues the backlash is the biggest boost yet for moving compute to space.
- **RSI evidence.** Ismail describes an 11-week METR study that found Anthropic's AI development running at about 1.9 times its historical rate, roughly 80% of it from AI assisting AI research. Wissner-Gross predicts that by early 2027 the frontier will move continuously rather than in discrete model releases.
- **Value per joule.** A paper testing 220 tasks across 44 occupations found GLM 5.2 produced about $70 per task, while Gemma 4 31B delivered 2.5 times more value per joule. Ismail compares New York City's GDP per gigawatt with Anthropic's revenue per gigawatt. Blundin warns that a few big buyers have reserved most compute and calls for some form of right to compute.
- **DNA synthesis rules.** On the White House NSTM-6 screening policy (sequences of 50 or more nucleotides, eight-year record retention), Wissner-Gross prefers controls on physical actions to controls on thought. He expects similar rules for robots.
- **A visible swarm.** The hosts discuss Brendon Larson's composite of 578 photos over Mount Taranaki showing about 1,400 satellites. Wissner-Gross reads it as an early Dyson swarm.
