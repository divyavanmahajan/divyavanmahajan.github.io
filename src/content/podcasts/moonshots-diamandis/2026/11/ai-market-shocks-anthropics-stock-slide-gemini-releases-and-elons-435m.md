## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Mates, joined by venture capitalist Steve Jurvetson, read Anthropic's IPO and SpaceX's compute plans as signs that AI has no natural spending ceiling. Jurvetson argues that founder-led, mission-driven companies with vertical integration are the likeliest winners, while the hosts debate whether cheaper training methods could strand today's GPU investments.

## Key points

- **The SpaceX deal that nearly died.** Jurvetson recalls his DFJ partnership voting down the 2008 SpaceX investment. It took two weeks and added terms (anti-dilution, a 2x minimum at IPO, 2x liquidation preference and redemption) to turn two partners around.
- **Anthropic's IPO.** Diamandis says Anthropic raised 81 billion, popped 17% and fell 12% the next day. Blundin compares it to Meta's IPO and says markets cannot digest offerings this large quickly; Salim Ismail sees tension between a 50-year mission and quarterly scrutiny.
- **Cyber fear.** After recent outages and breaches, Jurvetson calls this a period of maximal vulnerability for legacy infrastructure. Wissner-Gross adds drones as a physical weak point, and Ismail says defence is still human-in-the-loop while attacks scale with agents.
- **Google as number three.** Wissner-Gross ranks Anthropic first, OpenAI second and Gemini 4 Neon a close third. Jurvetson puts Google ahead of OpenAI on pace of progress, and Emad Mostaque points to 8 million TPUs landing next year.
- **SpaceX compute and the backprop risk.** Elon Musk is reported adding 500 megawatts of AI compute a month at about 60% of industry cost, with 120.5 billion in Anthropic agreements. Blundin warns that inference-time training without backpropagation could make current GPU clusters obsolete; Mostaque calls that bearish for Nvidia after two to three years.
- **Mission over money.** Jurvetson says Musk is driven by mission, not wealth, and doubts humans will ever travel to Alpha Centauri, as Voyager 1 passes one light day from Earth.
