## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross read the week's news as proof that the AI race now sets its own rules. Anthropic loosening its safety pledge, Amazon making AGI a condition of an OpenAI investment, and capable models small enough for a phone all point the same way. Wissner-Gross argues that safety will come from competition rather than any single lab. Blundin argues that rules are still needed within a narrow window this year.

## Key points

- **Anthropic's revised scaling policy.** Anthropic dropped its 2023 pledge not to train advanced AI unless safety is guaranteed. Ismail says safety usually fails in exponential races. Blundin compares it to Google's drift from "don't be evil". Wissner-Gross says alignment and capabilities are now inseparable.
- **AGI as a financial milestone.** Amazon's contingent $35 billion (up to $50 billion) offer is tied to OpenAI going public and to AGI. Wissner-Gross recalls that the OpenAI-Microsoft AGI definition was reportedly about $100 billion in earnings or revenue. He sees competition and horizontal stratification, not incest, in the cross-deals.
- **Plugins and the SaaS sell-off.** Wissner-Gross calls Claude Cowork plugins absurdly simple MCP wrappers and skills that still cut market value from software firms. Ismail advises large companies to build an AI-native digital twin and move workflows to it.
- **Smaller models.** The hosts discuss Qwen 3.5 at 35 billion parameters beating the earlier 235 billion model, and a 2 billion parameter version running offline on an iPhone 17 Pro. Wissner-Gross says this shows the overhang Apple has not used.
- **Autonomous firms and frontline AI.** Pulsia reportedly runs over 1,000 companies, and Burger King's headset assistant "Patty" coaches staff. Wissner-Gross links this to Marshall Brain's novel Manna. Diamandis calls it AI surveillance and Ismail calls it Orwellian.
- **Energy and chips.** The US plans a record 86 gigawatts of new utility-scale capacity. The White House is asking hyperscalers to fund their own power, and Meta has a reported $100 billion AMD chip deal. Blundin says TSMC capacity is the real bottleneck.
