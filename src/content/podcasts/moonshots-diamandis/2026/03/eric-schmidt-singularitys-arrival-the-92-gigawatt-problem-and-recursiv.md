## Thesis

Recorded on stage at the 2026 Abundance360 Summit, former Google CEO Eric Schmidt tells Peter Diamandis and Dave Blundin that the world is only 10-15% into the impact of AI. He argues that reasoning systems already change how software is written, that electricity rather than capital is the main US constraint, and that recursive self-improvement remains unproven. He frames China as a competitor, not an enemy, that may win low-cost robotics.

## Key points

- **The "San Francisco consensus".** Schmidt describes the Bay Area belief that once AI research agents can improve themselves, a lab could run perhaps a million of them, limited only by electricity, reaching superintelligence within two to three years. He says his recursive self-improvement reviews show scientists do not yet agree on an approach and current results are limited demos.
- **Coding has flipped.** Schmidt reports that Bay Area developers say the human/AI split went from 80/20 to 20/80, which he credits to deeper reasoning in the underlying models. He predicts top programmers become more valuable, plus a few very large companies and many very small ones, and proposes a first-semester prompt engineering course for all university students.
- **The 92-gigawatt problem.** Schmidt repeats his congressional estimate of a 92 GW US power shortfall by 2030, about 60 nuclear plants. He puts a gigawatt at roughly $50 billion of hardware, software and data centres, cites Jevons paradox, and says scaling laws show no asymptote yet.
- **Google history.** Schmidt credits Larry Page for the DeepMind deal, recalls AlphaGo's win in Korea, and says decade-old TPU design choices left Google well placed for inference.
- **Space and robots.** He supports orbital data centres, saying heat and radiation issues are solvable and placement is a business question. He argues China's EV supply chain gives it an edge in low-end robot hardware, while skilled rocket assembly stays beyond current robots.
- **Safety and values.** Schmidt describes, without endorsing, the idea that a small Chernobyl-like AI event may be needed to make governments act. He calls for high-skill immigration and for AI that reflects American values, and names harm to teenagers as a line not to cross.
