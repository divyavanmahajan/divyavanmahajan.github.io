## Thesis

Andrew Yang joins Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates to respond to Elon Musk's prediction of "universal high income". Yang argues that UBI has to come first as an intermediate step, because white-collar job losses are already under way while, as he puts it, Washington runs on a multi-decade tape delay. The hosts largely agree that the next few years carry the highest risk of social unrest.

## Key points

- **Two paths to UBI.** Yang sees either government getting its act together or wealthy individuals funding regional programmes. He cites Michael Dell's gift to children's accounts in Texas as an early sign, and expects philanthropists rather than companies to lead, since public companies would face shareholder lawsuits.
- **How much.** Yang campaigned on $1,000 a month in 2020 and now suggests roughly double that may be needed. Diamandis floats $50,000 a year for 200 million Americans, about $10 trillion a year.
- **Basic services versus cash.** Wissner-Gross pushes supply-side alternatives such as universal basic services and sovereign-fund dividends. Yang supports attacking costs (his company Noble Mobile targets wireless bills) but says cash transfers are faster. Blundin proposes subsidising payroll for new AI-oriented jobs; Ismail cites a 1970s Manitoba income experiment.
- **Jobs and the office.** Yang says one tech CEO privately plans successive cuts of 15% and 20%, and that his own CTO decided AI tools made a junior engineer hire unnecessary. He describes the career pyramid turning into "columns", leaving young people no entry point.
- **Advice for young people.** Yang stresses grit and limiting screen time, steering some towards trades, and expects human plumbers to be safe for at least ten years. He worries that young people socialise about 50% less and are turning to AI companions.
- **Politics and AI.** Yang describes an open, smartphone-based independent primary for 2028 through the Forward Party, and says lobbying money is almost entirely on the pro-industry side, although polls favour more AI regulation.
