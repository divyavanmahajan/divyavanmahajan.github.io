## Thesis

In a live episode at the Abundance Summit, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross and guest Emad Mostaque argue that AI has already entered recursive self-improvement, against Eric Schmidt's view that it is still some years out. The panel treats the week's news as evidence, and disagrees mainly on how hard the transition will be for workers.

## Key points

- **Announcements.** Diamandis launches the Future Vision XPRIZE with Google and Range Media Partners: $3.5 million raised for hopeful short films about the future, with finalists shown at a Moonshots Gathering in Los Angeles on September 25.
- **Self-improvement and GPT-5.4.** Wissner-Gross says frontier models are now largely designed and trained by their predecessors, and that GPT-5.4 solves about 38% of FrontierMath Tier 4 problems. Mostaque points to computer-use benchmarks passing human level and to OpenAI's Cerebras deal, which he says delivers about 1,000 tokens a second.
- **Agents as customers.** Meta's acqui-hire of the Moltbook team prompts a debate on whether advertising can work on AI agents. Wissner-Gross argues that game theory and scarce compute keep economics in place. Blundin doubts that agents will pay attention to paid ads.
- **Labs and research.** The hosts discuss Yann LeCun's $1 billion raise for world models. Blundin argues that scaled transformers will reach new ideas first. Andrej Karpathy's autoresearch, which ran about 650 experiments in two days, is presented as automated AI research.
- **Fruit fly upload.** Wissner-Gross describes Eon Systems' simulation of a whole fruit fly brain driving a virtual body that walks, grooms and feeds. He calls it an early experiment.
- **Jobs.** Blundin expects a sharp trough followed by a rebound in 2028. Ismail expects companies to need about 25% of today's staff but five times as many companies to exist, so employment holds. Mostaque argues for universal basic AI and money issued for being human.

## Quotes
