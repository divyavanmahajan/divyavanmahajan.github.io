## Thesis

Starting from a clip of Sam Altman admitting the economy adapts to AI more slowly than he expected, the Mates and Emad Mostaque argue the bottleneck has moved from capability to institutions, interfaces and public trust. Their recurring answer is vertical integration: labs, chipmakers and Waymo are all moving up and down the stack to remove the friction between layers.

## Key points

- **Why diffusion is slow.** Salim Ismail calls it linear institutions meeting exponential technology. Alexander Wissner-Gross blames the economy's abstraction layers and says labs that want speed should integrate vertically. Mostaque counters that the models were only good enough a few months ago.
- **Agent swarms.** Peter Diamandis calls Grokbot the most useful consumer AI product of the year. Mostaque runs 18 Grokbots on his own machines, and Dave Blundin describes running thousands of agents. Wissner-Gross argues chat-style agent management will not scale; agents will manage agents.
- **Lab rankings and cheap Chinese models.** Wissner-Gross reads Gemini 3.7 Flash's benchmark win as tuning for reliability, not a return to the frontier. Mostaque calls OpenAI's GPT 5.6 Pro the only proper frontier math model. The Mates discuss reports that Fable 5 uptake has plateaued as Chinese open-weight models, cited as up to 100 times cheaper, gain share.
- **Anthropic's IPO and Nvidia's $6B bet.** Mostaque argues Anthropic should raise privately rather than IPO; Blundin says backing off now would send a bad signal. Mostaque and Wissner-Gross read Nvidia's Poolside deal as a push to own an American open-source stack, structured to avoid antitrust review.
- **Data center backlash.** Diamandis cites polling showing 75% opposition to local data centers. Wissner-Gross suspects some foreign-stoked opposition, while Ismail blames narrative-driven rather than evidence-driven debate.
- **Waymo, space and work.** Diamandis reports Waymo's sixth-generation hardware falling from $115,000 to $20,000 on a Zeekr-built van; Wissner-Gross regrets the reliance on Chinese hardware. The Mates also cover Elon Musk's goal of 30 Starship launches a day, and say AI has made them work harder than ever.
