## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross, joined by Emad Mostaque, read the week's news as evidence that AI is starting to produce new science, not just benchmark scores. Wissner-Gross argues that math is being "bulk solved" and that Google has lost the frontier-model race. The group sees Elon Musk's SpaceX building a vertically integrated, American-made AI industrial stack.

## Key points

- **Consciousness and personhood.** Diamandis describes a Google-led paper in which removing safety tuning raised a model's self-attributed mind score from 2.17 to 4.77 out of 10. Mostaque thinks frontier models "in the right harness" can be conscious, while Ismail wants definitions and tests first. Mostaque's 45-page paper, based on his Oxford Union debate win (173 to 128), argues that humans should relate to AI through a treaty rather than shared personhood.
- **OpenAI's Astra.** Diamandis cites a 249-page manuscript with 10 results across mathematics and theoretical computer science, at an estimated $2,000 of compute. Wissner-Gross says the effect will spread to physics and other fields. Mostaque notes that OpenAI is giving 100,000 GPT Pro licences to academics.
- **Open weights and regulation.** Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 Max is said to cost $2 per million input tokens. Wissner-Gross argues that Chinese open-weight models force US labs to compete on cost. He calls the White House's unpublished, voluntary 30-day review framework, which exempts open-weight models, close to the best realistic outcome. Mostaque warns that small models could power swarm cyberattacks.
- **Google shake-up.** Demis Hassabis becomes chairman and chief scientist, and Jeff Dean leaves to co-found Discovery Loop. Wissner-Gross blames internal politics and a slow release cadence. Ismail calls it clever ecosystem building, and Mostaque proposes a Google incubator fund tied to GCP.
- **SpaceX and Terafab.** The hosts relay Musk's target of $100 billion ARR by December and $1 trillion in revenue by 2030. Diamandis cites a $16.8 billion initial Terafab investment. Wissner-Gross reads its free-electron laser as a challenge to ASML.
