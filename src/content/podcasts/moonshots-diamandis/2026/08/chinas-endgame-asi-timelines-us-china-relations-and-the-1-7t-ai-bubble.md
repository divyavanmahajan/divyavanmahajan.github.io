## Thesis

Alvin Wang Graylin, a former HTC China head now at Stanford HAI and the Asia Society, tells the Moonshot Mates that the US treats AI as a zero-sum race with a finish line that does not exist. He argues China is chasing "good enough" AI for industry while the US bets everything on ASI with borrowed money, and that cooperation on safety is the better strategy. Alexander Wissner-Gross and Dave Blundin push back, mainly on timelines and recursive self-improvement.

## Key points

- **China is not acting ASI-pilled.** Graylin points to Beijing telling labs not to buy H200s, CAC review of every model, and the AI Plus plan (70% of companies using AI within five years, 90% within ten). He expects the full transition to take decades.
- **Open source emerged bottom-up.** He says DeepSeek open-sourced without government direction and was at first reprimanded, before Xi Jinping endorsed open source. Export controls, in his view, forced efficiency and rescued Chinese GPU makers, while training simply moved to overseas data centres. Blundin calls the chip embargo "completely and utterly backfired".
- **Distillation and model risk.** Graylin estimates the queries in Anthropic's distillation complaint cost only $2-3 million and calls the issue overblown. His Cipher Brief paper finds no link between model size and real-world bio, chemical or cyber risk. Blundin counters that a self-improving model like Kimi K3 is a "burning match".
- **Stag hunt, not prisoner's dilemma.** Graylin frames the race as a positive-sum stag hunt: both sides should slow down, deploy useful AI, then pursue ASI together. For the 24 September US-China safety dialogue he names non-state actors as the top priority and says agreeing to a second meeting would count as success.
- **Bubble warning.** He claims AI is 45% of US stock-market value, the Buffett indicator is at 240% of GDP versus 120% in the dot-com peak, and hyperscalers carry $1.7 trillion of off-book debt. He compares the buildout to the Soviet arms race; Wissner-Gross calls it a "reverse SDI" moment.
- **AI Marshall Plan.** Graylin closes by urging the US to export AI infrastructure and safe open models to allies, as China is doing through its World AI Cooperation Organization.
