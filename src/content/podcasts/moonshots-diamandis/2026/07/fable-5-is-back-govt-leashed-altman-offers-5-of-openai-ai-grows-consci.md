## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates argue that frontier AI labs are becoming semi-public institutions, with duties to governments, pressure to share equity, and new tools for seeing inside their models. Alexander Wissner-Gross and Diamandis are mostly optimistic about this. Salim Ismail doubts that today's nation-state institutions can govern the technology at all, and Dave Blundin is sceptical of government ownership.

## Key points

- **Fable 5 returns.** Diamandis recounts that Anthropic's Fable 5 came back online on 1 July after an export-control shutdown, with three commitments to the US government: a targeted safety classifier, 24/7 jailbreak monitoring, and early government access to frontier models. Wissner-Gross calls a couple of weeks' outage the gentlest possible start to light-touch regulation.
- **A "J-space" inside Claude.** The hosts discuss Anthropic's paper on a global workspace in language models. Wissner-Gross reads it as a compression-driven phase transition. Ismail welcomes it as the start of "AI neuroscience" but warns against calling it consciousness, and notes the paper itself avoids that claim.
- **Altman's governance and equity moves.** Diamandis summarises Sam Altman's FT op-ed calling for a US-led international AI forum, and reports that Altman discussed a 5% OpenAI stake for the government, which he values at about $42.6 billion. Wissner-Gross proposes a "hyper tithe" that pays into a sovereign wealth fund. Blundin calls the idea irrelevant, and Ismail sees it as Altman protecting himself.
- **AI spend and hiring.** Diamandis cites a Ramp study of 21,559 US companies. It found that heavy AI adopters, spending $33 per employee per month, grew white-collar headcount by 10.2%. The authors call this correlation, not causation.
- **Palantir and Nvidia go open-weight.** Palantir is putting Nvidia's Nemotron models into its government stack. Wissner-Gross reads Alex Karp's rant as a counter-attack on labs that are building forward-deployed engineering teams.
- **AI designing chips, and patents.** The hosts cover a Princeton and IIT Madras system that designs RF circuits in minutes. They also discuss Japan's Supreme Court ruling that an AI cannot be listed as a patent inventor.

## Quotes

> "This is probably close to the best scenario we could have hoped for." — Alexander Wissner-Gross

> "So the idea that the government is going to set up some intelligent sovereign wealth equity thing is absolutely insane." — Dave Blundin
