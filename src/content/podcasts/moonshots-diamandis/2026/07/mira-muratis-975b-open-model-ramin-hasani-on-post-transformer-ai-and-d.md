## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates, joined by Liquid AI co-founder and CEO Ramin Hasani, argue that value is moving from raw frontier capability to models that companies can own, customize and run locally. They are sceptical of frontier-lab CEOs asking for regulation, and Hasani makes the case for small, efficient non-transformer models running outside data centres.

## Key points

- **An "AI FINRA".** Diamandis reports that Demis Hassabis wants a US-led, industry-funded standards body modelled on FINRA to test frontier models before release. Wissner-Gross calls it likely regulatory capture that could box out open-weight and university models. Salim Ismail says AI moves too fast for any static regulator.
- **A China-pegged ceiling.** On a reported White House idea to allow US open releases only up to China's best open-weight model, Wissner-Gross says it would give labs an incentive to let China win the race.
- **Thinking Machines' Inkling.** Mira Murati's lab released a 975-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts open-weight model that activates 41 billion parameters. Wissner-Gross notes that by its own evals it beats Nemotron but trails GLM 5.2. Hasani calls the bet on fine-tuning as a business smart. Wissner-Gross counters that reinforcement fine-tuning may only be a passing paradigm.
- **Recursive self-improvement.** A London startup claims eight days of AI self-improvement beat two years of human effort. Wissner-Gross sees it as early "sparks of ignition". Hasani is less impressed: no model weights change, and he estimates the method could not fine-tune a 2-billion-parameter model in a practical time.
- **Liquid AI on devices.** Hasani traces Liquid's roots to studying the 302-neuron C. elegans worm, and explains that an automated architecture search chose mostly gated convolutions. He describes a sub-1 GB multimodal model for in-car chips with 2-8 GB of RAM, delivered to Mercedes-Benz North America cars as a 600 MB over-the-air update, plus production work with Shopify.
- **Patents and health.** The Mates reject Palmer Luckey's proposal to expand secret national-security patents. They also discuss reports that GPT 5.6 and Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 beat physicians on medical benchmarks, though Wissner-Gross suspects some benchmark-maxing.
