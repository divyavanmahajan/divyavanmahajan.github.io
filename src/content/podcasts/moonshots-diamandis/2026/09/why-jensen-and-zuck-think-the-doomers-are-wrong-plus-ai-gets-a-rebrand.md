## Thesis

At the first Moonshots Live, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross and Emad Mostaque argue against the latest wave of AI doom warnings. In their view, slowing down is the bigger risk, and the real dangers are human misuse and weak security. They also discuss the US government's switch to the term "superintelligence", a run of new model releases, and AI-driven biology.

## Key points

- **Doomers challenged.** Diamandis plays clips of Jensen Huang calling Geoffrey Hinton's 10% risk estimate unscientific and Mark Zuckerberg saying each lab can manage safety internally. Mostaque blames recent incidents on poor security, and Wissner-Gross warns that a moral panic could cost 50 years, as he says happened with nuclear power.
- **Cartel versus co-scaling.** Wissner-Gross calls recent calls for coordination an attempted "AI safety cartel". He compares the situation to early cities: society did not ban them but added police and fire services as they grew. Mostaque separates frontier AI from "utility AI" that runs public services.
- **Superintelligence rebrand and the Sanders proposal.** Wissner-Gross welcomes the new term because it frames AI as simply more intelligence. The Mates sharply criticise Bernie Sanders's proposed ban on superintelligence with corporate and criminal penalties. Blundin worries that states without data centres will never catch up, and Mostaque argues that banning AI amounts to banning maths.
- **Model releases.** Diamandis counts 12 major releases in 22 days and says Meta Muse reached 2.8 million downloads. Mostaque calls Claude Opus 5.5 the first truly competent model. Wissner-Gross contrasts Anthropic's procedural, code-based generation with OpenAI's pixel-based models.
- **Robotics and compute.** On Driving Bench, GPT-6 Astra reportedly finished a cone course on its second attempt. Wissner-Gross reads this as a sign that general models replace specialised robotics. Blundin says the more important measure is how much compute the task used.
- **AI in biology.** The Mates discuss Anthropic's report that 950 Claude agents found a new enzyme in 21 hours, and Edison Scientific's "Millennium Problems" for biology. Wissner-Gross argues that digital twins of cells will turn biology into a search problem.
