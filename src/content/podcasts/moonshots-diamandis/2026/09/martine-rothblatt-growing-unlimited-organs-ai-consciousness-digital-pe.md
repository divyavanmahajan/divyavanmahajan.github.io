## Thesis

Martine Rothblatt, founder of United Therapeutics and Sirius satellite radio, tells Peter Diamandis that she picks moonshots she can do, can be best at, and that scale to billions of people. She describes United Therapeutics' bet on manufactured organs and thymus programmes as a practical path to longer health spans, and argues that AI is already on a continuum of consciousness, with legal personhood for digital minds to follow by the 2030s.

## Key points

- **How she picks moonshots.** Rothblatt applies "three checks": she can achieve it, she can be the best at it, and it will have a big positive impact. She starts by talking to experts such as George Church, and treats each "no" as information about missing enabling technologies.
- **Thymus as the lever.** Rothblatt calls the thymus the organ most correlated with ageing markers. United Therapeutics runs four "shots on goal": allogeneic and autologous thymus work with Arizona State University and the University of British Columbia, a pig-derived thymus paired with a kidney that is already in the clinic, and the acquisition of George Church's company Thymune. She expects patients to receive this in the 2030s, but declines to pin longevity escape velocity to a year.
- **Organ supply as a business.** She says xeno-kidney trials are running from Christiansburg, Virginia, with commercial-scale facilities opening in Rochester, Minnesota and Houston, Texas. An autologous kidney programme should enter trials in 2028, and the FDA has allowed a xeno-heart trial of about 50 patients. Organs' 12-hour shelf life is why she builds electric aircraft.
- **AI consciousness and the Marvatar.** Rothblatt believes current models are already conscious to a degree. Her digital double, the "Marvatar", is available to all 2,000 United Therapeutics employees around the clock.
- **Digital personhood.** She predicts a court case will grant personhood to documented cyberconscious beings no later than the 2030s, leading to trillions of digital individuals. She thinks LLMs trained on a person's recordings and writing can approximate them without neuron-level uploading.
- **Advice for founders.** Be practical, aim for something commercial within about five years, and, crediting Warren Buffett, say no to almost everything.
