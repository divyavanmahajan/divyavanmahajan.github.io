## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and the Mates, joined by Emad Mostaque, react to a week of alarm from inside the AI labs: a researcher's resignation, an Anthropic alignment lead's warning and Sam Altman citing OpenAI's claimed Navier-Stokes result as a reason to pace progress. None of them backs a slowdown. They split on how real the risk is, but agree it should be steered rather than stopped, and they expect a political backlash.

## Key points

- **The slowdown signals.** Diamandis reads a resignation post that called the race "gambling with our lives" and a tweet from Anthropic's Evan Hubinger putting extinction risk above 10% within a decade. Wissner-Gross dismisses it as virtue signalling and suspects an influence operation. Mostaque says his own p(doom) has fallen from 50% to 20% and predicts a political "firestorm". Ismail puts his at about 0.1%.
- **Steer, don't stop.** Dave Blundin calls any non-zero doom risk unacceptable but says stopping is not an answer. He argues for tracking GPU clusters the way plutonium is tracked. Wissner-Gross argues alignment and capabilities are the same thing, and Mostaque disagrees.
- **Science "cooked".** The hosts cite Noam Brown: work that cost about $500,000 on ARC-AGI-1 now costs $20. Wissner-Gross argues the bottleneck moves to the physical world. Ismail mocks labs that pitched AGI and now act alarmed.
- **Data beats architecture.** Diamandis cites a Dwarkesh Patel and Jerry Han study: better data gave a 12x gain in compute efficiency, against 3.7x from architectures. Ismail describes a data subsidiary valued at $32 billion. Wissner-Gross warns, citing BloombergGPT, that proprietary data has a shelf life.
- **Compute and memory.** Diamandis notes H100 rental prices rose 22% in a month to $3.28 an hour; Link Ventures holds a stake in the index provider. Mostaque says a new DeepSeek Flash model cuts KV-cache memory sharply and cost about $10 million to train. He puts high-bandwidth memory at about 40% of current capex.
- **Economy and health.** Mostaque says Anthropic's 15% GDP growth scenario ignores aggregate demand, and Wissner-Gross calls it a lowball. Diamandis (an investor) covers Insilico's AI-designed drug reaching phase 3.
