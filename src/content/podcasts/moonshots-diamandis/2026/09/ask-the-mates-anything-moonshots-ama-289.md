## Thesis

Peter Diamandis hosts the show's first live AMA over Zoom, with Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross taking listener questions. Their common answer is that individuals, from 18-year-old founders to mid-career engineers, should start using AI now. They argue that the transition will be disruptive, but that regulation and fear are bigger risks than moving too fast.

## Key points

- **Leapfrogging and slack.** Ismail tells an Australian founder that being "behind" lets emerging markets jump ahead, because the problem is mindset rather than infrastructure. He suggests keeping 10-20% of your time unscheduled to experiment.
- **Selling proprietary data.** A civil engineer with 30 years of project records asks how to reach the labs. Blundin says labs are eager to buy data-rich companies outright. His advice: package the data so it is easy to buy, name your own price, and fly to San Francisco to meet the buyer in person.
- **Regulation and government risk.** Wissner-Gross says the main risk is overregulation, and cites more than 150,000 deaths a day as the cost of slowing superintelligence for medicine. Blundin advises public servants to stake out expertise on a likely threat early, so that they are called in when a crisis hits.
- **Founders and hardware.** Blundin tells an 18-year-old drone founder to build a syndicate of VCs and industry co-investors before prototyping. One of the Mates adds that such a company should see itself as an intelligence company whose products contain atoms.
- **Education.** Ismail argues that schools still teach in a 19th-century way. He wants children to start from a problem they care about rather than a supply-side career path. Diamandis points to TKS and Neal Stephenson's Diamond Age.
- **Energy, architectures and the transition.** Wissner-Gross expects a "postfusion" energy era within ten years and says next-token prediction can scale to "solving everything". Blundin predicts compute will move to photonics. Ismail describes about 20 simultaneous "Gutenberg moments" breaking institutions, and Diamandis warns of two to eight years of possible civil unrest.
