## Thesis

Peter Diamandis walks the Mates through a week in which, as he reports it, Dario Amodei published an essay titled "We must pace the frontier" and Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis and Elon Musk said they agreed. Alexander Wissner-Gross and Salim Ismail treat the push as a "safety cartel" and safety theatre. Dave Blundin argues the real risk is bad actors using open models for weapons, not AI escaping. All of them prefer defensive co-scaling to regulation, though Peter expects some regulation to come anyway.

## Key points

- **The pacing essay.** Peter summarises Amodei's three steps: embedded third-party evaluators, coordination among US labs through legislation or an antitrust waiver, and international coordination starting with a bioweapon ban. Blundin says asking this administration for an antitrust waiver was a misstep. He likens the evaluators to bank regulation, which he says protects incumbents.
- **Musk's peer-testing idea.** Peter calls Musk's proposal, in which labs run each other's safety test harnesses before a release, the only one China might accept. Wissner-Gross suspects it would help SpaceX AI stay at the frontier.
- **Threat report and evaluators.** Ismail calls Anthropic's report of five possible bioweapon-related cases badly timed theatre. Wissner-Gross notes its overlap with a Novo Nordisk deal. He says he is not accusing any particular firm, but worries that evaluation vendors such as Irregular may have reason to overstate risk.
- **OpenAI's IPO.** Peter relays Altman's statement that OpenAI will not go public in 2026, citing safety. Wissner-Gross thinks OpenAI's financials are not ready yet. Blundin points to Anthropic's simpler business model, which he says has 80% gross margins.
- **Business and benchmarks.** The Mates discuss SpaceX AI building a company live with Grokbot, and GPT-6 Astra ending Andon Labs' Vending Bench 2 with $15,515 from $500. Blundin warns that coordinating many agents is still hard.
- **Education.** Blundin praises MIT's committee report on AI, which warns about "cognitive surrender". Wissner-Gross predicts universities will drift towards for-profit models. Ismail sees falling PISA scores as a natural change. Blundin disagrees and blames social media.
