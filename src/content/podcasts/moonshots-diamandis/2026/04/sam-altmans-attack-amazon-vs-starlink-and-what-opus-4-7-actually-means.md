## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that AI capability keeps compounding while public trust falls behind. They treat the attack on Sam Altman's home and local data center bans as early signs of a backlash. Their advice to young people is to start companies and move to where AI work is concentrated.

## Key points

- **Opus 4.7.** Wissner-Gross calls it a solid point release that looks smaller next to Mythos. He notes that knobs such as temperature are gone and behaviour is now set through prompts. Blundin says it updated its own tooling through computer use and quoted larger agent budgets. Ismail highlights that it accepts images three times larger.
- **Stanford AI Index 2026.** Diamandis cites a coding benchmark rising from 60% to 97%, a transparency score falling from 58 to 40, and 23% public optimism against 73% among experts. Employment of developers aged 22-25 is down nearly 20% since 2024. Wissner-Gross says an annual report is too slow, and the hosts urge entrepreneurship and moving to new places.
- **Backlash turns physical.** After the Molotov attack on Altman's house, Wissner-Gross argues that pausing AI is self-defeating because others race ahead. Ismail calls the violence lose-lose. On data centers (Festus, Missouri ousting four council members over a $6 billion project; Maine's 18-month moratorium), Blundin praises New Hampshire's right-to-compute law and dismisses water-use concerns.
- **Amazon vs. Starlink.** Amazon's $11.57 billion Globalstar deal and Apple partnership are, in Diamandis's view, about Globalstar's spectrum. Blundin explains that its lower frequency can reach phones directly. Wissner-Gross predicts Apple will keep both Amazon and SpaceX as vendors.
- **AI-run organizations.** The hosts discuss Jack Dorsey's goal of near-flat management at a 6,000-person company, Andon Labs' AI-run store and Google's TurboQuant. Blundin says TurboQuant gives models about 8x more context.
- **Human forks.** In a closing round on longevity, brain implants, space settlement and uploading, most of the Mates turn down a destructive upload. Wissner-Gross, who helped form Eon Systems, would accept one only if the alternative were death.
