## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that each new frontier-model feature can wipe out a software vertical. They also see every major lab, and now SpaceX, putting its money and focus on code generation. They then cover the Strait of Hormuz crisis as a threat to the materials behind AI hardware, and answer listeners who said their push for entrepreneurship ignores ordinary people.

## Key points

- **Claude Design hits SaaS.** Peter notes that Figma fell 10% and Adobe about 2% after the launch. Wissner-Gross calls it an "unhobbling" of skills the model already had. Blundin says Anthropic needs a public roadmap and a partner program for independent software vendors, and Ismail says such a program is starting.
- **Entry-level jobs.** Peter cites a small survey of Anthropic staff predicting that entry-level engineers will be replaced within months. Ismail expects AI-native startups to outpace big firms, which will then buy them.
- **Parameters versus density.** Wissner-Gross says Elon Musk's Grok roadmap, framed in parameter counts, looks like last year's race. He would rather see "intelligence density": the same capability from fewer parameters.
- **Cursor and the codegen race.** SpaceX has bought the right to acquire Cursor for $60 billion or pay a $10 billion walk-away fee. Wissner-Gross reads it as a possible reset of Grok's coding and SpaceX turning into a hyperscaler. He links it to OpenAI losing Kevin Weil and Bill Peebles to sharpen its focus.
- **Hormuz as a system shock.** The Mates cite a quarter of world oil passing through the strait, a third of helium coming from Qatar, and Taiwan holding 11 days of natural gas. Wissner-Gross calls for helium startups and strategic reserves. Peter compares data-centre capex of almost $1 trillion with Apollo's $257 billion.
- **Listener pushback.** Peter cites start-up costs down 99.7% and a mean founder age of 45 at the fastest-growing companies. Ismail reframes the goal as agency rather than founding a company. Blundin says the safety net is weaker now and that labs should map out new paths for displaced professionals.
