## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that capital, compute and security now set the pace of AI. SpaceX's IPO competes with OpenAI and Anthropic for a limited pool of capital. Anthropic holding back Mythos shows that frontier models are becoming security problems. Local resistance to land-based data centers, Wissner-Gross argues, pushes compute towards orbit.

## Key points

- **SpaceX IPO.** Diamandis, an early SpaceX investor, cites a target valuation of about $2 trillion and a $75 billion raise, with Starlink making up 75-80% of the value. Blundin says the multiple depends on whether the growth lasts. Wissner-Gross says demand for orbital data centers, not new supply, made the timing work.
- **Mythos held back.** Wissner-Gross notes that Anthropic launched Mythos as a cyber-defence coalition (Project Glasswing) rather than as a product. He calls it an upward jump in autonomous AI-research ability and roughly five times the cost of Opus. Blundin says the Polymarket odds of a near-term release collapsed.
- **Anthropic vs OpenAI.** Diamandis cites Anthropic at about $30 billion ARR against OpenAI's $24-25 billion, and the shutdown of Sora. Wissner-Gross argues that enterprise code generation, not personal superintelligence, is paying for AI. Blundin says the "co-pilot" idea underestimated demand for fleets of agents.
- **Cyber and bio warnings.** The Mates discuss Sam Altman's warning of a possible world-shaking cyber attack. Wissner-Gross argues for "defensive co-scaling", meaning defenders need capabilities equal to attackers'. Ismail reads the warning partly as an attempt to shape governance.
- **Data center crunch.** A chart shown on the episode says half of planned US data centers are delayed or cancelled and only a third are being built. Blundin blames builders who never secured chips. The Mates also discuss Intel's 18A process for Musk's Terafab and Google's lead in AI chips.
- **One-person unicorns and abundance.** The Mates point to Matthew Gallagher's GLP-1 company, valued at $1.8 billion, as an early one-person unicorn. Diamandis also lists signs of abundance: cheaper batteries and lab-grown diamonds, and robots installing solar.
