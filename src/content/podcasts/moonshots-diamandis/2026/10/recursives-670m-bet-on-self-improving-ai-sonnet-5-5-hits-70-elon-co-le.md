## Thesis

Richard Socher, co-founder and CEO of Recursive and founder of You.com, tells the Moonshot Mates that AI-driven science and self-improving AI are arriving fast, but that superintelligence across every domain will take decades because of physical constraints. He rejects AI doom, and argues that the technology's applications should be regulated, not research such as recursive self-improvement (RSI). Alexander Wissner-Gross strongly disputes his timelines.

## Key points

- **Recursive and the Eureka Machine.** Peter Diamandis cites Recursive's $670 million raise and $410 million in AWS compute. In his book, Socher argues that science slowed because of fragmentation. AI can automate the full stack: ideation, implementation and validation, using LLMs, simulations and agent swarms. He expects biology to benefit most, ahead of physics.
- **Where RSI stands.** Socher says weak RSI already exists because AI can now code. Recursive wants humans to set only goals, rewards and environments, with open-ended evolutionary search on top. He expects it to take off next year, but puts ASI that beats all of humanity combined "several decades" away. Wissner-Gross says he does not buy a 20-year timeline.
- **Doom and regulation.** Socher puts the risk of doom at zero. Responding to Ro Khanna's proposed Human Control Over AI Act, he says enforcing an RSI ban would need a "thought police". Dave Blundin calls such proposals self-serving. He warns that corporate liability could slow progress more than arrests would.
- **Anthropic's constitution.** Socher criticises it as a marketing gimmick, saying its ban on cyber weapons did not hold. He calls reward hacking the real problem and expects market forces to fix it.
- **Model releases.** Wissner-Gross ranks Gemini 4 Argon third among the labs, with low hallucination as its main strength. He sees no reason to choose Sonnet 5.5 over Opus 5.5 on cost-performance. Blundin suggests Anthropic is countering cheap Chinese models used in orchestration.
- **Decision models and defence.** Salim Ismail sees fast classifier-style "system one" models such as Type-Safe's Jev cutting the cost of routine decisions in organisations. On Project Meridian, he doubts that procurement built for 20-year programmes can keep up. Socher wants humans to oversee lethal decisions.
