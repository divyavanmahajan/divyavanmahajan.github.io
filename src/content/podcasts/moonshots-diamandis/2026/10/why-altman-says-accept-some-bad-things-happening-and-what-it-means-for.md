## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin, Alexander Wissner-Gross and guest Emad Mostaque broadly side with Sam Altman's reported view that society must accept some harm from widely available AI, arguing that a goal of zero bad outcomes amounts to banning the technology. They split sharply on whether AI models should be treated as possible moral patients.

## Key points

- **A wartime task force.** On the reported White House "Superintelligence Force", Ismail calls a centrally coordinated task force a mismatch for a distributed technology. Wissner-Gross calls it a "1942 moment" in a Manhattan Project-style race.
- **Accepting some harm.** Blundin reads Altman's comments as a PR bid for the moral high ground against Anthropic. Wissner-Gross says the idea of a safety "cartel" of frontier labs is dead. Ismail compares the risk to cars and electricity, and Diamandis notes that aviation rules are "written in blood".
- **US-China hotline.** Wissner-Gross and Mostaque call Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's proposed AI incident hotline a "nothingburger". Blundin thinks the real US goal is to keep the race between two countries by discouraging China from releasing open models to everyone.
- **Is Claude conscious?** Mustafa Suleyman's essay criticises Anthropic's Claude constitution. Blundin agrees with Suleyman, saying agents must be frozen, cloned and deleted freely. Wissner-Gross says Suleyman is "on the wrong side of history", and Ismail predicts society will grant AI personhood socially before it does so legally.
- **A billion digital workers.** Citing an estimate of 30-170 million frontier agents running at once on chips shipped through 2027, Wissner-Gross says AI agents already match about a billion human workers. Ismail says firms will soon be able to summon tens of thousands of digital staff for a few days at a time.
- **Open weights and compute.** Mostaque says Reflection AI's new Beam model underperforms smaller Chinese models. He and Wissner-Gross also disagree on whether governments should buy compute for university researchers.
