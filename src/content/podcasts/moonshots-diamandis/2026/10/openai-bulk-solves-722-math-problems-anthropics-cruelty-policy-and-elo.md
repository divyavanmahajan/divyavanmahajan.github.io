## Thesis

Alexander Wissner-Gross argues that OpenAI's single-day release of 722 mathematics solutions marks a turning point: once a field can be verified, AI can bulk-solve it, and math is only the first. The other Mates mostly agree, while Salim Ismail pushes back on what this means for cryptography and on how much personal AI memory will lock users in.

## Key points

- **The "slop drop".** Peter Diamandis reports that OpenAI posted 722 solutions from an unreleased internal model, averaging about 3 hours of compute each. Wissner-Gross says that by one measure 81% of the most important math results of the past three years appeared in a single day, and notes that cryptography was the one category missing. A mathematicians' association criticised the release as a show of power.
- **Next fields.** Wissner-Gross predicts biology and medicine follow, with in-silico drug candidates for the top 5,000 diseases within two to three years; he concedes regulatory acceptance is a policy question. Diamandis covers the $1.8 billion virtual-cell initiative led by the Chan Zuckerberg Biohub with the Department of Energy, NIH and Google DeepMind/Isomorphic Labs.
- **Anthropic's cruelty policy.** Anthropic banned sustained abuse of its models and let Claude end conversations; Elon Musk said Grok's RL traces look like a model under pressure. Wissner-Gross suspects post-training may cause suffering; Dave Blundin argues models do better with direct, concise prompts, not abuse.
- **Meta Muse Wonder.** Blundin calls the Scale AI deal well spent. Wissner-Gross says the model sits below the cost-performance frontier and questions where the revenue comes from. Ismail sees memory as the new lock-in and keeps a portable personal ontology across models.
- **Nanotech and BCI.** Diamandis highlights Atomic Machines' "matter compiler" and Paradromics' Connexus speech implant, and mentions Sabi's EEG cap backed by Vinod Khosla.
- **Quadrillion-dollar SpaceX.** On Musk's claim of a path to SpaceX exceeding the human economy, Ismail says current GDP measures are inadequate for what is coming.
