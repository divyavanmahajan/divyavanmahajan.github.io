## Thesis

All-In co-host Chamath Palihapitiya tells Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates that AGI has arrived but cannot spread through the economy. In his view the limit is physical build-out (power, permitting, supply chains, licensed electricians), not the models. The group also debates Anthropic's public benefit fund and Elon Musk's SpaceX share gift.

## Key points

- **Anthropic's fund.** Diamandis reads out Anthropic's pledge of 5% of its shares (put at $75-100 billion) to a public benefit fund. Palihapitiya thinks the founders are sincere but notes that large philanthropies rarely deliver measurable outcomes. He calls the Giving Pledge "reputation washing". Wissner-Gross wants a structure for donating equity to the general public instead.
- **SpaceX shares for kids.** Musk plans to give one SpaceX share to each of 72 million Americans under 18, about $10.5 billion by Diamandis's maths. Palihapitiya calls it "the better version of the giving pledge". Blundin argues that wealth will come from owning assets rather than wages, so teaching a generation to own equity matters most.
- **Power is the constraint.** Palihapitiya says the US will still be 1.4 terawatt hours short by 2050 even if every planned project is built. Solar was his fastest option, which led him to about 6 gigawatts of power assets and into data centres. He predicts a "massive" compute shortage within 3-4 years and says Anthropic's S-1 lists lack of hyperscaler compute as a risk.
- **How individuals can play.** He suggests residential solar with batteries or joining distributed compute pools. Wissner-Gross counters that distillation will cut energy needs and that demand for paid reasoning is not proven.
- **Chips versus data centres.** Palihapitiya, Groq's first investor, says new chip startups now struggle because memory, fab and packaging capacity are all taken. He prefers data centres, which he says others dismiss as too "blue collar", and applications.
- **Markets and crypto.** He says he is the least invested he has been in 15 years. He favours free-cash-flow yield over the S&P 500 index and expects Bitcoin to be capped and eventually hacked.
