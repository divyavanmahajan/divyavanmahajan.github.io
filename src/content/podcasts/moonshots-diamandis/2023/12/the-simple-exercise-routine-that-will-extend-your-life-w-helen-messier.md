## Thesis

Helen Messier, Chief Medical and Science Officer of Fountain Life, tells Peter Diamandis that if people could pick only one lifestyle pillar for healthspan, it should be exercise. Both argue that the evidence for exercise is less contested than the evidence for any particular diet. Their framework rests on a base of moderate aerobic training, then strength work and occasional high-intensity intervals, with injury avoidance as the main risk to manage with age. This is the second episode in their longevity series, after an episode on diet, and it is framed as general discussion, not medical advice.

## Key points

- **Exercise as the "blockbuster drug".** Diamandis says that if exercise could be bottled it would be a trillion-dollar drug. He cites a figure he heard from the CEO of Tonal: people over 60 who exercise at least once a week cut their cancer risk threefold and all-cause mortality by 50%.
- **Zone 2 as the foundation.** Messier describes zone 2 as the point where muscles shift from burning fat to burning glucose. She cites Iñigo San Millán's research and suggests about four hours a week. Diamandis works through the formula live (220 minus age, then 60-70% of that maximum) and says he takes board meetings on a Technogym bike.
- **Muscle mass and the startup angle.** Diamandis says he is trying to add 10 pounds of muscle in a year and has invested in startups working on sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss). He notes that a new XPRIZE includes reversing muscle loss as one of its parameters. Messier says these therapies still need people to actually use their muscles.
- **Injury is the real risk.** Diamandis says his father died after a fall at night, and argues that the biggest challenge of ageing is injury followed by bed rest. Messier recommends a functional movement screen, mentions the book Original Strength, and explains why stability is not the same as flexibility.
- **Supplement stack.** Messier discusses protein, about five grams of creatine, magnesium (she estimates 85% of people are deficient) and hormone optimisation, and says she will cover peptides in a later episode.
