## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Helen Messier, Chief Medical and Science Officer of Fountain Life, present diet as one of the cheapest and most accessible levers for extending healthspan. Messier argues that no single diet fits everyone or every life stage. Lifestyle matters far more than genes, so the main principles are balance, few sugars, whole plants first, and personal measurement. Diamandis frames the episode as part of a series based on their book Longevity Practical Playbook and stresses that it is not medical advice.

## Key points

- **Healthspan over lifespan.** Diamandis separates years alive from years lived in good health. Messier says that most healthcare spending goes into the final years of chronic illness, and that the goal is to "square off" the decline curve. She estimates that around 7% of longevity is down to genetics.
- **Sugar as the first absolute.** Messier explains glycation and inflammation, and gives an evolutionary reason for the sweet tooth. Diamandis describes a 22-day no-sugar group fast he runs with Abundance360 members.
- **Measurement with consumer devices.** Both recommend continuous glucose monitors (they name Dexcom, FreeStyle Libre and Levels) because foods spike blood sugar differently in different people, and sleep and stress change the response. Diamandis predicts that within three or four years everyone will have an AI system advising them around the clock.
- **Plants, fibre and meal order.** Messier says the USDA recommends 35 grams of fibre a day, while most people get five or ten. Diamandis and Messier suggest eating vegetables first, then protein, and carbohydrates last, and "eating the rainbow".
- **Protein, fasting and drinks.** Diamandis says he aims for about one gram of protein per pound of body weight, spread through the day, and has dropped daily intermittent fasting. He also uses the ProLon fasting-mimicking diet, which is a sponsor. Messier says there is no real benefit from alcohol and suggests slow caffeine metabolisers limit coffee.
- **Healthspan XPRIZE.** The episode is sponsored by XPRIZE Healthspan, which Diamandis describes as a seven-year, $101 million competition for therapeutics that target the biology of ageing.
