## Thesis

Singer-songwriter and entrepreneur Jewel argues that happiness is a learnable skill and that the tools for learning it can be taught at scale without a therapist in every session. Drawing on her own path from an Alaskan homestead and homelessness to fame, she makes the case that mental health support should scale through curriculum and technology rather than physical footprint. Peter Diamandis, a self-described techno-optimist, presses on cost, AI and what the model needs to grow.

## Key points

- **A supply problem.** Jewel says the US is about 500,000 therapists short today, and would be 5 million short if everyone who wanted care sought it. She does not think Zoom therapy or online-therapy platforms can make that math work.
- **Innerworld's model.** Innerworld is a for-profit, anonymous virtual world that runs on a phone, laptop or VR headset. Trained lay guides lead groups of 30 through CBT and DBT skills, which she calls "cognitive behavioral immersion". Premium costs $8 a month, with a free tier. She says over 60% of users are men, unusual for this category.
- **AI as oversight.** Jewel describes AI that flags talk of suicide or self-harm for intervention and can identify a bully within 20 seconds. Diamandis cites a JAMA study in which a panel preferred chatbot answers over physicians' 79% of the time.
- **Evidence and funding.** Jewel says Innerworld has a $2 million NIH grant for a randomised controlled trial, has published two papers showing results comparable to traditional therapy, and plans a seed round within six to twelve months. Diamandis estimates it is roughly a thousand times cheaper than therapy.
- **Scaling through curriculum.** Her Inspiring Children foundation in Las Vegas started as a tennis academy and entrepreneurship school. Her Sela programme embeds mindfulness tools in a core English language arts class so that teachers do not need extra time.
- **Her personal method.** Jewel describes logging when she felt "dilated" (relaxed) or "contracted" (anxious) to build a personal map for leaving stressed states. She warns that kids now confuse excitement with happiness.
