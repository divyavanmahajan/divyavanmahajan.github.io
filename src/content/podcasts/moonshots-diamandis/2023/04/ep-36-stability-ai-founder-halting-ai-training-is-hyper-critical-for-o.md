## Thesis

In a Twitter Spaces conversation with Peter Diamandis, Stability AI CEO Emad Mostaque explains why he signed the open letter calling for a six-month pause on training large models. He doesn't expect a real pause. He argues that the next generation of compute will soon spread GPT-4-class models widely, so now is the time to set up transparent governance and industry norms. He favours open, augmenting AI over centralised AGI.

## Key points

- **Compute ramp.** Mostaque says chips like Nvidia's H100 and TPU v5 scale almost linearly, enabling 30,000-100,000-chip supercomputers. He says GPT-3 took about three months to train two years ago, while the new machines could train four a day.
- **Why six months.** He says few organisations can train these models now, but that changes once the hardware lands. He calls for proper security, since a model could be copied to a flash drive and stolen, and says he was the only private AI company CEO apart from Elon Musk to sign the letter.
- **Risks.** Mostaque lists job displacement, rights over training data, copyright of outputs and existential risk. He cites GPT-4's closed-loop money-making test and Meta's Cicero diplomacy result as signs of emerging capabilities.
- **Open models as business.** He argues that value lies in private data, so nations and regulated industries will want their own models. He predicts only a handful of proprietary model builders, and says Stable Diffusion overtook Bitcoin and Ethereum in developer stars within about two months.
- **Education and health.** Mostaque says software from his co-founder's charity helped 76% of children in refugee camps reach literacy and numeracy in 13 months, and predicts a personal AI tutor for every child within five years. Diamandis predicts that diagnosing without AI will become malpractice.
- **Countermeasures.** Asked about mass AI persuasion, Mostaque says people will need their own protective AIs and stronger authentication. He stresses transparency for anyone deploying powerful AI.
