## Thesis

Lou Reese, co-founder and executive chairman of Vaxxinity (where Peter Diamandis is co-founder and vice chairman), argues that monoclonal antibody drugs are effective but expensive and reach very few people worldwide. His pitch is a vaccine platform that gets the body to make similar antibodies itself, which he compares to the printing press for books. Around that pitch, Reese and Diamandis talk about being present, raising children through hardship, and the early hustles that shaped them.

## Key points

- **The market problem.** Reese says biologics earned over $220 billion in global sales last year and that seven of the top 10 US drugs are monoclonals. He puts the average cost of a new one above $100,000 per patient per year and says they reach less than 1% of the world.
- **The platform claim.** Reese says Vaxxinity's cost of goods is around a thousandth of a monoclonal's, with doses every six months or once a year instead of every two weeks. He hopes for "tens of dollars" at scale but says he does not know the market price.
- **Pipeline as described.** Reese mentions a cholesterol (PCSK9) program going into first human trials in Australia, an Alzheimer's vaccine heading to a registrational trial, a Parkinson's readout in 2023, and early bone- and muscle-loss work. He says a phase three COVID booster trial showed better results than AstraZeneca and Sinopharm and comparability with Pfizer.
- **Adherence as the design target.** Reese cites low statin adherence as a reason to "make the right thing easy". He argues that occasional shots solve compliance as well as cost.
- **Markets and hubris.** On Sam Bankman-Fried, Reese blames hubris, FOMO and investors following each other with little diligence more than a master plan. Both say they would not bet against Elon Musk.
- **Formative hardship.** Reese describes his father's prison sentence in the savings-and-loan collapse and his father's later death. He says these shaped his purpose of eliminating unnecessary suffering, and he argues children benefit from obstacles they face themselves.
