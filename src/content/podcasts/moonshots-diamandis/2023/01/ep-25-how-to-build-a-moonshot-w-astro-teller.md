## Thesis

In a talk recorded at the 2022 Abundance360 summit, Astro Teller, head of Alphabet's X, argues that radical innovation is an exploration problem, not an exploitation one. Using a multi-armed bandit thought experiment, he says nobody is better than random at picking which moonshot will work, but an organisation can be far better than random at gathering evidence and stopping weak bets. Culture, not rules, makes that happen.

## Key points

- **The slot-machine room.** Teller compares moonshots to an infinite room of slot machines with unknown payoffs. Sticking with one machine is gambling; the right play is to sample many and shift towards the ones the statistics favour. X's job, he says, is to buy options on the future for Alphabet, not to build businesses.
- **What counts as a moonshot.** X requires a huge problem, a science-fiction-sounding solution and a hypothesis testable within about six months. Teller says X has run about 2,000 such "what ifs" in 12 years; Waymo and Wing came out of it, while Tapestry (grid), Mineral (agriculture) and Tidal (ocean health) were still inside.
- **10x and "monkey first".** Aiming for 10x rather than 10% forces teams to start over. He tells teams to tackle the hardest part first: train the monkey before building the pedestal, and favour fast, duct-tape "version zero" experiments.
- **Diverse teams and compost.** Teller wants teams that think as differently as possible. Lasers built for the closed Loon balloon project were reused by Taara, which he says delivers fibre speeds over up to 20 km at under 1% of the cost of trenching fibre.
- **Signals over tips.** Employees follow the thousand signals an organisation sends, he says. X rates hires on traits such as creativity, humility and growth mindset, judges promotions on process rather than outcomes, and marks the Day of the Dead to grieve cancelled projects.
