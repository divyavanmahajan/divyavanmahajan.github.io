## Thesis

Jack Hidary, CEO of SandboxAQ (spun out of Alphabet with Eric Schmidt as chairman), argues in this live Abundance360 conversation with Peter Diamandis that quantum technologies are useful now. Fault-tolerant quantum computers are still a longer-term goal. In his view the combination of AI and quantum moves the tech sector from manipulating bits to manipulating atoms: sensors, molecular simulation and encryption.

## Key points

- **Computers vs technologies.** Hidary says quantum computers need roughly a thousand physical qubits per logical qubit and can be built in seven different ways. SandboxAQ chose not to build hardware and works on the application layer across GPUs and QPUs instead.
- **Quantum sensing.** Hidary describes nitrogen-vacancy diamond magnetometers sensitive to about 10^-12 Tesla. SandboxAQ uses them for room-temperature magnetocardiography, a device now at UCSF, where he says more than two years of machine-learning work was needed to pull the heart signal out of noise.
- **Navigation without GPS.** Pointing to GPS jamming reported by Qantas and Maersk, Hidary says every square meter of Earth has a unique magnetic fingerprint. He says a SandboxAQ magnetic navigation unit is flying on a test plane of a major aircraft maker and could also guide underwater drones.
- **Post-quantum security.** Hidary explains how Shor's 1994 algorithm threatens RSA and how a government-led process narrowed 82 proposals down to lattice-based standards. He warns about "store now, decrypt later" attacks and points to legacy MD5/SHA-1 left over from bank and telco mergers.
- **Simulation for drugs and materials.** Hidary says GPUs became powerful enough for large quantum-chemistry simulation around 2021-2022. He hopes to cut the 13 years and $2 billion it takes to bring a drug to market by de-risking molecules earlier, and to apply the same simulation to batteries and perovskite solar.
- **Quantum divide and open stacks.** Hidary says about 20 countries have national quantum blueprints while 170 do not. SandboxAQ licenses its core stack, co-invests in startups, and runs an education initiative with the World Economic Forum.
