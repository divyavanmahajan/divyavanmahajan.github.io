## Thesis

Peter Diamandis argues that mindset is the single most important asset an entrepreneur has, and that it is learned from parents, schools, media and peers, so it can be retrained. With Roger Hamilton, founder and CEO of Genius Group, he works through seven mindsets in order: gratitude, curiosity, moonshot, abundance, exponential, purpose-driven and longevity. Hamilton links them to levels of consciousness, with each one making the next easier to reach.

## Key points

- **Value is in how you frame the problem.** Hamilton cites a Stanford MBA exercise in which students got $5 and two hours. The top team ignored the money and sold its five-minute class presentation slot to a sponsor, earning over $1,000.
- **Gratitude and curiosity come first.** Hamilton describes a daily set of eight self-questions, such as "why am I so happy". He argues that the opposite of curiosity is a fixed mindset, which schools reward. Diamandis contrasts the old "tyranny of certainty" of experts with cheap, rapid experimentation.
- **Moonshots need a separate team.** Citing Astro Teller, Diamandis defines a moonshot as aiming for 10x when others aim for 10%. He advises keeping the core business focused on delivery and putting the moonshot team on the edge, reporting to the CEO, so the organisation's "immune reaction" does not kill it.
- **The 6 Ds.** Diamandis explains digitization, deception, disruption, dematerialization, demonetization and democratization. His examples are Kodak, which invented the digital camera but went bankrupt, and Instagram, which sold to Facebook for a billion dollars with 13 employees.
- **Share what is abundant.** Hamilton says money shrinks when you share it, but ideas, connections and trust grow. Diamandis argues that technology keeps turning scarcity into abundance, for example in energy and photography.
- **Purpose and longevity.** Hamilton says Genius Group is approaching 5 million students, growing about 50% a year, and aims for 100 million by 2030. Diamandis says he hires for purpose. He also cites Ray Kurzweil's view that "longevity escape velocity" could arrive in 10 to 12 years.
