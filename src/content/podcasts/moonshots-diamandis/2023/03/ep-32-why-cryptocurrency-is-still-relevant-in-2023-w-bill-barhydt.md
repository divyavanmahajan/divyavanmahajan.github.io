## Thesis

Bill Barhydt, founder and CEO of the crypto bank Abra (Peter Diamandis discloses he is an adviser and a customer), argues that the failures of FTX, Celsius, Voyager and BlockFi came from leverage, rehypothecation and fraud at centralised companies, while the underlying protocols kept working. He compares the moment to the dot-com crash: markets punished retail investors, but the technology kept scaling. Diamandis uses the conversation as a crypto primer and to ask where prices and adoption are heading.

## Key points

- **Origins.** Barhydt traces Abra to his cypherpunk background and to costly remittance work in Haiti, where he says fees ran from 6 to 20%. He describes Bitcoin as deliberately inefficient because it was built to solve the double-spend problem without trusted third parties.
- **Primer.** Barhydt separates Bitcoin (decentralised money and "digital gold") from Ethereum (a "world's computer" for smart contracts, now on proof of stake). He names DeFi, stablecoins and NFTs as Ethereum's three main applications.
- **Why firms failed.** Barhydt compares the 2022 collapses to Long-Term Capital Management: firms lent the same collateral to one another. He says FTX went further by sending client deposits to Alameda and borrowing against its own token, while DeFi contracts kept liquidating as programmed.
- **Regulation and Abra Bank.** Barhydt says the rules FTX broke already existed. He announces plans for Abra Bank, a state-chartered depository institution, and argues exchanges should not operate under money-transmitter licences like Western Union.
- **Price predictions.** Barhydt predicts Bitcoin will hit $1 million within a couple of years of reaching a billion funded wallets (he estimates about 200 million today), with $250,000-300,000 possible in the next cycle. He opposes central bank digital currencies on surveillance grounds.
- **Allocation.** Both stress this is not financial advice. Barhydt says he puts new crypto money 60% into Ethereum and 40% into Bitcoin. Diamandis says he targets 20% of his portfolio in crypto, historically 80/20 Bitcoin to Ethereum.
