## Thesis

Marian Tupy (Cato Institute, HumanProgress.org) and Gale Pooley (BYU-Hawaii), co-authors of Superabundance, argue against the Malthusian view that more people means scarcer resources. Measuring prices in hours of work rather than money, they say abundance has grown faster than population across hundreds of commodities since 1850, because people create knowledge as well as consume. Peter Diamandis connects this to his own Abundance thesis and to exponential technologies.

## Key points

- **Time prices.** Pooley explains a time price as the money price divided by hourly income, which avoids inflation and currency adjustments. Tupy says the "basic 50" commodities cost the average world inhabitant 72% less work in 2018 than in 1980, and abundance grew about 250%.
- **Population multiplier.** Pooley claims that for every 1% rise in population, personal resource abundance rises 3 to 4%. The Simon Abundance Index, which grew out of a five-commodity bet and extends it to 50 commodities over 38 years, multiplies that personal gain by population.
- **Exceptions are regulatory.** Tupy says market-driven goods such as appliances, cars and even average US housing got cheaper relative to income, while health care, education and child care got more expensive, which he blames on subsidies and over-regulation. Cosmetic surgery, largely unsubsidised, got cheaper.
- **Knowledge plus atoms.** The guests use aluminium, sand-to-microchips and substitution (whale oil, lithium) to argue that resources are defined by know-how. Diamandis adds lab-grown diamonds from Diamond Foundry and the idea of nano assemblers.
- **Population peak.** Tupy cites projections of a peak around 10 billion between 2060 and 2080, Lancet estimates of 7 to 9 billion by 2100, and fertility of 0.9 in South Korea and 1.7 among US native-born women.
- **Risks to progress.** Tupy warns about degrowth theory and recalls Rome and Song China as periods of progress that ended. Pooley argues political power draws on a sense of scarcity. Their formula is superabundance equals population times freedom.
