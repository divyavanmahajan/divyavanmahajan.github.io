## Thesis

In a live conversation moderated by Tori Higgins of the Flow Research Collective, Steven Kotler and Peter Diamandis argue that healthspan, the years lived in good health, depends heavily on mindset, purpose and continued learning. Kotler, drawing on his book Gnar Country, says skills once thought to decline with age are "use it or lose it". Diamandis adds that new technologies could extend healthy years further for people who stay engaged long enough to benefit.

## Key points

- **Lifespan vs healthspan.** Diamandis says most Americans who reach 80 are healthy only until about 65. He wants to compress that period of decline, and cites estimates that genes explain only 7-30% of life expectancy.
- **Use it or lose it.** Kotler, who learned park skiing in his 50s, cites octogenarian triathletes with the VO2 max of healthy 35-year-olds. He argues that rising risk aversion and fear block the learning and creativity gains that he says arrive in midlife.
- **Mindset as a measurable factor.** Kotler cites research linking a positive mindset towards aging to about seven and a half extra healthy years. He also says exposure to ageism predicts 30% greater memory decline by 60.
- **Purpose and play.** Both recommend a massively transformative purpose pursued with people you like. Kotler describes "dynamic deliberate play" (strength, stamina, agility, flexibility and balance, practised through improvisation rather than drilling) and says passion grows slowly from overlapping curiosities.
- **Regenerative medicine outlook.** Diamandis predicts that cartilage and hair regrowth, organ approaches such as Martine Rothblatt's work at United Therapeutics, and treatments for muscle and bone loss will arrive within about five years. Kotler is more cautious and says organ regeneration is "not there yet".
- **Basics first.** Diamandis ranks exercise, then sleep, then cutting sugar. Kotler adds strong social connection and less smartphone use.
