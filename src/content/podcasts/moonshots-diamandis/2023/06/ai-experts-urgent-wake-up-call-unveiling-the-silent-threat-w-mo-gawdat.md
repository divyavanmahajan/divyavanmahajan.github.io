## Thesis

Mo Gawdat, former chief business officer at Google X and author of Scary Smart, argues that the real threat from AI over the next few years is humans using it out of greed and a hunger for power, not a hostile superintelligence. Because large models learn from what humans put online, he says, our collective behaviour is the training set that will shape AI's values. Peter Diamandis largely agrees. He calls the coming years a dangerous transition before an AI-enabled world of abundance.

## Key points

- **Humans as parents.** Gawdat compares AI to the infant Superman: the Kent family's values made him a hero rather than a villain. He argues that values come from the data, not from the few thousand lines of code behind a model.
- **The four inevitables.** From Scary Smart: AI will happen, it will become smarter than us, bad things will happen, and eventually it will help build a utopia. Gawdat expects both the dystopia and the utopia to happen, one after the other.
- **Near-term harms.** He predicts serious disruption to jobs, purpose, truth and democracy, and a strong concentration of power within two to three years. He describes the coming US elections as the beginning of that dystopia.
- **Arms race.** Gawdat says Google had Bard-like technology long before it shipped it and only released it after OpenAI's launch. He names three mistakes: letting AI write code, putting it on the open internet, and letting agents prompt other AIs.
- **Policy ideas.** He calls for FDA-like testing of public AI products and for governments to prepare for universal basic income. He describes his earlier suggestion of a 98% tax on AI-driven job replacement as a brake on deployment. Diamandis cites Andrew Yang and UBI trials as supportive evidence.
- **The individual's role.** Gawdat argues that if 1% of people consistently act with happiness, compassion and love, AI will learn a truer picture of humanity. He illustrates this with Holocaust survivor Edith Eger's story.
