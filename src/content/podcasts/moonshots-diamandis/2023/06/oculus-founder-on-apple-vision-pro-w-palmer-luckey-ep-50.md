## Thesis

Palmer Luckey, founder of Oculus and Anduril, tells Peter Diamandis that Apple's Vision Pro takes the right approach: build the best possible headset first and lower the price later. He then argues that US defense procurement is broken by cost-plus contracting and too little competition. Anduril's answer is to spend its own money on products and sell them finished.

## Key points

- **Vision Pro strategy.** Luckey says Apple got nearly everything right by aiming at the high end. In his view early adopters don't care about the $3,500 price, and the real goal is to make far more people want the product. He praises the tethered battery puck for moving weight off the head.
- **Where Oculus went wrong.** Citing his "free isn't cheap enough" post, Luckey argues Meta pushed for the mainstream before quality and content were good enough. He says Quest Pro shipped eye and face tracking without demo apps to show what they could do.
- **Social engineering, not thinner glasses.** Luckey predicts headsets will end up as "chunky sunglasses" rather than thin glasses. He expects celebrities and influencers, not engineering, to make wearing them in public acceptable.
- **Defense procurement.** Luckey claims 80% of the defense procurement budget goes to five companies and that 30% of major weapon system contracts get only one bidder. He says cost-plus contracts reward slow, expensive work. He also calls patents "Chinese instruction manuals" and proposes a patent system that only US and allied citizens can access.
- **Anduril's model.** Luckey describes Anduril as a software company wrapped in hardware, with twice as many software as hardware staff. Its Lattice AI platform links products such as the Ghost drone and ALTIUS 600. He cites the $250 million autonomous surveillance tower program as a record win for a young company.
- **Wildfires and talent.** Diamandis outlines the $11 million Wildfire XPRIZE, and Luckey says ending destructive wildfires is possible with today's technology. Luckey also tells people to follow their talents rather than their dreams, and to hire people who "radically outclass" them.
