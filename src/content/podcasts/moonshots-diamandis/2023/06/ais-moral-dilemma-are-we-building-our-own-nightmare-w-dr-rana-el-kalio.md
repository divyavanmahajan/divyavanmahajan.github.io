## Thesis

Rana el Kaliouby, co-founder and CEO of Affectiva, argues that AI is taking on human roles but lacks empathy. She says it can be taught to simulate emotional intelligence by reading non-verbal cues. In this stage conversation at Peter Diamandis's Abundance 360 summit (March 2023), she says ethics has to be built in from the start, through attention to bias in development and consent in deployment.

## Key points

- **Non-verbal signals.** El Kaliouby says 93% of communication is non-verbal. Her models started with three facial expressions and now detect more than 50 emotional, cognitive and behavioural states, from drowsiness to confusion. Using in-car videos of drowsy and distracted drivers, she argues that a car's response needs empathy, or drivers will switch the system off.
- **Practise empathy with machines.** Diamandis cites Mo Gawdat's book "Scary Smart" and says he thanks Alexa. El Kaliouby agrees that people should say please and thank you to AI so they do not lose the habit of empathy.
- **Ethics in two buckets.** On development, el Kaliouby warns that a model trained mostly on middle-aged white men will fail for diverse users. She says she tied her executives' bonuses to applying ethics in engineering and product work. On deployment, she points to the risk that personal data gets exploited.
- **Turning down money.** She says Affectiva, which she co-founded with Rosalind Picard, made consent a core value. In 2011, when it was nearly out of money, it rejected funding from an intelligence agency's venture arm that was tied to surveillance work.
- **Human relationships.** She cites a USC study in which PTSD patients were more open with a digital avatar than with a human therapist. She also describes her son's grief when the Jibo social robot shut down, and asks what this means for human-to-human relationships.
- **Mirror of society.** El Kaliouby notes there is no universal ethical framework, but points to the World Economic Forum and the Partnership on AI. She also sees AI health companions that could flag deviations in mental-health biomarkers.
