## Thesis

In this introduction to Exponential Organizations 2.0, Salim Ismail defines an exponential organization (ExO) as one that can scale the organization as fast as it scales technology, because its marginal cost of supply is close to zero. Peter Diamandis claims that companies which do not digitize, dematerialize, demonetize and democratize their offerings will be gone within ten years. Both say the shift from scarcity-based to abundance-based business models is unprecedented.

## Key points

- **Cost of supply.** Ismail says the internet cut the cost of demand. ExOs such as Airbnb and Uber also cut the cost of supply: adding a room or a car costs them almost nothing, whereas Hyatt has to build a hotel.
- **The 6 Ds and Kodak.** Diamandis retells how Kodak invented a 0.01-megapixel digital camera and ignored it to protect its paper and chemicals business. Instagram, with 13 employees, was bought by Facebook for a billion dollars the same year Kodak went bankrupt.
- **Changing the problem space.** Ismail argues that abundance turns a sourcing problem into a filtering problem and wipes out business models built on scarcity. Music moved from studios selling CDs to subscriptions on iTunes and Spotify, and he expects energy, healthcare and education to follow.
- **No slowdown.** Diamandis shows Ray Kurzweil's law of accelerating returns as a chain of nested S-curves. Ismail cites Ralph Merkle's claim of 10 orders of magnitude from reversible computation, and says a dozen technologies are now doubling at once.
- **Reinvent constantly.** Ismail advises reinventing the organization every year or two. He cites Illumina, which ran four 28-month development cycles in parallel to match a nine-month product shelf life.
- **Interface moments.** Diamandis points to Mosaic. Ismail adds the smartphone, Coinbase and ChatGPT (a million users in 5 days) as moments when a technology became usable and moved from deceptive to disruptive. Ismail says the only option for large companies is to spot and buy the "furry mammals", as Google did with YouTube.
