## Thesis

Naveen Jain, CEO of Viome, and Guru Banavar, its CTO and head of AI (formerly leading the IBM Watson team), argue that healthcare has failed at chronic disease because it works from shallow, analog data. Their bet is that collecting dynamic biological data (gene expression of both human cells and microbes) at consumer scale, then applying AI, enables personalised recommendations and early detection. Peter Diamandis, whose fund Bold Capital is a Viome investor and board member, frames it as a model moonshot company.

## Key points

- **Digitise the body first.** Jain says chronic disease takes most healthcare spending and that static DNA cannot track it; Viome licensed an RNA analysis technology from Los Alamos National Lab, originally built for biodefense, to measure what is actually changing.
- **Data from the biology, not the billing system.** Banavar says his IBM-era health data was mostly delivery-side (claims, notes). He credits new biological data, transformer-era algorithms and Nvidia compute for what became possible in the last five years. Jain cites over 600,000 samples and 52.5 quadrillion data points.
- **Consumer business as data engine.** Rather than funding disease-by-disease studies, Jain built a direct-to-consumer product (test, app, robotically compounded personalised supplements) that also collects symptom questionnaires, creating closed-loop, longitudinal data. He says Viome holds an FDA breakthrough device designation for an early oral and throat cancer test.
- **Biological digital twin.** Banavar describes per-person model collections used to simulate food responses, risk and interventions, and argues AI must move from correlation to causation through experimentation.
- **Future of medicine and work.** Banavar gives a 50% chance of human-level AI by 2050; Jain says around 2035. Jain expects clinicians to become AI-driven coaches valued for trust and empathy, and tells his children entrepreneurship is the safest skill; Banavar counters that deep scientists remain essential.
- **Advice for founders.** Jain says to focus on improving customers' lives rather than revenue, noting that in health you get one shot at trust.
