## Thesis

In an audience Q&A recorded at the Abundance360 Summit, Khan Academy founder Sal Khan argues that within ten years every student will have a capable AI tutor, with infrastructure, electricity and war as the main barriers. Acumen founder Jacqueline Novogratz focuses on what humans keep: she argues that care, community and moral character will be the skills people value and eventually pay for as AI absorbs routine work.

## Key points

- **A tutor for everyone.** Khan predicts AI tutors that could drive a student's whole education, with credentials for proven skills and automatic links to internships and jobs. He says he has never been more bullish on education.
- **Khanmigo economics.** Khan says GPT-4 is costly to run, on the order of $5-15 a month per user. During the beta, donors giving about $20 a month can join the waiting list. He expects costs to fall by a factor of 10 to 50 within 6 to 12 months.
- **Jobs and displacement.** Responding to an 11-year-old's question, Novogratz notes that India adds 20 million young people to its labour force each year and that a college degree no longer guarantees a job. She hopes technology augments workers rather than pushing them aside.
- **Human skills.** Khan says prompt engineering looked like the skill of the future until models became more steerable. He now points to critical thinking and human connection. Novogratz describes a "caring economy" in which community health accompaniment models keep people healthy and can be monetised.
- **Skills over degrees.** Khan says Khan Academy is talking to companies about treating learners who complete its material like graduates with a 3.5 GPA from Stanford. He wants to see vocational training beyond the four-year degree.
- **Funding gap.** Khan contrasts Khan Academy's budget of about $60 million a year with $5 trillion in global education spending. Diamandis relays will.i.am's remark that money to educate AIs is easy to raise and money to educate kids is hard.
