## Thesis

This X Spaces conversation was recorded during the days of turmoil on OpenAI's board. In it, Stability AI founder Emad Mostaque argues that AI is becoming knowledge infrastructure, and that no single company or unelected board should control it. In his view, safety comes from transparent, high-quality training data and from models owned locally by each nation, not from containment. Peter Diamandis pushes on governance and timelines and frames the outcome as a choice between abundance and disruption.

## Key points

- **The OpenAI board episode.** Mostaque calls it a lesson that "it's very hard to align humans." He finds it anti-democratic that a few unelected people could steer a technology that, by OpenAI's own account, could upend society.
- **Data standards, not containment.** Mostaque rejects containment as an option, citing the idea that bigger models are a shortcut for poor-quality data. He says a Stability study found that 92% of the data isn't used 99% of the time. He calls for ISO-style data standards, transparent training sets and model "credentials".
- **Nationally owned models.** Stability says it is training models for half a dozen nations, including Japanese models. Mostaque wants local "Stability" entities in which, for example, graduates get equity. He argues that culture should not be outsourced to foreign black boxes.
- **Graduate unemployment wave.** Mostaque predicts fewer graduate hires because AI raises productivity, and cites a reported 50% drop in hiring from Indian IITs. He favours "universal basic jobs" over UBI. Diamandis points to 2024 elections as another near-term risk.
- **Bold timelines.** Mostaque predicts GPT-4-level performance on a smartphone within 12 to 18 months. He says the need for supercomputers will fade in a year or two, and that turning a bedtime story into a movie is about two years away. Diamandis expects a century of progress between now and 2033.
- **Positive uses.** Mostaque describes using a thousand AI agents to research a loved one's pancreatic cancer misdiagnosis, and wants a global "cancer GPT". Diamandis previews the $101 million XPRIZE in health.
