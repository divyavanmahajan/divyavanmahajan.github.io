## Thesis

In this solo talk, Peter Diamandis argues that people are living through a "health span revolution". In his view, four forces make it happen now: investment capital, AI and quantum computing, a biotech revolution, and a diagnostics revolution. He presents ageing as a hardware or software problem that science may be able to slow or reverse, and he says the healthy human lifespan could be extended within this decade.

## Key points

- **Capital is flowing in.** Diamandis says more than $5 billion was invested in longevity in 2022 and predicts a tenfold rise this decade. He names Hevolution as a foundation funding research at the scale of billions.
- **Sequencing and editing costs collapsed.** He compares Craig Venter's $100 million, nine-month genome with today's roughly $200 and seven hours. He cites over 200 people treated with CRISPR therapeutics and predicts a near-term future without genetic disease.
- **Epigenetic reprogramming.** Diamandis presents ageing as a blurring of which genes are switched on or off. He points to George Church's and David Sinclair's work, including Sinclair's mouse study using three of the four Yamanaka factors, which he says is now moving into primates.
- **A pipeline of biotech examples.** He lists lab-grown O-negative blood, 3D-printed heart valves grown from iPS cells, and vaccines from Vaxxinity, a company he co-founded. He says its PCSK9 cholesterol vaccine might cost about $50 a year, compared with about $10,000 for antibody treatment.
- **Diagnostics and the economic case.** Diamandis promotes Fountain Life, which he co-founded, and says 14.4% of its first 5,000 seemingly healthy members had an actionable finding. He cites a Harvard/Oxford/LBS study valuing one extra year of health span at $38 trillion.
- **Longevity escape velocity.** He closes with the idea from Aubrey de Grey and Ray Kurzweil, and reports Kurzweil's prediction that it is 10 to 12 years away.
