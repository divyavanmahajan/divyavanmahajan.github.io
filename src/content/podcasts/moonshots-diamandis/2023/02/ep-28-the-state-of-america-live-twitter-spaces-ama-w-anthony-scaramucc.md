## Thesis

In a live Twitter Spaces AMA, Peter Diamandis and SkyBridge founder Anthony Scaramucci argue that living standards have risen sharply thanks to technology, even though many people feel pessimistic. Diamandis puts the gloom down to brain wiring and negative news media. Scaramucci adds a shaken world order and social-media comparison. The conversation then turns to education, careers built on passion, longevity and Scaramucci's account of being taken in by Sam Bankman-Fried.

## Key points

- **Abundance versus pessimism.** Diamandis says the work time needed to earn goods and services has fallen 72% over 40 years, and he contrasts the seven breakthroughs he counts for 1922 with today's pace. Scaramucci cites Charlie Munger's view that living standards rose sixfold in a century, with social media fuelling envy.
- **Longevity debate.** Diamandis wants to add 20-30 healthy years through epigenetic reprogramming. Scaramucci sides with Elon Musk's view that generational turnover has value. He also worries that the very wealthy would gain access first and concentrate wealth further.
- **Is an MBA worth it?** Scaramucci says it depends on the person. He values Stanford's network for his son AJ, but points to Michael Dell and his son Anthony as people who did well without formal degrees. A caller argues the basics of accounting can be learned online.
- **Education and opportunity.** Scaramucci argues for equal opportunity rather than equal outcomes and says the quality of K-12 schooling varies widely by ZIP code. Diamandis says AI-driven education and health care will eventually be the same for rich and poor.
- **FTX lessons.** Scaramucci says SkyBridge's core fund fell 38% in 2022 and that he sold a stake to Bankman-Fried. In his view, the fraud depended on a handful of insiders and on accounting back doors that hid customer withdrawals. He says it won't stop him taking risks.
- **Brain-computer interfaces.** Diamandis cites Ray Kurzweil's prediction of high-bandwidth brain-cloud links in the early 2030s.
