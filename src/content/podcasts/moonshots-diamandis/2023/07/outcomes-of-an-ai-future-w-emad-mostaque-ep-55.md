## Thesis

Stability AI CEO Emad Mostaque tells Peter Diamandis that generative AI is still at the equivalent of the iPhone's 2G-to-3G stage, yet it is already changing every knowledge industry. He argues that the biggest risk is what models are trained on: internet scrapes full of junk. His answer is high-quality, open, national datasets, together with open models that companies and countries can adapt to their own data.

## Key points

- **Adoption is early.** Mostaque cites a study finding that only 17% of Americans had used ChatGPT. He expects company-wide rollouts in knowledge work by the following year.
- **Media and Hollywood.** Recorded during the Screen Actors Guild strike. Mostaque points to a studio proposal to scan background actors once and reuse them, and predicts personalised movies in three to five years. He says Stability let creators opt 169 million images out of its datasets.
- **Coding and medicine.** He predicts that no coder will work without AI, which could make "a billion coders" possible. He also says Google's Med-PaLM 2 beat doctors on clinical diagnosis and on empathy scores. Diamandis predicts that diagnosing without AI will become malpractice within five years.
- **Compute and money.** Mostaque says Stability runs about 7,000-8,000 A100s and expects the equivalent of 70,000 next year. He forecasts a trillion dollars flowing into the sector.
- **Data as safety.** He cites DataComp, a 12-billion-image dataset, where a model trained on a curated subset beat OpenAI's image-to-text model with a tenth of the compute. He calls for every nation to build its own dataset and puts the window to act at "a couple of years".
- **Business model and risks.** Stability plans to earn revenue share, licence fees and royalties on base models that customers adapt to their private data. Mostaque calls himself "a pessimist at the core". He worries about deflationary shocks and calls the "pivotal action" approach some labs take terrifying. Diamandis argues that UBI may become necessary.
