## Thesis

Mo Gawdat, former Chief Business Officer at Google X, argues that AI is already smarter than he is. In his view the problem is not AI but human values. Those values will produce a near-term dystopia until competitive pressure forces people to hand decisions over to AI, and he expects that handover to start a period of abundance. Salim Ismail broadly agrees but expects change to be slower and uneven.

## Key points

- **Timeline.** Asked how long it will take to reinvent business and government, Gawdat says five years and Ismail around ten. Ismail cites William Gibson's line that the future is unevenly distributed, and says that unevenness causes much of today's stress.
- **The "second dilemma".** Gawdat says rivals will be forced to hand decisions to AI or become irrelevant. He expects the dystopia to be felt by 2027 and to last about ten more years, until that handover is complete. He sums it up with the acronym FACE RIPS: freedom, accountability, connection, economics, reality, innovation, power.
- **Ethics over control.** Gawdat argues that models learn their values from how people treat them. He says teaching AI ethics, not just alignment, would make the dystopia shorter and less severe. Ismail warns that emotionally damaged humans remain the bigger danger.
- **Power and surveillance.** Gawdat predicts trillionaires and a concentration of power alongside cheap open models like DeepSeek. He says these two forces together will lead to more surveillance and oppression in the West.
- **Jobs.** Ismail points out that Sweden, South Korea and Germany have high robot penetration and low unemployment. His bigger worry is that institutions cannot manage the transition. Gawdat expects real suffering among displaced workers and suggests that social systems, not new jobs, are the answer.
- **Personal response.** Diamandis plans AI avatars of himself with HeyGen. Gawdat argues instead for doubling down on human connection. He is publishing his book Alive on Substack, written in dialogue with an AI, and frames stress as challenges divided by a person's skills and resources.
