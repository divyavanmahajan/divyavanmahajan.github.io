## Thesis

Peter Diamandis asks his longtime co-author Steven Kotler, executive director of the Flow Research Collective, whether AI will enhance or destroy human creativity. Diamandis leans towards AI eventually matching humans in every creative field. Kotler argues that AI is a convergent pattern-matcher, while humans excel at far-flung lateral thinking. He also says flow training and neurotech are improving humans at the same time, so humans in flow working with AI will own the future for at least the next 10 to 15 years.

## Key points

- **AI lifts the bottom and the top.** Kotler says AI brings low performers up to the middle, and claims, without data he has seen, that it also lifts top performers higher. He notes that great writing breaks the expected pattern, which probabilistic LLMs are not built to do.
- **Convergent vs divergent.** Diamandis cites a study where an AI chatbot alone diagnosed about 95% correctly, against roughly 75% for physicians alone. Kotler replies that diagnosis is convergent thinking, not creativity.
- **Flow numbers.** Kotler cites a McKinsey finding that executives are 500% more productive in flow, and self-reports of 700% more creativity. He says 15,000 people have taken his Zero to Dangerous course, with an average 73.8% increase in time spent in flow. He also mentions portable EEG from his partner Vital Neuro, which picks up flow markers that once needed fMRI.
- **The challenge problem.** Diamandis raises the Universe 25 rat experiment and worries that automation may remove the challenge humans need. Kotler expects "made by humans" labels and AI-free countries. He also says AI can push people into cognitive overload within minutes.
- **The brain of a creative.** Kotler describes the executive attention network and the default mode network working together in creatives, with dopamine and norepinephrine boosting pattern recognition.
- **The Alliance.** Kotler announces an eight-month program for 100 "super creatives" that combines flow training, AI skills and accountability for finishing a masterpiece. He says loneliness is the problem that resonates most with creatives.
