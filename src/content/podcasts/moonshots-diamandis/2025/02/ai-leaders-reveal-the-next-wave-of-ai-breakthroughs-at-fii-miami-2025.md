## Thesis

Peter Diamandis moderates a panel at the FII Miami conference with five AI executives. Each argues that AI is moving past the "shiny object" phase into specialised, cheaper and more practical systems: narrow models for film, small on-device models, quantitative models for chemistry and open, low-cost hardware. McKinsey's Alexander Sukharevsky adds a caution: most enterprise AI projects never reach production unless top leadership commits fully.

## Key points

- **Generative video for film.** Prem Akkaraju (Stability AI) says Stable Diffusion has passed 270 million downloads. Stability is building dozens of "hyper narrow" models for individual visual-effects steps, such as rotoscoping. He predicts realistic on-demand video within 6-12 months and compares resistance to AI with the arrival of sound and colour in film.
- **Private, on-device AI.** Ramin Hasani (Liquid AI) describes liquid neural networks from MIT that need very little compute. He says they can deliver a ChatGPT-like experience locally on phones, for privacy-sensitive enterprises. He notes the US Air Force used the technology to fly a fighter jet.
- **Large quantitative models.** Jack Hidary (SandboxAQ) contrasts language models with models trained on molecules and atoms. He says these run quantum equations on GPUs today, with customers including Aramco. He expects useful quantum computers in five to seven years.
- **Open, cheaper hardware.** Jim Keller (Tenstorrent) says his company builds native tensor processors and has open-sourced its software stack. He targets systems 5-10x cheaper than current ones and has asked his software team to double productivity this year.
- **Enterprise reality check.** Sukharevsky says only about 11% of AI use cases outside tech companies reached production in five years, and perhaps 7% for generative AI. He argues that success requires the CEO's commitment, clean data and new architecture.
- **Small teams.** Hidary says an 11-person team armed with AI and quantum sensors solved GPS-jamming navigation for the US Air Force.
