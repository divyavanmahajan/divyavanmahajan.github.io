## Thesis

Jeremy Allaire, co-founder and CEO of Circle, argues that fully reserved, regulated stablecoins such as USDC separate payment money from lending and bring the internet's openness and near-zero transfer costs to the dollar. He frames the GENIUS Act as a way for the US to export dollar infrastructure the way it exported the internet. With Emad Mostaque and Salim Ismail, Peter Diamandis explores how AI agents and on-chain companies could make stablecoins the default rail.

## Key points

- **Full reserve backing.** Allaire defines a payment stablecoin as a fiat token backed one for one. He says about 90% of USDC reserves sit in the Circle Reserve Fund built with BlackRock (short-dated Treasuries and overcollateralized repo), with most of the rest held as cash at banks such as BNY Mellon. Diamandis cites a USDC market cap of $76 billion.
- **CBDCs vs private money.** Allaire links doubts about the dollar to rising debt and the sanctions on Russia. He says China's eCNY found few users because Alipay and WeChat Pay were more useful, and notes the US has effectively banned a CBDC while the digital euro is estimated for 2029.
- **Agents and programmable money.** Answering Mostaque, Allaire predicts most stablecoin transactions will be AI-intermediated within five years. He mentions Circle's x402 microtransaction toolkit, its Arc blockchain with sub-second finality, and an experimental refund protocol backed by an insurance pool. Mostaque asks whether central bankers are ready for faster monetary velocity.
- **On-chain corporations.** Allaire describes companies run in software, with a token-funded treasury, on-chain governance and work delegated to humans and agents. He cites Hyperliquid, which he says runs on about 11 people and more than a billion dollars of revenue. Ismail says he advises CEOs to build an AI-native digital twin of the company on chain.
- **Adoption today.** Allaire points to cross-border payments, dollar savings, card settlement through Visa and Mastercard, and Shopify and Stripe support. He admits retail e-commerce use is still small and expects on-chain treasury management to grow among CFOs.
