## Thesis

Anatoly Yakovenko tells Peter Diamandis and the Moonshot Mates that Solana is meant to be the execution layer for global markets, a single synchronised state machine for trading. He argues that today's financial system works but is human-bound and expensive, and that on-chain settlement, made possible by stablecoin legislation, will squeeze intermediaries out. The Mates push him on regulation, governance and what wealth will mean.

## Key points

- **Proof of history from Qualcomm.** Yakovenko traces Solana to a 4 a.m. idea in 2017: a cryptographic clock lets a blockchain avoid collisions the way TDMA does in cellular radio. His rough estimate was at least 10,000 times faster than Bitcoin. A consensus team from ETH Zurich is now replacing proof of history with newer protocols such as Alpenglow.
- **One layer for all markets.** He describes concurrent block producers in places like Singapore and New York feeding one global ledger, and calls it an engineering problem rather than a computer science one. He expects projected stablecoin issuance of 1 to 10 trillion dollars over five years to speed this up.
- **Finance as a tax.** Citing Figma's IPO costs, FTX's two-year bankruptcy and the near-absence of small IPOs, Yakovenko and Blundin argue that direct on-chain listings and programmatic liquidations (every 400 milliseconds on Kamino) would remove much of that friction.
- **What the token is for.** Yakovenko says SOL exists only to prevent spam. Its value comes from the opportunity cost of being late to a trade, which he did not plan for. He calls crypto mostly a B2B system that perhaps one person in 200 needs to understand.
- **Seeker phone and app stores.** Half of Solana's phone pre-sale was paid in stablecoins. Yakovenko also wants to undercut the 20% rake Apple and Google take on digital spending.
- **Governance and jobs.** Wissner-Gross asks whether law becomes code; Yakovenko thinks clear limits on liability matter more than new statutes, and points to futarchy and decision markets. He is more optimistic about AI and jobs than the hosts, saying the steam engine was a bigger labour shock.
