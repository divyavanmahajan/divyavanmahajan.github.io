## Thesis

Marc Benioff, chair and CEO of Salesforce, says he is focused on a single goal: Agentforce and delivering a billion agents. He describes agents as digital labor that works alongside human employees today, not as a future AGI. Peter Diamandis keeps pushing toward the long-term future, and Benioff keeps steering back to the present, stressing that no model is 100% accurate.

## Key points

- **Focus.** Benioff recounts Steve Jobs telling him that Apple had one good team that could do one thing at a time. He applies the same rule to Agentforce, which sits as an agentic layer on top of Salesforce's Customer 360 platform and Data Cloud.
- **Support numbers.** Benioff says help.salesforce.com handles about 36,000 inquiries a week. Agents now resolve about 31,000 of them, and 5,000 still go to humans. He plans to move a couple of thousand of his 9,000 support staff into other jobs.
- **Engineering.** Benioff says his head of engineering reported 30% higher productivity from AI tools and asked for no new engineers this year. At the same time, Salesforce is trying to hire 2,000 more salespeople.
- **Adoption and limits.** He reports more than 1,500 paid Agentforce deals and about 3,000 customers implementing it. He names Wiley, Disney and RBC as examples. He says the Atlas reasoning engine is "in the 90s" on accuracy, but not 100%.
- **Jobs and labor.** Benioff expects jobs to shift as they have with past technologies. He presents agents and humanoid robots as a response to declining birth rates and labor shortages.
- **Other bets.** Benioff's other four focus areas are humanoid robots, energy (he is an investor in fusion company Commonwealth Fusion), regenerative biology, and planetary health (the trillion-trees idea and ocean plastic cleanup). He gives founders a five-part framework: intention, values, plan, obstacles and KPIs.
