## Thesis

Recorded days after the Palisades fire in Los Angeles, the episode has Steven Kotler warning that experts expect most forests in the American West to burn within 10 to 20 years. He and Peter Diamandis argue that the technology to prevent this largely exists. In their view, what is missing is capital, scale and regulatory room for entrepreneurs. Diamandis also warns about a "disaster after the disaster": toxic smoke and water after the fires.

## Key points

- **Fuel loads.** Kotler says fires put themselves out when woody biomass is below 59%, but fuel loads across the West are above 60%. He estimates 248 million bone-dry tons need removing around Lake Tahoe alone, and says three years of his own efforts in LA, Nevada and Utah went nowhere.
- **Underfunded market.** Kotler cites a $250 billion damage figure for LA and criticises venture capital, saying there is one fire-tech fund with about $35 million. He calls disaster mitigation a trillion-dollar market.
- **Wildfire XPRIZE.** Diamandis describes the $11 million prize: detect any fire 3 m or larger in a thousand-acre area and put it out within 10 minutes, plus a space-based monitoring track. It has 29 semi-finalists from 135 teams. He asks the FAA for permission to run the drone-heavy finals in the US.
- **Insurance incentives.** Diamandis says insurance pays out after the loss and should instead pay for prevention. Kotler adds that roof sprinklers, which he says the data favours, can void a homeowner's policy.
- **Technologies to scale.** Kotler lists tree-clearing robots (BurnBot, TreeMech), FireWorld's jet engines for steering fires, Perimeter Solutions' plant-based retardant, biochar, and franchised modular sawmills. He notes that sawmill numbers fell 40% between 1985 and 2016.
- **Health fallout.** Diamandis says air-quality monitors measure particulates but miss metals, dioxins and volatile compounds from burned homes, cars and electronics. Kotler raises mental health, citing research from his Flow Research Collective on flow and PTSD.
