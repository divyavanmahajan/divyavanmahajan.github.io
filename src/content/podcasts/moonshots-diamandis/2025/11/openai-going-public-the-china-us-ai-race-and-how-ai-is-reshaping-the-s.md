## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alex Wissner-Gross argue that AI has become the core of the economy. They read OpenAI's restructuring, Nvidia's $5 trillion valuation and new chip, energy and robot projects as one loop in which AI builds the infrastructure for more AI.

## Key points

- **OpenAI's growth path.** Wissner-Gross thinks OpenAI could reach $100 billion in annual revenue by 2027 by running agents around the clock. Blundin notes that about half the forecast comes from subscriptions across 800 million users, and the other half from commerce that competes with Google and Amazon.
- **Stocks versus jobs.** Diamandis shows a chart where job openings fell from 11 to 7 million while the S&P 500 climbed from late 2023. Blundin sees a historic break. Wissner-Gross puts it down to interest rates and the post-COVID rebound.
- **Restructuring and IPO.** Under the new structure the OpenAI Foundation holds a $130 billion (26%) stake and Microsoft 27%, while Elon Musk's lawsuit heads to trial in spring 2026. Wissner-Gross welcomes the foundation's $25 billion disease pledge and argues an IPO would align retail investors with frontier labs. One Mate contrasts Sam Altman's $1 trillion-a-year spending plan with about $13 billion in current revenue.
- **Benchmarks and introspection.** Wissner-Gross explains a new AGI benchmark built on the CHC theory of human intelligence, which scores GPT-5 at 57%. He warns that the models were tested without scaffolding. He also describes Anthropic research where Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 detected injected "thoughts" about 20% of the time.
- **The innermost loop.** Examples include Samsung's planned 500,000-GPU chip factory, Foxconn's humanoid robots in Houston and SpaceX's orbital data centres. Wissner-Gross doubts Extropic's claimed 10,000-fold energy saving will survive against digital chips.
- **China and robots.** The hosts cite figures putting 70% of AI patents in China. In a clip, Eric Schmidt says the US leads on capital and chips. 1X's Neo home humanoid goes on early access at $20,000, which Blundin suspects is subsidised to gather data.

## Quotes
