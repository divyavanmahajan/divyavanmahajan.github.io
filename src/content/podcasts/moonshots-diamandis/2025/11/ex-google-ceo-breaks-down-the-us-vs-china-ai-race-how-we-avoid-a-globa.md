## Thesis

In a conversation with Dave Blundin, former Google CEO Eric Schmidt says the US looks likely to win the race to AGI because of hardware export limits on China. He adds that China is playing a different game: wide adoption, cheap robots and abundant power. He sees electricity, open-source proliferation and AI misuse as the main risks. His advice to founders is to build systems that learn and platforms that scale.

## Key points

- **Two different races.** Schmidt describes a "San Francisco consensus" that agents lead to recursive self-improvement and then superintelligence. He says China is focused on putting AI into every product. He no longer fears a superintelligence race that forces one side to pre-empt the other, at least for a few years.
- **Robots and power.** Schmidt expects China to dominate cheap humanoid robots, as it has with EVs. He cites the $6,000 Unitree R1, which he has ordered, and 172 gigawatts of Chinese solar added last year. His congressional testimony put US data center demand at 92 gigawatts by 2030. Some training may end up in Gulf states, he says.
- **Three near-term threats.** Schmidt names misinformation, cyber and biology. He expects one of them to cause a mini-crisis that pushes governments to act, much as Henry Kissinger used the nuclear era to negotiate treaties. Biology worries him most.
- **Proliferation.** Schmidt says he helped set the 10^26 FLOP reporting threshold, admitting that the number was made up. He worries more about open-source groups than big labs, and that poorer countries will standardise on free Chinese models. Blundin notes that distillation costs about 1% of original training.
- **Founder advice.** Schmidt says AI has cut the barrier to starting a company to near zero, so founders now compete with everyone. He advises building everything around learning, hiring networks of strong people, and building platforms with network lock-in.
- **This moment.** Schmidt compares non-human intelligence to electricity or fire. He says the next ten years will shape the next hundred.
