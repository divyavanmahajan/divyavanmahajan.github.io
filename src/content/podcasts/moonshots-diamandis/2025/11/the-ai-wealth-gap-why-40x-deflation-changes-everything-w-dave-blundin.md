## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alex Wissner-Gross treat the falling cost of intelligence as the central force of the week's news. Wissner-Gross calls it the "innermost loop" that will pull down prices everywhere. Diamandis sets this against survey data showing that most people worry first about cost of living and jobs. He argues that the next two to seven years, before abundance arrives, are the real risk.

## Key points

- **Anthropic's enterprise lead.** The hosts discuss reports that Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in enterprise API share, with projections of $70 billion revenue in 2028 at high margins. Wissner-Gross asks whether code generation is the critical path to recursive self-improvement. Blundin and Ismail say enterprises trust Claude with sensitive data.
- **Cheap open models.** Blundin calls Moonshot AI's Kimi models, reportedly trained for about $4.6 million, the biggest story of the month. Diamandis notes that cheap training weakens the advantage of efficient US capital markets. Wissner-Gross cites Sam Altman's figure of 40x yearly deflation in the cost of intelligence.
- **The wealth gap.** Diamandis presents an FII survey of 60,000+ people in 32 countries, which ranks cost of living, unemployment and inequality as top concerns. One Mate describes an Iranian family spending a third of its income on a phone and data plan. Ismail argues for UBI-style structures, Diamandis for universal basic services, and Wissner-Gross for benchmarks for cost of living.
- **Europe and regulation.** After meetings in Italy and Spain, Diamandis cites figures on AI audits costing about EUR 260,000 and taking 8-15 months. He discusses Brussels loosening GDPR to help AI.
- **Power and nuclear.** Wissner-Gross highlights the $80 billion Westinghouse/Brookfield/Cameco deal for AP-1000 reactors. Blundin explains the loan-guarantee deal structure and projects $1.2 trillion a year in data center spending by 2030.
- **Science and backlash.** The hosts cover Edison's Cosmos AI scientist, Chan Zuckerberg's shift toward AI biology, Boston unions opposing Waymo, and startups pursuing embryo editing. Wissner-Gross predicts most grand challenges in science will fall to AI within three years.
