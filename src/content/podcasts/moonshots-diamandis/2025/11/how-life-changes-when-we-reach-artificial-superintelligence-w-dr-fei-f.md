## Thesis

On stage at FII in Saudi Arabia, Peter Diamandis puts his abundance vision to Fei-Fei Li and Eric Schmidt. Both push back on parts of it. Li doubts that AI can yet produce Newton- or Einstein-level discovery and calls robotics hype premature. Schmidt warns that AI's wealth may concentrate in early adopters. They agree that humans working with AI, not AI replacing humans, is the likely future.

## Key points

- **Defining superintelligence.** Schmidt defines it as intelligence equal to the sum of all humans. He says a "San Francisco consensus" expects it within three to four years, but he thinks it will take longer. Li notes that AI already beats humans at translation, calculation and breadth of knowledge.
- **The creativity gap.** Li argues that an AI given all celestial data could not derive Newton's laws. Schmidt agrees that 1902-era knowledge would not yield relativity. He says superintelligence probably needs another algorithmic breakthrough to handle "non-stationarity of objectives", because brute-force reinforcement learning costs too much.
- **Abundance versus concentration.** Diamandis cites a projection of $15 trillion in AI value by 2030. Schmidt says network effects may focus the gains on a few countries, firms and early adopters, and he names Africa as his main concern. Li says productivity does not automatically become shared prosperity, which depends on policy.
- **Advice to nations.** Schmidt says the US leads thanks to capital markets and TSMC chips. He recommends partnerships such as Saudi Arabia's with US hyperscalers, or France's with Abu Dhabi. Li says not every country can afford its own data centers, but all should invest in human capital and AI.
- **Math first, reality later.** Schmidt expects the biggest near-term gains in math and software, because results can be verified. Li disagrees with Diamandis's five-year "solve everything" timeline, and the two make a bet.
- **World models.** Li says World Labs has built the first large world model. She expects work, education and medicine to become a hybrid of virtual and physical worlds.

## Quotes

> "Give that data to any AI algorithm. It will not be able to deduce Newtonian law of motion." — Fei-Fei Li

> "So I think that to get to real super intelligence, we probably need another algorithmic breakthrough." — Eric Schmidt
