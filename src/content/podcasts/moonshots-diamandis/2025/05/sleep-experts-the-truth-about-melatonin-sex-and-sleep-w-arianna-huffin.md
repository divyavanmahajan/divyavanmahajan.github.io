## Thesis

Recorded as an audience Q&A at the 2025 Abundance Summit, this session has sleep scientist Matt Walker and Thrive Global founder Arianna Huffington answering questions put by Peter Diamandis and attendees. Walker's line is that there are few "free lunches" in sleep biology: pills and supplements help at the margins, while timing, light and routine do most of the work. Huffington argues that behaviour change, cutting out news and notifications, and measures like joy matter as much as the numbers on a tracker.

## Key points

- **Behaviour over products.** Huffington describes Thrive Global's foundation and nonprofit work around two slogans: health is what happens between doctor visits, and behaviour change is a miracle drug.
- **A product built on consequences.** Walker says he has just raised a funding round and is about to start clinical trials on compounds meant to protect people from the effects of short sleep, rather than to make them sleep longer.
- **Supplements are a "wild west".** Walker says most people take far more melatonin than needed. He cites a study of 20 vendors where actual content ranged from 83% less to 464% more than the label, and argues melatonin shifts the timing of sleep rather than producing it.
- **Jet lag as a protocol.** Walker's travel routine: set clocks to the destination on boarding, avoid alcohol and caffeine in flight, nap briefly and not after 1 p.m., and get morning daylight and exercise on arrival.
- **Information diet.** Asked about stress from geopolitics and markets, Huffington recommends no doom scrolling at night and only allowing notifications from people you love; another panellist says they stopped reading the news altogether.
- **Trackers are proxies.** When an attendee reports good wearable data on short sleep, Walker says stress scores do not reach the cell and suggests comparing blood panels from rested and travel periods. Huffington adds that joy, empathy and reactivity are measures too.
