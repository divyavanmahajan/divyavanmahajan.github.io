## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail treat a busy week of AI news as a fight over who controls the consumer front end. As they see it, OpenAI wants its own devices, Google is willing to cannibalise search to stay ahead, and Anthropic keeps leapfrogging both. Blundin argues that demand for compute will rise faster than efficiency gains can offset it.

## Key points

- **OpenAI buys Jony Ive's startup.** Blundin calls the $6.5 billion purchase of a no-revenue company a brilliant, aggressive move. He argues that every big AI player needs a direct-to-consumer interface. He compares it to Google paying $3 billion for Character.AI to bring back Noam Shazeer.
- **Benchmark leapfrogging.** Gemini 2.5 Pro topped LMArena, and Claude 4 retook the coding lead within about 24 hours. Blundin says autonomous long-form coding only becomes reliable once SWE-bench scores pass roughly 80%.
- **Efficiency and Jevons paradox.** On a chart projecting 30-240x algorithmic gains, Blundin argues that quantisation, chain-of-reasoning and model subsetting multiply together into more like 1,000-10,000x. He adds that cheaper AI will push up demand for chips and data centres, not lower it.
- **Google I/O.** The hosts go through agent mode, personalised smart replies, Google Beam, live translation in Meet, AI Mode in search, Android XR glasses and the $250-a-month Ultra tier. Blundin argues that Google is moving low-revenue queries such as sports scores and shopping into AI Mode first.
- **Veo 3 and Hollywood.** Blundin says on-demand generated media will flip the industry, and Diamandis asks how the studios survive two or three years. Ismail is sceptical that consumers will make their own entertainment. He expects the biggest gains to go to professional filmmakers.
- **Compute as a national issue.** Blundin warns that about 180 of 200 countries have no compute plan, which would leave their citizens without agent features like Project Astra. Ismail adds that most governments make policy defensively.
