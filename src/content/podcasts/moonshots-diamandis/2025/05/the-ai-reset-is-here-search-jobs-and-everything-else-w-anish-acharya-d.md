## Thesis

Anish Acharya, a general partner leading consumer investing at Andreessen Horowitz, argues that AI is "the most human technology" ever built: after 40 years of tools that extended the intellect, it can now address emotion, companionship and creativity. He sees consumer AI as a product cycle at least as big as the internet and a real threat to Google's search business. Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail add a week of AI news (Google I/O, GPT-5 rumours, Grok 3.5, chip shortages) and warn that job disruption is coming faster than companies expect.

## Key points

- **Moats.** Acharya argues that moats built on adaptive systems, such as networks and culture, hold up, while moats built on static systems like integrations and systems of record are at risk. New markets spread apart rather than converge; his example is Midjourney, Krea and Ideogram each taking a different aesthetic.
- **Consumer pricing.** Acharya compares Spotify's top $20 plan with ChatGPT at $200 and Google's new $250 tier, and predicts software will join food and rent as one of consumers' three biggest spending categories.
- **Search under threat.** Acharya says Google's AI mode felt watered down because 20 years of ads commitments hold it back. Blundin links the stock dip during I/O to the risk of cannibalising the core business.
- **Sovereign AI and chips.** Blundin says GPUs are physically scarce, with 20 million made this year, and that countries such as India need their own fabs. Acharya expects a "Jones Act for AI" for sensitive uses, since models carry values.
- **Founders and agents.** Acharya sees technical founding teams winning again and calls OpenAI's Operator underrated, describing an agent that refinances his loans while he goes about his day.
- **Jobs.** Reacting to a speculative 2025-2040 timeline that puts the end of white-collar work at 2029-2031, one of the Mates (apparently Blundin) says CEOs are "way under planning" and should get every white-collar employee using AI now.
