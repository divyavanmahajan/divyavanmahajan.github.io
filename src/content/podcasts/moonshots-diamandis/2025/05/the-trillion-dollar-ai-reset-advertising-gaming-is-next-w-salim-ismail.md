## Thesis

In this "WTF Just Happened in Tech" roundtable, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Dave Blundin argue that AI is moving faster than schools, governments and incumbent business models can adjust. Their main example is advertising: Diamandis predicts that personal agents will buy things for users, and Blundin argues that this squeezes Google's search-ad model. They also discuss AI-written laws, AI-generated code, synthetic data, gaming, space and Bitcoin treasuries.

## Key points

- **Education and law.** The hosts welcome a US executive order that brings AI into classrooms. Ismail cites estimates that people learn 2x to 4x faster with AI. On the UAE's plan to draft laws with AI (a claimed 50% cost cut and 70% faster lawmaking), Ismail calls the targets conservative. Blundin argues that only AI can write regulation fast enough to keep up with AI.
- **OpenAI stays nonprofit-controlled.** Blundin reads OpenAI's decision to drop its for-profit conversion as a win for Elon Musk. Ismail thinks it mainly limits legal exposure and changes little.
- **AI-written code.** On Satya Nadella's forecast that 95% of code will be AI-generated by 2030, Blundin says his roughly 24 Link Studios startups are AI-native. He says Blitzy signed $8 million in new deals in one week. He adds that Mark Zuckerberg has described AI research as compute-bound, not idea-bound.
- **Scaling and energy.** The hosts accept David Sacks' estimate of a millionfold gain in four years, from 3-4x yearly improvements in algorithms, chips and data centres. Blundin expects inference chips and algorithms to beat it. On Eric Schmidt's estimate of 29 GW more power by 2027, Ismail argues that solar economics, not policy, will drive new supply.
- **Agents replace ads.** After Mastercard's Agent Pay launch, Blundin cites Richard Socher's tests suggesting that revenue per AI session is one-tenth to one-hundredth of a Google search.
- **Other stories.** The hosts also cover NVIDIA's purchase of Gretel for synthetic data, New Limit's $130 million round, Roblox's 97.8 million daily users, Project Kuiper, Starship launch limits, and MicroStrategy's roughly half a million bitcoin.
