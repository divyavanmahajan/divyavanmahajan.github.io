## Thesis

Vatom founder Eric Pulier joins Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Salim Ismail to argue that the White House crypto strategy, the GENIUS Act and tokenization amount to the biggest economic change of their lifetimes. Once AI agents can use programmable money, Pulier expects the economy to accelerate. The hosts mostly agree. Blundin adds that the SEC still has to write the rules well. The episode also covers chip geopolitics, space, robots and fusion.

## Key points

- **Chips and Intel.** Blundin calls chip fabs the real bottleneck in the US-China AI race. He describes the 15% toll on Nvidia and AMD exports to China as a good deal but a slippery precedent. He cites TSMC's 66% share of advanced chips and Intel's paused Ohio fabs.
- **Stablecoins and tokenization.** Pulier says stablecoins allow instant settlement in place of wires and card float. He also argues that tokenized assets can be verified and used as collateral in seconds. Ismail says requiring dollar backing (T-bills) buys the US dollar a few more years.
- **Programmable loyalty.** Pulier predicts that loyalty points will be pegged to stablecoins and earn yield. He also expects tokenized shares to let companies reward the shareholders they can now identify. Ismail sees credit unions issuing local community tokens.
- **Risks and clarity.** Blundin, who chairs Vesmark, recalls the $60 trillion of synthetic instruments before 2008 and warns the same could happen if SEC guidance is weak. He still praises clear rules, since unclear ones mainly benefit bad actors.
- **AI deregulation.** Diamandis reports that a DOGE AI tool is reviewing 200,000 federal regulations, with the aim of cutting 50% by 2026. Ismail proposes "minimum viable regulatory."
- **Fusion and power.** The hosts discuss Helion's 50 MW Orion plant (contracted to Microsoft for 2028), Commonwealth Fusion's 200 MW offtake with Google, and China's extra $2.1 billion. Blundin calls these fusion bets low-risk because the grid and generator work pays off either way.
