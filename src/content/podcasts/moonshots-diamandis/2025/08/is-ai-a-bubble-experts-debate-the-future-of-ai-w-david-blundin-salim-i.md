## Thesis

In this news roundup Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail, Dave Blundin and Alexander Wissner-Gross argue that AI is not a bubble. Blundin says bad investments will fail, but the underlying shift is real and compute demand is effectively unlimited. Wissner-Gross argues that "overhangs" in data efficiency, small models and AI-assisted maths will keep capability rising even if raw compute scaling slows.

## Key points

- **Overhangs everywhere.** Wissner-Gross describes a data-efficient distillation paper in which a 32-billion-parameter model set a new frontier on AIME. Blundin reads the chart as roughly equal capability from 1% of the training data, which he says opens the door to startups building specialised foundation models.
- **Bubble or not.** Discussing OpenAI's reported $1 billion a month in revenue and Sam Altman's bubble remarks, Blundin calls Altman's comments a deliberate "downplay" and says only the charlatans will fail. Ismail sees them as managing market cap while employees sell shares on the secondary market.
- **Land grab and compute.** The hosts treat OpenAI's push into India, a possible ChatGPT Plus deal for the UK, and its Texas Stargate and Norway data centres as a race for distribution and power. Wissner-Gross calls a country-wide deal a form of "universal basic compute".
- **Talent wars and zombie startups.** Blundin describes Meta and Microsoft fighting over researchers, and warns that licence-and-hire deals (character.ai, Windsurf) leave behind shell companies and create uncertainty for venture investors.
- **Why pilots fail.** On the MIT study claiming 95% of generative-AI pilots show no financial return, Blundin tells listeners not to bother with such reports. Ismail's fix is an AI-native "edge organisation" reporting to the CEO, which gradually takes over from the core business.
- **Small models and robots.** Google's Gemma 3 270M and frontier-level models running on a single RTX 5090 lead Wissner-Gross to predict a small "nano kernel" model with knowledge stored externally, and robots running AI locally at low latency. The episode closes on Figure's laundry-folding robot and China's humanoid robot games.
