## Thesis

In an audience Q&A at a Padres y Hijos event in El Salvador, Peter Diamandis argues that the hardest problem AI creates is purpose. He believes people will need to set much bigger goals once machines take over routine work. He is optimistic about the long run: he thinks more intelligent systems will be peace-loving and that AI-driven education, healthcare and longevity therapies will become cheap. His main worry is human misuse of AI over the next five to seven years.

## Key points

- **Purpose over jobs.** Diamandis says most people do jobs they never dreamed of, and that work is a recent invention. In his view, the goal is to separate what people love doing from what they must do to survive. He says there is no on/off or velocity switch for this change.
- **Data quality and values.** Asked about "garbage in, garbage out," he cites Mo Gawdat's book *Scary Smart*: training AI is like raising a child, and its values depend on what it is taught. He says his concern is "human stupidity," not AI.
- **Building a country today.** He points to Abu Dhabi's announced move to AI-driven governance and says he would start a nation on that basis. He would also position El Salvador as a longevity and health destination through regulatory arbitrage.
- **Longevity economics.** Diamandis says Fountain Life costs about $19,500 a year, with a $6,500 version. He expects age-reversal to arrive as gene therapy and argues that mRNA vaccines show such therapies can be made cheaply at scale. He cites a study valuing one extra productive year of life at $38 trillion.
- **Education.** He says school systems are "massively broken." He wants children to find their passion and learn to ask good questions, and thinks schools should use AI to raise the level of the work rather than ban it.
- **Biggest obstacle.** He names human stubbornness, pride and emotion. He suggests using language models to see opposing views or to ask how a company is most likely to be disrupted.
