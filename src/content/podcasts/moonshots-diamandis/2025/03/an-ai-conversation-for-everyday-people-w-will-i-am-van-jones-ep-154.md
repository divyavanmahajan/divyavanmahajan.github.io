## Thesis

will.i.am and Van Jones explain their Make Wakanda Real campaign, which aims to bring AI to Black and other underrepresented communities. They argue that because almost nobody understands AI yet, everyone starts out roughly equal. Jones says the remaining gap is "wetware," meaning mindset, not hardware or software. Peter Diamandis frames AI as a tool for meritocracy, though he also raises concerns about job losses and a fracturing society.

## Key points

- **Make Wakanda Real.** Jones describes it as hope that people can act on today, using the fictional, technologically advanced African nation from Black Panther as a metaphor. It began as an event at will.i.am's company FYI, where his AI persona held a live debate with him.
- **Africa and compute.** will.i.am says most of the world's young people live in Africa, yet the continent has no GPUs. He says FYI is working with partner Cassava on a GPU cluster to be announced on March 17. Diamandis adds that culturally specific models are needed because today's LLMs reflect a Western canon.
- **Personal AI.** will.i.am argues that people should own their AI and data, comparing shared AI to buying a house with communal toilets. He predicts that "level five companies" with no humans in them will be a common term by around 2027.
- **Jobs and problems as assets.** will.i.am predicts that one lawyer or financier with AI will replace whole firms. He argues that communities that have lived with hard problems are best placed to build tomorrow's industries, citing Uber as an example. Jones notes that data centres also create skilled jobs, and he warns about energy costs if demand rises 23-34%.
- **Wetware problem.** Jones argues that preparing to pivot now matters more than planning. Diamandis notes that most of the world's 8 billion people carry smartphones and so have access to frontier intelligence.
- **News and tools.** Jones says his positive CNN segments got the network's worst ratings. He presents his Rapport app, a 15-second emoji check-in that helps managers coach remote teams.
