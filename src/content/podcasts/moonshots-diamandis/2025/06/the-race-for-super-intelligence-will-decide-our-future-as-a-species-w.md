## Thesis

Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Dave Blundin read the week's AI news as a race toward digital superintelligence. Blundin argues the race is winner-take-all because AI will soon improve itself, and that this explains record valuations and pay packages. The hosts also argue that the US could fall behind China on energy, the input that will limit AI, because it does not plan or invest over the long term.

## Key points

- **Timelines and control.** The hosts discuss Elon Musk's claim that digital superintelligence could arrive this year or next, and a Jeff Clune clip suggesting the first ASI may be the last. Ismail expects governments to nationalize such a system. Blundin expects the US to follow the defense-contractor model instead.
- **Meta's talent war.** Diamandis cites Safe Superintelligence's $32 billion valuation, Sam Altman's claim of $100 million sign-on bonuses, and Meta's $14.8 billion purchase of a 49% non-voting stake in Scale AI. Blundin explains that this structure avoids Hart-Scott-Rodino review and financial consolidation.
- **Capital-efficient startups.** Diamandis highlights Cursor reaching $500 million ARR in under three years. Blundin cites the "Fred Wilson rule" of backing teams of three or more technical best friends, and says a pre-existing friendship protects startups from talent raids.
- **AI in government.** The hosts welcome the planned AI.gov launch and the Army's appointment of executives from Palantir, Meta and OpenAI as lieutenant colonels. Ismail describes a wind-turbine approval process that went from two years to 30 seconds.
- **Robotaxis.** Tesla launched its robotaxi in Austin at a $4.20 flat fee. Blundin predicts a 70/30 market split in Tesla's favour over Waymo, while Ismail is 50/50 because he prefers lidar. The hosts compare the torching of Waymo vehicles to the Luddite revolt.
- **Energy and crypto.** Diamandis contrasts China's nuclear build-out with US licensing times of 10 to 12 years. Blundin says China produced about 700 gigawatts of solar panels in 2024 and calls cheap storage a trillion-dollar opportunity. He also argues that Circle's IPO enables micropayments between AI agents.
