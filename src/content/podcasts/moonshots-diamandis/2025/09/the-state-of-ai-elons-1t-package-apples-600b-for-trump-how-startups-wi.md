## Thesis

Peter Diamandis frames the episode as David versus Goliath: with trillion-dollar pay packages and capex commitments, how can a startup compete? Blitzy co-founders Brian Elliott and Sid Pardeshi argue that a startup should build on top of the frontier labs, so each new model makes its product better, and go deep on enterprise problems such as legacy code. Alexander Wissner-Gross sees this as the start of a "solve everything" era. Salim Ismail is absent.

## Key points

- **Trillion-dollar news.** The hosts discuss Tesla's proposed pay package for Elon Musk, tied to an $8 trillion market cap, and the $600 billion US investment pledges from Tim Cook and Mark Zuckerberg at a dinner with Trump. Wissner-Gross argues that trillions in AI capex will have to be justified by automating labour and by new discoveries.
- **Young founders.** Dave Blundin says Link Ventures invested in Mercor about two years ago at roughly $30 million. It now has an offer at a $10 billion valuation and a half-billion-dollar revenue run rate.
- **What Blitzy does.** Elliott describes an autonomous development platform that ingests codebases of over 100 million lines, runs jobs for hours or weeks, and handles conversions such as COBOL or PL/1 to Java. He says it does about 80% of the work and passes the rest to human developers, giving about 5x faster delivery.
- **Benchmark claim.** Pardeshi says Blitzy scored 86.8% on SWE-bench Verified, against a published leaderboard top of 75.2%, without custom scaffolding. Blundin argues this effectively saturates the benchmark and says Blitzy is working with MIT on a new one.
- **Orchestration over models.** Elliott says Blitzy plays Gemini, Anthropic and OpenAI models against each other through "extended inference time validation". He puts its cost per line of code at about 100x other tools, but still far below human developers.
- **How to compete.** Pardeshi says founders should understand an enterprise problem deeply and stay nimble while big companies struggle with bureaucracy. Blundin advises being a good partner, and a paying customer, of the labs.
