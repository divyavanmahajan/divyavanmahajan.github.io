## Thesis

With Salim Ismail away in India, Peter Diamandis, Dave Blundin and Alex Wissner-Gross argue that frontier AI has become a brute-force hardware race. Wissner-Gross calls it Richard Sutton's "bitter lesson" applied to hardware, and Blundin says the race is winner-take-all. The hosts also argue that cheap, general models will undercut single-purpose software companies unless those companies rebuild around AI.

## Key points

- **Colossus 2 and capital.** Diamandis describes xAI's 1-gigawatt Memphis data center as built for 500,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs. Blundin estimates that a million Blackwells at $30,000 each would mean about $30 billion in chips alone. Wissner-Gross notes that Colossus 1 generates its own power on site, and Blundin says large generators are sold out.
- **Grok Code Fast 1.** Diamandis puts its price at $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.50 per million output tokens, against $1.25 input for GPT-5. Blundin calls this a fight for market share rather than a race to the bottom. Wissner-Gross notes it is only available through coding tools such as Cursor and Windsurf.
- **Talent and purpose.** Diamandis contrasts Musk recruiting 14 Meta engineers with mission and equity against researchers leaving Meta's superintelligence lab despite nine-figure offers.
- **SaaS under pressure.** The hosts point to Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) as a threat to Photoshop, and to Google's live translation, which coincided with a roughly 10% drop in Duolingo's stock.
- **Chips, markets and policy.** Wissner-Gross sees the beginnings of non-Nvidia chip architectures in China, such as Cambricon and Huawei. Diamandis argues that export limits push rivals to build their own products. On Nasdaq valuations, Wissner-Gross argues that a soaring ratio to the money supply is what the eve of superintelligence would look like. The hosts disagree about whether people will ever vote AI into power.
- **Robots.** The hosts discuss Nvidia's Jetson Thor, Tesla moving Optimus training to video only, Apple pushing its suppliers to automate, and the claim that one Waymo outperforms 99% of Uber drivers on daily trips.
