## Thesis

SkyBridge Capital founder Anthony Scaramucci joins Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Dave Blundin to ask whether the US administration is still tech-forward after Elon Musk's departure. Scaramucci says it is, crediting David Sacks and Sriram Krishnan, but argues that money in politics, culture-war energy policy and voter complacency hold back AI-driven productivity. He also says Bitcoin will force banks to adapt. The views are his own and he states them openly.

## Key points

- **Tech policy after Musk.** Scaramucci argues Musk was hurt by being too visible around the president, while Sacks works quietly. He credits Sacks with the Bitcoin reserve executive order. Diamandis cites Sacks's post that more than a thousand state bills aim to restrict AI.
- **Solar and energy.** Scaramucci calls resistance to solar a culture-war issue. Ismail says radical energy expansion is the root problem, and Diamandis raises projections that China will add US-scale power generation every year by 2030.
- **China.** Scaramucci calls himself a "China rationalist" who wants deeper economic interdependence and stronger US soft power. Blundin argues that chip export controls already locked in an AI arms race that will peak around the 2028 election cycle, together with job displacement and a data-centre buildout.
- **Third parties and reform.** Scaramucci says a centrist party like the one Musk floated would need about $50 billion. He also calls for constitutional reform, an end to gerrymandering and the reversal of Citizens United. Blundin says making voting easier would matter more.
- **AI for everyone.** Scaramucci proposes a package of healthcare, education and an AI tutor for every child, and mentions the UAE making ChatGPT free for citizens. Ismail cites an ExO study in which the ten most adaptable Fortune 100 firms delivered 40 times the shareholder returns of the bottom ten.
- **Bitcoin.** Scaramucci says about 70% of his net worth is in Bitcoin. He predicts $200,000 by year-end, $500,000 in five years and $1 million in ten, and says banks must change the way telecom companies did.
