## Thesis

In this news roundtable, Peter Diamandis, Salim Ismail and Dave Blundin argue that AI has started a new wave of wealth creation. Small, young teams are reaching billion-dollar valuations and meaningful revenue in a fraction of the time it used to take. Ismail adds that large companies can only take part if they run disruptive projects at the edge of the business or buy them.

## Key points

- **Speed to scale.** Blundin points to 36 new unicorns in the first half of 2025. Diamandis reads figures showing the median time to $1M of annual revenue fell from 16 months to 5, and to $5M from 41 months to 13. Blundin says these teams stay at 30-50 people, where he once needed 200 to reach a $1B valuation.
- **Getting access.** Blundin says Link Ventures manages about a billion dollars of seed money. He says Link was the first money into Mercor at roughly a $30M valuation, and that Mercor later closed a $2B round, with rumours of an $8-10B offer. He argues the software layer offers better returns than capital-intensive chips and power.
- **Innovator's dilemma.** On reports that Apple is weighing Anthropic or OpenAI to power Siri, Ismail argues that big companies are built for efficiency and predictability. He says disruption only works at the edge, and points to Walmart needing four attempts at e-commerce. Blundin notes that Nvidia, at $3.92 trillion, has passed Apple.
- **Compute and power.** Diamandis says xAI has about 340,000 GPUs, is aiming for a million by year-end, and bought an overseas gas power plant. One of the Mates says 100 gigawatts will be needed by 2029.
- **Talent war.** The hosts discuss Meta's nine-figure pay offers, a reported $1B offer that was turned down, and the new Superintelligence Labs. Blundin passes on hearsay that a bug ruined the GPT-4.5 training run.
- **Jobs and safety.** Ismail disagrees with Vinod Khosla's prediction that AI will replace 80% of jobs by 2030, arguing that jobs are bundles of tasks. Responding to Roman Yampolskiy, Blundin argues that AI self-improvement can be logged and limited to algorithmic changes.
