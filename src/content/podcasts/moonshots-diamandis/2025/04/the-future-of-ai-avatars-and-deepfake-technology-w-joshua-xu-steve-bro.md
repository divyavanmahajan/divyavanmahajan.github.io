## Thesis

At the 2025 Abundance Summit, HeyGen co-founder and CEO Joshua Xu argues that AI avatars will "replace the camera" for business video. He expects video calls to become the most natural way people interact with AI agents. Steve Brown, Abundance360's chief AI officer, adds that an avatar only becomes a useful agent once it is trained on its owner's knowledge.

## Key points

- **Origin.** Xu says HeyGen started in 2020, before the term "generative AI" existed, after he worked on AI camera features at Snap. Its original mission was replacing the camera for people who are camera-shy or can't afford video production.
- **How it works.** Xu describes a human-centric video model trained on about two minutes of footage that learns a person's facial movements, gestures and body language. An LLM and knowledge base can sit behind it. The demo claims support for more than 170 languages, and an audience member's avatar took about four minutes to record.
- **Agents in parallel.** Xu predicts that every business will make about 100x more video. He also expects people to "agentize" themselves, running many meetings in parallel and across languages. Diamandis imagines video calls with a team of 20 AI marketing agents.
- **Consent and moderation.** Xu says every HeyGen avatar requires first-party video consent, and that hate speech, fraud and political campaigns are blocked by AI plus human moderators. Diamandis advises families to agree on a code word to defend against deepfake scam calls.
- **Agents that know you.** Brown describes agents that interview users about their values and life story to build a legacy clone. He then demos "Hippocrates," a medical agent connected to his own health record. He says it identified the pattern behind a diagnosis his specialists had missed. He argues that many agents with different viewpoints act as different search strategies across the data.
