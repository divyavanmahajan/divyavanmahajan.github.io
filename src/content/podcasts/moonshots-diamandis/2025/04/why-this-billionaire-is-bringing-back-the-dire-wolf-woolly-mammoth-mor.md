## Thesis

Ben Lamm, co-founder and CEO of Colossal Biosciences, argues that the dire wolf pups announced in April 2025 show de-extinction is now an engineering and funding problem rather than a science-fiction one. He describes Colossal as a full technology stack built on a 50-year horizon. Peter Diamandis, a seed investor and advisor to Colossal, and Salim Ismail present it as a moonshot case study: a CEO with no biology background, a big purpose and fast-improving tools.

## Key points

- **A messy launch week.** Lamm says The New Yorker broke the embargo a day early. After that, he says, the coverage turned into a debate about what counts as a species and largely ignored the science, including four cloned red wolves made with a less invasive cloning technique.
- **The stack.** Lamm splits the work into ancient DNA, the closest living relative and editing tools. He says dire wolves are 99.5% the same as grey wolves and that the team made 20 edits using multiplex editing. Ismail compares this to rebuilding the whole computing stack.
- **Spinouts.** Colossal has spun out Form Bio and Breaking, a plastic-degrading microbe company. Lamm says a third spinout is already valued at over $100 million at seed. He predicts a 17-person artificial-womb team will produce the first fully ex utero mammal birth by the end of 2026.
- **Business model.** Lamm puts Colossal's valuation at $10.2 billion, four years after its 2021 founding. He describes three revenue legs: technology spinouts, biodiversity and "nature" credits that turn rewilded animals into annuities, and paid government work. Colossal open-sources its conservation tools, which Lamm compares to the Red Hat model.
- **Roadmap and limits.** Lamm says the mammoth is on target for the end of 2028 and calls it a three-horse race with the thylacine and dodo. The Steller's sea cow will need artificial wombs. Colossal will not work on humans or non-human primates. Lamm says there is no dinosaur DNA.
- **Funding as the bottleneck.** Lamm estimates that treating DNA as editable software is five years away with focused funding and ten without it. He calls AI the biggest accelerant and quantum computing still too early.
