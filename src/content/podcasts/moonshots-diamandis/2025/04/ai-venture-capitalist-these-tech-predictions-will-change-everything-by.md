## Thesis

Speaking on stage at the 2025 Abundance Summit with Peter Diamandis, Vinod Khosla argues that technology turns scarcity into abundance. He sees three shifts: AI makes expertise almost free, robots multiply the supply of labour, and new energy and transit systems remove physical limits. He stresses that the timing is uncertain, but he expects much of it to be visible by 2030.

## Key points

- **Robots bigger than cars.** Khosla repeats his forecast of a billion bipedal robots by 2040, which he calls an underestimate. He predicts the robot business will be larger than the auto industry within 20 years, and says only Elon Musk among carmakers has noticed.
- **Free expertise.** He tells professionals to plan on "five AI interns" each within five years. He asks healthcare boards how they would design a system if all expertise were free, and raises the policy question of how to pay people once a farm worker has the same AI-level expertise as an oncologist.
- **Advertising and deep research.** Khosla expects advertising to move back towards information as agents filter purchases. He describes OpenAI's deep research producing an 18-page report that matched his PhD chief of staff's work. Khosla Ventures committed to OpenAI in 2018, which he says was twice the size of any first investment he had made before.
- **Programming.** Khosla says the future of programming is "both" no programmers and everyone a programmer. He cites portfolio companies Cognition (an AI software intern) and Replit (coding in plain English), and argues that computers will learn humans instead of the other way round.
- **Energy.** Khosla says Commonwealth Fusion expects net energy output by early 2027. He forecasts that fusion boilers will repower existing coal and gas plants, and that super-hot geothermal, drilled to 400-450 degrees, could deliver ten times the power per well and undercut natural gas before 2030.
- **Transit and minerals.** He proposes driverless personal transit running in bicycle-lane-width corridors, which he says could give a street 10x its capacity. He also argues that mineral scarcity disappears if exploration looks a kilometre underground.
