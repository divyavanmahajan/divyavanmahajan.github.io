## Thesis

Recorded live at the Lake Nona Impact Forum, Peter Diamandis talks with Magic Johnson about his move from the NBA into business. Johnson argues that investing in inner-city America is both profitable and a way to create jobs. He says his early wins were meant to show Fortune 500 companies and institutional investors that minority communities are viable markets. He credits his parents and his sports career for his approach.

## Key points

- **Origin of the business dream.** Johnson says meeting two Black businessmen in Lansing, Michigan, as a teenager changed his goal from only reaching the NBA to becoming a businessman. Cleaning their office building, he would sit in the CEO's chair and imagine running the company.
- **Starbucks deal.** Johnson says his first Magic Johnson theater ranked among the top 10 highest-grossing in the US. He used that result to pitch Howard Schultz, then built 125 Starbucks stores in 40 markets and later sold them all back. He also missed a Nike stock offer in 1979, which he says analysts value at about $1.5 billion today.
- **Buy, improve, resell.** Johnson describes buying about 40 Burger King outlets cheaply from an owner who had to exit, improving their results and reselling them. He did the same with 24 Hour Fitness clubs. He also mentions real estate funds of $300 million, $600 million and $1 billion.
- **Sports ownership.** Johnson says critics thought the $2.2 billion Dodgers purchase was too much. He credits the team with two World Series titles and 12 years at the top of attendance. At the Washington Commanders, he says the first step was to rebuild employee morale.
- **Leadership habits.** Johnson says he gets up at 4 a.m., values staff but cuts underperformers, and does not want to be "the only smart guy in the room."
- **HIV and public health.** Johnson recalls his HIV diagnosis and his wife Cookie's support. He says he has worked for 20 years with Elizabeth Taylor raising money for HIV and AIDS. He also asks for continued public funding for drug access and frames it as a non-partisan issue.
