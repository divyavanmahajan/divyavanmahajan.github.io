## Thesis

Francis Pedraza, CEO of Invisible Technologies, argues that services, not software, are the large market Silicon Valley has failed to disrupt. He describes Invisible as "operations as a service" that combines AI tools with human workers, raises as little capital as possible, and is majority-owned by its team. Peter Diamandis presents the conversation as a master class in an alternative way to build a company.

## Key points

- **Three-part disruption.** Pedraza names Accenture as the "Blockbuster" Invisible wants to replace. His three levers are results-based pricing (per claim, per menu digitised for DoorDash, per conversation trained for OpenAI), a "digital assembly line" that integrates 300 third-party AI and automation tools with more than 3,000 people in over 100 countries, and a culture in which the team owns 70% of the company.
- **Venture game vs. sovereignty game.** Pedraza says Invisible reached profitability on about $6 million raised over six years after VCs turned down a services company. He reports growth from a $1M run rate in 2020 to $100M at the end of the last year, with targets of $200-300M. He expects returns to come from buybacks, secondaries and dividends rather than an IPO.
- **Partner pay.** Early partners took steep salary cuts, at one point about $1,000 a month, in exchange for equity. Pedraza holds open monthly reviews of the financial statements and rewards people who flag problems.
- **Hiring as a business.** He says head of hiring Mark Gray's team took more than 100,000 applications last year, hired about 1%, and is being spun out as Zero, an external hiring-as-a-service company.
- **Incubating against the innovator's dilemma.** Pedraza's incubator, Infinity, ring-fences new companies in areas such as strategy, marketing and finance, each with its own CEO and cap table. He recommends three books: Outsiders, Seven Powers and The Innovator's Dilemma.
- **Founder roles.** Pedraza, now a nomad and chairman, separates the founder, CEO, president and chairman roles and talks about abstracting himself out of each one.
