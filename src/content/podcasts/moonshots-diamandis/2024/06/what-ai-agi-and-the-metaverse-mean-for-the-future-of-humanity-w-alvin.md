## Thesis

Alvin Wang Graylin, Global VP of Corporate Development at HTC and co-author of "Our Next Reality", argues that the metaverse is the 3D version of the internet and that AI is what makes it useful. He believes the speed of AI-driven disruption leaves society little time to adapt, so countries should pool compute and data in one global effort rather than build rival sovereign AIs. Peter Diamandis largely agrees and steers the conversation toward abundance, purpose and education.

## Key points

- **Hardware path.** Graylin shows HTC's Vive XR Elite, which he says weighs about 200 grams, roughly a third of the Apple Vision Pro. He expects devices under 100 grams that look like glasses within one to two years, comparable to 50-gram Ray-Ban sunglasses.
- **AI assistant on your face.** Diamandis describes "history" or "health" overlay modes. Graylin says today's AI can already do this, and that 2-3 billion parameter models running on a phone would ease privacy concerns.
- **Against sovereign AI.** Graylin argues that 200 national AIs would divide compute, energy and data and treat each other as enemies. He suggests Jensen Huang favours sovereign AI because it sells more chips, and calls for a CERN-style global programme instead.
- **US-China tension.** Graylin cites a report that more Chinese-born than US-born researchers work among top AI researchers in the US, and warns that hardware export restrictions push the two countries toward separate tech stacks.
- **Recursive improvement and sandboxes.** Responding to Mustafa Suleyman's TED call to bar AI from programming AI, Graylin says self-improvement is the only route to ASI. He proposes testing powerful AIs inside simulated metaverse worlds first.
- **Purpose and young people.** Graylin compares AI and robots to Renaissance patrons who free people for their "vocation". He advises broader curricula, soft skills, sports, sleep, limits on gaming, and asking good questions.
