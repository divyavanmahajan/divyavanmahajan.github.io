## Thesis

Brett Adcock, founder and CEO of Figure, argues that humanoid robots became possible only recently, because AI models, electric actuators and batteries matured at the same time. He calls Figure an AI company that happens to build robots. In his view the hard part is not building thousands of units but making them do useful work every day without failing. Peter Diamandis presents humanoids as a route to an "age of abundance".

## Key points

- **Figure 02 upgrades.** Adcock lists three times the onboard CPU/GPU, a battery of about 2.3 kWh, internal wiring, an exoskeleton shell that carries loads, six cameras and fourth-generation hands. Figure has more than 100 engineers and builds about one robot a week.
- **Iterate on hardware.** Adcock says it takes at least three hardware versions to reach commercial reliability. He adds that the next generation has to cut cost by well over an order of magnitude. Over time he expects prices below $20,000 a unit.
- **Market size.** Adcock repeats his projection that 3-5 billion humanoids could join the workforce and that household demand could push the total higher. Diamandis cites a figure of 10 billion by 2040. Adcock says Figure raised $675 million at a $2.6 billion valuation from investors including OpenAI, Microsoft and Nvidia, and recently ran a trial of almost two weeks at BMW.
- **AI turned out easier than expected.** Adcock expected 90-95% of the stack to be hand-coded heuristics. Instead, neural networks for perception, planning and speech-to-speech reasoning have largely flipped that ratio. The supply chain was the surprise in the other direction: Figure designs roughly 70-80% of the robot itself, down to custom speakers and force sensors.
- **Competition.** Adcock calls Tesla's Optimus "a really real player". After a recent trip to China, he expects Chinese firms to be strong long-term competitors because of their work ethic and the sheer number of projects.
- **Safety and scope.** The plan is to keep robots isolated from people in work cells at first, with read-only low-level firmware and fleet-wide learning. Figure refuses all defence and policing work. Adcock expects pilots in homes within three years and a production line in California next year.
