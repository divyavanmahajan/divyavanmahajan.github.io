## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail run through a month of tech news (AI, Bitcoin, Neuralink, robots, longevity, Mars) and disagree often. Diamandis takes the more aggressive view that AI and robots will eventually do whole jobs and that intelligence is the key asset. Ismail pushes back: intelligence is poorly defined, jobs are bundles of tasks, and organisational design matters more than brain power or office hours.

## Key points

- **Why Google is behind.** Diamandis plays Eric Schmidt's comment blaming work-from-home and work-life balance, and largely agrees. Ismail says Schmidt conflates two things; he cites Facebook letting developers ship straight to production and Yahoo's matrix structure, and claims ExO-style Fortune 100 firms delivered 40 times the shareholder returns over seven years.
- **Superintelligence race.** Vitalik Buterin's argument that a step-function breakthrough could hand one lab permanent dominance gets Diamandis' support. Ismail objects that IQ-style intelligence ignores emotional and other facets, citing Hod Lipson's view that self-awareness depends on modelling oneself in the future.
- **Jobs as tasks.** Ismail, drawing on a working group with Erik Brynjolfsson, argues AI automates some of a role's tasks (say 10 of 27 for a financial advisor) rather than whole jobs. Diamandis expects full automation eventually; Ismail points to slow self-driving adoption as a sign that edge cases take a long time to solve.
- **Energy for AI.** Diamandis cites an estimate that by 2030 AI will need the equivalent of today's entire US grid output and backs Gen 4 nuclear. Ismail expects data centres to move to stranded renewables, as Bitcoin mining did.
- **Neuralink and robots.** Diamandis wants a Neuralink implant; Ismail would wait. They discuss Elon Musk's claim of a 10-billion-unit humanoid robot market at about $20,000 per unit and Figure's robots; Ismail cites the Roomba as a reason to expect slow progress in homes.
- **Bitcoin.** Both hosts say they are bullish, citing Morgan Stanley letting advisors pitch Bitcoin ETFs and Jeff Booth's debt argument. Ismail calls Bitcoin hard to ban in the US, pointing to the PGP First Amendment precedent.
