## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail review the week's tech news. Both see AI and crypto speeding up, but they disagree on how quickly that will change established industries. Diamandis predicts fast disruption of Hollywood and a humanoid robot boom. Ismail expects slower change in creative work and calls humanoid robots a bubble, while being even more bullish than Diamandis on Bitcoin and on what X could become.

## Key points

- **AI customer service.** They discuss Klarna's OpenAI-based assistant, which reportedly handled 2.3 million conversations, did the work of 700 agents and cut resolution time from 11 minutes to 2. Ismail calls the release a "puff piece" but also a sign of things to come.
- **Compute and AGI.** They play Elon Musk's claim that AI compute grows tenfold every six months. Ismail expects the gold rush to run for 12-18 months before it consolidates. He says AGI has no clear definition and proposes "artificial common sense" (ACS) as a better milestone.
- **Sora and Hollywood.** Diamandis says Hollywood is finished and imagines films personalised to each viewer. Ismail argues that directing and editing will hold out for years, and that AI will mostly lift new creators.
- **Bitcoin.** With Bitcoin at about $67,000, Ismail says he is in the "million dollar" camp. They point to MicroStrategy's treasury bet under Michael Saylor and to Bitcoin ETFs as signs of institutional buying.
- **Permissionless innovation.** Ismail proposes "permissionless disruptive innovation" (PDI) and cites Ethereum and OpenAI as examples. He also suggests X give every user a crypto wallet.
- **XPRIZE and robots.** Diamandis announces a $119 million water desalination XPRIZE and a $5 million Google Quantum prize. He relays Figure CEO Brett Adcock's argument for humanoid robots. Ismail compares humanoid robot investment to the 1999 internet bubble.
