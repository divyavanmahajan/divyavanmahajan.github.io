## Thesis

Peter Diamandis moderates a panel at the 8th Future Investment Initiative (FII) conference. The panelists broadly agree that AI is a generational shift that companies and countries must act on now. Jack Hidary frames it as "AI or die". The panel disagrees on where value will accrue and how dangerous the technology could become. Eric Schmidt warns that humanity is not ready for systems that may soon match top experts in every field.

## Key points

- **Bigger is better, for now.** Travis Kalanick argues AI automation of support, onboarding and sales will mostly strengthen large incumbents. He says his CloudKitchens food robots already run restaurants with one or no staff, and workers can talk to the robots.
- **Large quantitative models.** Hidary says SandboxAQ trains models on data generated from physics and chemistry equations rather than internet text. He cites a biomarker partnership with Paul Hudson's pharma company and plans to turn low-value refinery output into carbon composites.
- **Path to AGI.** Schmidt predicts specialized "savants" within five years and self-improving code after that. He sees a single system at 80-90% of expert ability in every field around 2030-2032, and names proliferation of cheap models, especially in biology, as the main danger.
- **Responsibility and speed.** Ruth Porat calls AI-for-good versus profit a false choice, since guardrails are what make the upside possible. She notes that Google added 110 languages to translation in six months.
- **Where value accrues.** Ben Horowitz warns that bottlenecks move, comparing the situation to the bandwidth glut after 1999, so debt-financed data centers are risky. He also warns that today's model leaders could be displaced. He cites a dev-tool portfolio company that went from zero to 40 million dollars in revenue in three months.
- **Creators, moderation and AI factories.** Shou Chew describes TikTok's AI labelling rules and AI-assisted moderation. Jay Puri urges countries and companies to build "AI factories" on NVIDIA's accelerated computing platform, which he says rests on CUDA rather than a single chip.
