## Thesis

At a Future Investment Initiative panel recorded in October 2024, Peter Diamandis asks how AI can democratize health for the "rising billion". The panelists agree that the technology for an AI doctor in every pocket largely exists. They see adoption, not capability, as the bottleneck: incentives, regulation and clinician workflows. Several of them expect countries with centralized health systems, such as Saudi Arabia, or with little legacy infrastructure to move faster than the US.

## Key points

- **Disruption over incrementalism.** Diamandis wants to reinvent healthcare the way mobile phones made landlines irrelevant. He predicts continuous sensing in clothes, cars and beds, with AI monitoring the data.
- **Measurement first.** Will Ahmed (Whoop) describes shrinking hospital-grade tools into consumer wearables. He says less than 1% of humanity measured their sleep last night, and expects user demand, not bureaucracy, to drive the change, much as generative AI forced schools to adapt.
- **Fitting AI into workflows.** Amir Dan Rubin (Healthier Capital, formerly One Medical) explains how One Medical used NLP and generative AI to triage and draft replies. He says this took 50% of tasks off physicians. He warns that big incumbent healthcare tends to win against outsiders who ignore how the ecosystem works.
- **Narrowing variation in care.** Jim Tananbaum (Foresite Capital, a $3.5 billion fund) expects AI to make standards of care more uniform within five years. He argues that adoption is easiest where doctors accept the tools and capitated payment rewards efficiency.
- **Honest advice and productivity.** Mehmet Öz argues that AI gives blunt, unbiased assessments. He describes a Sharecare program with Saudi Arabia's National Council on Occupational Health that aims to reach more than 10 million workers, and he frames workforce health as an economic driver rather than a cost.
- **Malpractice poll.** Asked when diagnosing without AI will count as malpractice, the panelists' answers range from one year to "within five years".
