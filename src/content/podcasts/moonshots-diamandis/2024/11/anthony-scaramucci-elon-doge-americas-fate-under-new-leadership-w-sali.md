## Thesis

Financier Anthony Scaramucci, a former White House communications director, joins Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail after the 2024 election. He argues that deep austerity will not fix US finances. In his view, the fix is budget discipline built into law and an updated Constitution. He is also very bullish on Bitcoin, and the hosts share that view.

## Key points

- **Musk and DOGE.** Scaramucci predicts that Elon Musk will last about 330 days in the administration. He describes Washington's immune system as working to wear outsiders down, and notes that DOGE is not a real department unless Congress creates one.
- **Debt and PAYGO.** Scaramucci says the roughly $6.7 trillion budget might yield $300-500 billion in cuts, but not $2 trillion. He proposes bringing back pay-as-you-go rules, which he credits for the Clinton-era surplus, as a 15-year path to balance. Ismail argues that the debt curve is the main structural risk.
- **Constitutional reform.** Scaramucci wants amendments to overturn Citizens United and end gerrymandering, but he defends the Electoral College. Ismail compares the Constitution to software that needs updating.
- **Bitcoin outlook.** With Bitcoin at $94,000, Scaramucci says 55% of his net worth is in it, and Ismail puts his own share at 35-40%. A clip shows Cathie Wood's 2030 targets: around $650,000 in her base case and $1-1.5 million in her bull case. Scaramucci argues that Bitcoin can reach gold's $18 trillion market cap. He cites a Pennsylvania reserve bill and Wisconsin's $150 million IBIT stake.
- **Beyond Bitcoin.** Scaramucci prefers Solana for tokenised stocks and payments. Ismail sees three categories: Bitcoin as the safe asset, meme coins for speculation, and utility tokens for specific uses.
- **Health and immigration.** Scaramucci and Diamandis back food-system reform under RFK Jr. Diamandis argues that FDA regulation holds back biotech. On immigration, Scaramucci warns against mass deportation, while Diamandis favours removing criminals and giving green cards to foreign PhD graduates.
