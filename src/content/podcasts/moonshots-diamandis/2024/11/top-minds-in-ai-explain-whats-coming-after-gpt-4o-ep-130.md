## Thesis

At the FII Summit, Peter Diamandis moderates a panel with Prem Akkaraju (Stability AI), Richard Socher (You.com) and Kai-Fu Lee (01.AI) on what comes after ChatGPT. The panellists agree that AI will spread into new modalities and much more of everyday work. They argue that this will be driven by falling costs and by workers learning to direct AI agents, not by bigger models alone.

## Key points

- **Generated, not rendered, media.** Akkaraju says some Avatar 2 frames took 6,000-7,000 hours of compute to render. He predicts most film and TV will be generated within 5-10 years, with 5-20x more content, but says a human should still start the creative process.
- **New modalities.** Socher sees the next step as multimodal models, including proteins. He says that in 2020 his Salesforce team generated a new protein 40% different from any natural one and synthesised it in the lab.
- **Limits of self-improvement.** Socher argues AI can become superhuman in domains that can be simulated, such as Go and programming. Where data is hard to collect, it is slower; he jokes that plumbers are the safest from disruption.
- **Workers become managers.** Socher says individual contributors will have to learn to manage AI. He cites a Mimecast workshop where a marketing workflow worth 6-20 hours every other week was automated by describing it to an agent.
- **Cheap models from constraints.** Lee says 01.AI trained a top-ranked model for $3 million, against $80-100 million for GPT-4. He credits limited access to GPUs (about 2,000) and says inference costs 10 cents per million tokens. Socher frames the result as Jevons paradox: cheaper intelligence means more use, not less.
- **Advice to 20-year-olds.** Akkaraju says not to spend time learning to code because English will be the new programming language. Socher disagrees and recommends programming plus a second passion. Lee says people should follow their hearts.
