## Thesis

In their monthly news roundup, Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail split on humanoid robots. Diamandis argues that generative AI lets robots learn by watching, so humanoids will become cheap and common. Ismail argues that coping with the messy physical world is the real bottleneck, and that robots should target dangerous or drudge work. Both are enthusiastic about brain-computer interfaces after Neuralink's first human implant.

## Key points

- **Compute over people.** Diamandis cites Google spending $30 billion and Microsoft $50 billion on data centres. He suggests chip compute has passed, or is about to pass, the combined compute of human brains.
- **Figure and Optimus.** Diamandis, whose venture fund invested in Figure, describes how the robot learned to make coffee by watching humans. He mentions reports that Microsoft and OpenAI were in talks to put $500 million into the company. Using Elon Musk's figure of $20,000 per Optimus, he pictures leasing a robot for about $500 a month.
- **Ismail's skepticism.** Ismail compares humanoids to the Roomba and to self-driving cars, which have run years behind their predictions. He calls physical adaptation robotics' "uncanny valley" and asks why robots need human form at all. He sees sewing, crop picking and surgery as the stronger cases.
- **Big forecasts.** Diamandis cites Figure's forecast of single-digit millions of humanoids by 2030 and Musk's forecast of a billion by 2040. He pictures surgical robots that upload every operation to the cloud.
- **Scarcity drives innovation.** The hosts point to a mass-produced sodium-ion EV with a 250 km range and a Department of Energy report of a 3,400-kiloton lithium reserve at California's Salton Sea. Ismail adds the Materials Project database, which lets researchers search for compounds by their properties.
- **Brain interfaces.** Diamandis discusses Musk's "Telepathy" product, Kurzweil's forecast of high-bandwidth BCI by 2033, and alternatives such as Paradromics, Blackrock and Mary Lou Jepsen's Openwater. Both say they would get an implant.
