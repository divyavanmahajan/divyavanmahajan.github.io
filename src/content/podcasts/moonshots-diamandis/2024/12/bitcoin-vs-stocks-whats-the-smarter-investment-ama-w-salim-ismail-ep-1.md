## Thesis

Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail answer listener questions from X, arguing that exponential technology rewards adaptability. Ismail says the most flexible companies win, while Diamandis prefers Bitcoin to broad index funds. Both are optimistic about space, BCIs, AI medicine and energy.

## Key points

- **Index funds vs Bitcoin.** Ismail cites an ExO study of the Fortune 100 in which the most adaptable firms delivered 40 times the shareholder returns of the least agile over seven years. Diamandis expects small biotech to recover, calls Bitcoin's outperformance "off the charts" and repeats his prediction of $300K by the end of 2025, with Bitcoin at about 106K at recording.
- **Quantum risk.** Ismail is not worried about quantum computers breaking wallets because quantum-safe standards are emerging. He says older encrypted archives are the real exposure. Diamandis worries more about nuclear codes and banking.
- **Mars and the Moon.** Diamandis says Elon Musk told him Starship would go to Mars "aggressively the next couple years". He predicts a White House call for boots on Mars by 2030, with an Optimus robot as the first boots. Both hosts would go to the Moon first.
- **BCIs and VR.** Diamandis highlights Max Hodak's Science, which he says works with tens of millions of connections where Neuralink uses about a thousand fibres. Both would get an implant, and both say headset hardware is a year or two from being usable.
- **AI diagnosis and regulation.** Diamandis gives rough figures: physicians alone diagnose with about 70% accuracy, physicians with a chatbot about 75%, and AI alone about 90%. Ismail blames regulatory capture and seven-year FDA timelines for slowing healthcare.
- **War and energy.** Ismail notes that the Russia-Ukraine war is being fought with roughly half a million drones. He argues that cheap solar plus storage will beat fusion. Diamandis touts perovskite solar at about 30% conversion and a $100 million carbon-removal XPRIZE funded by Musk, to be awarded in April 2025.
