## Thesis

Jack Hidary, CEO of SandboxAQ, calls the choice between return-to-office and work-from-home a false binary. He and Nadia Harhen, general manager of AI simulation, describe a "third way": no permanent offices, outcome-based goals, frequent in-person offsites and teams embedded with customers. Hidary credits this model for the company's research breakthroughs, and Peter Diamandis frames it as the agility an exponential organization needs.

## Key points

- **The three C's.** Hidary's model rests on collaboration across disciplines (he cites 80+ PhDs and about 70 engineers), customer (one five-person team spent two weeks inside a customer's offices, and about 10 staff will be placed permanently at a partner) and community, under the motto "work where you thrive".
- **Economics.** Hidary says the company's CFO calculates savings of millions of dollars a year from having no permanent offices, even after paying for travel and offsites. He links big-company RTO mandates to sunk real-estate costs and traditional thinking.
- **Distributed teams.** Harhen says her team of about 60 spans six or seven time zones, with hubs built around where people already live. Annual company offsites are held in university cities, most recently Cambridge, Massachusetts.
- **Treat people like adults.** Hidary describes unlimited vacation in the US, OKRs, service leadership with no CEO office, and media training for every new hire, who may give talks without asking permission.
- **Large quantitative models.** Harhen explains LQMs as physics-based simulation that generates training data where none exists. Hidary cites magnetic-field navigation tested on a US Air Force C-17 with GPS switched off four times. Harhen describes computing a PFAS bond energy as a first step towards breaking down "forever chemicals".
- **Predictions.** Hidary predicts that AI will create millions of net new jobs, that sectors will diverge between AI adopters and laggards, and that global GDP (about $110 trillion by his figure) will double. Diamandis cites forecasts that humanoid robots will cost about $30,000.
