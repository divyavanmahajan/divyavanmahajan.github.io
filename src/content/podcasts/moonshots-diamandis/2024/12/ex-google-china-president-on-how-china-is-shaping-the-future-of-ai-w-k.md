## Thesis

Kai-Fu Lee, CEO of 01.AI and chairman of Sinovation Ventures, argues that US labs lead on breakthroughs but Chinese teams win on execution. Once a frontier lab proves something can be done, a small team with tight constraints can match it far more cheaply. For Lee, the real bottleneck for AI applications is inference cost, and he expects that cost to keep falling quickly.

## Key points

- **Why he became a founder.** Lee says he started 01.AI 17 months before the interview because OpenAI was not available in China and he did not want China left behind in generative AI. He says that with ChatGPT available there, his odds of founding it would have been about 50/50.
- **Frugal frontier models.** Lee says 01.AI trained on only 2,000 GPUs. He says pre-training Yi-Lightning cost a little over $3 million, roughly 3% of GPT-4's cost, with a project team of 20 to 30 people. By his account, the model ranked sixth in the world in October.
- **Inference economics.** Lee puts Yi-Lightning at 10 cents per million tokens in cost and 14 cents in price, against $4.40 for GPT-4o. He estimates that a GPT-4o-based AI search would cost about 10 cents per query, while Google earns 1.6 cents per search. He credits mixture-of-experts, KV-cache work and co-designing the model with its hardware.
- **Google's dilemma and Beago.** Lee argues that Google cannot switch to single-answer search without hurting its ad revenue. Beago, an AI search engine from Rhymes Technologies, a US company he venture-built, is his bet on that shift.
- **Open source and regulation.** 01.AI publishes all but its best model under Apache. Lee prefers enforcing existing laws on fraud and theft against misuse of AI over AI-specific regulation.
- **Jobs and risk.** Lee expects white-collar work to be disrupted within five years and robots to arrive more slowly. Diamandis, an investor in Figure, pushes back on that timeline. Lee expects AGI in 5 to 10 years.
