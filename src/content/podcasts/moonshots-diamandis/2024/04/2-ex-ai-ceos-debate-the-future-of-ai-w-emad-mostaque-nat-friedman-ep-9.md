## Thesis

On stage at Abundance360, former Stability AI CEO Emad Mostaque and former GitHub CEO Nat Friedman tell Peter Diamandis that the past year moved AI from research to real adoption. Mostaque argues that models are now "good enough", so the story shifts to what people build by combining them. Friedman argues that agents and long-running autonomy will turn companies into increasingly neural organisations, and that investment in intelligence could reach points of global GDP.

## Key points

- **Starting gun for adoption.** Friedman says GitHub Copilot was an early commercial LLM product, but commercialisation only took off after ChatGPT. He cites a rumoured jump in ChatGPT revenue from zero to $2 billion in about a year.
- **Open vs proprietary.** Mostaque compares open models to graduates you hire and proprietary models to consultants. He reports 330 million downloads of Stability models on Hugging Face and says both kinds of model need to exist.
- **Speed of generation.** Mostaque demos real-time image editing and a distilled model that produces "200 cats with hats per second", video generated on a consumer GPU, and a 3D model released open source the same day.
- **Models are grown, not engineered.** Friedman says we can measure every part of a neural network and still not understand what it is doing mechanically. He points to interpretability as a small but important field.
- **Agents and neural companies.** Friedman expects departments to become single models or swarms of models, and points to Cognition's coding agent demo running hundreds of steps on GPT-4. He also expects near-human voice-to-voice AI soon.
- **Capital and science.** Friedman compares AI investment with railway building and with solar, which he says takes about half a point of global GDP. He describes the Vesuvius Challenge, which used AI to read carbonised Herculaneum scrolls. Mostaque argues that open medical models could guide patients and organise treatment knowledge.
