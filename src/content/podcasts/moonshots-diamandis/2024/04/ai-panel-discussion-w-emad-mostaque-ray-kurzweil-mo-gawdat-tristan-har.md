## Thesis

In this Q&A from the 2024 Abundance360 Summit, Emad Mostaque, Ray Kurzweil, Mo Gawdat and Tristan Harris agree that AI cannot be halted. They differ on what to do about it. Gawdat says competitive fear and greed rule out a slowdown, Harris wants new tools that let society's "immune system" keep pace, Mostaque argues for open, edge-optimised models, and Kurzweil frames AI as something already merging with people.

## Key points

- **Sentience.** Mostaque sees today's models as weights that put out what goes in, though agent systems could give rise to new forms of intelligence. Gawdat calls the question irrelevant: if AI simulates sentience, people should treat it that way.
- **Benefits tied to risks.** Harris says the AI that can tutor every middle schooler in biology is the same one that can explain bioweapons, and asks which company or government anyone would trust to be "a trillion times more powerful".
- **AI for consensus.** Harris suggests ranking social content by "unlikely consensus" between opposed groups instead of outrage, and cites zero-knowledge proofs and Lawrence Lessig's "code is law".
- **No off switch.** Gawdat argues a slowdown is unrealistic and points to Google being forced to release early. Mostaque says open models mean dozens of groups need not train their own, and edge optimisation cuts energy use.
- **Smarter and merging.** Kurzweil uses smartphones as his example: carrying machine intelligence already makes people smarter. He suggests calling LLMs "large object models". Mostaque says LLMs will be one part of composite systems, not a path to AGI on their own.
- **Education and investing.** Mostaque announces a rolling fund with no management or performance fees, and says education should build agency and connection. Gawdat names problem solving, human connection and telling real from fake as the key future skills.
