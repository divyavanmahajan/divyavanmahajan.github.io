## Thesis

In a "WTF just happened in tech" news roundup, Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail argue that AI-driven simulation is becoming the main engine of progress. They point to drug discovery with AlphaFold 3 and robot training with Nvidia's simulation tools. They also debate where humanoid robots and AI-generated media will actually land. The episode closes with Ismail's case for Bitcoin as the one cryptocurrency that combines decentralization, security and scalability.

## Key points

- **AlphaFold 3 and in silico medicine.** Diamandis says AlphaFold 3 predicts how proteins interact with other molecules, which he sees as a step toward simulating cells, organs and eventually personalized drug testing. Ismail expects it to cut drug development costs by filtering candidates before human trials.
- **Simulation transfers to the real world.** Diamandis highlights Nvidia's DrEureka, which trained a robot dog to balance on a yoga ball in simulation that then worked zero-shot in reality.
- **AI media and normalisation.** Ismail calls Sora's character swaps incremental and doubts personalized films will appeal, since he watches to see a director's choices. Both note how fast people normalise new AI capabilities.
- **Humanoid robots.** Ismail questions why robots need human form and raises battery life and liability concerns. Diamandis counters that the built world is designed for humans, and cites a figure of $40,000-100,000 per Figure robot, or roughly $400-1,000 a month if leased.
- **Enterprise readiness.** Ismail describes an OpenExO workshop for helping companies prepare for AI. He says implementation is harder than consumer use, and that persona prompts improve results.
- **Bitcoin.** Ismail predicts Bitcoin reaches $1 million within three to four years, citing US debt service costs. He argues that the Lightning Network solves its scalability gap, and says his holdings are now 85-90% Bitcoin.
