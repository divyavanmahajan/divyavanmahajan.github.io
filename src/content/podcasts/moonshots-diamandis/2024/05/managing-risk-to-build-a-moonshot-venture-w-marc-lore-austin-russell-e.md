## Thesis

Recorded at Abundance360 2024, Peter Diamandis talks with Marc Lore (Diapers.com, Jet.com, Wonder) and Austin Russell (Luminar) about risk and resilience in moonshot ventures. Both argue that outsiders drive most industry disruption, that skepticism should be tested rather than feared, and that founders must stay objective enough to pivot when the data points elsewhere.

## Key points

- **Test the skeptics.** Lore recalls that the industry, including Procter & Gamble and Kimberly-Clark, said overnight diaper delivery at Walmart prices could not work. His test: ask experts why the thesis fails, and if their answers do not convince you, you have something.
- **Wonder's model.** Lore describes Wonder as a vertically integrated "super app for meal time" that owns its restaurants and cooks about 30 cuisines from a 2,800 sq ft kitchen with three pieces of electric equipment. He says it recently raised $700 million on a SAFE note.
- **Pivoting away from a working business.** Wonder ran 400 cook-on-the-way trucks in New Jersey that were unit-economics positive, Lore says, but brick-and-mortar tests had better economics and allowed multi-restaurant orders. He pushed to take revenue to zero and start over, treating past work as sunk cost.
- **Safety over robotaxis.** Russell says Luminar cut lidar cost from about $100,000 to under $1,000 and bet the company on safety systems for production cars (Volvo, Mercedes), which he says was contrarian when driverless ride-hailing was the consensus.
- **Betting on yourself.** Russell favours SAFE notes to avoid heavy dilution when perceived and actual value diverge. He briefly discusses his abandoned Forbes deal and Luminar's stated goal of saving 100 million lives over 100 years.
- **Relentlessness.** Lore says the hardest parts shift by stage (talent early, capital later) and that taking risk, often personal risk, is frequently the best way to raise the odds of success.
