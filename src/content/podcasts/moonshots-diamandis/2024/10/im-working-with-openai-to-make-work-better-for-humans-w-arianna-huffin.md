## Thesis

Arianna Huffington, founder of Thrive Global, argues that daily behaviours drive both health outcomes and business performance, and that the way to change them at scale is small habits delivered where people already work. She presents Thrive AI Health, a joint venture with OpenAI, as the next step: an AI coach that personalizes those habits. She and Peter Diamandis disagree on whether humans will merge with AI.

## Key points

- **From awareness to a behaviour-change business.** Huffington says she left The Huffington Post in 2016 because a media company could only raise awareness. Thrive Global sells to enterprises and frames well-being as "productivity multipliers" so it survives budget cuts. She names Accenture, Salesforce, Walmart and AT&T as partners.
- **Meet users where they are.** Huffington says Pfizer employees engaged with Thrive content at 14% in the Thrive app but 76% when the same content ran inside Microsoft Teams. Diamandis draws the lesson for founders: reduce friction by going to the platforms users already use.
- **Short resets.** Thrive's 60-second "Resets" combine breathing, music and images. Huffington says 76% of Synchrony contact-centre agents chose three Resets a day over three 5-minute breaks.
- **Thrive AI Health.** The new company is funded by Thrive Global and OpenAI, with Eli Lilly and the Alice Walton Foundation as strategic investors. Huffington says the coach is trained on peer-reviewed literature, not the open web, and is sold B2B and B2B2C. One example is Lilly Direct, where she says a free coach helps with medication adherence.
- **Why Sam Altman joined.** Huffington says Altman wanted a fuller story about AI for everyday people, beyond productivity tools and flashy features.
- **Soul vs. merger.** Diamandis cites Ray Kurzweil's view that humans will merge with AI. Huffington disagrees: she says AI is non-judgmental and useful, but humans have a soul and AI does not.
