## Thesis

In this second conversation with Peter Diamandis, SandboxAQ CEO Jack Hidary argues that you don't need fault-tolerant quantum computers to make money from quantum technology. He says combining quantum sensors and quantum equations with "large quantitative models" produces products that Global 1000 customers buy now. In his account, the hard part of each product is usually the AI that separates signal from noise, not the quantum hardware.

## Key points

- **A real business.** Hidary says SandboxAQ spun out of Alphabet about two and a half years ago, raised around $500 million and now serves customers in pharma, aerospace, energy and batteries.
- **AQNav, navigation without GPS.** Hidary describes GPS as brittle: easy to jam or spoof, and useless underwater or in tunnels. He says jamming is already disrupting thousands of flights. AQNav reads the Earth's crustal magnetic field with an optically pumped magnetometer and matches it against magnetic maps. He says it is air-gapped, cannot be jammed or spoofed, and has been publicly tested by the US Air Force alongside inertial navigation.
- **AQMed, magnetocardiography.** A quantum sensor reads the heart's magnetic field and is in clinical trials (not approved) at Mount Sinai and Mayo Clinic. Hidary pitches it as emergency-room triage in under five minutes, compared with hours of blood draws for the 8 million chest-pain visits a year in the US. An elevator shaft at UCSF forced a redesign, and the device is now on its seventh iteration.
- **Drug discovery.** Hidary cites 90% clinical-trial failure and only about a dozen first-in-human novel drugs approved worldwide each year. SandboxAQ generates training data from quantum equations on Nvidia GPUs and says this has helped a Nobel-winning UCSF lab refine molecules.
- **AQtive Guard and post-quantum migration.** Pointing to Peter Shor's 1994 result and the post-quantum standards published on August 13, 2024, Hidary says banks need seven to ten years to migrate. He urges the blockchain community to start now, because Bitcoin and Ethereum rely on RSA or ECC. AQtive Guard inventories an organisation's cryptography and gives security teams a migration roadmap. It also flags hashes that are already broken, such as MD5.
