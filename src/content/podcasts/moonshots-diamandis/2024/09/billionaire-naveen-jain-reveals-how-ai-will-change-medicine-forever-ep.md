## Thesis

Naveen Jain, founder and CEO of Viome, and host Peter Diamandis argue that the US healthcare system is built for acute care and pays providers when people are sick, not when they stay healthy. They expect AI, fed by continuous personal data, to make prevention personalised and cheap. Jain names education and healthcare as the two areas AI will change most, and predicts that India, with less legacy baggage, may get there first.

## Key points

- **Misaligned incentives.** Diamandis lists US figures he cites, including health spending at 18% of GDP and life expectancy down to 76. Jain says over 90% of healthcare dollars go to chronic disease and holds up fixed-fee prevention models such as Fountain Life as a way to align incentives.
- **Data at scale.** Jain says Viome has analysed close to one million samples and over 100 quadrillion biological data points, and uses AI to generate personalised food and supplement recommendations that change as the user's data changes.
- **Seven AI shifts.** Diamandis lists AI health coaches, wearables and implantables, AI analysis of biomarker data, molecular modelling in the style of AlphaFold 3, AI imaging, personalised medication, and robot surgeons. Jain adds nanobots on a 10-15 year horizon and predicts an integrated health agent within 18-24 months.
- **India leapfrogging.** Jain compares healthcare to India skipping wireline telephony, arguing its national system and scale will let it deploy AI health and education faster than the US, where he says FDA approval slows new technology.
- **Employer economics.** Jain says Viome gives employees a 75% discount rather than free access so they take responsibility, and pitches the service to employers as a way to lower insurance costs.
- **Raising moonshot kids.** Jain describes growing up poor in India and arriving in the US with $45. He says he taught his children that success is measured by lives improved, and recommends "nested moonshots": imagine success, then ask what comes next.
