## Thesis

Cathie Wood, founder of ARK Invest, argues that costs are falling so fast across converging technologies that the change will come faster and hit harder than equity markets are pricing in. Peter Diamandis agrees and frames AI as the asteroid that lets agile startups displace lumbering incumbents. All the forecasts below are ARK's or Diamandis's views, not established facts.

## Key points

- **Apple, OpenAI and Google.** Wood describes two views inside ARK: analyst Nick Grous expects a strong iPhone upgrade cycle, while Brett Winton thinks Apple's focus on privacy will stop it adopting bolder AI ideas. Diamandis sees the partnership as a direct challenge to Google, and both argue that AI agents threaten ad-funded search. Wood notes that Bing is gaining share.
- **Nvidia's margins at risk.** Wood says ARK made nearly 100x on Nvidia. She now questions whether a $3 trillion company with gross margins in the high 70s can defend them against a concentrated customer base of hyperscalers that design their own chips, Tesla among them.
- **Enterprise AI readiness.** Drawing on Palantir, Wood says large companies must first gather, clean and map their data, which could cause a pause and a temporary GPU oversupply. Diamandis expects AI-first startups to win out, as digital retailers did against Toys "R" Us.
- **Bitcoin.** Using on-chain analytics, ARK estimates the bull market is about halfway through. Its 2030 base case is around $650,000 per coin and its bull case is over $1.5 million. The case rests on wealth-management platforms listing spot ETFs, Bitcoin taking 50% of gold's role, and Bitcoin acting as insurance in emerging markets such as El Salvador.
- **Robots and Wright's Law.** Wood estimates humanoid robots as a $25 trillion market. She says industrial robot costs fall 50% with every cumulative doubling of units, starting from only 700,000 to 800,000 units worldwide.
- **Robotaxis and biotech.** ARK would be "shocked" if Tesla's robotaxi does not launch within two years, and it sizes autonomy at $8-10 trillion in revenue. Wood calls CRISPR Therapeutics' approved one-time cures undervalued, and Diamandis describes DeepMind's AlphaFold 3 roadmap toward modelling a whole cell.
