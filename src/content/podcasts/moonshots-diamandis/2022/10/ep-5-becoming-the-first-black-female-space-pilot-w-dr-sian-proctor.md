## Thesis

Sian Proctor, geoscientist, artist and pilot of the all-civilian Inspiration4 mission, argues that a curiosity mindset and persistence through rejection are what make big goals reachable. Peter Diamandis frames her story as a moonshot that only came together because she kept preparing while SpaceX and Jared Isaacman built the opportunity.

## Key points

- **From near miss to reframing.** Proctor says she reached the final 47 candidates in NASA's 2009 astronaut selection and was turned down. She first tried to remake herself to fit NASA, then chose to celebrate being "almost" an astronaut and became an analog astronaut, including four months in the HI-SEAS habitat in Hawaii.
- **Winning the Prosperity seat.** She describes entering Inspiration4 through a Shift4Shop video, applying as an artist and poet rather than a scientist. With about 6,000 Twitter followers, she says her entry reached 70,000 views in three days.
- **Mission design.** Proctor explains Isaacman's four pillars (leadership, hope, generosity, prosperity), tied to a $200 million fundraising goal for St. Jude Children's Research Hospital. Diamandis notes Isaacman paid for the flight and gave away the seats.
- **Flight and "Earth light".** She recounts a smooth launch, a sustained 6 G re-entry, and the cupola window, which she credits with a stronger form of the overview effect.
- **Passion versus purpose.** Proctor separates passions, which come and go, from purpose, which persists; she says she found a new purpose in art and poetry during the 2020 lockdown. Diamandis adds that being authentic and getting into flow are signs of purpose.
- **Next moonshots.** She promotes a "JEDI" (just, equitable, diverse, inclusive) space industry and a planned nonprofit, One Sky One Ocean. Her proposed XPRIZE: a sustainable replacement for plastic packaging, combined with ocean cleanup.
