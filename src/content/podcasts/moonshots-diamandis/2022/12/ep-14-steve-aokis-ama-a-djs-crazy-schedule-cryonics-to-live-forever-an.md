## Thesis

DJ and entrepreneur Steve Aoki and host Peter Diamandis work through a list of "life's big questions". Both say they are optimists. They argue that happiness comes from serving other people and that technology will push humans towards merging with machines. Aoki explains why he has signed up to be frozen after death, and describes MetaZoo as the business he is most excited about outside music.

## Key points

- **Happiness through service.** Aoki says he finds happiness by giving it to crowds at his shows. Diamandis sums this up as finding happiness by supporting it in others. Both say everyone can find a purpose, even a small one.
- **Humans become cyborgs.** Aoki predicts humans will eventually become "Androids", half organic and half technological. Diamandis passes on Ray Kurzweil's forecasts of human-level AI by 2029 and high-bandwidth brain-computer interfaces in the early-to-mid 2030s. He also describes a DARPA monkey experiment with robotic arms controlled through neural implants.
- **Cryonics and uploading.** Aoki says he has signed up for a full-body freeze with Alcor and is setting up a separate trust so his descendants cannot keep his money from him if he is revived. Diamandis explains the process at a high level and raises the question of identity that comes with brain uploading. He recommends the "We Are Bob" book series.
- **Saying no.** Aoki says he quit heavy drinking at shows in 2009 after a DJ friend died of an overdose. Diamandis reads a line from Martine Rothblatt: very successful people say no to almost everything.
- **MetaZoo numbers.** Aoki is a co-founder of MetaZoo, a trading card game built on cryptids such as Bigfoot. He says it grew from a Kickstarter to more than $50 million in gross sales in 16 months, and that 50,000 boxes sold out in about 20 seconds. He thinks it could become a billion-dollar company.
- **Pursuit of Happiness.** Aoki sped his 2009 remix of Kid Cudi's track up to 128 BPM to make it a festival anthem. He says it only took off three years later, after it was used in the film Project X.
