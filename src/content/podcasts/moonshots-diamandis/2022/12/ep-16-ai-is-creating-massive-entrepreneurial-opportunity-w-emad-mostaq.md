## Thesis

Emad Mostaque, founder and CEO of Stability AI, argues that generative AI should be treated as open infrastructure, a commons rather than something controlled by a few corporations. He expects it to disrupt every information-based industry within a few years, and he thinks individuals and small groups building on open models will outcompete incumbents. Peter Diamandis frames the moment as a period of hyper-disruption and opportunity.

## Key points

- **Stable Diffusion as a primitive.** Mostaque says Stable Diffusion compressed about 100,000 gigabytes of image-text pairs (2 billion images) into a 1.6 GB file that runs offline. He claims image generation went from 5.6 seconds at launch to near real time after a 30x speed-up.
- **Open beats closed.** Mostaque reports a community of about 120,000 people across vertical projects: EleutherAI's GPT-Neo/X (which he says has 25 million downloads), Harmonai's Dance Diffusion for audio, and OpenBioML for protein work. He also cites a 4,000-A100 cluster on Amazon's cloud.
- **Advice to builders and CEOs.** He tells 20-year-olds to drop everything and focus on generative AI, and predicts $100 billion and then $1 trillion flowing into the sector. Companies, he says, should set up small teams with freedom and work backwards from a 5-10 year view.
- **Personalised and national models.** Mostaque describes foundation models as "pizza bases" that companies, cultures and individuals can fine-tune with as few as 10 images. His example is Japan Diffusion, where "salary man" means a sad man rather than a happy one.
- **Education as the key moonshot.** He cites Imagine Worldwide's rollout of the Global Learning XPRIZE winners and says 76% of children in refugee camps learned literacy and numeracy in 13 months without internet. He proposes national AI tutors that learn from the children they teach, funded through results-based bonds.
- **Risks and IP.** Mostaque says that nobody yet knows who owns AI-generated output. His own view is that it should belong to the person who writes the prompt. He describes opt-in and opt-out mechanisms for artists, and he warns that closed AI could become a tool of manipulation and control.
