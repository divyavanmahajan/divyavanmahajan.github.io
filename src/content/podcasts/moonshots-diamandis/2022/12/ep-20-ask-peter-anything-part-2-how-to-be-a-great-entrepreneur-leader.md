## Thesis

Peter Diamandis mentors five listeners (named in the show notes as Julian Alvarez, Yahli Admati, Naisukhy Laurent, Diana Bald and David Thiel) on their ventures and lives. His recurring advice: pair a big massive transformative purpose (MTP) with near-term revenue, start with a very narrow niche, and remember that early investors back the founder, not the company. He closes with how he balances being a father with running many companies.

## Key points

- **When to pivot.** Alvarez's personal-growth app MindFlow had about 700 downloads in four months. Diamandis tells him to interview customers, solve a must-have pain point (aspirin rather than vitamins), iterate rapidly, and pivot only after repeated attempts fail. He adds that funders rarely back companies that go years without revenue.
- **Fundraising.** With a $220,000 pre-seed at an $8 million valuation and $70k left, Alvarez asks about valuation. Diamandis, who says he has raised billions for 26 startups, warns against raising valuation too fast because down rounds are hard, and urges founders to give early backers a good deal.
- **Narrow the niche.** He tells Admati to pick one audience, such as high-school students in Tel Aviv, and cites the XPRIZE starting as a single suborbital spaceflight prize. He also relays the Siri founders' practice of building for where technology will be in three years.
- **Early capital.** To Laurent he lists friends and family, customers (pre-sales via Kickstarter) and suppliers. He tells the Stone Soup fable and how Draper Labs printed and mailed his student newsletter instead of giving a $5,000 grant.
- **Measurable communities.** For Bald's Activist360, he suggests objective scores and an "impact pledge" of public, measured commitments, and asking experts very simple questions.
- **Parenting.** Diamandis tells Thiel he wants his sons to find passion, ask great questions and develop grit. He blocks out family time, wears a watch that reads "NOW", and hires CEOs while he serves as chairman.
