## Thesis

The panel debates the Future of Life Institute letter calling for a six-month pause on training systems more powerful than GPT-4. Anton Troynikov (Chroma) doubts the danger is well argued; Flo Crivello (Lindy) thinks the danger is real but that a pause will not help. Nathan Labenz, drawing on his GPT-4 red teaming, argues that the few leaders able to scale further should voluntarily wait.

## Key points

- **Motives and timing.** Troynikov questions why the letter arrived now and suggests the least charitable reading is that dominant labs use safety to cement their lead. Crivello asks for steelmanning instead, and explains Eliezer Yudkowsky's case through instrumental convergence.
- **Danger without a cure.** Crivello puts existential risk at perhaps 5 to 10%, but says China will not pause, a "temporary" policy tends to become permanent (he cites the TSA), and a pause grows a compute overhang.
- **Red-team evidence.** Labenz says GPT-4 is safe to deploy only because its power is limited, and that harmful exploits he reported during red teaming still work in production. He points to GPT-4 solving a hindsight-bias test that earlier models got worse at, as a sign that capabilities jump unpredictably.
- **Physical limits.** Troynikov and Crivello argue over robots: Crivello says software is the bottleneck, while Troynikov says complex systems are fragile and that data centres depend on human upkeep.
- **Misuse is the shared worry.** Troynikov is more concerned about general-purpose reasoners lowering the expertise needed for mass-casualty attacks. Crivello adds that attackers adopt new tools faster than defenders.
- **Voluntary versus imposed.** Crivello and Troynikov say they could accept a pause backed by a concrete safety roadmap. Labenz stresses he wants leaders to choose to wait, not government regulation.
