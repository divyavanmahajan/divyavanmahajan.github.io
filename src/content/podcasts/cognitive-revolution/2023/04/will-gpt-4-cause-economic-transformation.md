## Thesis

Nathan Labenz, drawing on his red-teaming of GPT-4, argues that the model can already handle the first conversation with a doctor, lawyer or service worker, and that this will transform the economy by breaking jobs into tasks rather than replacing whole jobs at once. Erik Torenberg pushes on whether this time is different and on how incumbent professions and white-collar workers will react.

## Key points

- **Red-teaming as role play.** Labenz describes casting GPT-4 as a doctor, dentist, immigration lawyer, fence-dispute mediator, personal trainer, garage receptionist and hardware store associate. In one case his family skipped a call to the pediatrician based on its advice; weak spots were college-level maths and chemistry, where a confused "student" could mislead it.
- **Unbundling jobs.** Citing the "GPTs are GPTs" paper, Labenz notes that almost no job can be done entirely by a language model, but about half of all jobs have about half of their tasks exposed. He expects a new Taylorism for knowledge work, like the move from artisanal car-making to the assembly line.
- **Cost ratios.** Labenz estimates 10-to-1 to 100-to-1 cost reductions: a $100 doctor's appointment could become a 45-minute conversation for about a dollar, with escalations to humans falling from 5% to 1% to 0.1% of cases.
- **Is this time different?** Responding to Torenberg's farmer analogy, Labenz says there is no obvious third category after physical and cognitive labour, and worries that change over two to five years is too fast for the generational adjustment society relied on in the past.
- **Regulation and interest groups.** Labenz expects the AMA, ABA and FDA to move to restrict medical and legal models, but thinks distributed access will make this a question of speed. Torenberg is more pessimistic, predicting a "bifurcated economy" between regulated and unregulated sectors.
- **White-collar morale.** Torenberg expects a large hit to the status of white-collar workers; Labenz points to a viral TikTok of a deflated doctor whose top comments came from women hoping ChatGPT would listen to them.
