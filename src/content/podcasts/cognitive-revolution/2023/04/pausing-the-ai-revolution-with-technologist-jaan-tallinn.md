## Thesis

Jaan Tallinn, co-founder of Skype and an investor in DeepMind, Anthropic and Conjecture, tells Nathan Labenz that the frontier AI labs are locked in a race he believes is too risky. He presents the Future of Life Institute's open letter calling for a six-month pause on systems more powerful than GPT-4 as a way to create common knowledge of that concern and to answer the question the labs leave open: when to slow down.

## Key points

- **Investing for influence.** Tallinn says he invested in fundamental AI research labs to have a voice inside them, not to make money. He thinks applied AI start-ups built on LLMs risk having their work made obsolete by each new model generation.
- **Risk per generation.** He estimates the risk of a life-ending disaster at 1 to 50% per 10x increase in training compute, roughly 7% as a geometric mean. With 10x steps arriving every 6 to 18 months, he says a few more generations would make doom more likely than not.
- **The key threshold.** Tallinn's biggest worry is AI that can develop AI better than human researchers can. He also cites inner versus outer alignment: training selects models by behaviour, not by what they want.
- **Race dynamics.** He calls the reported Anthropic fundraising plan, if accurate, strong evidence of a dangerous race among US labs. He contrasts that with Conjecture, which composes less capable models. On China, he argues it does not currently seem to be racing as intensely as US labs.
- **What a pause buys.** Tallinn points to interpretability work, such as Neel Nanda's 200 open problems, and to ARC's evals project. He favours compute governance, comparing the visibility of large training runs to uranium enrichment in nuclear control.
- **Reaction.** He says Sam Altman expressed agreement with the letter's spirit, with caveats, and that Sundar Pichai called for government involvement. He puts the chance of an actual pause at double-digit uncertainty either way.
