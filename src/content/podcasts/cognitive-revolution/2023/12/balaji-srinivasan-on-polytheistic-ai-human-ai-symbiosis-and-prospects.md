## Thesis

Balaji Srinivasan argues that AI doom scenarios skip the hard step of physical actuation, and that AI will remain amplified intelligence: a human-machine symbiosis that dates back to fire and tools. He expects a "polytheistic" world of competing AIs from US, Chinese and decentralised blocs, and sees US regulation as a bid for control. Nathan Labenz, who calls himself a techno-optimist libertarian, argues that losing control remains plausible enough to take seriously.

## Key points

- **Static versus changing rules.** Srinivasan says current AI, both DeepMind-style reinforcement learning and generative models, works best where rules are static. He calls time-varying, adversarial systems such as markets the real frontier, and suggests open weights let anyone generate adversarial inputs that break a model.
- **Constraints on superintelligence.** He cites chaos and turbulence limits on prediction, cryptography an AI cannot break without private keys, and physical friction. He says an AI cannot live off the land without hundreds of millions of connected robots, and that Apple, Google and Microsoft hold an off switch.
- **Where is the actuator?** Srinivasan argues harm must come through humans radicalised by an AI, a conventional terrorist-cell threat, or through drones that do not yet exist at scale. Labenz counters with habitat degradation and gradual economic marginalisation, citing a Google DeepMind diagnosis study where AI alone beat humans with AI, and NVIDIA's Eureka.
- **A tripolar world.** Srinivasan predicts US, Chinese and global decentralised tech blocs. He expects China to exploit centralised data and India to build "generic AI" trained on copyrighted works, and calls AI, Bitcoin and China the "ABCs of economic apocalypse for Blue America".
- **Regulation as control.** He likens the 10^26 compute threshold in the executive order to the start of a software FDA. He argues that a government he credibly alleges funded the COVID virus cannot be trusted on AI safety, and that proliferation is good because no one gains an advantage.
- **Labenz's counterpoints.** Labenz says jailbreaks show control lagging capability from GPT-2 to GPT-4, and compares mass proliferation to mailing gain-of-function lab kits to every home.
