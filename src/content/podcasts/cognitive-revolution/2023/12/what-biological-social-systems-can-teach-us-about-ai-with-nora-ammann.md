## Thesis

Nora Ammann, co-founder and director of PIBBSS, argues that AI safety faces an "epistemic access" problem: the systems of most concern do not exist yet, so the trial and error that science normally relies on is unavailable. Besides studying today's ML models or idealised rational agents, she proposes a third route: learn from how intelligent behaviour is implemented in brains, organisms, ecologies and economies, and trust conclusions only where several independent perspectives agree. Nathan Labenz, who met her as a recommender in the Survival and Flourishing Fund's fall 2023 grant round, backs the approach as thinking at the right scale.

## Key points

- **Three premises of risk.** Ammann frames AI risk simply: systems are becoming more capable, they are put in charge of more consequential decisions, and nobody yet knows how to make them reliably do what we want.
- **Epistemic pluralism.** No single analogy is true, she says; confidence should rise only where multiple independent maps converge. Loose analogies generate hypotheses, while mathematical models can test them.
- **More than borrowing.** Transfer between fields ranges from single facts (mammalian brain sizes) to frameworks such as game theory and principal-agent models, to practices. She cites bioengineering, where simulation-heavy engineering stays tied to theory, as a model for alignment work.
- **Agency and hierarchy.** PIBBSS fellows ask whether agency can be measured the way temperature eventually was, and study hierarchical misalignment, with cancer and mesa-optimisation as examples. Ammann wonders what a "vertical game theory" would look like.
- **Theory for evals and interacting systems.** She argues that evaluations need precise definitions of terms like deception, and wants interpretability for interacting agents. Early ecology-inspired and LLM-interaction experiments have not yet given her strong evidence either way.
- **Programmes.** PIBBSS runs a three-month summer fellowship pairing mostly PhD-level domain experts with alignment researchers, a newer research affiliate track, and a reading list. Labenz urges researchers in mature fields to move into AI.
