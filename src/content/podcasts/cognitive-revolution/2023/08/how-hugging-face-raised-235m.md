## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and Erik Torenberg analyse Hugging Face after its $235M Series D at a $4.5B valuation. Labenz argues the company is the undisputed hub for AI discovery, but that its compute business gets little lock-in from the community, and its ideological commitment to open source limits how it can make money. Both see a sale to a strategic buyer such as Google or Amazon as the most plausible path to justify the price.

## Key points

- **The numbers.** Labenz notes the round brings total funding to just under $400M, with investors including Google, Amazon, Nvidia, Intel, AMD, Qualcomm, IBM and Salesforce. On his estimate of roughly $30M in annual revenue, the valuation is about 150 times revenue.
- **Product sprawl.** Labenz describes the stack: the transformers and diffusers libraries, the model hub, Spaces demos (helped by the Gradio acquisition), a $9 pro tier and inference endpoints. He has used both the Expert Acceleration Program and the inference endpoints beta.
- **Community is not a moat by itself.** Torenberg points to Digg users leaving for Reddit almost overnight. Communities become defensible when users store identity and data on the site, as with LinkedIn or GitHub, and not when the value resets every day, as with Product Hunt or Hacker News.
- **Lead gen, not lock-in.** Labenz argues that open models make it easy to take a workload to AWS or Google Cloud, so the inference product has to win head to head. He also criticises the open LLM leaderboard for leaving out GPT-4, GPT-3.5 and Anthropic models.
- **GitHub for AI.** Labenz suggests the most likely large outcome resembles Microsoft buying GitHub, a defensive purchase of a hold on developers. He contrasts that with Replit's focused execution and with BLOOM, which he says never matched GPT-3.
- **Model companies are more exposed.** Labenz judges Runway and Character harder to defend than Hugging Face, because a better model from a rival such as Adobe Firefly could pull users away at almost no switching cost.
