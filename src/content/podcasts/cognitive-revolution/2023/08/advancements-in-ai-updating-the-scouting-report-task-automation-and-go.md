## Thesis

Nathan Labenz revisits his AI Scouting Report a month after release. His main revisions: humans still beat AI on robustness to strange or adversarial input, while AI wins on consistency. That consistency makes routine, well-defined business tasks the best targets for automation, provided someone states explicitly what good output looks like. He also argues the transformer will not be the final architecture.

## Key points

- **Robustness as a new category.** Following feedback from Zvi Mowshowitz, Labenz will add robustness to his "tale of the cognitive tape". He cites the universal jailbreak paper, where a nonsense suffix optimized on an open model transferred to OpenAI models while Claude was much less affected, and a "backspace" paper co-authored by Div Garg of MultiOn.
- **Consistency over bedside manner.** Human performance varies far more than AI performance, so Labenz will generalize his "bedside manner" item to consistency.
- **Good automation targets.** Labenz suggests repetitive, high-volume work such as screening 1,000 resumes with a five-band rubric, or 500 daily support tickets where 80% fall into 10 categories. Parallel calls, even at Claude's default limit of 5, can process them in minutes.
- **Defining "good" is the hard part.** He estimates task automation is one part AI skill and two parts organizational work, since subject matter experts often disagree on what good looks like. Prompting itself comes down to six to ten best practices; with a ghostwriter, the human now concentrates on the hook while the AI drafts the rest.
- **Live players in tiers.** Zvi's list of 7 or 8 (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Microsoft, Meta, chip supply chain, the Chinese government, regulators) becomes Labenz's tier 1, with Stability, Replit and Character in tier 2.
- **Google and post-transformer signs.** Multimodal Med-PaLM takes in images, patient history and genomic data; clinicians preferred its radiology reports over a human radiologist's 40% of the time. Labenz also points to Meta's MegaByte and Microsoft's retention architecture as possible transformer successors.
