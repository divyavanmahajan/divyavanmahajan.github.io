## Thesis

Nathan Labenz reviews the "live players" in AI with Zvi Mowshowitz of Don't Worry About the Vase. Mowshowitz says he has updated positively on OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind and on government, but sees Meta's open release of Llama 2 as the most dangerous recent move. On safety, he argues the White House commitments are useful but unenforced, and that the real bottleneck is people able to lead serious alignment research.

## Key points

- **Is GPT-4 at undergrad level?** OpenAI's Jan Leike called GPT-4 roughly a well-read college undergrad. Mowshowitz rates it well below human level, because it fails at original, out-of-distribution work. He calls Leike's clarification, that superalignment aims to align a human-level researcher expected within four years rather than build one, a "hugely positive update".
- **Adversarial attacks.** Labenz notes that a universal jailbreak string transferred across Llama, Bard and GPT-4 but worked on Claude 2 only 2% of the time. Mowshowitz credits Anthropic's constitutional AI training but calls this promising, not conclusive, until the attack is optimised against Claude directly.
- **Google's output.** Labenz highlights RT-2 robotics and multimodal Med-PaLM, whose radiology reports were preferred over a radiologist's some 40+% of the time. Mowshowitz says Bard and the Workspace features still fall short and Google needs to ship Gemini, and that healthcare adoption is held back by regulatory, legal and cultural barriers, not capability.
- **Meta and Llama 2.** Mowshowitz reads Meta's strategy as undercutting competitors through open source. He calls open-sourcing frontier models "the worst thing you can do", noting that an unrestricted fine-tune appeared within days. Labenz struggles to see the business case.
- **Who counts as live.** Mowshowitz's short list is OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, maybe Microsoft, China and regulators. He does not see Character AI, Inflection, Replit or xAI shaping the frontier; Labenz thinks companion apps could still change society.
- **Safety funding and policy.** Mowshowitz supports mandatory liability insurance for harms up to a certain level, wants at least three independent red-teaming organisations, and predicts GPT-5 testing will find problems that get patched over. He says grant funding should back principal investigators who assume alignment is hard.
