## Thesis

This bonus episode replays part 1 of a Future of Life Institute Podcast interview, in which host Gus Docker talks with Conjecture CEO Connor Leahy (the published transcript labels Docker's lines as Nathan Labenz). Leahy argues that today's large models are "magic": computation nobody understands, so nobody can bound what they will do. His alternative is cognitive emulation (CoEM): systems that reason and fail in human ways, are capped at human level, and give a causal story for why their outputs can be trusted.

## Key points

- **GPT-4 is about reliability.** Leahy says GPT-4 is on the trend he predicted. Its striking feature is not new abilities but consistency, which he credits to fine-tuning and RLHF as much as size. That same ability to act for users is what he says makes it more dangerous.
- **Plugins and proto-AGI.** He calls "large language model" a misnomer for general cognition engines. He mocks OpenAI's timing: Jan Leike suggested slowing down before hooking models into everything, and six days later ChatGPT plugins launched.
- **The shoggoth mask.** Leahy says RLHF is a smiley-face mask on an alien base model. Models are not human just because they train on human text, and outside the tested distribution they fail in non-human ways.
- **Black boxes, white boxes and bounds.** He compares safe AGI design to designing a secure data center: explicit assumptions, bounded black-box calls, and as much cognition as possible moved into understandable white-box components.
- **1,000 parallel humans, not one superhuman.** A good CoEM system should make a user work like 1,000 bounded "1x AGIs", like a CEO running a company. Leahy sees serial reasoning depth and self-improvement as the main danger, more than speed.
- **Policy and outlook.** Leahy now spends much of his time with journalists and politicians to "create the demand for safety". He describes a race led by a small group of techno-optimists, and says he does not expect humanity to make it through this century by default.

## Quotes

> "It should make you parallelizable, not serially intelligent, because if you're 1000x intelligent, who knows? That is dangerous." — Connor Leahy
