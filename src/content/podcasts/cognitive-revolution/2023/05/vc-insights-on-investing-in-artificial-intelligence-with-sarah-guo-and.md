## Thesis

Investors Sarah Guo (Conviction) and Elad Gil, co-hosts of No Priors, argue that generative AI is the first ML wave where startups, not only incumbents, can build very large companies. Both say investors should follow real usage and outlier demand, even when products look weird or not yet defensible, rather than projecting today's view of where value sits.

## Key points

- **Software 3.0.** Guo describes her $100 million Conviction fund as a bet that AI opens categories that were never big software markets, such as copywriting, illustration and law. She says a $1-10 billion company can now be built by about 20 people.
- **Startups versus incumbents.** Gil says that for about a decade ML mostly helped incumbents (Facebook News Feed, Alexa). After GPT-3 he could not persuade anyone to build an OpenAI competitor, so he backed applications such as Character, Perplexity and Harvey.
- **Overlooked areas.** Gil lists voice, social, big B2B applications and infrastructure. Guo adds tool use, which she sees as RPA in the style of UiPath done flexibly in natural language, plus retrieval, memory and media creation. She points to Character and Replika, where users spend hours a day.
- **Interfaces will fragment.** Guo says prompting is hard for 99.99% of people. She expects prompt templates and passive context, and thinks search will break into agent-driven tasks such as travel planning. Gil frames interface choice by how busy the user is and the context of the task.
- **Usage over defensibility.** Gil sees endless closed waitlists as a warning sign and says early defensibility questions are overdone. He advises bootstrapping viral apps, noting some Stable Diffusion apps reached $200 million in revenue in nine months and then faded. Guo says only a tiny share show sustained usage, which is normal for venture.
- **Hopes and fears.** Guo worries about regulatory capture and says alignment research is tied to capability research. Gil is optimistic for health and education over five to ten years, calls himself a long-run "doomer", and warns that the next US election could trigger a regulatory backlash against AI.

## Quotes

> "I tried to find people to build an OpenAI competitor, and I couldn't convince anybody to do it." — Elad Gil

> "it's very easy to feel smart by being cynical and dismissive in venture and in investing in general. And this is totally useless." — Sarah Guo
