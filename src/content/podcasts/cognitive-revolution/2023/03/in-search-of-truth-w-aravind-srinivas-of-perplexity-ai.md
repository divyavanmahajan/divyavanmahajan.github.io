## Thesis

Aravind Srinivas, founder and CEO of Perplexity AI, tells Nathan Labenz that Perplexity aims to be the world's most trusted information service by only saying what it can cite, a habit he takes from academic paper writing. He argues that differentiation comes from product, user experience, speed and search ranking rather than from spending most of a funding round on training a model that OpenAI will soon surpass.

## Key points

- **Citations first.** Srinivas says Perplexity Ask launched about a week after ChatGPT, combining search results with GPT-3.5 to produce short, cited answers that users can verify, and that the goal is maximum value per unit of time, not time on site.
- **Different from Bing.** He describes Bing's Sydney as a general chatbot plus search, slower for search tasks, while Perplexity wants users to get an answer and leave. Source editing lets users remove outlets they distrust, and permalinks make answers shareable.
- **Free and no sign-up.** Because the alternative is google.com, Srinivas says requiring accounts would be a mistake; he cites a tweet liked by Mike Krieger arguing sign-ups held ChatGPT back.
- **Feedback and speed.** Only about 10% of users give feedback, he says, so product use itself must generate signal, as with Google and TikTok. Streaming answers changed perceived latency, and his team pushed a streaming fix to NVIDIA's Triton.
- **Business and competition.** There is no revenue model yet; ads must not lower answer quality. He expects search margins to shrink as LLMs reduce the need for a world-best index, and calls cutting off search APIs anti-competitive.
- **AGI views.** Srinivas leans towards weak AGI in under five years, maybe two to three, sides with OpenAI's practical, economic view over benchmark tests, and worries about short-term income inequality.
