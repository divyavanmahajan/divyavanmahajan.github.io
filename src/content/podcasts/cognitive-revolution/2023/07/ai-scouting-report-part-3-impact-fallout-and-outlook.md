## Thesis

In the last part of his AI Scouting Report, Nathan Labenz tells Erik Torenberg that current models already perform at or above average human level on many tasks and are approaching expert level. He thinks the economic and social fallout is only starting. His position: deploy AI "servants" widely now, but be very careful about scaling general pre-training further, because nobody knows what the next jumps in scale would produce.

## Key points

- **Applications.** Labenz cites GPT-4 beating human LeetCode users on hard problems when given five attempts, Tanishq's AI staining of tissue images during surgery, fMRI image reconstruction trained on about 1,000 images, Google's PaLM-E robot, and his own 8 hours in a Tesla running FSD, which he finds plausibly safer than a human driver.
- **Moats.** He argues OpenAI and Google, with DeepMind and the Anthropic partnership, have real moats: safety work, product feedback loops, pricing power, privileged compute, talent and distribution. He quotes Emad Mostaque saying open source will always lag closed models.
- **Falling prices.** In his account OpenAI's prices fell about 98% in a year, a GPT-3-class model can be trained with MosaicML for under $500,000, and Databricks bought MosaicML for over $1 billion.
- **Jobs and data.** He cites an OpenAI and University of Pennsylvania paper showing the most qualified job bands are the most exposed. He also points to the writers' strike, and to sites like Reddit and Stack Overflow restricting access to their data.
- **Regulation and chips.** Labenz describes a spectrum: Europe regulating, China drafting strict standards, the US mostly laissez-faire, and Japan permissive. In his view, US export controls on chips raise the risk of conflict over Taiwan.
- **Safety.** He calls OpenAI's alignment plan thin, says some of his GPT-4 red-team prompts still work, and notes that 48% of surveyed researchers gave a 10% or higher chance of an extremely bad outcome.
