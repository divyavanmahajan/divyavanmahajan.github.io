## Thesis

This four-minute audio release is a teaser. The full Part 2 of the AI Scouting Report is on YouTube. Part 2 is meant to be practical: now that frontier language models like GPT-4 exist, what can people do with them, and how can their weaknesses be worked around? The show notes say Nathan Labenz wants to give viewers the equivalent of a high school AP course in about 90 minutes.

## Key points

- **Recap of Part 1.** The speaker (labelled Erik Torenberg in the published transcript) argues that, with web-scale data and compute available, a working algorithm was bound to appear, and that the transformer was the first one to generalise.
- **Grokking debate.** The recap says it is still unclear whether models like GPT-4 have memorised statistical correlations or have actually grokked concepts. It notes that new grokking results have appeared since Part 1 was published.
- **Models and fine-tuning.** According to the show notes' timestamps, the video covers custom hardware, startups versus incumbents in foundation models, the history of fine-tuning, mode collapse, and the idea that better data produces better models.
- **Chat products.** The notes list sections on chat assistants, Claude 2's advantages, Character AI, Replika's romance app and an AI girlfriend story posted on LessWrong.
- **Implementation trends.** The listed topics include chain-of-thought prompting, memory and retrieval with embeddings and vector stores, hallucinations, models that teach themselves to call APIs, Perplexity AI for search, AI agents, and incumbents adding AI compared with AI-native companies.
- **Efficiency and multimodality.** The video ends with LLMs as planners and lifelong learners (including in Minecraft), model-to-model communication through embeddings, multimodal inputs and outputs, Gato, quantisation, distillation and mixture of experts.
