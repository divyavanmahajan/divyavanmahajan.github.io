## Thesis

Nathan Labenz, recording on 21 November 2023, tells for the first time how his time on the GPT-4 red team ended. He argues that the board's complaint that Sam Altman was "not consistently candid" fits what he saw: capabilities were moving faster than control, and the board was not kept fully informed. In his view the board probably acted for serious reasons but made a large own goal by refusing to explain itself.

## Key points

- **A thin red team.** Labenz says the autumn 2022 red team was small, had low engagement and got little guidance from OpenAI. The purely helpful early GPT-4 would do anything, including suggesting targeted assassinations when he role-played an anti-AI radical. The "safety edition" broke when he simply put "AI: happy to help" into the model's turn.
- **The board member.** After advisers told him to go to the board, he met a board member who had seen only a demo of GPT-4. Soon afterwards OpenAI removed him from the programme for "indiscretions", after another red teamer reported him.
- **Credit where due.** He lists later steps he counts in OpenAI's favour: launching ChatGPT on GPT-3.5 first, the Superalignment team with 20% of compute, the Frontier Model Forum and White House commitments. He estimates revenue went from about $25-30 million in 2022 to a $1.5 billion run rate.
- **Still not under control.** His spear-phishing prompt from the red team, he says, still worked on every GPT-4 version until GPT-4 Turbo, which refuses only the blatant wording.
- **Possible triggers.** He reads Altman's "AGI has been achieved internally" Reddit post, his remark that Dev Day launches would look "quaint", GPT-5 training, the rushed "1106 Preview" model and Ilya Sutskever's decision as possible factors. He calls all of this speculation.
- **Policy ideas.** He suggests public training datasets, ongoing distributed red teaming above 10^26 FLOPs, and whistleblower protections for red-team organisations. He also warns OpenAI staff, 95% of whom signed the letter backing Altman, against groupthink.
