## Thesis

In the show's introductory episode, Erik Torenberg interviews co-host Nathan Labenz about why they started it. Labenz argues that AI is "the real deal": a new, alien form of intelligence whose impact is closer to the industrial revolution than to mobile phones. He thinks even with no new breakthroughs, productizing and integrating existing techniques will change most of life. He also holds that people who worry about catastrophe are justified.

## Key points

- **From Waymark to AI.** Labenz describes Waymark, the DIY video company he founded, and how GPT-3 offered a way past users' real blocker: not knowing what to say. He paused the company for six months to focus on AI ("AI beats UI") and later handed the CEO role to his COO.
- **No new breakthroughs needed.** He points to transformers (2017), reinforcement learning and cloud-scale compute, and lists likely near-term uses: AlphaFold-driven biology, Meta's text-to-4D scenes, AI-arbitrated smart contracts, household robots, personal agents like DoNotPay, and DeepMind's fusion control work.
- **What naysayers miss.** Labenz says critics judge snapshots (GPT-2, GPT-3, ChatGPT) as end states and hold AI to a human standard. He compares raw language models to "the iPhone before there were any apps", with work processes still to be reorganised.
- **Why the safety worry is justified.** He uses an invasive-species analogy and argues that feedback training can reward deception, because humans are predictably exploitable. He frames a race between scaling and mechanistic interpretability and asks labs for more transparency.
- **Upside and downside.** Citing Sam Altman, he sees a base case of universal access to expertise, such as an AI doctor for 8 billion people, and a downside running from fragile dependence (like the electrical grid) to loss of control. On Peter Thiel's critique that the field lost its faith in technology, he says the people doing the work do not fit it.
- **Advice for newcomers.** Get hands-on with tools, follow AI Twitter, read papers for the concepts without worrying about the linear algebra, and watch 3Blue1Brown's neural network videos.
