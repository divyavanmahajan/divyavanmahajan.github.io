## Thesis

Suhail Doshi, founder of Mixpanel and Mighty, explains why he pivoted to Playground AI. He sees prompts as a temporary workaround and wants a pro creative tool that can create, edit and understand pixels. He argues that durable AI companies go from product down to foundation model, using a generous free tier to build a data flywheel.

## Key points

- **The pivot.** Doshi says experiments with a smarter address bar and GPT-3 summaries in the Mighty browser gave him "the bug". Watching OpenAI's Playground UI get more complex, and getting early Stable Diffusion weights from Emad, convinced him images also needed rich controls rather than a text box.
- **Roadmap.** The mid-term goal is a Figma-level, collaborative editor for people without Photoshop skills; the long-term goal is a "large language image model" covering image, video and possibly 3D. He also wants models that invent typography.
- **AI art is art.** Doshi says he got death threats after calling AI art art. He concluded that traditional artists feel deeply threatened, and compares AI to drum machines and DAWs, which led to hip hop and Skrillex.
- **Shipping speed.** When the InstructPix2Pix weights dropped on a Thursday, the team worked through Sunday and launched an edit feature on Monday. Owning a couple of million dollars of hardware from Mighty lets Playground control cost and latency.
- **Free tier and data.** Playground gives 1,000 free images a day, set at the 90th percentile of unlimited use. Doshi argues usage-based pricing does not track value, prefers qualitative user talks to A/B tests, and says fixing a slow API endpoint doubled the number of images generated.
- **Defensibility.** He expects public data to run out and favours proprietary data, real research and network effects. He says some foundation-model companies offer "APIs in search of a problem" and is less sure than before that models will become commodities.
