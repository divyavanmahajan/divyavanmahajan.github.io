## Thesis

Dean W. Ball, who favours light-touch self-governance, and former OpenAI researcher Daniel Kokotajlo, who wants stronger regulation, agree that transparency is the one thing worth mandating now. Their Time op-ed proposes four requirements for frontier labs. Both argue that a better-informed public and government can adapt as AGI approaches, which they expect within a few years.

## Key points

- **Kokotajlo's exit.** He spent about two years on OpenAI's policy research team and left after losing hope the company would act responsibly. He refused to sign the exit agreement, risking millions in vested equity; OpenAI later dropped the non-disparagement terms.
- **Forecasting record.** Ball says Kokotajlo's 2021 essay "What 2026 looks like" described life in 2024 accurately in many details. Kokotajlo puts AGI around 2027 and expects automated AI R&D to speed progress about tenfold or more.
- **The four proposals.** Ball lists: disclose when capability thresholds (such as Anthropic's ASL-3) are reached and publish a scaling policy; publish model specs; publish safety cases; and protect whistleblowers, which he calls essential for enforcement.
- **Safety cases.** Kokotajlo says a safety case for GPT-4 could be one paragraph, but should grow to many pages of experiments as models could plausibly be faking alignment. He notes the o1 system card reported hallucinated links about 1% of the time.
- **No plan.** Kokotajlo says the closest thing OpenAI has to a technical alignment plan is a two-page appendix in the weak-to-strong generalization paper, which most people he spoke to expect will not work.
- **Disagreements.** Ball defends OpenAI's record, compares the doom narrative to predictions about Apple, and prefers private governance modelled on insurance. Kokotajlo wants government review, including an anonymous hotline to a body such as the AI Safety Institute.
