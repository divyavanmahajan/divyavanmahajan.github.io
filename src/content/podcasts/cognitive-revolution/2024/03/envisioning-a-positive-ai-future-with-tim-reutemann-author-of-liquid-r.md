## Thesis

Nathan Labenz talks with Tim Reutemann, a climate-collaboration economist whose 2018 novel Liquid Reign imagines a society where AI assistants, cryptographic identity, VR and liquid democracy make political participation almost effortless. Reutemann argues that such a world only stays humane if identity and data are controlled locally and people can always fall back on talking to their neighbors. He admits he is less optimistic now than when he wrote the book.

## Key points

- **Delegated votes.** Reutemann describes a vote as a pool of energy split by topic: he would hand his psychiatry vote to a nurse friend, who could pass it on to her professor, or give his food-price vote to an AI and withdraw it at any time. Some forum rooms require 100 delegations, a "troll wall".
- **Identity at the community level.** One human body, one vote, tied to an implanted private key and a hash of seven biometric markers. He criticizes Worldcoin for rooting identity in an international body rather than the local commune.
- **Swiss and Taiwanese models.** Swiss initiatives need 100,000 signatures within a year, but turnout is about 50% and the rooms are dominated by the same people. Taiwan's deliberation tools under Audrey Tang, which surface agreement, are his model for scaling that up.
- **Interfaces and security.** He rejects Neuralink-style implants as too invasive, preferring transcranial magnetic stimulation and an in-ear assistant, and predicts VR breaks through as fitness equipment, citing Beat Saber and Skyrim VR. Against backdoors, the final override is physical: roll back and reboot.
- **Power and privacy.** The novel's revolution starts when a Walmart heir buys all of Wyoming; the answer is a Georgist-style tax rising to 100% above roughly 50,000,000 per person. Surveillance footage would need a warrant to decrypt plus brute-forcing the last 10%, while public servants should accept full logging.
- **Sequel.** A prequel-style sequel, Liquid Rising, is being written natively in HTML.
