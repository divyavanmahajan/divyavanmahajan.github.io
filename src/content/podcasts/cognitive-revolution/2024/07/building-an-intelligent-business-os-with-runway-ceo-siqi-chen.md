## Thesis

Siqi Chen, founder and CEO of the finance platform Runway, says the hard part of AI in finance is not the model but the substrate. Spreadsheets cannot hold business intent such as roadmaps or hiring plans, so Runway built abstractions that can. On top of that, AI works best when it explains things quietly in the interface rather than through a chatbot. He admits the flashier features, such as generating whole scenarios, are not yet reliable.

## Key points

- **Origin.** As CEO of Sandbox VR when COVID hit, Chen planned scenarios in spreadsheets and laid off most of a 400-plus person company. Naval Ravikant, the first investor, named Runway and talked him out of "cfo.ai" because every product would soon be AI.
- **LLM plus calculator.** For a prompt like "3x revenue in 12 months", the LLM gets the model's dependency tree, guesses which drivers to change and when, and Runway's engine does the maths. It loops until the result fits. AI also pre-writes descriptions of how each metric is calculated to give the model context.
- **Ambient intelligence.** Explain mode shows a pre-generated explanation when a user holds Control and hovers over a driver, report or model pull request. Chen says this is mostly a design problem solved by tracing the DOM, and compares it with Apple's approach.
- **Context through integrations.** Runway connects to about 680 data sources, including Jira, so work can be tied to financial outcomes and budget-versus-actual gaps can be traced automatically.
- **Internal use.** An AI SDR enriches sign-ups via Clearbit, qualifies them and leaves drafted emails in Gmail for sales staff. A custom GPT turns Grain interview transcripts into notes. The company aims never to exceed 100 employees.
- **Big picture.** Chen sees self-reflection as the threshold for AGI, is sceptical of compute regulation, prefers mechanistic interpretability, and expects things provably made by humans to keep their value.
