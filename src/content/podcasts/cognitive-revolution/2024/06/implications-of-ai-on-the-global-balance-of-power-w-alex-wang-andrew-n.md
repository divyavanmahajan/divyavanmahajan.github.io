## Thesis

In a Hill & Valley Forum panel moderated by Senator Cory Booker, Scale AI's Alex Wang, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark and AI Fund's Andrew Ng largely agree: regulate applications rather than the underlying technology, push government and defence to adopt AI faster, and do not assume China will fall behind. In his introduction, host Nathan Labenz says he shares the view on China but is uneasy that so much of the analysis is framed as US-versus-them rivalry.

## Key points

- **Regulate applications, not models.** Ng compares regulating foundation models to asking solar panel makers to guarantee their electricity is safe for every appliance; he wants rules for self-driving cars, underwriting software and medical devices instead. Clark backs product-style safety testing and a shared global framework.
- **Multi-use, not dual-use.** Ng argues AI is a general-purpose technology like electricity, with far more civilian than military uses. Clark says the one area needing new tests is national security misuse; otherwise existing bodies like the FDA and FAA can cope.
- **Defence adoption is too slow.** Wang says his biggest fear is that the DOD and intelligence community use AI too little, because process and bureaucracy keep them behind China and Russia.
- **Don't underestimate China.** Wang strongly disagrees with a RAND suggestion that China won't produce major advances, pointing to Chinese open-source models and a "triple whammy" of fast technology, rising conflict and ageing leaders like Xi Jinping and Putin. Clark notes China's work on distributed training in response to export controls, and Ng says Tsinghua University produces about as much agentic-AI research as American universities.
- **Infrastructure as leverage.** Clark calls for using the US supercomputing base to give startups and academics compute, and jokes that the path to AGI runs through US permit reform for data centres and power. Wang expects the Middle East to build huge data centres regardless.
- **2055 vision.** Clark expects personalised tutors for everyone; Ng says AI is making intelligence cheap, so anyone could hire "an army of specialists".
