## Thesis

Nathan Labenz replays a talk he gave to retail investors, mostly doctors and lawyers. His argument: since 2022 the best AI systems have beaten the average human at the average task, and they are getting close to expert level on routine work. They pick up concepts nobody explicitly taught them, so their specific abilities are hard to predict. Labenz says people should use today's systems everywhere they can, but society would be wise to slow the push for 10x and 100x more scale.

## Key points

- **Medicine is the showcase.** Labenz cites Google's MedPalm 2 scoring 86 percent on a US medical licensing exam and AI beating primary care physicians at differential diagnosis. He also mentions Hippocratic AI's nurses, billed at $9 an hour for follow-up calls rather than diagnosis.
- **Where it stops.** General-purpose models still struggle with novel problems, so Labenz now says "precious few eureka moments". One exception he cites: GPT-4 wrote better reward functions for robot reinforcement learning than human experts did.
- **How learning works.** He explains loss functions, backpropagation and gradient descent. He calls the move from small labelled datasets to web-scale unsupervised learning (next-token prediction, image denoising) the key change.
- **Emergence and grokking.** A model trained only to predict Amazon reviews ended up with a sentiment neuron. Othello models build an internal picture of the board. Modular-addition models generalise only after very long training. Labenz takes from this that specific capabilities "remain hard to predict".
- **Not investment advice.** Labenz argues the big four in compute (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta) have data, compute and algorithms all at once. He says chipmakers, above all NVIDIA, look like the safest bet. He lists OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral and Character AI as live players.
- **Risk.** He points to a survey in which 48% of AI researchers put at least a 10% chance on human extinction from AI. He notes that the labs are racing ahead without a real plan for control.
