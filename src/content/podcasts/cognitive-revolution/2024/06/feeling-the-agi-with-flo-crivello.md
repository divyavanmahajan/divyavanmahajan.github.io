## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and Lindy CEO Flo Crivello discuss Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness essay. Crivello argues AGI is about five years away, superintelligence will follow quickly, and anyone who understands this should be alarmed. Labenz finds no major hole in the argument but fears a US-China arms race and would rather try cooperating with China. Both are libertarians who now reluctantly support regulation.

## Key points

- **No wall.** Crivello cites Gemini 1.5 Flash, which he says matches GPT-4 at about a hundredth of the cost, and context windows that grew from 4,000 to 1,000,000 tokens. Labenz says Flash summarised about 250,000 tokens of his emails in 45 seconds for under 20 cents.
- **Two updates from the essay.** Crivello now thinks that if AGI does not arrive by about 2030, it may not arrive by 2040, because the one-time gains from data, money and unhobbling will be used up. He also expects takeoff to take 1-4 years, as AGI automates AI research.
- **Misuse before rogue AI.** Citing Dario Amodei's "invisible protection", Crivello warns AI will arm people who mean harm, especially in cyber. He describes hackers repeatedly taking down the grid and banks, and says Bank of America and PG&E will adopt AI years after attackers do.
- **Race dynamics.** Crivello says Google, Anthropic and OpenAI would be in an arms race even without China. He adds that $10 trillion to automate knowledge work is cheap.
- **What to do.** Crivello wants to stop open-sourcing frontier models, starting with Meta. He wants a Manhattan Project for alignment, with a dollar and a flop on alignment for each spent on training, and expects serious regulation only after a 9/11-style AI catastrophe. Labenz calls himself an "adoption accelerationist, hyperscaling pauser" and accepts one more model generation. Crivello is comfortable with 2-3 more orders of magnitude, but not 6-10.
- **Builders and labs.** Crivello says Lindy builds model-independent layers such as RAG-based memory, which he says costs about 100 times less than relying on long context. He blames OpenAI's safety departures on hypergrowth chaos like he saw at Uber, and names Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta as the remaining frontier players.
