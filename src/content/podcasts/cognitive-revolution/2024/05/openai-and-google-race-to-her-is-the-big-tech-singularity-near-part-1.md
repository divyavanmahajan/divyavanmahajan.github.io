## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and Zvi Mowshowitz agree that the OpenAI and Google launches were technically impressive but shown through rushed, thin demos. They split on market structure: Labenz worries that returns to scale point toward a "big tech singularity", while Mowshowitz argues that limited bandwidth, culture and regulation at the giants leave plenty of room for smaller companies.

## Key points

- **Demos versus products.** Mowshowitz says GPT-4o is what you can use now (fast, half the API price, multimodal), but the real prize is integration into email, documents and calendars. He reads the weak demos as a sign the capabilities are new and that neither company optimized for the stage.
- **The "Her" question.** Mowshowitz sees AI used as tutor, coach or sounding board as clearly good, but treating it as a real emotional partner as harmful, comparing the risk to how television ate people's time. Labenz cites Replika CEO Eugenia's research on reduced loneliness and her view that relationship may be the moat in AI.
- **Switching costs.** Labenz fears one-line API switching creates a winner-take-all race; Mowshowitz counters that users keep several "AI friends" for different tasks, and that he has moved between GPT-4 Turbo, Gemini, Claude and GPT-4o himself.
- **Big tech singularity.** Labenz raises Andrew Critch's idea, noting Google's $100,000,000,000 in cash could buy Tenet (58 hospitals, about $13,000,000,000 market cap). Mowshowitz doubts Google is culturally or legally able to do that, and prefers an AI private equity model of buying and upgrading ordinary companies.
- **Which startups survive.** Citing Sam Altman, Mowshowitz says companies that patch model weaknesses get flattened, while those built to improve with every new model benefit. He expects little measurable productivity gain in 2024 or 2025.
- **Safety departures.** Mowshowitz counts 8 safety researchers who left OpenAI in 6 months, including Ilya and Jan, and reads it as a hostile internal climate rather than an imminent danger.
