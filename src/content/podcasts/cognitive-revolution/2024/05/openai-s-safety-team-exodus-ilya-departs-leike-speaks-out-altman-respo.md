## Thesis

Recorded the day Jan Leike explained his exit, this bonus episode has Zvi Mowshowitz arguing that until OpenAI shows otherwise, it should be treated as a fully for-profit, move-fast startup run by Sam Altman, with a culture hostile to safety. Nathan Labenz agrees the compute story is troubling and asks what outsiders can do in response.

## Key points

- **Broken compute promise.** Zvi notes Leike's statement that the superalignment team could not get the compute it asked for despite the 20% commitment, which TechCrunch confirmed. He calls 20% of current compute modest anyway, since OpenAI will have 10 times as much compute in 2 years.
- **Non-disparagement clauses.** Kelsey Piper reported lifetime non-disparagement terms, backed by the threat of losing vested equity. Zvi says he is confused why that is legal; Labenz suggests banning such clauses as one way to strengthen the 1047 bill sponsored by Scott Wiener.
- **Schulman takes over safety.** Labenz points to John Schulman's Dwarkesh interview, where he expects AGI in 2 to 3 years without a robust plan. Zvi argues that relying on post-training and RLHF for superintelligence is "a pipe dream", but accepts "we don't have a plan" as an honest second-best starting point.
- **Testing needs trust.** Zvi says the preparedness framework and Anthropic's responsible scaling policy only work if companies honour their spirit. Without that trust you need third-party testing, and he says he no longer has it for OpenAI.
- **An alignment idea to watch.** Zvi highlights a Chinese proposal he calls Sofan: trapping an open model in a local maximum so that fine-tuning cannot teach it a chosen topic such as biology.
- **What "doomer" means.** Zvi calls the word a misused slur. A real doomer believes nothing can be done, he says, while anyone who thinks their choices matter is not one.
