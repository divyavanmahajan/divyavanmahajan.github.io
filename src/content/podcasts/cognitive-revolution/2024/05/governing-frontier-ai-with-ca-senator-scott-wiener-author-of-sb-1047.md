## Thesis

California State Senator Scott Wiener, who represents San Francisco, tells Nathan Labenz that SB 1047 sets minimum safety standards for frontier models without micromanaging developers. He argues that voluntary testing by responsible labs is not enough because not every lab will do it, and that the bill aims to reduce catastrophic risk rather than eliminate it. Labenz presses him on open source, race dynamics and independent third-party testing.

## Key points

- **What the bill requires.** Wiener says models above the Biden executive order threshold of 10^26 flops must undergo a safety evaluation, such as red teaming, before release. If testing shows significant risk of catastrophic harm, such as nuclear, chemical or biological weapons or $500 million in damage to critical infrastructure, developers must take reasonable mitigations.
- **Enforcement.** Wiener confirms Labenz's summary that honest testing and reporting protects developers from criminal liability, and adds that only the California attorney general can sue. Labenz notes fines of 10 to 30% of training cost; Wiener points to punitive damages based on net worth.
- **Not regulatory capture.** Wiener says none of the big tech companies support the bill and that TechNet, their trade association, opposes it. He says the ideas came from technologists and AI safety researchers, and that several startups support it.
- **Open source.** He says he wants to protect open source and is open to ideas such as shifting responsibility to whoever adds, for example, 10% more compute to a derivative model.
- **Third-party testing.** The bill asks for third-party testing "when appropriate". Labenz, drawing on his GPT-4 red team experience, argues for accredited testers who can report findings. Wiener cites his carbon disclosure law, where a state board certifies auditors, as a possible model.
- **Scope.** Wiener says other California bills cover algorithmic discrimination, watermarking and deepfakes. He asks people not to trust what they read about the bill on Twitter.
