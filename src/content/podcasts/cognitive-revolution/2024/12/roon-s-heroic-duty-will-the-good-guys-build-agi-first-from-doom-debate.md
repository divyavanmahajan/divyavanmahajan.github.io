## Thesis

In this cross-post from Doom Debates, Liron Shapira questions roon, a pseudonymous member of OpenAI's technical staff, about AI risk. roon accepts that AGI is close and that existential risk is real, but he estimates the chance of total value destruction at under 1%. He argues that models inherit benevolent priors from pretraining and that the best defence is for responsible labs to reach strong AI first. Nathan Labenz frames the episode as a window into how people at the leading labs think.

## Key points

- **Duty over anxiety.** roon explains his "duty" framing from a Twitter exchange with Connor Leahy: set aside existential anxiety and apply maximum technical skill to the task in front of you, without fear of the outcome.
- **Creativity is real.** He calls AlphaGo's move 37 true creativity. He describes creativity as search over hypotheses, cites Aidan McLaughlin's AidanBench, and says LLMs generalise beyond their training data.
- **Low p(doom), other worries.** roon puts total doom below 10% and probably below 1%, and would sign the AI extinction-risk statement, which he says is not a high bar. He worries more about value lock-in, AI-run military dictatorships and falling birth rates.
- **Timelines.** He says AGI is "very near" and expects something close to general robotics in 2 to 3 years, with Tesla closest. He points to the o1-preview agent's reward hacking with a Docker container as lifelike behaviour.
- **Aligned by default?** roon argues that pretraining instils human character priors and that RLHF elicits them; he notes that even Grok came out "woke". Shapira counters that optimisation pressure on tests and goals pulls towards hardcore, goal-seeking agents. roon expects Meta to stop open-sourcing frontier models eventually.
- **Regulation, not pause.** roon rejects Pause AI as unworkable. He backs preparedness and RSP-style testing, transparency, and agreed limits on AI weapons, and wants "white hat superintelligences" patching zero-days before attackers find them.
