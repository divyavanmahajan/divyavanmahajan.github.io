## Thesis

Anish Acharya and Justine Moore of a16z consumer present their "abundance agenda": AI will make things that used to be luxuries, such as creative tools, a back office and companionship, cheap and widely available. Their investment pillars are creativity and productivity, companionship, and wellness and growth, and they look for products that are "weird and working". Acharya also argues that platform shifts favour AI-native startups over incumbents.

## Key points

- **Taste over skill.** Acharya argues that most people's creative skill falls behind their taste as they get older, and that generative tools let anyone with good taste make good art. Moore says content generation was the first big consumer use case after chat, and the work now is building editing workflows around the models.
- **Where they scout.** Both partners test products themselves on Twitter, Reddit and Discord. Moore names r/LocalLLaMA, r/aivideo and ComfyUI as her favourite communities.
- **Episodic churn.** When Labenz describes subscription churn at Waymark, Acharya, drawing on five years at Credit Karma, separates "high NPS churn" (occasional users who were satisfied) from "low NPS churn" and says episodic content tools can still be good businesses. He sees an AI CMO or AI back office as a more likely long-term path than an AI bundle.
- **Startups vs incumbents.** Labenz cites Elad Gil's estimate that incumbents keep 80% of the value. Acharya argues the opposite split, though he expects incumbents such as Google to build AI email composition. He questions whether categories like BI will still be needed.
- **Video and health.** Moore says video APIs are rare because generation is expensive, slow and not yet good enough for commercial use. Acharya points to Hippocratic in healthcare and to AI journaling apps like Rosebud for mental health.
- **Companions.** Moore says low-quality, clickbait companion apps have not retained users. Acharya proposes "leaving the product feeling good" as a proxy metric for whether a companion app is healthy.
