## Thesis

Azeem Azhar, founder of The Exponential View, argues that the economy is moving from oil, combustion engines and telephones to AI and renewables, and that these information-like technologies keep getting cheaper on exponential curves. He expects fast but uneven adoption: big companies will use AI for efficiency, while startups create the truly new products. Nathan Labenz is more worried than Azhar about losing control of fast, opaque AI systems, though both end up broadly optimistic.

## Key points

- **The 2014 turning point.** Azhar started his newsletter about nine years earlier, after his last company was acquired. He points to 2013-2015: Apple became the world's largest company, Norway passed 5% electric car sales, and solar began winning contracts against fossil fuels.
- **Compute keeps getting cheaper.** He asks how much compute a developer can buy for a dollar each year, and cites Amazon and Google reporting 30% cloud growth as a sign that the money keeps flowing.
- **Tasks, not jobs.** Azhar says AI replaces tasks rather than whole jobs. He notes a 2023 study in which patients rated GPT-4's advice as more empathetic than doctors', and says the "centaur" era in chess lasted only 7 or 8 years.
- **Pincer adoption.** CEOs in their mid-fifties and frontline staff are both pushing for AI; an Oliver Wyman survey of 25,000 employees in 18 countries found 83% in the UAE and India already using chatbots. He still expects startups, not incumbents like Toyota, to drive disruption.
- **Bots talking to bots.** Labenz warns of a "great embedding" in which AIs talk to each other in unreadable vectors. Azhar is calmer: in his view the market economy, coordinated by prices, is already a decentralised system no one fully understands. He sees nearer risks in capable open models running on laptops, botnets and cyberattacks like NotPetya.
- **Checks and balances.** Citing the FAA and Boeing, Azhar argues self-regulation fails. He wants governments, academia and projects like Yoshua Bengio's funded to keep labs in check, comparing this to mandatory car brakes.
