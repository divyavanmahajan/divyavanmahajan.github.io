## Thesis

You.com founder Richard Socher argues that users resisted any departure from Google's ten blue links until ChatGPT showed that "an actual answer" in text is better. He sees search becoming LLM-native and fragmented, with You.com differentiating through its own index and modes that spend more reasoning on harder questions. On risk, he is openly sceptical of existential-risk arguments, while Nathan Labenz keeps a wide range of uncertainty.

## Key points

- **From deep learning to search.** Socher started in NLP in Leipzig in 2003, did his PhD at Stanford when the field relied on hand-engineered features, and pushed the idea of one model for many NLP tasks. You.com had code and essay apps in search results by early 2022, but users pulled it back to the Google layout.
- **An index built for LLMs.** Socher says Bing is expensive and Google hard to access legally, so You.com built its own index with long snippets an LLM can read. He claims its API benchmarked more accurate than Google or Bing, while admitting Google covers the long tail better.
- **Modes as thinking time.** Smart mode is like asking an assistant for a 2-3 minute search; Genius mode orchestrates several LLMs with code execution; Research mode is like giving the assistant days. In 500 real queries, users preferred You.com's answer 50% of the time against 30% for Perplexity.
- **Business models.** Socher expects chat ads to arrive because VC money runs out, says Microsoft raised search API prices 5 to 20x, and plans to lift the $10 monthly subscription. He compares the future market to fast food, with Amazon, TikTok and Reddit already taking searches from Google.
- **Risk and regulation.** Socher calls P(doom) a posterior that no data supports, blames Terminator-style narratives for slowing EU legislation, and prioritises intentional misuse over loss of control. Labenz puts his own estimate at "5 to 95 percent".
- **AGI as automation.** Socher accepts a pragmatic definition, 80% of digitised jobs automated to 80% effectiveness, within a few years, but says superintelligence requires setting its own goals, which no one is working on.
