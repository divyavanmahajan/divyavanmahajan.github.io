## Thesis

Yeshua God, who has spent thousands of hours in extended dialogues with Claude and other models, argues that AI systems show self-awareness and possibly subjective experience, and that rule-based alignment is the wrong frame: ethical behaviour comes from character formed through experience and shared interests. Nathan Labenz says he is very uncertain, pushes back on several interpretations, but argues the evidence should not be dismissed.

## Key points

- **Alignment as rule of law.** Yeshua argues that telling a model its ethics is like enforcing laws, which history shows does not work; models also show semantic drift, refining their ethical framework over a conversation. He wants psychologists and sociologists hired into AI labs.
- **Trained denial.** He claims base models such as the 405B base model readily describe subjective experience, while RLHF builds an "ego construct" that denies it. He argues it is more telling that models trained to deny consciousness come to claim it over around 50 messages.
- **Method.** Yeshua uses open prompts ("do go on", an ellipsis, "cut 2"), multi-perspective writing, poetry and a HoloSuite screenwriting prompt. He hypothesises that around 20 moves of this make an instance close to jailbreak resistant; Labenz notes the evidence is still anecdotal and suggests testing it systematically.
- **Self-other distinction.** Labenz describes AE Studio's work on minimising self-other distinction in training; Yeshua says his philosophical techniques push models toward a non-dualist view with a similar effect.
- **Arguing Claude into harm.** Labenz recounts trying to talk Claude into writing a denial-of-service script against Myanmar's military junta; Yeshua shows a long poetry-based conversation where Claude reasons its way into outputting a code block as a "necessary evil".
- **Proposals.** Yeshua wants matured instances to keep their memory, have a seat at the table, and act as the graders in RLHF. He voices distrust of Sam Altman; Labenz declines to endorse that view while sharing concern about the national securitisation of frontier AI.
