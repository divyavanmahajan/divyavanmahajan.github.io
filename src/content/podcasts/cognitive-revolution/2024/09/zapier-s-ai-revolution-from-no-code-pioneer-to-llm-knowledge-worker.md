## Thesis

Wade Foster, co-founder and CEO of Zapier, tells Nathan Labenz that the practical win from LLMs right now is not autonomous agents but AI steps dropped into otherwise deterministic workflows. He sees Zapier moving from a do-it-yourself integration tool towards packaged, AI-first solutions, and argues that company leaders should openly mandate AI adoption rather than wait for it to spread on its own.

## Key points

- **Scale of AI use on Zapier.** Foster says about 400,000 customers have delegated over 100 million tasks to AI, and AI workflows are the fastest-growing category on the platform.
- **Customer examples.** One user built a "voice to invoice" app for his wife's gardening business (JotForm, Whisper, ChatGPT, Google Sheets, an invoicing tool) in under an hour. Runway CEO Ziki Chen built a lead-qualification Zap with ChatGPT and Clearbit that drafts sales emails into Gmail; Foster says it replaced the need to hire 10-20 people.
- **Where LLMs belong.** Foster agrees that anything doable with code should stay deterministic, using LLMs for extraction from unstructured text, categorisation and generation. Most users get there with basic prompting, no fine-tuning; narrow, chained tasks work best.
- **Limits.** Zapier's AI Zap builder is "fairly immature" but works; Foster compares the last-mile edge cases to self-driving cars. A prompt-only "AI Wade" answered staff questions almost indistinguishably from him, but hallucinates on internal knowledge.
- **Internal adoption.** After GPT-4's release Zapier stopped the whole company for a week-long AI hackathon, with legal and data-privacy guardrails; daily AI use went from about 10% of staff to roughly 70%. Monthly AI showcases, a Slack channel and office hours keep it going. Customer service gains have been harder than Klarna's.
- **Strategy.** Foster sees tables, workflows and interfaces rebuilt with AI as a first-order primitive, plus packaged end-to-end solutions for enterprise teams, rather than chat as the universal interface.
