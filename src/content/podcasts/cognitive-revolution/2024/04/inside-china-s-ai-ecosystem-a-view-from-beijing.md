## Thesis

L-squared, an anonymous tech worker in Beijing, and Nathan Labenz conclude that the Chinese and US AI ecosystems are closer to mirror images than opposites: big tech funds startups and owns the compute, and Chinese models trail GPT-4 only modestly. The main differences she highlights are political content controls, a lack of a public jailbreaking culture, and a chip shortage whose full effect has not yet arrived.

## Key points

- **Many players, no front runner.** L-squared says Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and ByteDance all have models; Alibaba has invested in at least 5 LLM startups, and Tsinghua spin-offs such as Moonshot AI and Zhipu AI compete alongside them. Some in China call this crowding wasteful given hardware limits.
- **Adoption and quality.** ByteDance's chatbot led Chinese GenAI apps with about 17 million monthly users, against roughly 27 million for ChatGPT in the US. Baidu's Ernie tops the third-party SuperCLUE benchmark among Chinese models, and Labenz notes Alibaba's Qwen 1.5 72B sits near the original GPT-4 on the LMSYS leaderboard.
- **Rules on paper vs. in practice.** Providers must register and pass safety tests on training data and outputs, and a new standard bars data from blocked sites such as Hugging Face and Wikipedia. L-squared expects the government to turn a blind eye, noting only about 40 registrations despite many apps.
- **Censorship without a big performance cost.** Open models on Hugging Face (such as 01.AI's Yi-34B) answer more freely than China-facing products, which add keyword filters. She says users avoid posting jailbreak screenshots out of caution.
- **Export controls lag.** Chip stockpiles and overseas cloud access have cushioned Chinese labs so far; she expects the impact to show in 2-3 years and sees Huawei, working with SMIC, as the main domestic hope.
- **Safety and dialogue.** She notes BAAI's safety conference and Chinese attendance at the UK AI Safety Summit, but less alignment research and no responsible scaling policies, and urges Western audiences to read nuanced sources such as Jeff Ding and Matt Sheehan.
