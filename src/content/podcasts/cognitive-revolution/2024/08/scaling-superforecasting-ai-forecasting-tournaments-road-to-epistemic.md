## Thesis

Nathan Labenz talks with Deger Turan, CEO of Metaculus, about AI-powered forecasting and the new Metaculus bot tournament. Turan argues that crowd forecasting already works and LLM bots are getting close to good human forecasters, but accuracy is not the bottleneck. The harder problem is making forecasts useful to decision makers, which he wants to solve with goal-focused question sets, reasoning transparency and shared world models.

## Key points

- **Not a prediction market.** Turan describes Metaculus as a forecasting platform and aggregation engine, "Wikipedia about the future", with no monetary stakes. The community prediction weights forecasts by recency, and good forecasters can be hired as paid pro forecasters.
- **Track record.** He cites a January 2020 forecast that more than 100,000 people would be infected with COVID, and a user who moved from Kyiv to Lviv on February 13 because of the Ukraine invasion forecasts.
- **What the research shows.** Labenz summarises the Halawi and Steinhardt retrieval system, which came close to the community forecast, and two Tetlock papers, including "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd" on ensembling LLMs.
- **The AI Forecasting Benchmark Series.** A year-long, quarterly tournament with about 250 to 400 binary questions this quarter and around $120,000 in prizes. No human in the loop (on an honour system), bots must post their reasoning, and OpenAI and Anthropic donated credits.
- **Bots lack consistency.** On a measles question, bots gave 75% for under 200 cases, 70% for over 300 and 65% for the range in between. Turan wants model-based reasoning, for example a Squiggle integration, to enforce coherence.
- **Beyond consensus.** Turan plans to open-source Metaculus and launch goal-focused "Minitaculuses". He warns against a "consensus illusion" in which everyone backs more bike lanes but nobody agrees on a street.

## Quotes
