## Thesis

Nathan Labenz revisits California's SB 1047 with cosponsor Nathan Calvin (Center for AI Safety Action Fund), critic Dean W. Ball (Mercatus Center) and Steve Newman. Everyone agrees the amended bill is much improved and has become mainly a transparency bill. Calvin argues it is a cheap hedge against serious risks, while Ball argues there is not yet enough evidence that frontier AI is different enough to justify model-level regulation by a state. Labenz says he would sign it.

## Key points

- **What changed.** Calvin explains that the Frontier Model Division is gone and some of its functions moved to the Government Operations Agency. "Reasonable assurance" became "reasonable care", perjury penalties and cloud pricing rules were dropped, and third-party audits start in 2026. Thresholds are $100,000,000 for training and $10,000,000 for fine-tuning.
- **Labenz's view.** He reads Anthropic's letter as a tacit endorsement and dismisses OpenAI's opposition. His remaining criticism is that the $10,000,000 fine-tuning threshold is too high.
- **Ball's objections.** GovOps is a back-office agency, like the federal GSA, and its non-binding "soft law" guidance can be arbitrary. He also argues that Sacramento should not set technology rules for the whole country, and that model-based regulation is a radical break from how software is governed.
- **Calvin's case.** Researchers such as Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio take catastrophic risk seriously, voluntary commitments weaken under commercial pressure, and Congress is slow. He warns that doing nothing may lead to a Patriot Act-style reaction later.
- **Liability and prosecutors.** Ball fears an ambitious prosecutor and a single judge setting a national standard. Calvin replies that software liability waivers do not bind harmed third parties, and he points to the Purdue Pharma lawsuits.
- **Predictions.** Ball expects the legislature to pass the bill and Governor Newsom to veto it. Newman warns about COVID-style policy fatigue and says only constructive debate leads to good policy.
