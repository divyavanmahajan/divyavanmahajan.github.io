## Thesis

Suhail Doshi, founder and CEO of Playground AI, tells Nathan Labenz that despite photorealistic output, image models remain closer to GPT-2 than GPT-3 in general utility: they make art, remove objects and backgrounds, and little else. He argues that a unified vision model that creates, edits and understands pixels will replace today's patchwork of fine-tunes and control tools, and that multimodal language models will speed that up.

## Key points

- **Disappointing progress.** Despite DALL-E 3, Midjourney v6 and SDXL, Doshi says utility has barely grown. Labenz notes that Playground v2, built on the SDXL architecture and open-sourced, is preferred to SDXL about 70% of the time.
- **Three pieces.** Doshi frames graphics as creating, editing and understanding pixels. Playground's 2024 focus is multitask editing of real photos, from local edits (making a child smile, aging a face) to global ones (turning a winter scene into summer).
- **No fix-hands buttons.** Doshi says Playground shipped about 100 things last year and reads every user complaint, but rejects stacking specialist models for hands, eyes and character consistency because it complicates the product and raises inference time.
- **Data and multimodality.** Doshi argues synthetic data suits vision, citing InstructPix2Pix, and that better captioners such as GPT-4V and LLaVA fix the poorly annotated LAION dataset, as the DALL-E 3 team showed by recaptioning. He notes GPT-4V is weak at segmentation compared with DINO plus Segment Anything.
- **Artists and rules.** Doshi says Playground links back to Greg Rutkowski when prompts use his name, favours rules on commercial use over training data, and doubts per-use revenue sharing, citing streaming payouts in music.
- **Safety.** Playground trained its own safety filter to block nude and illegal content. Doshi wants companies to build open safety models together, pointing to OpenAI's free moderation model.
