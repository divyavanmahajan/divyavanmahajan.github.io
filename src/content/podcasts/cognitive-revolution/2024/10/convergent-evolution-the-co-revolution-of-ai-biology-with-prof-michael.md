## Thesis

Starting from their July paper on universal multilayer network embedding, Tufts researchers Michael Levin and Leo Pio-Lopez discuss with Nathan Labenz what machine learning can and cannot do for biology. Pio-Lopez presents a data-driven method that unifies separate biological databases into one network; Levin argues the bigger prize is using AI as a translator to living systems he sees as goal-directed at every scale, and warns that collective intelligences, biological or artificial, need a new science of where goals come from.

## Key points

- **One network from many datasets.** Pio-Lopez explains that he combined genes, drugs and diseases into one multilayer network, learned embeddings using random walk with restart, validated on a 30% hold-out and ran link prediction. One prediction, a link between GABA signalling and melanoma, was later confirmed in the lab.
- **Data is limited in kind, not volume.** Pio-Lopez says the omics data is plentiful but lacks bioelectrical measurements. Levin adds that what matters is intervention-to-outcome data on shape and function: his lab's hand-coded regeneration database took about four years and holds only hundreds of entries. He also blames poor journal standardisation and hopes robot scientists will help.
- **AI as translator, not micromanager.** In what Levin calls a controversial view, tissues and even gene pathways can learn, so AI should help build a "theory of mind" of the system rather than target single pathways, much as people train dogs without knowing any neuroscience.
- **Wellness models need a goal.** In response to Labenz's idea of a next-check-in wellness model, Levin expects it would predict well but warns we do not know what to optimise for. He links this to debates about outsourcing willpower and relationships to AI "prosthetics".
- **Bio-inspired AI and digital life.** Pio-Lopez points to neural cellular automata. Levin argues that biology's strength comes from working on unreliable hardware. He sees synthetic and digital life mainly as a way to cure human "mind blindness" towards unfamiliar minds.
- **Careful with collective minds.** Levin warns that merging individuals into a collective gives no guarantee that the collective will care about its parts.
