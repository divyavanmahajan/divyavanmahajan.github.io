## Thesis

Thomas von Tschammer, co-founder of Neural Concept, argues that product engineering is in its third revolution: after physical prototypes gave way to CAD and finite element simulation, physics-aware AI models now return results in minutes instead of days. He sees AI as an assistant that widens early design exploration, with numerical simulation moved later and human engineers keeping the final trade-off decisions.

## Key points

- **From 50 to 1,500 designs a day.** Von Tschammer cites Jaguar Land Rover, which presented at NVIDIA GTC that its external aerodynamics workflow went from about 50 evaluations per day to 1,500. Battery cool-plate suppliers cut development cycles by 80% and found designs cooling 20% better while 15% lighter.
- **Per-customer models plus frontier reasoners.** Models are fine-tuned on each OEM's simulation and wind-tunnel data, which also retains company know-how. Citing Jensen Huang's five-layer cake, he expects general frontier models to call Neural Concept's physics models, CAD and solvers as tools; he calls aerodynamics foundation models the low-hanging fruit.
- **The China gap.** Western OEMs take 48 to 60 months to develop a car versus 18 to 24 in China. He attributes this mostly to plant agility and freedom from legacy processes.
- **Formula 1 as a testbed.** F1 teams face CPU-hour caps on aero simulation that shrink the better they finished, which Labenz flags as a possible AI governance precedent. Engineers generate tens of thousands of designs overnight and pick from a dashboard in the morning.
- **Move 37 moments.** Engineers report AI designs they would have scrapped that outperform their own, though von Tschammer stresses the models stay bound by physics.
- **Roadmap and value.** He proposes AI-led iteration in crash, aero and powertrain in year one (20-40% speedups), then cross-discipline orchestration for 50-60% shorter cycles. He expects pricing to move from seats to value.
