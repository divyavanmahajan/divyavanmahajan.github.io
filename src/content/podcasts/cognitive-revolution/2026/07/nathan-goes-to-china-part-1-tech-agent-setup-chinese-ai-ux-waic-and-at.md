## Thesis

In the first of three solo episodes on his July 2026 trip, recorded under Chatham House rules, Nathan Labenz gives a ground-level report on getting online, daily life and Chinese AI products. He argues that China feels more technologically modern than the United States, that non-frontier Chinese models already serve many everyday uses well, and that the decisive AI channel there will be agents built into super apps people already use.

## Key points

- **Getting online is easy.** On international roaming, traffic routes through the home carrier, so Labenz says the Great Firewall was essentially a non-issue: Gmail and the Play Store worked, and his backup VPN went almost unused. He travelled with an Android burner and left a Claude agent on a Mac Mini at home to send email digests twice a day.
- **Modern and cashless.** He describes DiDi showing traffic-light countdowns, medicine delivered by Meituan in twenty minutes, and payment fees of about a quarter of a percent, compared with 2-3% in the US. He thinks most people comparing New York with Shanghai would guess China is the richer country.
- **Chinese AI as tour guide.** He found DeepSeek, Kimi 2.6 and MiniMax fast and accurate guides to historical sites and rated Kimi best. He also saw answers retracted mid-response, and when he woke up covered in hives he downloaded ChatGPT.
- **Doubao as companion.** He reports that ByteDance's Doubao, not DeepSeek or Qwen, is the mass-market assistant, used by parents and for emotional support. A Forbidden City guide told him it won't judge you.
- **WAIC and robots.** Labenz found the conference floor familiar, like CES. The robot demos had plenty of hardware but little autonomy; one walking robot turned out to be teleoperated.
- **Surveillance and contradiction.** He weighs pervasive cameras and very high public safety against succession risk, and passes on the view that Chinese society tolerates unresolved contradictions, such as VPNs being illegal yet universal.
