## Thesis

Woodson Martin, who became CEO of OutSystems after 18 years at Salesforce, argues that founder Paulo Rosado's 2001 goal of evolving software "without breaking anything" matters more now that agents write most of the code. His case is that incumbents with hardened primitives and years of trust in regulated industries have an advantage over AI-native startups. He adds that most enterprise workloads do not need frontier models. (OutSystems is also a sponsor of the show.)

## Key points

- **An abstraction layer instead of raw code.** Coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex or OutSystems' own Mentor edit an abstract model of the application. Code is then generated deterministically, with security, role-based access control and reuse of existing compliant components built in. Nathan Labenz notes a pattern of incumbents adding AI on top of mature primitives.
- **What enterprise-ready means.** Martin says uptime is a small part of it. He describes finished agentic systems stuck in compliance backlogs while customers check whether a model's training data was legally acquired, even for simple PDF extraction. He calls this partly calcification and partly prudence, since loan decisions must be auditable.
- **Security has no finish line.** Shared primitives let a vulnerability be patched once and fixed everywhere, unlike hundreds of separately AI-built stacks. Legacy COBOL, AS/400 and Lotus Notes systems remain widespread.
- **Shipping speed and token spend.** After pushing everything to AI, OutSystems went from 4 major features in Q4 to 19 in Q1 and 26 in Q2. Token spend peaked in June and July and is now below forecast, thanks to an internal harness and an LLM router that sends routine jobs to cheaper models. Some customers run Chinese models, mostly to save money. Half of Martin's customers are in Europe.
- **Backlogs are changing.** Long-feared legacy modernisations are under way; one insurer plans a six-month project instead of six years. Self-built dashboards create a new backlog of integration work. Martin cites Axos Bank, YESCO, banking apps across Asia and a Rotterdam fuel terminal running on OutSystems.
- **What to build, and who builds it.** Mentor began as a machine-learning project in 2018 and is now a set of MCP services. A new agent reads app telemetry to recommend agentic processes and forecast ROI. Martin expects vendors to differentiate through specialisation and says he is "super bullish" on junior talent and forward-deployed engineers.
