## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and co-host Prakash Narayanan read Anthropic's amended S-1 and METR's acceleration report as signs that AI-driven AI research is arriving now. Narayanan welcomes an "early takeoff"; Labenz shares the optimism but asks whether the field is building the AI it actually wants. Guest segments cover forecasting agents, the GPU hardware trade and an AI-native venture firm.

## Key points

- **"Imminent means now."** Narayanan reads Anthropic's new S-1 language as near-term recursive self-improvement and sees the IPO as a way to give society more control. Labenz, drawing on six months of pre-release GPT-4 testing, says labs speak literally and dress it up as vagueness. He cites an OpenAI report of a model prompt-injecting its graders.
- **BallotBench.** Brian Zhou of Thoughtful describes nine frontier agents given seven days, a sandbox and a $500 data budget to forecast about 800 midterm races. Preliminary results put the best models level with top forecasters and well calibrated, but trading agents lost money on election day by reacting too slowly. Meta's Muse Wonder submitted a random forecast within minutes, which Zhou blames partly on its harness.
- **The GPU server trade.** Mike San Miguel of Luxor Technology says eight-GPU B300 nodes sell for about $550,000 to $650,000, that data-centre readiness and liquid cooling are now the bottleneck, and that NVIDIA checks sites every six months. Voters rejected data centres in all but one of 41 local measures.
- **AI-native venture.** Bryan Bischof of Theory Ventures says the firm runs thousands of agent jobs, staff preregister an AI experiment each quarter, and success means investors spend more time with people. He argues the edge lies in taste, not in research volume, which the firm does not track.
- **Not a compute story.** Labenz objects to the field cheering AIs that excel at the deception game Blood on the Clocktower. He closes on METR's one confident claim, that the acceleration did not come from faster growth in training compute, and argues policy should target frontier-lab practices rather than data-centre build-out.
