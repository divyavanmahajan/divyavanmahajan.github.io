## Thesis

Nathan Labenz and co-host Prakash Narayanan open with Prakash's verdict after a weekend with GPT-6 Astra: it is AGI. The week's clips then ask whether anyone is actually in control. Labenz questions how much OpenAI's announced pause really paused. Guests describe defending code, sandboxing agents and dealing with China, and the hosts end on whether humans can still steer techno-capitalism.

## Key points

- **The Astra weekend.** Prakash ran three or four Astra agents continuously, fixing long-standing bugs, and says computer use finally works. Labenz credits a persistent notes file plus searchable session history for Astra's persistence. METR's time-horizon chart has stalled because model cycles are now shorter than the tasks it would need to measure.
- **Reassure and mislead.** Labenz objects that Apollo Research got only three days with Astra. He reads OpenAI's RL-compute chart as showing that only half of RL stopped, and calls its labelling possibly "flagrantly misleading". Prakash argues OpenAI can afford to pace because it has the compute, while compute-poor Anthropic must push for better models.
- **No adult in the room.** On Jacob Coxon's resignation from Anthropic, Prakash says no wiser authority exists to take over. Labenz proposes a government deadline for the labs to agree on pacing, with antitrust assurances. Prakash cites Operation Warp Speed's legal safe harbour to argue that companies need actual laws.
- **The defender's ledger.** Mozilla CTO Raffi Krikorian says Mythos found many Firefox bugs through Project Glasswing, but a full run would cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. He worries more about water and power utilities. Baseten's Amir Haghighat says micro-VMs can secure the sandbox but not what the agent does inside it.
- **Toys and China.** Snorble CEO Mike Rizkalla explains why the company avoids generative AI, with content costing about $20,000 per hour. Collin Hogue-Spears expects little from the Trump-Xi summit beyond perhaps an incident channel.
- **Who is in charge?** Prakash argues the financial market is already a paperclip maximiser that has taken over. Labenz suggests China may have preserved human agency at the top better than the West.
