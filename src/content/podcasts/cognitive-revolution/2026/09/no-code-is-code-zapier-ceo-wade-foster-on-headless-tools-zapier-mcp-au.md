## Thesis

Wade Foster, co-founder and CEO of Zapier, tells Nathan Labenz that most people have settled on one AI "daily driver" such as Cursor, Claude Code or ChatGPT. The winning strategy is therefore headless tools like Zapier MCP that bring context into that harness. He argues that AI should build and repair workflows, while the workflows themselves stay mostly deterministic code.

## Key points

- **Headless wins.** Foster contrasts companies that force users onto their own agent platforms with headless players such as Salesforce, and backs the latter. His own setup is Cursor plus Zapier's internal harness, connected over MCP.
- **AutomationBench.** Zapier's benchmark has about 600 knowledge-work tasks across marketing, sales, HR and operations. GPT-6 Astra leads at about 40%, while Gemini 3.7 does well at a fraction of the cost, which is why companies want to swap models per task.
- **No-code is code.** Foster says that around 80% of what people use agents for should be old-fashioned deterministic code. Agents now build, visualise and troubleshoot the workflows. Zapier's support system runs five independent agents, and when four agree, the diagnosis is usually right.
- **The real competitor is non-adoption.** Recalling Paul Graham's YC advice, Foster says the labs are now big tech and cannot build everything. He thinks most users are "candidly not doing much". A weekly automation that reads his Gmail, Slack and browser activity and proposes tools has worked for him, and he expects Zapier to productise it.
- **Pricing and people.** Foster calls seat-based pricing "dead or dying". AI transformation sits with the chief people officer because, with close to 100% daily AI use, the bottleneck became job design, pay and re-skilling. Public Slack channels by default give agents more context.
- **Security, tokens and slop.** Mythos-calibre models make vulnerabilities easier to find and to patch. Engineers spending $30,000 a month on tokens are outliers, and he expects token budgets tied to AI fluency. On AI writing, his rule is to own, understand and verify what you send.
