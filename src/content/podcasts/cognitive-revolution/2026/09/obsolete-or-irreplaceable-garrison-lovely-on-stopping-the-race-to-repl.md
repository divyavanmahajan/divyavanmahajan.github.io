## Thesis

Journalist Garrison Lovely, author of "Obsolete", tells Nathan Labenz that the problem is not AI but what he calls "the obsoleting project": the labs' effort to build a general substitute for human labour. He argues for freezing the frontier until there is strong public buy-in and scientific consensus that it can be done safely, while using narrow AI and industrial policy to deliver the medical and scientific benefits boosters promise.

## Key points

- **His own use.** Lovely does not write with AI but uses transcription, NotebookLM across 20 interviews at once, and fact-checking. He describes "borderline Claude psychosis" while writing the book, chasing AI feedback and fixes while burned out, and has since cut back.
- **Why the left missed it.** He cites deference to the Stochastic Parrots authors, the left's academic base, pattern-matching to crypto and the metaverse, dislike of Musk, Altman, Yudkowsky and Bostrom, and Ed Zitron's bubble thesis, which he calls comforting because it requires no action.
- **Motives and leverage.** Lovely thinks lab leaders are driven less by money than by "being in the room where it happens", a line he credits to Yudkowsky in 2023. He says researchers' bargaining power is near its peak because they are the first targets of automation, and urges them to unionise around safety, citing the Superalignment team's unmet 20% compute pledge.
- **Alignment polycrisis.** He describes technical, normative, economic and geopolitical alignment as interacting layers; solving the technical layer makes the product more useful and the race faster. RLHF, developed for safety, is his example because it made ChatGPT possible.
- **Freezing the frontier.** His proposal: no training run larger than the last, no more RL from verifiable rewards, no recursive self-improvement, auditors embedded in labs, and criminal penalties. Internationally he wants a US-China treaty first, verified with chip inventories and on-chip monitoring, citing Toby Ord's example of Soviet bombers cut in half for satellites to check.
- **China and organising.** Lovely argues a US slowdown also slows China's fast-follow approach, and that the Chinese Communist Party would never accept removing humans from the loop. He is donating his royalties to Irreplaceable, founded by 350.org co-founder Phil Aroneanu.
