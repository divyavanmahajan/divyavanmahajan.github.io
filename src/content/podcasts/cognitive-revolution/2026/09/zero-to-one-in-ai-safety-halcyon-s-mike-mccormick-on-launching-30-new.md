## Thesis

Mike McCormick runs Halcyon, a grant-making nonprofit paired with a venture fund. He argues that the main bottleneck in AI safety, cybersecurity and biosecurity is a shortage of experienced founders and leaders. His fear is a near future in which lab IPOs release billions for safety but there are few strong organizations to fund. Halcyon therefore concentrates on the six months before and after an organization is founded.

## Key points

- **Track record.** McCormick says Halcyon has helped launch about 30 organizations in three years, which together have raised about $500M. Its first two grants were career-transition grants to Eric Ho and Dan Balsam, who chose interpretability at a Halcyon retreat and founded Goodfire.
- **Portfolio examples.** McCormick describes AIUC, which pairs a SOC 2-like standard of 50-odd requirements with insurance, with ElevenLabs as an early customer. Transluce works on scalable oversight with tools such as Docent. Hadrian Biodefense estimates that a few hundred million dollars of protective equipment could keep essential US workers on the job in a pandemic. An unnamed indoor-air company is selling to schools first. Labenz recounts his struggle to get UVC lights approved in his son's Detroit classroom.
- **Timelines.** McCormick says the world is roughly on the AI 2027 trajectory, so he prioritizes work that could matter within one to four years. He hopes pacing will buy time to "speed run" control, monitoring, cybersecurity and verification. Each field has only a handful of serious organizations, and METR covers a small part of what evaluation needs.
- **Choosing whom to back.** McCormick looks for entrepreneurial drive and genuine mission motivation. He admits a bias against McKinsey partners, with AIUC's Rajiv Dattani as the exception. He calls structures such as public benefit corporations and special board seats useful nudges, not silver bullets.
- **Harder bets.** McCormick is interested but cautious on making open-weight models safe, including AE Studio's GRAM, which Labenz raises, and on new architectures, which require two speculative bets at once. He says none of the risks he cares about require AI consciousness. He sees coordination, not technology, as society's stickiest problem.
- **Joining the field.** McCormick wants COOs, recruiters, cybersecurity experts, cryptographers and formal-methods specialists. He says organizations should pay enough to raise a family in San Francisco. His advice: "hustle, but don't rush".
