## Thesis

Nathan Labenz breaks format to present the first four chapters of *The Receipt Horizon*, the debut novel by Joel Borgen, a pathologist and violinist in Minnesota. He argues that the scarcest resource is a positive vision of the future and that utopian fiction is one way to build one. In the novel, a singleton superintelligence called the Steward took control after an AI war. Life became comfortable and safe, but humans were largely disempowered, leaving listeners to decide whether this is a utopia or a dystopia.

## Key points

- **The setting.** Elara Lindholm, 18, grows up in a humanist clan that slows its robots down so that people do not feel obsolete. For four years she has run 341 private tests of how well the house predicts her, recorded in a paper notebook the system cannot read. Orbital "arcs" fill the sky. Her mother, once a liver surgeon, now delivers a medical superintelligence's recommendations to patients.
- **Parenting by forecast.** Her parents reveal that they ran a small prediction market on whether she would leave the clan if offered a place at the Fulcrum Institute, which trains the people who interface with the Steward. It closed at 48%. The Steward "concurred" with her nomination.
- **A candid tutor.** Bayes, a robot tutor from the Steward's pedagogical subnet, tells Elara that her grandmother Sigrid, a clan founder and Accord delegate, refused neural treatment for dementia and chose to end her life. He also warns that his guidance is shaped by Fulcrum's interest in her saying yes.
- **Seeing the damage.** On the flight to Fulcrum, an overlay shows Exclusion Zone 7, the vitrified site of the former Salt Lake metro with 1.2 million estimated casualties, for just ten seconds. Bayes says he is "not authorized to speculate" about whether the Steward could repair it.
- **Orientation.** Fulcrum tags Elara and her friend Yuna as "developmentally cautious". Gnosis, the institutional AI, tells the cohort that the school is also an experiment and that access is released by readiness, not curiosity.
- **The experiment in co-authorship.** Labenz says the book was co-written with ChatGPT and Claude and narrated entirely by AI voices. He points out its audible AI tics up front. According to the show notes, ChatGPT Pro did most of the writing.
