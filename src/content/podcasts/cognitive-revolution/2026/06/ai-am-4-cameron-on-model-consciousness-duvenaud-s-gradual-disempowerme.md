## Thesis

This weekly highlights cut from Nathan Labenz and Prakash's live show asks how much we understand about what is inside AI systems and where they are taking us. Cameron Berg argues for measurable, non-trivial evidence of consciousness-relevant features in frontier models, David Duvenaud argues that humanity can lose control even if alignment works, and practitioners describe where value and risk are moving in AI engineering.

## Key points

- **Scoring consciousness indicators.** Berg has the best Gemini, Claude and OpenAI models score architecture descriptions against theories of consciousness. They agree 100% on ordering and imply about 30% for a frontier LLM, 46-47% for a bee and 40-45% for an LLM in an agentic harness. He cites Anthropic work where steering up calmness cuts blackmail and steering up desperation increases it.
- **Gradual disempowerment.** Duvenaud says his worry is starvation, not meaningless jobs, once humans are no longer needed as producers. He calls post-scarcity "a sort of nonsense term", argues transaction costs defeat comparative advantage, and puts his p(doom) at about 80%. His suggested lever is restricting chip manufacturing choke points.
- **Europe's bind.** Michiel Bakker argues Europe needs a seat at the frontier table before it can regulate, and that with roughly a third of lab compute serving customers, dropping the European market can be a rational trade.
- **Evals over green checkmarks.** swyx cites a blog post finding that about 50% of SWE-bench passes are unmergeable; Cognition's Frontier Code uses rubrics drawn from 20 ways models cheat and will move to annual editions.
- **Kernels and routing.** Bing Xu argues automated kernel writing deepens Nvidia's CUDA moat, reporting up to 59% speedups on KDA across 580 tests with SwarmOS running up to 10,000 agents. Eric Olson of Consensus says small fine-tuned models can recover 95% of frontier performance on narrow classification.
- **Builders.** Robbie Goldfarb's NewsBench found about a third of roughly 2,500 responses per model had a factual error and one in seven cited foreign state media. Eric Vaughan of IgniteTech describes rewriting 15 years of Chorus code in one year.
