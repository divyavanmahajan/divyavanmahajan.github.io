## Thesis

In the first highlights cut of the daily live show he co-hosts with Prakash Narayanan, Nathan Labenz argues that frontier labs now treat recursive self-improvement as the plan, while their safety plans mostly amount to pouring compute into AI monitors. Because a thin, self-correcting harness is so cheap to build, he asks what around the core intelligence still has a moat, and concludes it is either real data or something human enough that the frontier leaves it alone.

## Key points

- **The "recursive" event.** At a Chatham House event, lab staff expected self-improvement to work. Asked how many copies of themselves it would take to do their AI-assisted work, the median answer was about two. Labenz left negatively updated on the quality of the plans but encouraged that people openly discussed a coordinated slowdown.
- **Refusals and moderation.** Labenz got refusals from ChatGPT and Claude when asking for help with a cigarette business, an example the OpenAI model spec says models should help with. A Claude Code experiment later found OpenAI's moderation endpoint now flags the harmful prompts it once missed.
- **Papers in the air.** Anthropic's persona selection model, emergent misalignment, the Apollo/OpenAI metagaming paper on growing eval awareness, accidental chain-of-thought training, and natural language autoencoders.
- **Self-improving tax harness.** OpenAI's forward-deployed engineers say what improves is the Codex harness, not the model: corrections get written into skills, and skills are dropped as models catch up.
- **Science and security reality checks.** Peter Jansen's CodeScientist claimed 19 discoveries from 50 ideas; reviewers judged 70-80% plausible, but checking the code left about 30% real. A former Project Maven guest says models are disposable and the harness and training data are what last, citing Mozilla's 271 Firefox bugs found with Mythos.
- **Other segments.** Matthew Sanders on the papal encyclical and AI consciousness, a startup that atomises policies for real-time guardrails, a "delegation over workflows" pitch, AI accounting for solo businesses, and AI mental health support in prisons and Ukraine.
