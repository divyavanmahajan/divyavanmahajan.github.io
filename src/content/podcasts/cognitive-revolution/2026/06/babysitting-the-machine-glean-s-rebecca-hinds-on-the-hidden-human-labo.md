## Thesis

Rebecca Hinds, head of the Work AI Institute at Glean, argues that AI adoption is above all a human change problem. Glean's Work AI Index 2026 finds large individual productivity gains that do not reach the organisation, which she attributes to hidden "botsitting" labour, missing context and perverse incentives. Host Nathan Labenz, a heavy AI user, pushes back on why this labour feels so onerous.

## Key points

- **The headline gap.** The survey of 6,000 knowledge workers (3,000 in the US, 1,500 each in the UK and Australia) finds 87% use AI, 73% say it makes them more productive and they report saving about 13 hours a week, yet only 13% say their organisation performs significantly better.
- **Botsitting.** Feeding context, overseeing, debugging and cleaning up after AI takes 6.4 hours a week. Hinds says 36% of AI sessions fail, and feeding context and debugging are the most exhausting parts.
- **Botshitting.** 69% admit to passing on AI work they cannot fully explain or defend, and 40-41% say they ship AI work they could not explain if asked. Hinds sees it as the endpoint of exhaustion, "good enough" satisficing and rewards for token counts and clicks. Both behaviours correlate with wanting to leave.
- **Coordination neglect.** One person expands a bullet into a five-page report with AI, a colleague compresses it back to a bullet: both look productive, and the organisation gains nothing. Workers also hide AI use to avoid being given more work.
- **Automating the meaningful parts.** The employees most afraid of AI automate most, often the work they value, such as customer relationships. She cites a Stanford finding that 41% of Y Combinator AI startups automate tasks people would rather keep human.
- **What works.** Effective firms measure, share data with employees, reward collaboration (hackathon prizes for co-creation and before/after prompts), give a clear mission and frame AI as a teammate without shifting blame to it. She warns against copying layoff percentages or the management style of Nvidia's Jensen.
