## Thesis

Andrew Lee, CEO of Tasklet, believes "everyone is building the same thing": a general-purpose agent that can replace most knowledge-work SaaS. He expects only three kinds of software company to survive, and is moving Tasklet from a Claude-only product to a neutral horizontal platform, partly because his main competitor is now his main supplier, Anthropic.

## Key points

- **Rebuilt again.** In the six months since his last appearance, Lee says nothing substantial has survived. Tasklet moved from workflow automation to one long chat per agent, which needed a new context manager: the full history lives in the file system and is summarised with decreasing fidelity for older turns, so an agent can fire 10,000 times a year and still behave sensibly.
- **Supplier and rival.** Lee says about 80% of users who churn go to an Anthropic product, usually because they already pay for Claude Max; he and host Nathan Labenz both guess Max gives roughly five times the tokens per dollar of the API. Opus 4.7's tokenizer raised Tasklet's costs by about 30%, so it stays on Opus 4.6 by default.
- **Going multi-model.** Lee rates GPT-5.5 as close to Opus 4.6 for his harness, has signed a deal with OpenAI, and is testing Kimi, DeepSeek and Google models. He pitches Tasklet as a neutral arbiter with equal margins on every model.
- **Harness versus model.** He agrees better models beat better harnesses over time, but says harnesses decide cost, reliability, speed and oversight, which matter more once a task already works. He prefers the term "mecha suit".
- **Three survivors.** Horizontal platforms (few winners), headless API companies such as Stripe, and solutions companies selling outcomes. He thinks Salesforce is "in real trouble" as systems of record lose value.
- **Details.** Internal token spend is about 5-10% of payroll; Lee praises sandbox vendor Blaxel and is sceptical of Anthropic's unreleased Mythos until he can use it.
