## Thesis

In this cross-post from The Intelligence Horizon, Yale students Owen Zhang and Will Sanok Dufallo interview Nathan Labenz. He argues that transformative AI, better than most people at most cognitive work, is clearly coming, and that energy, chips and RL limits are not fundamental barriers. With no single safety technique that "really works", he puts his hope in layered defences, a multipolar field of AIs, and cooperation rather than a race with China.

## Key points

- **Persistent disagreement.** Labenz finds it strange that informed experts still disagree on the big questions even as timelines have compressed; citing Helen Toner, he notes that expecting AGI only by 2035 now makes you a bear.
- **RL and world models.** He thinks RL scaling is probably enough for most economic cognitive work, pointing to DeepSeek R1's "aha moment" and OpenAI's HealthBench with 49,000 evaluation criteria. Against Yann LeCun, he argues models are no longer trained only on next-token prediction, and that interpretability results such as Golden Gate Claude show real world models.
- **Medicine.** After using frontier models through his son's cancer treatment, he says they are on the level of attending physicians.
- **Bottlenecks.** Labenz calls energy limits political, not physical, and says an H100 draws about as much power as a microwave. He sees a disruption to chip fabs, for example over Taiwan, as the most plausible tail-risk brake.
- **Risk and optimism.** His p(doom) remains 10-90%, but he is somewhat more optimistic because models understand human values better than expected and scaling laws seem to rule out one runaway system. He backs defence in depth: Goodfire's intentional design, Redwood's AI control work, formal verification for security, and pandemic preparedness. The title echoes Holden Karnofsky's "success without dignity".
- **Governance and China.** He opposes nationalisation and most protectionist regulation, wants government to address race dynamics and extreme risks, supports Anthropic in its dispute with the Defense Department over mass surveillance, and calls for researcher-level exchanges with China, perhaps a CERN-style joint project.
