## Thesis

Sam Stephenson, co-founder and designer of the AI note-taking app Granola, argues that AI products should be designed for users in a reactive, overwhelmed "system one" state rather than a calm, focused one. Granola's discipline of doing one job well, staying minimal, and spending a lot of time with real users runs counter to the vibe-coding habit of shipping many features, and Stephenson credits it for the company's growth.

## Key points

- **Design for the extreme user.** Borrowing the idea he attributes to the kitchen brand Oxo, Stephenson designs for someone in back-to-back meetings all day, on the basis that a product that works for them works for everyone. He calls Granola "surprisingly unambitious" about which tasks it promises to handle.
- **Grounded user research.** Rather than asking abstract questions, he has users open their Granola home screen or calendar and walk through real meetings, because people's descriptions of their own behaviour are unreliable.
- **Growth through sharing.** Labenz cites a Ramp report placing Granola second only to Anthropic for new customers in January. Stephenson says growth is almost entirely word of mouth and shared notes, such as polished notes appearing in Slack seconds after a call.
- **Costs and transcription.** Granola streams microphone and system audio to Deepgram and AssemblyAI, splitting traffic for redundancy. At one point transcription was half the company's burn rate. Granola has no credits or limits for now, but Stephenson expects usage-based pricing as it does more agent-style work.
- **Privacy choices.** Granola runs at the operating-system audio level instead of joining calls as a bot, defaults notes to private and discards audio. Stephenson says the team stored audio for about a week before deciding it felt too creepy, and suggests forgetting could be a useful feature for agents.
- **Team practice.** With about 60 staff, designers prototype in code with AI tools; Figma is now a specialist tool for exploring options. Stephenson's main fear is a few AI giants absorbing specialised products.
