## Thesis

Andreessen Horowitz partners Olivia Moore and Anish Acharya tell Nathan Labenz that voice, the original form of human communication, has only now become addressable by technology. Basic latency and understandability are largely solved; the remaining gaps are emotion, interruption handling and conversational nuance. For now, the traction is in vertical B2B voice agents with deep integrations, not in horizontal consumer products.

## Key points

- **Scouting by demand pull.** Moore watches YouTube, TikTok and Instagram as much as Twitter. When people push ChatGPT into off-label roles such as therapist or coach, she reads it as a sign that standalone products will follow. Acharya's advice is simply to use the products.
- **Happy Robot negotiates with truckers.** Acharya describes the a16z portfolio company's voice agent for freight brokers, which negotiates and discloses that it is an AI. Moore explains that it puts callers on hold to "talk to my supervisor" before improving its offer, which raises acceptance rates.
- **Pipelines still dominate.** Most builders still chain speech-to-text, an LLM and text-to-speech. The guests say voice-to-voice models are more expensive and less mature, and that interruption handling is still weak. Capability alone gets a startup into the conversation; integrations and workflows are what build a moat.
- **Incumbents are struggling.** Acharya calls Siri "a stick in the eye" and argues that large companies' committees and lawyers clash with how messy AI is. Moore points to Google Labs products such as NotebookLM as a better pattern, even though they ship more slowly.
- **Coaching and labor effects.** Real-time coaches for salespeople and HVAC technicians are working. Labenz presses on whether call centers could see a 90% headcount reduction. Acharya says they have not seen headcount cuts yet, because jobs bundle many tasks.
- **Kids and companions.** Acharya wants AI that models good social behavior, such as a Minecraft companion. He also says companion apps are used heavily by women, more as interactive fiction. Replika and Stanford research on reduced suicidal ideation come up as evidence of benefit.
