## Thesis

Economist Noah Smith tells Nathan Labenz, who calls himself a China dove, that the US and China have no fundamental reason to be enemies but that Xi Jinping chose rivalry and the US must respond. In Smith's view the next ten years are the peak risk of war, and the Cold War model of deterrence, including targeted export controls, is the best path back to friendly relations.

## Key points

- **History of cooperation.** Smith argues the two countries were de facto allies for most of the twentieth century, apart from the Korean War, and that their cultures are similar. He thinks the similarity pushes them to compete for the same niche.
- **Xi moved first.** Citing Rush Doshi's book The Long Game, Smith says Xi's slogan "great changes unseen in a century" reflects a goal of overthrowing the US-led order, and that Made in China 2025 pursued decoupling before US tariffs or export controls.
- **What China wants.** Smith says the threat is control of sea lanes and Internet cables, plus "sharp power" and algorithms used to amplify division in American society, so the US loses its say in world affairs.
- **Purpose of export controls.** He says they exist to keep a military technology edge, especially chips for precision weapons and autonomous drone swarms. He calls the controls on equipment and personnel effective, pointing to China's low-volume 7 nanometer chip, but sees the ban on selling Nvidia chips as largely cosmetic.
- **DeepSeek is not the threat.** Smith does not see DeepSeek as a danger and calls LLMs borderless and commoditized. On the AI-supremacy plans Labenz attributes to Dario Amodei and Alex from Scale, Smith says he has no strong opinion but finds lasting LLM supremacy unrealistic.
- **A decade of deterrence.** Smith expects Xi's bellicosity to fade once he is about 80, as Soviet aggression eased after Stalin, and argues that deterrence and stability are the quickest way to repair relations.
