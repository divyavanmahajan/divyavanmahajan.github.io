## Thesis

In this 80,000 Hours crossover, Google DeepMind's Director of Frontier Safety and Governance, Allan Dafoe, tells Rob Wiblin that individuals shape technology less than they think: competition forces adoption of advantageous technologies. Influence therefore comes from steering the order of development, for example making AI cooperatively skilled early, and from evaluations, staged deployment and multilayered governance.

## Key points

- **Determinism, explained.** Dafoe reconciles micro-level "constructivist" histories with macro trends like Moore's Law through levels of selection. His case study is Tokugawa Japan, which abandoned firearms for roughly 200 years until Commodore Perry's steamships arrived in 1853.
- **Differential development.** The seat belt could have come before the car. Dafoe argues that market demand already funds much alignment work (RLHF arguably arrived only a couple of years early), so marginal effort should go to problems like deception that the market will not solve in time.
- **Cooperative AI.** Dafoe claims alignment is neither sufficient nor strictly necessary if global coordination fails or succeeds. He cites the 2010 flash crash, a DeepMind "Habermas machine" for deliberation, and warns that collusion between AIs and the exclusion of outsiders are real downsides.
- **Dangerous-capability evals.** In DeepMind's paper, Gemini scored 3 out of 5 on persuasion, about 2 on cyber and self-proliferation, and poorly on self-reasoning. Dafoe points to Project Zero's Project Naptime to show that capability elicitation matters.
- **Proliferation and governance.** Citing Epoch AI, Dafoe says algorithmic efficiency rises 3 times per year, so a 100,000,000 model costs 10,000,000 two years later. His "win condition" is that the best models stay responsibly controlled and are used to defend against misuse of older ones. He defends staged release over open weights.
- **Benefits.** He cites Waymo data (6 times fewer injury crashes), AlphaFold, AI tutoring, fusion plasma control and weather prediction.
