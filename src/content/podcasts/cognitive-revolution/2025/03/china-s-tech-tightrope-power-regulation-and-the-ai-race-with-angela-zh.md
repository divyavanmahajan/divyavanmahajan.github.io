## Thesis

Angela Zhang, a USC law professor and author of Highwire, argues that China governs its tech sector through a "dynamic pyramid": power flows from the top leadership, effectively Xi Jinping, down through regulators to firms and platform users. The result is hierarchy, sharp swings between lax and harsh regulation, and fragile outcomes. She holds that each country's main risk is its own governance rather than the other, and that US chip controls may speed up Chinese self-sufficiency.

## Key points

- **Power imbalance.** Zhang places China at one extreme, where firms almost never confront the state, the EU in the middle, where companies appeal Commission decisions in court, and the US at the other extreme, where Congress has passed no law to discipline its own big tech firms beyond the TikTok divestment law.
- **Censorship as a regulatory tool.** She describes how the Cyberspace Administration of China opened a cybersecurity review of Didi two days after its US listing, sending the stock down more than 10%; state media then cast Didi as a traitor.
- **The regulatory pendulum.** Zhang says consumer tech had a light-touch decade until Ant Group's IPO, valued at $320 billion, was halted in late 2020, followed by about 18 months of crackdown. She thinks AI regulation in China is currently lax.
- **Diffusion, not breakthroughs.** She argues China is weak at going "from 0 to 1" but very strong at adoption and diffusion, and does not count DeepSeek as a scientific breakthrough. DeepSeek, she notes, started an AI price war in mid-2024.
- **Export controls may backfire.** Zhang expects "another DeepSeek moment" in semiconductors, because the controls align private firms with the government, and she argues the DeepSeek episode reflects US industry concentration around Nvidia and the cloud providers.
- **AI safety cooperation.** She says she cannot know whether Xi is a "doomer," but notes both countries are pushing AI at full steam. She expects no serious US-China AI cooperation before a contained disaster, and supports an IPCC-style international body to share evidence on AI capabilities.
