## Thesis

Rune Kvist and Rajiv Dattani, co-founders of the AI Underwriting Company (AIUC), argue that security and progress reinforce each other. They want to recreate for AI agents the flywheel that fire insurers and Underwriters Laboratories built for houses and electrical products: a standard that codifies best practice, independent audits that check it, and insurance that prices the remaining risk and gives the auditor skin in the game. Host Nathan Labenz, an investor in the company's seed round, probes how this can work when there is almost no loss data.

## Key points

- **Coverage is ambiguous today.** Kvist says current policies neither mention nor price AI risk, much as computer risk was unclear in the early 2000s until cyber insurance split out as its own product. Dattani cites an EY study putting enterprise AI losses in the million-dollar range.
- **Red teaming as synthetic loss data.** Dattani argues insurers cannot wait for historical data, so evals and red teaming supply the data to price risk. Kvist says first-round testing can find failure rates of up to 25% on some attacks.
- **The AIUC-1 standard.** Built from conversations with more than 500 enterprise security leaders and general counsels, it covers data and privacy, security, safety, reliability, accountability and societal risks. Kvist says most of it was uncontroversial; the debate sits around societal harms such as large-scale cyber misuse.
- **Business model.** Developers pay yearly for a one-year certificate that requires quarterly technical tests; Labenz suggests prices in the five-to-six figures and Kvist calls that broadly correct. A gap analysis can be done within 24 hours.
- **Avoiding the ratings-agency trap.** AIUC will act as a managing general agent whose pay depends on underwriting results, which Kvist contrasts with Moody's before 2008. For tail risks he points to US nuclear insurance, where the government covers damage above $15 billion.
- **Early adopters.** Kvist names Ada, Intercom, Cognition and Recraft. Dattani says the enterprise consortium includes JPMorgan Chase, Confluent's CISO and Anthropic's deputy CISO.
