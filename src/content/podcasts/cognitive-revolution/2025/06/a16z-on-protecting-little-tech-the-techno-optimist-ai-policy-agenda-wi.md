## Thesis

Matt Perault, head of AI policy at Andreessen Horowitz, argues that policymakers should regulate harmful uses of AI rather than the research and development process, because rules aimed at a handful of frontier developers would create the market concentration "little tech" fears. Nathan Labenz agrees on the dangers of concentrated power but pushes back that some harms, like a lab leak, may be irreversible by the time they happen.

## Key points

- **Long horizon, not deregulation.** Perault says a16z is not seeking zero AI policy. Its funds run 10 years, so it needs a healthy ecosystem that users trust, not a boom followed by a crash driven by harms.
- **Development is "just science".** Using the bio-research analogy Labenz raises, Perault argues society permits risky research for its upside and penalises misuse, citing Cambridge Analytica as a case of misuse after a legitimate data transfer.
- **Thresholds that capture only a few firms.** On bills like New York's RAISE Act, Perault says covering just five to 15 companies is "the thing we fear most". He favours revenue thresholds, and says compute or $100 million training-cost thresholds fail because a colleague told him every model costs $100 million to build; cumulative counting could eventually catch any startup.
- **AI model facts.** Perault says many transparency mandates probably violate the First Amendment and do little for consumers. He proposes a simple fact sheet with items such as knowledge cutoff date.
- **SB 813 and tort liability.** Perault welcomes California's SB 813 for tackling tort liability, but warns that if the liability shield costs too much, only incumbents will buy it and startups will carry the legal risk.
- **What to watch.** He points to the Trump administration's national AI action plan, expected in June or July, and notes that Colorado's governor backed the federal AI moratorium.
