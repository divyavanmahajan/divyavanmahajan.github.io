## Thesis

MacKenzie Price, founder of Alpha School and 2 Hour Learning, argues that AI finally makes Bloom's one-to-one, mastery-based tutoring scalable. At Alpha, adaptive apps and an AI tutor handle all academic instruction in two hours each morning, while human guides spend their time on motivation, emotional support and life skills. Host Nathan Labenz sees it as an area where AI can transform a sector without disrupting its labour market.

## Key points

- **Measured results.** Price says Alpha students reached 2.3 times learning in the '24-'25 school year, measured against NWEA MAP growth projections, and that fifth-grade math takes about 20 to 30 hours instead of the 200 or more in a traditional school.
- **No chatbots for students.** Price argues a chatbot interface mostly gets used to cheat. Instead, the Dash platform sends each child to the right lesson in apps such as IXL, Alpha Math or AlphaRead, and a vision model watches how students work so the tutor can coach habits like skipping explanations.
- **AI drove the step change.** Learning rates rose from 1.5 to 2 plus between 2021-2022 and 2022-2023, after Alpha added the AI tutor and stopped all academic teaching by adults in the fall of 2022.
- **Data-driven fixes.** When January 2025 MAP results showed every grade and subject at the 99th percentile except fifth-grade math at the 93rd, the academic team investigated and found, for example, that Math Academy suits only about 25% of students.
- **Guides, not lecturers.** Only about 15% of guides come from traditional teaching; many are former athletes, coaches or entrepreneurs. Guides start at 100 grand a year and are judged on three commitments: students love school, learn twice as fast in two hours, and build life skills.
- **Economics.** Tuition runs from $40,000 to $65,000, with $25,000 and $15,000 schools, a virtual charter school in Arizona and a homeschool program, Alpha Anywhere. Price says the Brownsville campus consistently outperforms the others on learning rates.
