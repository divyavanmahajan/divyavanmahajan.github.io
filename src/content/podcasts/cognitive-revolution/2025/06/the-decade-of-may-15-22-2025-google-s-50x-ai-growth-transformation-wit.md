## Thesis

Logan Kilpatrick of Google DeepMind argues that Google's turnaround from "sleeping giant" was mainly a people and organisational story, backed by incentives that tie model quality to every Google product. He expects frontier labs to diverge as the easy gains run out, and he believes startups building at the application layer face the lowest barriers ever. Host Nathan Labenz is more sceptical, suspecting big tech can win whatever it chooses to.

## Key points

- **From 10 to 500 trillion tokens.** Google's monthly token volume grew from 10 trillion a year earlier to 500 trillion, which Labenz notes is about 50,000 tokens per person on Earth. Kilpatrick credits the 2023 merger of Brain, part of Research and DeepMind.
- **Divergence over convergence.** Kilpatrick says the low-hanging fruit has been picked, so structural advantages such as Google's infrastructure will matter more, and some labs may specialise (he floats Anthropic focusing on coding as a hypothetical).
- **Startups and speed.** He argues startups win on speed and focus. On Anthropic cutting off Windsurf, he has some empathy, but says Google Cloud's mandate makes it unlikely Google would keep models for its own products first; internal billion-user teams often switch models more slowly than external developers.
- **Scaffolding moves into models.** Kilpatrick expects reasoning models to absorb tools and scaffolding. Josh Woodward told him NotebookLM audio overviews went from a 14-step process to four steps. Labenz describes a heavily scaffolded image workflow at Waymark that became a prompt to Gemini Flash.
- **Long context and diffusion.** Labenz describes debugging a codebase of 400,000-500,000 tokens in Gemini 2.5 Pro. Both are excited by the speed of Gemini Diffusion, which Kilpatrick thinks could make personal generative UI possible.
- **AGI as a product experience.** Kilpatrick expects the "AGI moment" to come from a product that combines a strong model with memory, and says he writes about 95% of his own work with no AI involved.
