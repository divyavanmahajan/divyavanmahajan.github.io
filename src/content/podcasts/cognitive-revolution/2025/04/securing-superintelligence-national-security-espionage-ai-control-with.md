## Thesis

Jeremie and Edouard Harris, founders of Gladstone AI and authors of the report "America's Superintelligence Project", call AI strategy an over-constrained problem. They say superintelligence may not be controllable, yet a deal with China cannot be verified today. They do not say whether a Manhattan Project for AI is wise. Instead they set out what it would take to secure one, and conclude that most of the measures are needed either way. Nathan Labenz says he read the report as a warning against such a project.

## Key points

- **Two camps not listening.** Edouard Harris describes AI safety people who see a coordination problem, and national security people who distrust China. He says each side plays down the other's evidence. Jeremie argues the two worries are logically independent and should fit in one head.
- **Lab culture.** Jeremie says OpenAI researchers they interviewed were nervous and asked not to be named, and that what they believe does not match what executives say in public. He says Anthropic staff seemed comfortable voicing disagreement.
- **Cheap attacks on data centres.** The brothers toured a data centre with former tier 1 special operations staff. One of them, they report, came up with low-cost ways to take a $10 billion facility offline for about a year. They were asked not to publish the details.
- **One-way doors.** If timelines to 2027 are plausible, Jeremie argues, design choices made today lock in later options. One example is spacing compute racks away from walls to defend against TEMPEST electromagnetic attacks.
- **Supply chain.** Jeremie points to Aspeed's roughly 80% share of baseboard management controllers as a "dream backdoor". Edouard says China has planted backdoors in grid transformers that could hold US infrastructure at risk.
- **Deterrence and verification.** Edouard argues security should make attacks costly enough to be visible and punished. Jeremie backs FlexHEG-style secure chips as a basis for any grand bargain, but thinks they will take too long to build.
