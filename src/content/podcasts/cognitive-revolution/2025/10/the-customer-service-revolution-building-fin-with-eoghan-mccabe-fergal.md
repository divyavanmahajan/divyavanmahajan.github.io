## Thesis

Intercom CEO Eoghan McCabe and Chief AI Officer Fergal Reid tell Nathan Labenz that raw model intelligence is no longer the main bottleneck for customer service automation. Reid argues GPT-4-level models already saturated most support tasks, and that Fin's gains came from retrieval, re-ranking, prompting and production A/B testing. McCabe adds that deployment, adoption and simple value-based pricing matter as much as the technology.

## Key points

- **One point a month.** Reid says Fin's resolution rate rose from about 35% at launch to about 65%, roughly one percentage point per month, with core LLM improvements contributing only a couple of points.
- **Test in production.** Reid is skeptical of evals hype: offline backtests show signal, but Intercom relies on large A/B tests and cares about changes of a tenth of a percentage point.
- **Own small models.** Query summarization moved from GPT-3.5 Turbo and others to a proprietary encoder-decoder plus a fine-tuned Qwen 3 model, which Reid says is cheaper, faster and more reliable; Intercom also trains a custom re-ranker. He says switching to a fully agentic architecture today would lower answer quality.
- **Jobs and capacity.** Reid says most support teams are about 30% underwater, so Fin mostly brings them to parity; BPO contracts are the exception. Intercom's own support headcount, projected to grow 25%, stayed flat. McCabe doubts Klarna's 2024 headcount claims.
- **Pricing.** McCabe says Fin initially cost $1.21 per resolution against a $0.99 price, but now earns software-level gross margins; Intercom's own human cost was $26 per resolution. Intercom's own resolution rate is in the high 60s, with some customers in the high 80s and 90s.
- **Beyond service.** McCabe expects one coordinated "customer agent" across sales, onboarding and service, previewed for Intercom's Pioneer event; Reid describes a 2X engineering productivity push driven by tools like Claude Code.
