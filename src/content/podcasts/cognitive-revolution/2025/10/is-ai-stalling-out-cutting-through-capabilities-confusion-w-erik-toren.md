## Thesis

In a conversation first aired on the a16z Podcast, Erik Torenberg asks Nathan Labenz whether AI is slowing down. Labenz separates two questions that Cal Newport blends: whether AI is good for us, where he shares many of Newport's worries, and whether capabilities are still advancing, where he says they clearly are. He blames much of the GPT-5 disappointment on OpenAI's naming and a botched launch, and expects labour-market effects to be undeniable by 2027 or 2028.

## Key points

- **Scale still pays.** Labenz notes GPT-4.5 rose from about 50% (o3-class models) to about 65% on the SimpleQA trivia benchmark, picking up a third of what earlier models missed.
- **Reasoning gains.** He cites IMO gold medals from pure reasoning models at several companies, and FrontierMath scores rising from about 2% to about 25% in roughly a year.
- **GPT-5 vibe shift.** Labenz says expectations were set with Death Star imagery, the model router launch was technically broken, and OpenAI was simplifying a confusing model lineup for consumers.
- **Jobs and engineers.** He expects fewer engineers in five years, would often pick models over a junior marketer or engineer, and notes a roughly 95% per-token price drop from GPT-4 to GPT-5. He questions how the METR study showing slower developers was read.
- **Agents and task length.** Citing METR's doubling trend, he says agents handle about two-hour tasks today; at a four-month doubling that is two days in a year and two weeks in two years. Replit claims its Agent V3 runs 200 minutes.
- **Beyond chatbots and China.** He points to Nano Banana, MIT's AI-designed antibiotics and robotics, and accepts that Chinese models are now the best open-source options, though most startup tokens still go to commercial APIs.
