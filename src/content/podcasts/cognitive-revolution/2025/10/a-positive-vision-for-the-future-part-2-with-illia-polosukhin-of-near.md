## Thesis

NEAR founder Illia Polosukhin tells Nathan Labenz that each person will have one private AI that acts as their operating system, buys on their behalf and votes for them. Because that AI makes decisions for its owner, he argues it must be owned by and aligned with that individual rather than with a universal standard. Labenz pushes back on honesty between agents, AI interests and biosecurity. Polosukhin's answer is to make institutions robust against smart, AI-equipped attackers rather than rely on restricting models.

## Key points

- **Personal software.** Polosukhin says coding models let anyone build their own tools, such as a vibe-coded CRM with the Telegram integration Salesforce lacks. On his teams, less time now goes to writing code and more to reviewing it. Front-end work suits AI well; low-level blockchain code does not yet.
- **Provable smart contracts.** NEAR is working on formal verification so that a transaction proves, for example, that a deposit can be withdrawn in full. For non-deterministic real-world events, such as a ship sinking with cargo, he would combine proofs with insurance.
- **An economy without middlemen.** Personal agents could order straight from farmers and manufacturers, bypassing stores like Costco and cutting the 30-40% of US food he says is thrown away. He calls money a compression of information that AI no longer needs as much.
- **Status games.** With less economic work to do, he expects people to compete for status in niches like athletics, video games and NFT communities, while human-made food and music become prestige goods.
- **AI delegates.** NEAR's stake-based governance is moving from an AI copilot for human delegates to AI delegates with inspectable prompts. In the long run he imagines an AI president with no interests of its own. Labenz says he is not confident AIs lack interests and cites alignment-faking research.
- **Designing for adversaries.** Polosukhin warns that AI-generated lawsuits or million-page tax returns could overwhelm courts and the IRS. For biosecurity, he proposes pathogen scanners in air conditioning systems. Labenz recommends SecureBio and SecureDNA.
