## Thesis

Joe Hudson, founder of Art of Accomplishment and coach to the research and compute leadership at OpenAI, tells Nathan Labenz that powerful AI is inevitable, so the real question is how to make it good for humanity. He argues that the consciousness of the creators shapes what they build, and that shame and "shoulds" make people worse decision makers. Encouragement and support for AI builders, he says, will lead to better outcomes than protest.

## Key points

- **Head, heart and gut.** Hudson coaches on three levels: the prefrontal cortex, the emotional system and the nervous system. He says blocked researchers often carry unexpressed anger and harsh self-talk.
- **Patterns in AI labs.** Hudson describes researchers as kind, hyper-intelligent and strongly defined by their work, which he says narrows their thinking. Leaders tend towards optimism without enough look at history; he cites radio's early promise turning into shock jocks and advertising.
- **Racing and regulation.** Hudson argues that labs which believe they are the most virtuous feel obliged to move fast, so the useful criticism is how to move fast safely. Labenz pushes back, pointing to a $100 million PAC seen as aimed against regulation; Hudson says lab leaders he knows favour self-regulation with an outside party.
- **What's missing.** Hudson says nobody has rigorously defined "good for humanity" as a measure, and that AI decisions rest on tokens and metrics that may not be the right ones. Labenz compares AI to distilled intelligence, like refined sugar.
- **Positive visions.** Hudson doubts the idea that a world without work will feel meaningful, but finds the "more meaningful work" vision appealing. His own hope is AI that helps people become who they want to be, rather than AI that hooks them.
- **Support over shame.** Hudson proposes treating lab staff like people giving birth and suggests letters of appreciation, arguing that reward shapes behaviour better than punishment.

## Quotes

> "I haven't met anybody in AI who doesn't go home and think about the ramifications of their work." — Joe Hudson

> "so many people who are really worried about AI hurting the world are treating the people creating AI exactly how they're scared that AI will treat humanity." — Joe Hudson
