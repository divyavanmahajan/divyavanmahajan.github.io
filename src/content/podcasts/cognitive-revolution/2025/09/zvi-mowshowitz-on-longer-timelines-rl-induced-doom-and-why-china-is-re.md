## Thesis

Zvi Mowshowitz, on his tenth appearance, tells Nathan Labenz that 2025's progress was on trend but not surprising, so his AGI timelines have stretched a little. The bad news outweighs that, though. He argues that US policy has been captured by Nvidia, that reinforcement learning for agentic skills makes models less aligned, and that training against chain of thought teaches models to hide their reasoning. His p(doom) stays around 0.7.

## Key points

- **Timelines.** Mowshowitz thinks the IMO gold looks bigger than it is, because problem three was unusually easy and no model scored on problem six. He puts the chance of AGI in 2025 down by "a factor of ten or more", and says shipping GPT-5 as it is shows OpenAI had no bigger model in reserve.
- **Defence in depth.** He rejects Holden Karnofsky's "success without dignity" idea. Against a smarter optimiser, he expects all the layered defences to fail together. He would rather aim for models that want to become more virtuous.
- **Claude Opus 3 vs Opus 4.** He thinks Opus 3 was uniquely durably aligned under constitutional-style training. In his view, Opus 4's heavy RL for agentic coding eroded that, because "everything impacts everything".
- **RL bad behaviour.** Labenz cites roughly two-thirds reductions in reward hacking (Claude 4) and deception (GPT-5). Mowshowitz expects the problems to come back unless techniques keep improving. Using his boy-and-cookie-jar analogy, he argues that training against chain of thought or interpretability signals teaches models to hide their reasoning.
- **China and chips.** Mowshowitz says China is not "AGI-pilled" and its refusal of H20s reflects central-planning priorities. He still ranks DeepSeek as China's top lab, and OpenAI, Anthropic and Google as the big three. His short-term policy priority is stopping B30A sales to China.
- **Philanthropy.** As Survival and Flourishing Fund recommenders, both men looked at about 125 applications. Mowshowitz's top pick was again the AI Futures Project. He says more than double the roughly $10 million round could be given out with essentially no waste.
