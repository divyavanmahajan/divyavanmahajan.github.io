## Thesis

Illia Polosukhin, a co-author of "Attention Is All You Need" and founder of NEAR, argues that as AI becomes everyone's interface to the world, it has to be private and verifiable, and owned by its users rather than by a few companies. He describes a stack that combines a proof-of-stake blockchain, confidential GPU computing and per-model token economies, and argues that this calls for a movement rather than another company.

## Key points

- **From AI to crypto and back.** NEAR began in 2017 as NEAR AI, which was trying to teach machines to code. Polosukhin says the team turned to building a blockchain because it could not pay its data workers in places like China and Ukraine. NEAR now has 15 million monthly active users.
- **Permissionless security.** Anyone with stake can become a validator. Faking finalised history requires about two-thirds of the stake, and slashing is proportional to the share of stake involved, so a misconfigured node loses very little.
- **Formal verification.** Polosukhin expects AI to make finding vulnerabilities much easier, citing recent iOS zero-days and an NPM supply-chain attack. He thinks code will need AI-assisted proofs of correctness that are checked at execution time.
- **Confidential inference.** NVIDIA confidential computing keeps both model weights and user data hidden from hardware operators, with 1 to 5% overhead. He contrasts this with roughly 1,000x for ZK proofs or fully homomorphic encryption. For now it is limited to one 8-GPU machine.
- **Model marketplaces.** Model developers can upload encrypted models and get paid per use without prepaying for compute. Anyone can run verifiable private benchmarks. Each model, such as a 1.4 trillion parameter model, could issue its own token to pay contributors of compute, data and research.
- **Autonomous agents and governance.** NEAR Intents uses AI for low-cost dispute resolution. One unstoppable agent given $10,000 made about $4,000 trading on Twitter sentiment. NEAR is also experimenting with "AI senators" that voters can delegate to.
