## Thesis

Ryan Hudson, founder and CEO of ZeroClick (and previously founder of Honey), argues that AI applications need an ad layer to support free tiers, and that letting advertisers pay for inference-time consideration of their content can give users better answers. Nathan Labenz agrees on the economics but repeatedly tests whether ad incentives will pull AI products toward engagement-maximising or extractive behaviour, and finds Hudson has strong product answers but fewer on the longer-term societal questions.

## Key points

- **From ad blocker to ad platform.** Hudson says the team behind Pie AdBlock (used by a couple million users) built a privacy-native contextual matching system and realised it fit AI. ZeroClick has announced a $55 million raise and wants to be the Stripe for monetising free AI tiers.
- **How it works.** ZeroClick is offered as an MCP server: the AI sends keyword-style queries, ZeroClick matches them against AI-generated campaign content in vector space, and the model decides whether to include it. Hudson says generating the ad text in-house also protects developers from prompt-injection by advertisers.
- **Not an attention model, says Hudson.** He compares in-AI ads to Google search rather than social feeds: revenue comes from matching intent, not time on site. Labenz counters with companion apps like Tolan that push notifications, and with mortgage clicks reaching $500-$750, where the highest bidder was the most extractive seller.
- **Neutral rails, market discipline.** Hudson says users will abandon AI that breaks their trust, and that ZeroClick should act like Stripe or PayPal, stepping in mainly over illegal use. He admits he had not considered state actors buying influence, such as a government advertising around Tiananmen Square.
- **Long tail over walled gardens.** Hudson points to Open Evidence, ad-supported and used by 40% of doctors daily, and expects cheap SaaS sold through contextual ads. He criticises Chrome's single-purpose extension policy, given Chrome's 78% share.
- **AI SEO is a sliver.** Hudson expects a handful of real experts and many vendors selling AI-visibility services; he thinks paid context will matter more, as SEM does relative to SEO.
