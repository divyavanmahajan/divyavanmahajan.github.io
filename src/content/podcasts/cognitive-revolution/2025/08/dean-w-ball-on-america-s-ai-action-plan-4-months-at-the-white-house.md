## Thesis

Dean W. Ball, back from four months as senior AI policy adviser at the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, argues that the AI Action Plan was designed as an executable to-do list rather than a strategy document. In his view, the margin that matters for the US is not frontier model development but deep adoption of AI across the economy, backed by energy infrastructure and exports to partners.

## Key points

- **A to-do list, not a manifesto.** Ball describes three pillars (innovation, infrastructure, international diplomacy and security), each with strategic headers and short lists of recommended actions. The request for information drew about 10,000 submissions.
- **Inside government.** Ball says LLMs are not permitted on White House computers because of the Presidential Records Act, so he used AI only as a research assistant and chief of staff. He also points to the Paperwork Reduction Act, which kicks in when an official asks more than 9 members of the public for the same information.
- **Adoption over standards.** Ball argues the global standard will be set by whoever finds product-market fit. He sees China's government AI adoption as KPI-driven and often shallow, though he concedes the US military is less agile than he would like.
- **Unlocking the grid.** Citing a Duke report by Tyler Norris, Ball says data centres that accept curtailment for 0.25% of the year could unlock 76 gigawatts without new physical infrastructure; FERC interconnection rules stand in the way.
- **Exports and chips.** Ball calls the Biden diffusion rule's tier 2 placement of Brazil and India an "unnecessary self own". He defends the UAE deal on the grounds of reciprocal investment, and he flags legacy chips such as 45 nanometer as an underrated supply risk.
- **Timelines and labs.** Ball expects a cognitive Boeing 737, useful but not human-like AGI, between 2027 and 2030. He still counts GDM, Anthropic and OpenAI as the frontier firms. He is restarting Hyperdimensional and joining the Foundation for American Innovation.
