## Thesis

Logan Kilpatrick, product manager for the Gemini API and AI Studio, argues that Google DeepMind's move to own research, models and products end to end will speed up both model and product progress. On launch day for Gemini 2.0 Flash he makes the case that low cost per intelligence opens new use cases, that reasoning is the real unlock for long context and agents, and that model choice still matters because models differ beneath similar benchmark scores.

## Key points

- **The launch.** Kilpatrick lists Gemini 2.0 Flash in production at 10 cents per million input tokens and 40 cents per million output tokens, a preview of Flash Lite, an experimental 2.0 Pro and a Flash reasoning model. Flash Lite keeps the 7.5-cent price point of 1.5 Flash so developers do not see a price rise, but it will not offer native image or audio output.
- **Text-to-app economics.** He names Bolt.new and Cursor as adding Gemini support, and estimates that startups spending $40,000-50,000 a month on LLM usage could pay about $1,000 with Flash, a 40x reduction.
- **Long context needs reasoning.** After talking with Jack Rae, Kilpatrick suggests models struggle to combine many items across a 2-million-token window, and that thinking models plus tools will make long context truly useful.
- **Pro and the missing Ultra.** He says Pro targets coding and repeats his claim that Google will have the best coding model; Ultra was dropped because smaller models soon overtook it, though Google is still scaling pre-training.
- **Evals are thin.** Kilpatrick describes a coding prompt that failed in Gemini, worked in Claude, then worked in Gemini after a reset, as evidence that model choice is often vibes; he argues models are not commoditizing and cites GitHub Copilot's model dropdown. Labenz admits Waymark lacks evals beyond basic guardrails.
- **Startup bets.** Kilpatrick points to vision language models replacing domain-specific computer vision, reasoning making agents work, evals, and tooling for websites that must deal with non-human visitors. Fine-tuning for 2.0 Flash is not yet available.
