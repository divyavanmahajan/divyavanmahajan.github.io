## Thesis

Inna Tokarev Sela, CEO of illumex, tells Nathan Labenz that business users should not have to switch between many applications to get answers. Her bet is an "application-free" future where people ask questions in tools like Slack or Teams. That only works, she argues, with a shared business context built from pre-made industry ontologies that are mapped automatically onto each customer's metadata, never its raw data.

## Key points

- **Canonical ontologies.** illumex built business ontologies per industry, from pharma to insurance, as a knowledge graph of semantic embeddings. Onboarding retrains them on a customer's schemas, query logs and APIs in a few days. She compares the idea to Palantir's ontologies, but automated.
- **Usage over names.** Tokarev Sela says they "do not trust semantics". An unclear column's meaning is inferred from how it appears in dbt pipelines and BI formulas, cross-checked against known metrics. Concepts not grounded in customer data are dropped. On the Spider text-to-SQL benchmark they scored 91%.
- **Metadata only.** illumex generates queries; execution happens on the customer side and results return to the chat interface. She says this was an early security decision that also shortens vendor reviews.
- **Trust and cost.** Domain experts certify definitions, conflicting logic is flagged for humans, and users can question every answer. Pre-built context saves up to 80% of tokens, and she says general-purpose models show no real gains in business understanding yet.
- **Pricing and users.** Pricing is fixed tiers by number of data sources, with no seat limits, because surprise costs slowed enterprise adoption. Demand in the last six months shifted from analysts to business-user self-service.
- **Careers.** She expects analysts to shift from content creators to content moderators, since only about 10% of possible data work gets done today, and finds industry a better source of ideas than academia.
