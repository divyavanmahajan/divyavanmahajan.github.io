## Thesis

Eric Simons, founder and CEO of StackBlitz, argues that software is the first domain AI is rewriting, because code is text-based and outcomes can be checked. Bolt's edge, he says, is the WebContainer technology StackBlitz spent seven years building: a full development environment in the browser that removes setup and deployment friction for non-developers. He prefers short, supervised loops over long-running agents until models become more reliable.

## Key points

- **Traction.** Simons says Bolt went from zero to $20M ARR in its first 8 weeks, with churn around 25%. He names Cursor (about $100M) as the only AI coding startup ahead of it. Host Nathan Labenz notes a later $105M funding round.
- **Who uses it.** About 30-40% of users are developers and 60-70% are PMs, entrepreneurs and similar. A Fortune 500 company uses it to prototype instead of Figma; one freelancer reportedly spent $7 of inference on a dashboard billed at $9,000.
- **Browser-native OS.** StackBlitz wrote its own Unix-compliant operating system in WebAssembly that boots in about 100 milliseconds. Simons compares this to Figma's early WebGL work and says Node.js is the best-supported ecosystem, then PHP, with basic Python.
- **Pricing and openness.** Plans run from $20 to $200 a month, plus extra token purchases; volume discounts let Bolt price below direct Claude 3.5 Sonnet access. The open-source Bolt.diy, including the system prompt, runs with any model, including DeepSeek locally.
- **Opinionated path.** Vite and React, Supabase for auth and database, Stripe for billing, and one-click deployment via a Netlify integration.
- **Agents later.** Simons says long-running agents like Devin fail more as steps pile up, and thinks reliable longer agentic runs are "a model or 2" away.
