## Thesis

New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy tells Nathan Labenz that AI is a "realm" that will touch every part of life, not a narrow technology lane. He does not claim to know when superhuman AI arrives, but argues the state should assume it will and build the talent, institutions and services to benefit from it. Labenz pushes him on job losses, energy, China and regulation, and Murphy admits several of those questions remain open.

## Key points

- **A dinner that started it.** Murphy says a dinner with AI leaders in the Bay Area about three years ago, including Mustafa Suleyman, convinced him that talent drives the industry, and that New Jersey, with Princeton, Rutgers and the Bell Labs legacy, could compete.
- **AI inside state government.** About 13,000 of roughly 60,000 state employees use an AI assistant based on Microsoft Copilot, with free training. Murphy cites a 50% boost in call resolution and one program's phone wait cut from 40-plus minutes to about 90 seconds.
- **NJ AI Hub.** The hub with Princeton University, announced about a year earlier, now has Microsoft and CoreWeave as founding corporate partners. The Office of Public Defender is building an AI "brief bank" to help draft legal briefs.
- **Jobs and fairness.** Murphy sees so much undone work in government that he does not expect a near-term trade between efficiency and headcount; any reduction would come gradually through attrition. He says states must lead on fairness and access.
- **Energy and data centers.** He wants AI growth without breaking the 2035 100% clean energy goal, pointing to stalled offshore wind, existing nuclear plants and a former pharmaceutical campus in Kenilworth reused for a data center deal.
- **China and regulation.** As a former US ambassador, Murphy calls the competition with China a race and sees little room for cooperation. He prefers federal AI rules, but says New Jersey will at least enforce existing anti-discrimination laws on AI use.
