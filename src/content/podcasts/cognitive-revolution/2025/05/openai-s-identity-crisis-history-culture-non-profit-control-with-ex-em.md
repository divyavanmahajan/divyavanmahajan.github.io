## Thesis

Steven Adler, one of 12 former OpenAI employees who filed an amicus brief in the Elon Musk versus OpenAI lawsuit, argues that nonprofit control is what keeps OpenAI legally accountable to its mission rather than to shareholders. Drawing on four years inside the company, he describes how competition between labs pushes safety practices downward, and he calls for binding floors, such as a minimum testing period, instead of voluntary commitments.

## Key points

- **The Anthropic split.** Adler joined in December 2020, when the applied team had about 30 people and the company about 180. He says the departure of roughly seven people to found Anthropic played out over two or three months, and that leadership's renewed commitment to the nonprofit mission helped hold the company together.
- **Race to the bottom on policy.** Running product safety, Adler saw OpenAI weigh other developers' decisions when setting its own content rules. He warns that a "race to the top" offers no protection against a losing lab taking bigger gambles.
- **GPT-4 and acceleration.** OpenAI commissioned superforecasters to predict how different launch styles for GPT-4 would affect the wider race. Adler describes a split between staff focused on acute misuse and staff worried about kicking off a race.
- **Evals advice.** Adler warns against easy multiple-choice evals. He recommends interactive tasks, separating the eval from the "solver" (scaffolding), and frameworks such as the UK AISI's Inspect or OpenAI's NanoEval. He wants evals standardised across labs, much as car crash tests are.
- **Personhood credentials.** On the AGI readiness team under Miles Brundage, he worked on privacy-preserving proof that a real person stands behind an online account or agent. He compares this to the move from HTTP to HTTPS, and says it could build on chipped passports rather than biometrics.
- **Culture shift.** At a safety off-site, Adler counted only about four of 60-70 attendees who had joined before OpenAI became a commercial business. He praises Anthropic's public list of its commitments as a model for others.
