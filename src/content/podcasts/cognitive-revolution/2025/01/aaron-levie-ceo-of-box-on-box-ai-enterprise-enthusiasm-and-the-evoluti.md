## Thesis

Aaron Levie argues that enterprise leaders are more energised by AI than by any technology in his career, including cloud, and that software is entering an era of "systems of intelligence" that can automate judgment-based work. He is equally clear that at-scale deployment is early: reliability, cost and human change management will make this at least a decade-long shift.

## Key points

- **AI versus cloud adoption.** Levie contrasts early cloud, marked by skepticism over security and new vendors, with today's CIOs looking for as many AI use cases as possible, often more than is practical.
- **IT becomes the HR department of AI.** Citing Jensen Huang, Levie says IT will move from deploying tools to deploying AI labour, such as agents that review contracts or invoices, which forces IT to understand the business much better.
- **Hubs fixed Box's RAG problem.** Box AI sits on more than 100 billion files for about 115,000 customers. Levie says Hubs, built a year before ChatGPT, lets users curate authoritative documents, avoiding "earnings_draft_1" confusion; he claims this makes Box's RAG at least 100 times better than broad retrieval.
- **Agents and systems of intelligence.** Levie traces enterprise software from deterministic systems of record (CRM, ERP) through systems of engagement (Slack, Box) to AI systems that combine structure with flexibility.
- **Barriers to deployment.** Regulated workflows need near-perfect reliability, not 98%; costs must fall; and a 40% saving often ranks ninth on a manager's list. Net-new productivity, as with Copilot or Cursor, is easier to adopt.
- **Startups and pricing.** Levie advises startups to avoid thin layers on OpenAI or Salesforce and to build cross-platform workflows incumbents will not pursue. He sees Klarna's homegrown approach as an exception, and expects per-seat, consumption and outcome pricing to coexist.

## Quotes
