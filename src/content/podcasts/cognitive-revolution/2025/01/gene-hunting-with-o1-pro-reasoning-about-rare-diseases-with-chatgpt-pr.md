## Thesis

Catherine Brownstein of Boston Children's Hospital and Harvard Medical School argues that genome sequencing is now cheap, so diagnosing rare diseases depends on human capacity to read the literature and on data sharing. She sees large language models, including o1 through her OpenAI ChatGPT Pro grant, as a practical fix for the analysis bottleneck. Data sharing, she says, needs changes to norms and regulation instead. Host Nathan Labenz expects models like o3 to be solving some cases within a year.

## Key points

- **Hard cases left over.** Brownstein says that when she started in 2011 an exome cost close to $4,000 and her team diagnosed about 80 percent of cases. Now it costs $160, easy cases are solved earlier in the system, and her Manton Center team diagnoses about 10 percent.
- **Pipelines plus reanalysis.** Phenotypes coded in HPO and VCF files go through several genomic pipelines. When those come back negative, she looks at structural variants, AlphaFold protein structures and reanalysis a year later, and uses tools like Matchmaker Exchange to find matching cases.
- **Diverse teams win.** In a 2014 international challenge with 23 teams, two of three families were diagnosed. Teams that mixed counselors, clinicians and bioinformaticians did better.
- **Data hoarding and red tape.** Brownstein says researchers who hold on to cases for credit, biobanks that are still opt-in, and doctors who will not arrange CLIA confirmation all hold back diagnoses. She tells families to enroll in several registries.
- **How she uses LLMs.** Her biggest gains come from summarizing papers and genes, ranking 20 candidate genes before meetings, and checking pathway gene lists from a cohort of about 500 bladder-pain patients for links to the bladder. She mostly uses o1, not web search, because of "garbage on the web", and she checks the answers.
- **Too much reasoning, and Move 37.** Sometimes o1 makes inference chains that go further than she asked for. Labenz compares this to AlphaGo's Move 37, and Brownstein says she has "faith in it" but cannot yet tell whether those leaps are right.
