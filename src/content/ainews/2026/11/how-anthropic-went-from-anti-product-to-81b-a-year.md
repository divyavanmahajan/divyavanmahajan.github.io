## Overview

This episode is a holiday excerpt shared in place of a normal AI Daily Brief episode. The host, NLW, plays chapters two and three of an episode of his longer-form weekly show, *How We Got Here with NLW*. The full episode is titled "How Anthropic Went from AI Safety Outcast to the World's Most Important Company."

The argument is that Anthropic began as a research lab that did not want to make products. Commercial pressure, the FTX collapse and the launch of ChatGPT pushed it into the AI race. It then overtook OpenAI on revenue by betting on coding and agentic AI, and by selling AI by the token instead of by the seat. This matters because, in the speaker's view, it changed how the market understands the AI business model.

- **Speaker:** NLW, host of *The AI Daily Brief* and *How We Got Here*
- **Source link:** No URL was provided with this transcript.

## Prerequisites

- **Large language models (LLMs):** a basic idea of chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude, and of how they are trained and used.
- **AI industry players:** OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon, and the hyperscalers that supply cloud compute.
- **Effective altruism (EA):** a philanthropic movement closely tied to the AI safety community.
- **Startup financing terms:** Series A, valuation, equity, annual recurring revenue (ARR), run-rate revenue.
- **Software development basics:** what writing, running and testing code involves.
- **Tokens:** the units of text that models process and that AI companies charge for.

## Main Points

### 1. Anthropic's founding as an anti-commercial research lab

- In the early pandemic days, staff meetings were held outdoors in a park on the north slope of San Francisco's Bernal Hill (the transcript calls it "Presidio Park"). People brought laptops and folding chairs and sat six feet apart.
- Jared Kaplan suggested the name "Anthropic," from the Greek word for human, and the domain was available.
- The founders seriously considered becoming a non-profit to avoid the commercial pressure they felt had bent OpenAI. They concluded they could not raise enough money or hire the best people that way. Instead they formed a **public benefit corporation**, with a trust that would eventually choose most of the board.
- Dario Amodei's memo *Vision for a New Co.* proposed a lab of about 50 people. It would build, in order:
  1. a model as good as GPT-4
  2. a coding assistant
  3. AGI
- Every co-founder was asked to pledge 80% of their equity to charity. Tom Brown's reason for not haggling: "this will either be a trillion-dollar company or a zero-dollar company."
- Talking about money was effectively taboo. Several co-founders, Dario included, ruled out commercialising anything. The few money ideas that came up were licensing the model to nonprofits or running an AI-powered hedge fund.

### 2. Early research agenda

- The work was safety research on Anthropic's own large model, trained on chips rented from Amazon.
- Chris Olah's team worked on interpretability, trying to reverse-engineer what happens inside models. They published *A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits* in December 2021.
- Amanda Askell, a philosopher who taught herself to code, led work on what an aligned AI should be.
- Papers in 2022 covered:
  - training an assistant with human feedback
  - red-teaming (attacking their own models)
  - constitutional AI
- None of this was a product, but all of it needed a large model, and so it needed money.

### 3. Fundraising and dependence on the safety movement

- VCs hesitated because there was no product and no clear intention to build one.
- Eric Schmidt thought the ideas were vague and the 50-person cap unrealistic, but invested because they were good scientists.
- The May 2021 Series A raised $124 million. It was led by Jaan Tallinn (Skype co-founder), with participation from Dustin Moskovitz.
- In 2022 Sam Bankman-Fried (FTX, Alameda Research) offered $500 million for about 13% of the company, with no board seat or other strings. Dario was uneasy about that much crypto money from a single investor, but accepted.

### 4. Claude is built and held back

- In early 2022 the model finished training and was turned into a chatbot for study purposes. It was named Claude after Claude Shannon, the father of information theory.
- It was switched on in an internal Slack channel on February 4, 2022. By the fall, a few dozen testers had access through a simple research webpage.
- Anthropic chose not to release it, fearing it would trigger a race to build bigger and potentially more dangerous systems. Dario later said: "I suspect it was the right thing to do, but it's not totally clear-cut."

### 5. November 2022: the FTX collapse and ChatGPT

- FTX filed for bankruptcy on November 11, 2022, and Bankman-Fried was revealed to be running a fraud.
- The impact on Anthropic:
  - Most of the $500 million was already spent, and a clawback in the bankruptcy was a possibility.
  - Future investors might shy away from a company whose largest shareholder was a fraud.
  - Effective altruism, to which Anthropic was closely tied through employees and early funders, became suspect.
- Jack Clark told staff to stay quiet: no tweets, no statements, no soul-searching blog posts. He warned that being pulled into the FTX story could kill the company.
- The FTX estate sold the Anthropic stake in 2024 for about $1.3 billion. Analysts estimate that, kept intact, it would have been worth $40 billion or more at the time of the IPO.
- Nineteen days after the FTX filing, OpenAI released ChatGPT. Ironically, this was partly a response to a rumour that Anthropic was about to launch a chatbot.
  - OpenAI employees mostly guessed it would get 10,000 to 50,000 users. Altman guessed a million.
  - It reached a million users in five days and an estimated 100 million by January.
- The chatbot Anthropic held back to avoid starting a race "had helped start one anyway."

### 6. Anthropic joins the race (2023)

- At a January 2023 offsite, Dario told staff the race "will never be chill again."
- His reasoning for competing: if Anthropic sat out, the frontier would belong to labs that cared less about safety. Frontier research costs billions, and only a product could pay for it. "It's sad that we have to make this trade-off, but it's going to be worth it."
- Claude launched on March 14, 2023, the same day OpenAI released GPT-4.
- A fundraising deck leaked that spring described "Claude Next," a model meant to be 10 times more capable than the most powerful models of the time.
- In May 2023 Anthropic announced a 100,000-token context window, roughly a novel's length. In a demo, Claude found a single altered line in *The Great Gatsby* in 22 seconds.
- Amazon committed up to $4 billion in September 2023, and Google committed $2 billion in October.
- During OpenAI's November 2023 board crisis, Anthropic was approached about Dario replacing Altman and possibly merging the two companies. Anthropic declined, and Altman returned within five days.

### 7. Why coding became "the use case"

- For most people, AI is a chatbot, "a better Google." Those uses are real but are not where the money is.
- Claude's consumer app has only about 5% as many users as ChatGPT, yet Anthropic passed OpenAI on revenue. The money is in work.
- In January 2023 Andrej Karpathy wrote: "The hottest new programming language is English."
- Reasons code came first:
  - Software runs nearly everything, and programmers are among the most expensive workers, so even a slice of their work is a huge market.
  - Code can be checked by running it, which makes it ideal for training by trial and reward.
  - A model that writes code can help write the next model.
- The evolution of AI coding tools:
  1. **Autocomplete:** GitHub Copilot (June 2021).
  2. **Chat:** developers pasting code into ChatGPT and pasting answers back.
  3. **Autonomous agents:** BabyAGI, GPT Engineer (the forerunner of Lovable), and Cognition's Devin (March 2024).
- Early agents fell short. In early 2025, Answer.AI researchers gave Devin 20 real tasks over a month, and it finished only three. The scaffolding existed, but no model was good enough to drive it.

### 8. Claude 3.5 Sonnet and the coding breakthrough (2024–2025)

- Coding was second on the founding memo's list. Kevin Roose called Anthropic's 2024 push into coding a "prescient bet," partly because every gain also made Anthropic's own engineers faster.
- By March 2024 Claude 3 had roughly caught up with GPT-4, which had then been out for nearly a year.
- In June 2024 Claude 3.5 Sonnet took the lead on code. Praise from users included:
  - Shubham Saboo: "10x more efficient" than GPT-4o.
  - Allie K. Miller: it built a working game of Mancala from one screenshot in 25 seconds.
  - Other users: "AGI vibes" and "the best model I've used to write code."
- Non-coders started building software:
  - Replit's agent (September 2024) ran on Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Replit's ARR went from $10M at the end of 2024 to over $100M by June 2025.
  - Lovable reached $100M ARR eight months after launch, $200M by the end of 2025, $300M in January 2026 and $400M in February 2026.
- In February 2025 Karpathy coined "vibe coding": "fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget that the code even exists."
- The significance: when knowledge workers who are not engineers use these tools, a much larger share of knowledge work enters AI's total addressable market.

### 9. Claude Code and Opus 4.5

- A **harness** is an application that you run a coding model through.
- In fall 2024, engineer Boris Cherny connected Claude to his laptop's command line and let it read files, write files and run programs. Asked what music he was listening to, Claude wrote code to inspect his Spotify window and answered correctly. Cherny's reaction: "oh, it can just do anything."
- Within two months, 40% of Anthropic's engineers used it as their main coding tool.
- Release timeline:
  - February 2025: research preview as Claude Code.
  - May 2025: general availability alongside Claude 4.
- The speaker argues that the release of Claude Opus 4.5 was the most significant moment in AI product history since ChatGPT. Its impact became clear after about a month and a holiday break:
  - One engineer: "I went and ate a burrito, I came back, and it was done."
  - David Holz (Midjourney): more personal coding projects over Christmas than in the previous 10 years.
  - Nikunj Kothari described "token anxiety." Dinners now opened with "how many agents do you have running?"
- On January 17 the Wall Street Journal ran "Claude is taking the AI world by storm." People talked of getting "Claude-pilled" and going on a "Claude-bender."
- At Davos, Dario said AI could do most or all software engineering end to end within 6–12 months. By late January, people at both Anthropic and OpenAI said close to 100% of their own code was AI-written.
- A personal agent called Clawdbot went viral in late January. After a trademark notice from Anthropic it was renamed twice, landing on **OpenClaw**, and it became the most popular project on GitHub by mid-February.

### 10. Assisted vs. agentic AI and the token business model

- **Assisted AI:** you ask, it answers, and you do the work. Usage is limited by your time.
- **Agentic AI:** you hand it a job and it does it. It splits the job into pieces, spins up sub-agents, checks its own work and keeps running, consuming tokens at every step.
- By late 2025, investors worried that hyperscaler capital spending and data-centre commitments outpaced revenue. The core question was whether AI demand and pricing would grow fast enough to match infrastructure spending. Under a per-seat model, the math "wasn't mathing."
- Per-seat economics: even a power user brings in roughly $30 a month.
- Per-token economics: a single heavy user can spend thousands of dollars a month. A company with 10 engineers each running 50 agents, possibly with thousands of sub-agents, is buying something very different from 10 seats.
- **The SaaSpocalypse:** the fear that companies could build their own software instead of renting SaaS. In the first week of February 2026, Anthropic released plugins for Cowork, its simplified Claude Code for non-coding knowledge workers launched in January. More than $1 trillion came off software stocks. The WSJ called it "the week Anthropic tanked the market."

### 11. Revenue trajectory and passing OpenAI

Anthropic's run-rate revenue:

| Date | Run-rate revenue |
|------|------------------|
| January 2024 | ~$87M |
| End of 2024 | ~$1B |
| End of 2025 | ~$9B |
| End of February 2026 | ~$14B |
| End of April 2026 | ~$30B |
| End of July 2026 | ~$65B |

- Dario said growth ran about eight times faster than internal forecasts.
- Menlo found that by mid-2025 Anthropic accounted for 32% of enterprise AI model usage, against OpenAI's 25%. In coding the share was 42%, double OpenAI's.
- By April 2026, driven by agentic coding, Anthropic was booking more revenue than OpenAI.
- The excerpt ends on a teaser: the U.S. government was also taking notice of Anthropic.

## Key Concepts

- **Public benefit corporation (PBC):** a for-profit company legally committed to a stated public mission alongside profit. Anthropic's version includes a trust that eventually picks most of the board.
- **AGI (artificial general intelligence):** an AI system that performs as well as or better than humans in most professional domains.
- **Interpretability / transformer circuits:** research that reverse-engineers the internal computations of neural networks.
- **Constitutional AI:** training a model to follow a written set of principles.
- **RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback):** training an assistant using human judgments of its outputs.
- **Red-teaming:** deliberately attacking your own models to find failure modes.
- **Effective altruism (EA):** a philanthropic movement linked to AI safety, discredited by association after the FTX collapse.
- **Context window:** the amount of text a model can consider at once, measured in tokens.
- **Token:** the unit of text that models process and that AI providers bill for.
- **Vibe coding:** Karpathy's term for building software by describing it in natural language without engaging with the code.
- **Harness:** an application through which a coding model is run and given tools, such as Claude Code.
- **Claude Code:** Anthropic's command-line agentic coding tool, which grew out of Boris Cherny's internal side project.
- **Cowork:** Anthropic's simplified version of Claude Code for non-coding knowledge workers.
- **Assisted AI:** AI you use to help you do work, where usage is bounded by human time.
- **Agentic AI:** AI that autonomously carries out jobs, including spawning sub-agents. Its usage, and so its revenue, scales without a per-person limit.
- **Per-seat vs. per-token pricing:** subscription revenue capped by headcount, versus usage-based revenue that scales with agent activity.
- **SaaSpocalypse:** the market fear that AI coding lets companies replace rented SaaS with software they build themselves.
- **Token anxiety:** the urge to keep as many agents running as possible.
- **OpenClaw:** a viral open-source personal agent, formerly Clawdbot, that popularised code-driven personal agents.

## Summary

NLW traces how Anthropic went from a safety-focused research lab that refused to build products to a company out-earning OpenAI. Founded as a PBC whose founders saw commercialisation as almost taboo, Anthropic built Claude in 2022 but held it back to avoid starting an arms race. Then, in November 2022, its biggest investor, Sam Bankman-Fried, was exposed as a fraud, and ChatGPT started the race anyway. Dario Amodei concluded that frontier safety research needed frontier models, and that only a product could pay for them. After a year of catching up, Anthropic pulled ahead by betting on coding, the use case where AI's value is measurable and largest. Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude Code and especially Opus 4.5 drove that shift. Agentic AI breaks the per-seat ceiling and sells by the token, and the speaker says this change in the business model reframed AI economics for Wall Street. It produced Anthropic's revenue climb from about $87 million to about $65 billion in run rate, and it made Anthropic the revenue leader by April 2026. One note on the title: it cites $81B a year, but the latest figure in this excerpt is the ~$65B run rate at the end of July 2026.
