## Overview

This episode of **The AI Daily Brief**, a daily podcast about AI news, is about **Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical, *Magnifica Humanitas*** ("On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence"). The host's main claim is that most social media reactions misread the document. In the host's view it is neither anti-AI nor pro-AI. It is an "AI realist" text that sets out basic principles for discussion. Its core argument is that **human value cannot be reduced to an intelligence benchmark**: even if AI becomes "smarter" than people, it stays a different kind of thing from them.

The episode starts with headlines: an update on Anthropic's Mythos rollout (Project Glasswing), a US intelligence-agency compute budget, DeepSeek's permanent price cut and funding round, and xAI's Grok updates.

## Prerequisites

- **Basic knowledge of frontier AI labs and models**: Anthropic (Claude, Opus, Mythos), OpenAI (GPT), DeepSeek and xAI (Grok).
- **Token pricing**: how commercial LLM access is billed per million input and output tokens.
- **Vulnerability disclosure norms**: the industry practice of waiting before publicly disclosing software bugs, which the transcript puts at three months.
- **Catholic social teaching basics**: what an encyclical is, and the role of *Rerum Novarum* (1891) in the Church's response to the Industrial Revolution.
- **Current AI debates**: AI cognition and personhood, transhumanism, AI-driven job loss, and concentration of data and power.

## Main Points

### Headline 1: Project Glasswing results for Anthropic's Mythos
- Anthropic released Mythos to about 50 partners in a limited way through **Project Glasswing**, which focuses on cybersecurity. The project has found **more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities**. Most partners found hundreds of severe bugs in just over a month. Testing on open-source repos showed a **false-positive rate below 10%**.
- Mozilla reported 271 vulnerabilities found and fixed, more than 10 times what it found with Opus 4.6. Palo Alto Networks' latest release had five times as many patches. In finance, one firm used Mythos to stop a $1.5 million fraudulent wire transfer.
- Anthropic says the bottleneck has moved from *finding* vulnerabilities to *verifying, disclosing and patching* them. Some maintainers have asked Anthropic to slow its disclosures.
- Box CEO Aaron Levie called this a microcosm of AI deployment in general. AI makes issues easier to find, but human triage and judgment are still needed. He expects a "security engineer boom" and called it "Jevons paradox all over again."
- European, UK, Canadian, Australian and Asian governments have all asked for access. Anthropic says no company yet has safeguards strong enough for a public release of a Mythos-class model. Testing Catalog spotted a "Claude Mythos 1 Preview" being prepared for Claude Code and Claude Security, probably for wider access by trusted partners.

### Headline 2: US intelligence agencies' $9 billion inference cluster
- The New York Times reports that the White House approved a secret **$9 billion budget request** from the CIA and NSA to build their own inference cluster with NVIDIA Blackwell chips. Their current classified systems cannot run the latest models.
- A White House spokesperson strongly criticised the leak but did not confirm the details.
- White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles reportedly approved NSA use of Anthropic models, even though the Pentagon has designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk. A contract for classified use is being finalised.
- The intelligence agencies are reportedly more flexible than the Pentagon on guardrails. They are already barred from collecting intelligence inside the US, so they are comfortable with outright bans on using AI for domestic surveillance.

### Headline 3: DeepSeek makes its discount permanent and raises funds
- DeepSeek V4 launched in April with a 75% token discount, which is now the standard price: **$0.44 per million input tokens and $0.87 per million output tokens**, for a model roughly equal to Opus 4.5.
- DeepSeek is raising **$10 billion at a $45 billion valuation**, its first outside funding. Expected participants include the Chinese government's AI Industry Investment Fund, Tencent and JD.com.
- Founder Liang Wenfeng says the company will stay focused on open-source models and AGI research, not on making money.
- Bloomberg analysts describe Asian AI moving toward a "token-based economy" with an "industrial approach" to AI. The host asks whether US companies facing tighter token budgets will turn to open-source alternatives.

### Headline 4: Grok V9 Medium and Grok Build
- Elon Musk announced that **Grok V9 Medium** (1.5 trillion parameters) has finished training, with additional training on Cursor data. Public release is expected in two to three weeks. It replaces the 0.5-trillion-parameter V8 Small that currently serves all Grok production traffic.
- **Grok Build**, xAI's competitor to Codex and Claude Code, is now available to Super Grok and X Premium subscribers. Before, it was limited to the $300/month Grok Heavy tier. Early user feedback is positive.

### What an encyclical is and why this one matters
- An encyclical is a major teaching letter from the Pope. They date back to physical letters passed between early Christian communities. They usually interpret existing doctrine in light of new circumstances rather than creating new doctrine.
- *Rerum Novarum* (Leo XIII, 1891) dealt with labour, capital, wages and workers' rights during the Industrial Revolution.
- Popes publish encyclicals about every two to three years. A first encyclical often signals the main themes of a papacy.
- Pope Leo XIV was elected in May 2025. He chose his name in tribute to Leo XIII and named AI as the central social question of his papacy, calling it "another industrial revolution." He is the first American pope and uses modern technology such as a phone, a computer and an Apple Watch.

### Reactions: many people unhappy from opposite directions
- The host recommends reading the document itself, echoing AI policy researcher Miles Brundage ("most of the takes are bad").
- **Critics who said the Church was too close to industry:** Timnit Gebru objected to an Anthropic representative speaking at the ceremonies.
- **Critics who said it was too hostile to markets:** entrepreneur Blake Scholl criticised the line that "the pursuit of greater profits cannot justify choices that systematically sacrifice jobs," arguing that tech revolutions create jobs as well as destroy them.
- **People who said it supported their views:** Senator Chris Murphy said AI threatens basic human functions. Karen Hao said the encyclical shows AI is produced by an exploitative supply chain and could fuel a new colonialism.
- **Critic on AI cognition:** Dean Ball called the encyclical's "axiomatic denial of AI cognition" a "punt of the highest order." He later clarified that he wants the Church to think about what humans should do once they are no longer the smartest entities on the planet. Others, such as Jonathan Lytle and Josh Barro, replied that it is unsurprising for the Pope to believe machines cannot have souls.

### What the encyclical actually argues, according to the host
- Catholic commentators read it most accurately. Christopher Hale called the Pope an **"AI realist"** who accepts that AI's growth is inevitable and wants it to serve the human person. The Catholic Sat account summarised it as: AI is "not inherently evil, but is never neutral." It risks concentrating power, increasing inequality and eroding human dignity, and the Church offers principles for discernment, not blanket rejection.
- The document places us "at the beginning of the beginning." Its warnings are about what *could* happen, and it sets out principles, not fixed conclusions.
- **Core plea:** real, living humans, not AI and not anonymous market forces, should be the measure of success for any technology.
- The jobs passage reflects a long-standing tension: markets as efficient allocators of resources versus markets as servants of human needs. AI intensifies that tension but did not create it.
- **"New colonialism" (paragraph 178):** this is a warning about tomorrow, not a claim about today. Personal data, including health data "often collected under the pretext of aid, research, and innovation," gives its holders structural leverage. Shared knowledge should become a common good, not an instrument of dominance.

### Main argument: human value is not an intelligence benchmark
- **Paragraph 99:** AI systems "merely imitate certain functions of human intelligence." They can exceed humans in speed and computation, but they do not have experiences, bodies, joy or pain, relationships, or moral conscience. Their "learning" is "statistical adaptation based on data and feedback," not inner growth through "choices, mistakes, forgiveness, and fidelity."
- The host argues that this view, though it may surprise AI researchers, matches what most people believe, including daily AI users. The host also expects the question to become more contested as AI advances, and sees the encyclical as **"a flag planting"** in preparation for that debate.
- **Paragraph 118:** society tends to treat limitation, illness, old age, suffering and vulnerability as "a defect to be corrected." The Pope argues that "humanity flourishes not despite limitations, but often through them."
- Together, these paragraphs lay the groundwork for an essentially **contra-transhumanist** position.

### Why it matters that the document exists
- Boyan Tungus is quoted (name as transcribed): "the most important thing about *Magnifica Humanitas* is that it exists." A major global institution has given AI the highest priority, and he hopes governments and professional bodies will do the same.
- The host notes that, despite some soundbite reactions, the discussion of this document has been better than usual AI discourse. The host encourages listeners to read it in full or discuss it with an AI.

## Key Concepts

- **Project Glasswing**: Anthropic's limited, security-focused rollout of the Mythos model to about 50 partners.
- **Mythos**: Anthropic's frontier model, not yet publicly released because of misuse concerns, especially in cybersecurity.
- **Jevons paradox**: the idea that making something cheaper or more efficient can increase total demand for it. Here it is applied to security engineering work.
- **"Any lawful use" clause**: the Pentagon's preferred contract language, which conflicts with Anthropic's limits on autonomous weapons and domestic surveillance.
- **Token-based economy**: the framing of AI tokens as tradable, industrial inputs, used to describe China's approach to AI.
- **Grok Build**: xAI's AI coding tool, a competitor to Codex and Claude Code.
- **Encyclical**: a major papal teaching letter that interprets existing Church doctrine for new circumstances.
- ***Rerum Novarum***: Leo XIII's 1891 encyclical on labour and capital during the Industrial Revolution, and the model for Leo XIV's focus on AI.
- ***Magnifica Humanitas***: Leo XIV's first encyclical, "On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence."
- **AI realism**: the view, attributed to the Pope, that AI's growth is inevitable and must be directed to serve the human person.
- **AI cognition / personhood**: the debated question of whether AI systems think or deserve moral status. The encyclical denies that they do.
- **New colonialism (data)**: the risk that whoever controls large stores of data, such as health data, gains structural power over whole populations.
- **Contra-transhumanism**: rejecting the idea that human limitations are defects for technology to remove.

## Summary

The host argues that *Magnifica Humanitas* has been widely misread through selective quotes. People across the spectrum attacked it for not supporting their views, or claimed it supported all of them. Read in full, it is an "AI realist" document. It does not call AI good or evil. It says AI is never neutral and sets out principles for discernment: human dignity first, markets that serve people, and watchfulness about data-driven power. Underneath all of this is one claim: however capable AI becomes, it imitates functions of human intelligence without experience, conscience or inner growth, so human value cannot be measured by intelligence. The host calls this a "flag planting" ahead of a debate that will grow as AI advances. The host values the document mainly because a major global institution has treated AI's impact on human life with this level of urgency, and urges listeners to read it themselves.
