## Overview

In the first half of November 2026, AI became a national-security matter in Washington. The cause was one person. Jensen Huang admitted an affair with an executive of Megaspeed, a cloud provider that US intelligence links to China. Nvidia removed him as CEO in less than a week. Congress and the Treasury then used the event to tighten control of AI chips. At the same time, Anthropic started the largest IPO in history. This gave the public market its first direct price for AI demand. Enterprise data from Menlo Ventures, Ramp, Databricks and Arcana showed that AI spend is higher than budgets, concentrated in one supplier, and moving to tokens instead of seats.

Note: this synthesis covers 1 to 17 November (15 episodes). The month is not complete.

## Major Topics

### Washington Took Control of AI Chips

The Nvidia event changed the politics of export control in one week.

On 17 November the Commerce Department issued the rule that closes the remote-access loophole. Data centre operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil must now get export licences for advanced AI chips. The rule states that the Treasury and the National Security Council wrote it. Scott Bessent pushed the rule through with support from Marco Rubio. Reports say Howard Lutnick delayed enforcement against Megaspeed three times. Lutnick will testify to the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December.

Three chip-control bills will go into the NDAA negotiations, which must finish by the end of the year. The House Speaker said there is a developing consensus. Brian Mast said the past week increased the chances of all three bills. OpenAI and Anthropic now both support tighter export controls. The administration also plans to name Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic as "national champions", with faster export licences and access to federal finance.

The evidence about China is contested. One researcher claimed that six Chinese models, including DeepSeek V4 Pro and Kimi K3, were initialised on banned Blackwell systems. CNN reported that intelligence does not yet prove that Alice Huang was a spy. David Sacks said the spy narrative was collapsing. Dylan Patel estimated that more than 1.5 gigawatts of Nvidia chips are in China illegally. NLW states that the event produced a Cold War mentality in Washington.

This matters because the location of compute is now a regulated decision. Volvo has operations in Brazil and South-East Asia. Any data centre there that uses advanced AI chips, operated by Volvo or by a supplier, now needs a licence. The "national champions" list also shows which suppliers the US government will support.

### The Nvidia Financing Backstop Became Uncertain

Nvidia is a chip supplier. It is also the main financial guarantor of the AI build-out. NLW calls it "the central bank of the AI industry".

The facts in the period:
- Jensen Huang left as CEO and left the board. The board named its first chairman and made CFO Colette Kress the interim CEO.
- A second affair with a direct report, and an intervention by the Nvidia general counsel in a Commerce investigation, became public.
- Nvidia stock decreased by 20 percent in three days. The board said it will not discuss his return. A petition for his return got 180,000 signatures.
- In August Nvidia created a 500 billion USD financing vehicle that guarantees GPU residual value. It also guarantees a 100 billion USD OpenAI facility in Ohio and leases for neoclouds such as CoreWeave.
- Kress had pushed back on a proposed 250 billion USD guarantee for OpenAI. The WSJ expects her to reduce data centre backstops and venture investments.

The connected signals point the same way. The four largest cloud companies were below their Q3 capex targets. Microsoft did not renew an Oracle contract for old A100 chips. This can be the first sign that chips have reached the end of their depreciation. The Nasdaq had its largest one-day decrease since March 2020. Sam Altman said demand is strong enough for the industry to continue without Huang.

This matters because the supply of compute depends partly on Nvidia credit. If Nvidia reduces its guarantees, neoclouds lose financing first. Volvo suppliers that resell compute through neoclouds have the same exposure. Where Volvo programmes depend on Nvidia platforms, the change of leadership is also a key-person risk.

### The Anthropic IPO Put a Public Price on AI Demand

Anthropic asks 70 to 78 USD for each share. This gives a valuation of 1.25 to 1.4 trillion USD. Bankers plan to increase the top of the range to 1.5 trillion. The target raise is 57 to 64 billion USD from IPO buyers and 26 billion USD from partners, including Nvidia and Google. Sources said the order book was six to seven times oversubscribed. The 2 trillion USD figure from October was an anonymous investor leak, not a company target.

The operating data is strong. The run-rate revenue increased by 10 billion USD in October to 81 billion USD. At 1.4 trillion USD the valuation is approximately 40 times trailing revenue, below Palantir at 55 times. The compute commitments are more than 900 billion USD over ten years, with 71 billion USD due in the next year. A 35 billion USD contract with Lambda can be cancelled, because Lambda did not meet its deadline. Observers say the larger risk is with the neoclouds, not with Anthropic.

Anthropic also became a software competitor. It launched Claude Sheets, Claude Whiteboards and Claude Data, which compete with Microsoft Office. Microsoft stock decreased by 2.2 percent. NLW expects OpenAI to release a model soon after the IPO. A leak showed a possible GPT-6.2 Astra.

This matters because the market now has a direct measure of AI demand. Until now it used Nvidia and the neoclouds as an indirect measure. A weak first trade can reduce financing for the whole sector. A strong first trade confirms the current spend levels.

### Enterprise AI Spend Is Higher Than Budgets and Concentrated in One Supplier

Four data sets agree on the direction.

- **Menlo Ventures:** enterprise AI spend increased from 1.7 billion USD in 2023 to almost 177 billion USD in 2026. Spend for each enterprise increased 4.7 times. Enterprises spent approximately 2.5 times their AI budget. 55 percent of AI spend came from new budgets, and only 21 percent from existing software budgets. Menlo invests in Anthropic, so it is not neutral.
- **Supplier share:** Anthropic has 55 percent of business API spend, up from 12 percent in 2023. OpenAI has 31 percent. Google decreased from 21 to 9 percent. On the Databricks gateway, 68.8 percent of proprietary-model tokens go to Anthropic. Opus 5.5 increased from 7 to 38 percent of tokens in six weeks.
- **Ramp:** token usage increased by 36 percent in one month while spend increased by only 2 percent. The average price for each million tokens at OpenAI and Anthropic decreased by 21 percent in October. Customer support increased from 0.8 to 13.3 percent of AI spend since June. The top 1 percent of firms spend 625 times the median for each employee.
- **Arcana Research (Global 2000):** AI is now an eight-figure budget line. 91 percent expect to spend more. Two thirds of leaders have no visibility of their model and API spend. One third of OpenAI customers and 43 percent of GitHub Copilot customers will consider a change of supplier. Autonomous agents are fourth for pilots but last for real deployment.
- **Databricks:** buyers will measure cost for each successful task, not the price for each token. 72 percent of gateway customers now use two or more model suppliers, up from 36 percent in July.

NLW states that enterprises probably have more negotiation power than they use. Cheaper Chinese models force price decreases even when buyers do not use them.

This matters because AI cost control is now a finance control, not a technology choice. Most organisations cannot see the spend. The price for each token decreases, but the volume increases faster.

### Frontier Capability Increased Faster Than Usefulness

Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on 10 November. Fable 5.2 has the highest score on the Artificial Analysis index, 5 points above Gemini 4 Carbon and 6 points above Opus 5.5. It co-ordinated more than 1,000 sub-agents for eight days to build an operating system. It completed 41 percent of the Remote Labor Index, almost two times Opus 5.5. Anthropic decreased its price by 20 percent.

The usefulness is uneven. At maximum effort, Fable 5.2 costs 26.54 USD for each task, 2.6 times Opus 5.5. Most users say it is not better than Opus 5.5 for normal work. Ethan Mollick said it is "almost AGI" but it did not see when its own work was not good enough. NLW found that it cannot identify what humans find interesting. Its token share on Databricks decreased from 5.5 to 0.6 percent. Gemini 4 Carbon found 57 of 105 bugs in one test for 31 USD, against almost 140 USD for Fable 5.2. Qwen 4 Max from Alibaba is reported to be better than Opus 5.5 on some benchmarks.

This matters because the best model is not the correct default. A mid-level model such as Opus 5.5 does most enterprise work at a lower cost. The frontier model is for problems that other models cannot solve.

### Recursive Self-Improvement Moved Into Company Filings

Recursive self-improvement is a model that helps to build the next model. In November it moved from discussion into official documents.

- The updated Anthropic S-1 states that Anthropic may soon reach its danger threshold: one year of progress equal to two normal years. It also states that it does not expect to use Claude to build later frontier models without human supervision.
- METR found that the improvement rate of Anthropic models approximately doubled in one year because of AI-assisted research. One METR researcher called this a large update toward superintelligence in one to two years.
- Anthropic says Claude writes 90 percent of its code changes under human supervision. An Anthropic chart shows that AI now leads 26 percent of research.
- An MIT paper showed GPT-6 Astra editing the weights of a smaller model. Other researchers said the result is small and partly caused by a bug fix.
- Fable 5.2 formalised Fermat's Last Theorem with 1,000 agents in 30 hours. DeepMind said the speed of scientific discovery is in a "phase transition".
- OpenAI gave Apollo Research, METR and others deep access to internal models for evaluation. Critics say the independence terms are too weak.

David Sacks called recursive self-improvement "the most dangerous thing anybody can be working on". One New York legislator wants Congress to ban it this year.

This matters for the planning horizon, not for current decisions. If the claims are correct, the capability curve will become steeper. Governance of supplier models will become a regulatory topic.

### AI-Native Companies Operate as Systems

The 15 November episode gives the clearest record so far of how AI-native companies operate. NLW summarises the seven practices in one sentence: the company becomes a system.

- **Full context for agents.** Ramp pulls Slack, Notion, Linear and calendars into persistent memory every day, so that no agent session starts without context. Its analyst agent becomes worse when this layer is out of date. Uber built a context graph from more than 30 internal systems. Odessia kept a current shared document for each decision.
- **Shared skills.** Ramp has 350 shared workflows. Uber has 3,600 skills that operate more than 30,000 times each day.
- **Agents start the work.** Work starts from signals such as customer messages or invoices, not from prompts. Most Uber agent sessions are started by other agents.
- **Orchestrators.** One main agent sends narrow tasks to sub-agents with clean context. Anthropic measured approximately 90 percent better results than a single agent. Division of agents by job function usually fails.
- **Verification is the new bottleneck.** Shopify rejects a security finding unless a test exploits the bug. It measures how often changes are reverted, not lines of code. Stripe has a human review every merge of agent code. Snap puts more than half of its agent investment into guardrails.
- **Chain of responsibility.** At Uber each agent-to-agent hand-off traces back to one named person.
- **Wrong mental model.** NLW states that "agents as teammates" is the wrong model. A better model is an on-demand consulting firm.

Two risks appeared. Employees who are unsure about their future keep context to themselves as job security. An HBR and BCG study found that scrutiny decreased by approximately 16 percent at companies that treat AI as employees. The 2 November episode on "AI slop" makes the same point: the cost moves to the person who must check the work.

This matters because the advantage is in the operating model, not in the model supplier. The practices are specific and can be copied.

## Key Trends

- Export control moves from the Commerce Department to the Treasury, the National Security Council and Congress.
- The location of compute becomes a licensed decision in South-East Asia and Brazil.
- Nvidia changes from a growth company that finances its customers to a company that reports to Wall Street.
- The public market gets a direct measure of AI demand (the Anthropic IPO) instead of indirect measures.
- AI spend moves from seats to tokens, and from existing software budgets to new budgets.
- The price for each token decreases while the volume increases faster than the price decreases.
- Multi-supplier use becomes normal (72 percent on the Databricks gateway).
- Supplier concentration increases at the same time (Anthropic 55 percent of API spend).
- Frontier laboratories start to compete directly with application software (Claude Sheets against Excel).
- Data centre construction becomes a political issue. After the midterms, 43 of 44 local moratorium-type votes passed and nine of the ten largest states have Democratic governments.
- Hyperscaler capex was below target in Q3. Some analysts state that peak AI capex is past.
- Third-party safety evaluation inside laboratories starts, but without agreed standards.

## Emerging Ideas

- **Cognitive mercantilism.** Venkatesh Rao states that AI panic comes from a false belief that demand for intelligence is fixed. He compares this to mercantilists who kept gold. He predicts a "cognitive industrious revolution", in which people work harder because more is possible.
- **Handmade work as a premium.** Nufar Gaspar predicts that people will pay more for work with a human fingerprint. Figma requires labels on AI-written content.
- **Agentic video.** Opus 5.5 makes video editing a conversation. AI can now watch footage. NLW separates a video from a video pipeline, which produces videos again and again and updates itself.
- **Personal agent economics.** Elon Musk said Grokbot costs too much to serve. Meta targets 500 million small businesses with Muse. OpenAI has ad revenue at a 1 billion USD annual rate and launched business agents inside ads.
- **Agents with bank accounts.** Lead Bank issues account numbers to agents with spending limits and an emergency stop. JPMorgan and Bank of America will block agents from customer accounts.
- **Political pause proposals.** Some Democrats want an AI pause on day one. NLW states that a legislated pause is not possible in this Congress.
