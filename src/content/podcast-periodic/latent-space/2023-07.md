## Overview

July on Latent Space followed two big releases with emergency Twitter Spaces: ChatGPT Code Interpreter reaching all paying users and Meta's Llama 2. Around them, the hosts and guests kept returning to data: how training sets are built, why quality beats size, and what synthetic data can do. Tri Dao closed the month with a systems view of making attention fast.

## Main themes

### Code Interpreter as the first real agent

- Simon Willison calls Code Interpreter “the most exciting tool in AI at the moment” because it writes code, runs it, reads the errors and retries, sometimes five or six rounds without his input ([Code Interpreter](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/code-interpreter-gpt-4-5-w-simon-willison-alex-volkov-aravind-srinivas/)).
- swyx calls it the most advanced agent he has seen. Willison compares it to a coding intern that is smart and stupid at once but never gives up ([Code Interpreter](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/code-interpreter-gpt-4-5-w-simon-willison-alex-volkov-aravind-srinivas/)).
- Willison analysed about 250,000 San Francisco police calls from a single prompt. Speakers list the sandbox limits: no network access, a 100 MB upload cap and locked-down subprocesses ([Code Interpreter](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/code-interpreter-gpt-4-5-w-simon-willison-alex-volkov-aravind-srinivas/)).
- Several speakers suspect a separately fine-tuned GPT-4 behind it, and one participant sees business analysts as the killer use case if it gets direct data access ([Code Interpreter](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/code-interpreter-gpt-4-5-w-simon-willison-alex-volkov-aravind-srinivas/)).

### Llama 2 and open models

- The panel agrees the commercial licence matters most. Willison says “Meta kept their promise of doing a version of Llama that is use- usable for commercial purposes” ([Llama 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/llama-2-the-new-open-llm-sota-ft-nathan-lambert-matt-bornstein-anton-t/)).
- Alessio lists 7B, 13B and 70B models on 2 trillion tokens, with a 34B model held back for lack of red-teaming time. Nathan Lambert notes detailed RLHF methods but almost nothing on the training data ([Llama 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/llama-2-the-new-open-llm-sota-ft-nathan-lambert-matt-bornstein-anton-t/)).
- Matt Bornstein expects startups to route work between GPT-4 and fine-tuned open models. Tri Dao also sees the licence as a positive shift away from a few API providers ([Llama 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/llama-2-the-new-open-llm-sota-ft-nathan-lambert-matt-bornstein-anton-t/), [FlashAttention 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/flashattention-2-making-transformers-800-faster-w-o-approximation-with/)).

### Data quality and synthetic data

- swyx and Alessio push back on the idea that GPT is trained on the whole internet. Common Crawl covers only part of the web, misses JavaScript-rendered pages and is only 46% English ([Datasets 101](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/ai-fundamentals-datasets-101/)).
- They trace scaling ratios from Kaplan to Chinchilla to about 200x tokens per parameter, and cite Phi-1 matching much larger code models as evidence that curation now matters more than size ([Datasets 101](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/ai-fundamentals-datasets-101/)).
- Ronen Eldan and Yuanzhi Li show that models under 33 million parameters, trained on GPT-4-written TinyStories, learn coherent English and basic reasoning. They argue reasoning arrives late because web text rarely requires it ([Tiny Models](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/cognitive-revolution-the-tiny-model-revolution-with-ronen-eldan-and-yu/)).
- swyx notes that under 1% of Segment Anything's masks were human-generated ([AI Trends](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/practical-ai-ai-trends-a-latent-space-x-practical-ai-crossover-pod/)).

### Evaluation and enterprise practice

- Dan Whitenack argues enterprises need a new workflow of prompting, chaining, structured output and evaluation before fine-tuning, and says most stop at a single prompt ([AI Trends](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/practical-ai-ai-trends-a-latent-space-x-practical-ai-crossover-pod/)).
- Alessio notes that benchmarks are mostly multiple choice while production work is open-ended. Tri Dao says “evaluation is important and often a little bit underrated” ([AI Trends](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/practical-ai-ai-trends-a-latent-space-x-practical-ai-crossover-pod/), [FlashAttention 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/flashattention-2-making-transformers-800-faster-w-o-approximation-with/)).

### Faster attention through systems work

- Dao explains that attention is slow because of memory traffic. FlashAttention keeps data in on-chip SRAM, giving linear memory and a 2-4x speed-up with exact results ([FlashAttention 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/flashattention-2-making-transformers-800-faster-w-o-approximation-with/)).
- FlashAttention 2 is about twice as fast again. Dao is personally bullish on state space models and RNNs such as RWKV, though unproven at scale ([FlashAttention 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/07/flashattention-2-making-transformers-800-faster-w-o-approximation-with/)).
