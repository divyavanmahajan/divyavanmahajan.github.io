## Overview

Latent Space spent 2026 following coding agents as they wrote whole codebases and then spread into general knowledge work. Much of the year covered what agents need around the model: harnesses, sandboxes and clouds. Evals were a second thread, as SWE-bench Verified was declared dead. AI for science and world models ran alongside all year.

## Main themes

### Coding agents take over the codebase

- Doug O'Laughlin says about 4% of public GitHub commits were signed by Claude Code, later about five ([Claude Code for Finance](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/claude-code-for-finance-the-global-memory-shortage-doug-o-laughlin-sem/)).
- Cursor engineers describe a third era of cloud agents, with so many agents pushing code that CI/CD got overloaded ([Cursor's Third Era](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/cursor-s-third-era-cloud-agents/)).
- Ryan Lopopolo's team shipped over a million lines with no hand-written code, making the engineer's job harness engineering ([Extreme Harness Engineering](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/04/extreme-harness-engineering-for-token-billionaires-1m-loc-1b-toks-day/)).
- By July OpenAI had merged the Codex harness into ChatGPT Work after non-developers adopted it ([Codex from 0 to 10M Users](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/07/codex-from-0-to-10m-users-building-chatgpt-work-akshay-nathan-openai/)).

### Harnesses, computers and clouds for agents

- Aaron Levie says of coding: “The agent didn't really adapt to how we work. We basically adapted to how the agent works” ([Every Agent Needs a Box](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/every-agent-needs-a-box-aaron-levie-box/)).
- Felix Rieseberg argues agents work best with their own computer next to the user's files ([Claude Cowork](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/03/why-anthropic-thinks-ai-should-have-its-own-computer-felix-rieseberg-o/)), while Ivan Burazin bets on fast Windows sandboxes for computer use ([Daytona](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/05/giving-agents-computers-ivan-burazin-daytona/)).
- Satya Nadella wants the harness to define the models, data and tools in one loop ([Satya Nadella](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/satya-nadella-no-priors-x-latent-space-crossover-special-at-microsoft/)). Anthropic's platform leads keep the harness apart from its sandbox so agents are secure by default ([Brain from the Hands](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/how-anthropic-ships-the-1-llm-in-the-world-safely-separating-the-brain/)).
- At Modal, sandboxes grew into a third of revenue ([Modal CEO](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/why-ai-compute-comes-in-rectangles-erik-bernhardsson-modal-ceo/)).

### Evals under strain

- Anastasios Angelopoulos pitches LMArena as an always-fresh leaderboard ([LMArena](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/state-of-evals-lmarena-s-1-7b-vision-anastasios-angelopoulos-lmarena/)).
- OpenAI's Mia Glaese and Olivia Watkins call SWE-bench Verified saturated and contaminated ([End of SWE-Bench Verified](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/the-end-of-swe-bench-verified-mia-glaese-olivia-watkins-openai-frontie/)).
- Andon Labs lets models run businesses ([Reality: The Final Eval](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/reality-the-final-eval-lukas-petersson-and-axel-backlund-of-andon-labs/)); Richard Socher stresses reward engineering against reward hacking ([Recursive](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/humanity-s-last-invention-richard-socher-of-recursive/)).

### Lab economics and distillation

- Martin Casado and Sarah Wang argue frontier labs could outraise the app ecosystem built on them ([Bitter Lessons](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/bitter-lessons-in-venture-vs-growth-anthropic-vs-openai-noam-shazeer-w/)).
- Jeff Dean says “you have to have the frontier model in order to then distill it into your, your smaller model” ([Jeff Dean](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/02/owning-the-ai-pareto-frontier-jeff-dean/)).

### AI for science and world models

- Andrew White aims to automate the scientific method with agent loops and a shared world model, and says “I overestimate the speed of things on month scale, and I underestimate things on year scale” ([Automating Science](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/01/automating-science-world-models-scientific-taste-agent-loops-andrew-wh/)).
- Materials guests at Radical AI and Periodic Labs argue experiments, not models, are the bottleneck ([Self-Driving Lab](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/06/the-self-driving-lab-joseph-krause-radical-ai/), [Periodic Labs](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/10/synthesis-superintelligence-from-semiconductors-to-superconductors-per/)).
- Moonlake and Runway disagree on whether scaled video prediction is enough for world models ([Moonlake](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/04/moonlake-causal-world-models-should-be-multimodal-interactive-and-effi/), [Runway](/podcasts/latent-space/2026/09/runway-s-worldprompt-and-the-engineering-of-real-time-worlds/)).
