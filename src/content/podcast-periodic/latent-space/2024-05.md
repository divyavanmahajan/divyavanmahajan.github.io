## Overview

May 2024 was a short, research-heavy month for Latent Space with three episodes and no shared thread between them. One covered how Gradient stretched Llama 3's context window to a million tokens, another toured ICLR 2024 award talks, and the third looked at an unusual production agent business built on open-source models.

## Main themes

### Long context in practice

- Mark Huang says Gradient picked Llama 3 because its 8,000-token context was the main criticism, and its 15 trillion training tokens suggested it would adapt well. One million tokens was a North Star, not a promise ([Million Context LLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/how-to-train-a-million-context-llm-with-mark-huang-of-gradient-ai/)).
- The key setting was RoPE theta: raising it lets the model interpolate positions rather than extrapolate. Huang says you know scaling works “if the early steps in the perplexity go straight down” ([Million Context LLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/how-to-train-a-million-context-llm-with-mark-huang-of-gradient-ai/)).
- He stresses data that forces the model to use the whole window, and links the quality drop at four million tokens to floating-point precision as theta grows ([Million Context LLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/how-to-train-a-million-context-llm-with-mark-huang-of-gradient-ai/)).
- Huang calls needle in a haystack a basic test and prefers the harder Ruler suite. He sees evaluation as the open problem now ([Million Context LLM](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/how-to-train-a-million-context-llm-with-mark-huang-of-gradient-ai/)).

### Representations as compression at ICLR

- Kingma and Welling's VAE paper won the first ICLR Test of Time Award. Kingma presents diffusion models as a special case of the VAE and names a VAE at the largest scales as an open problem ([ICLR 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-imagegen-vision-transformers-state-space-m/)).
- In a Simons Institute clip, Ilya Sutskever says “compression is prediction. Every compressor can be turned to a predictor and vice versa.” He frames unsupervised learning as joint compression of datasets ([ICLR 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-imagegen-vision-transformers-state-space-m/)).
- Christian Szegedy of xAI calls the adversarial examples paper his proudest result and his worst-written paper, and says he never tells young researchers an idea is trivial ([ICLR 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-imagegen-vision-transformers-state-space-m/)).
- Other talks covered register tokens for vision transformers, KV cache savings, and state space models; a closing paper tempers the claim that SSMs beat transformers ([ICLR 2024](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/iclr-2024-best-papers-talks-imagegen-vision-transformers-state-space-m/)).

### Agents that emulate a person

- Jesse Silver built a tool that takes over one-on-one fan chat for creators on adult fan platforms, where he says about 80% of a profile's revenue comes from chatting ([NSFW Chatbots](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/emulating-humans-with-nsfw-chatbots-with-jesse-silver/)).
- A friendly bot earned almost nothing for a creator whose brand was being inaccessible. Silver says it was “puncturing the fantasy that the creator is inaccessible”, so the agent now models each creator's archetype ([NSFW Chatbots](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/emulating-humans-with-nsfw-chatbots-with-jesse-silver/)).
- Cost and restrictions pushed the team from OpenAI to fine-tuned open-source models, a state machine and DSPy, with layered evals and hard price limits as guardrails ([NSFW Chatbots](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/05/emulating-humans-with-nsfw-chatbots-with-jesse-silver/)).
