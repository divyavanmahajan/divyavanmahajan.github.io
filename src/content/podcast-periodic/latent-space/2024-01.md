## Overview

January on Latent Space mixed a look back at 2023 with three deep dives. swyx and Alessio Fanelli sorted the year into four wars over data, GPUs, multimodality and RAG tooling. Nathan Lambert explained RLHF and DPO, Hugging Face's M4 team described training an open multimodal model, and Steve Ruiz showed how tldraw's canvas became a place to prompt GPT-4 with Vision.

## Main themes

### The four wars and inference economics

- swyx frames 2023 as four contests: data, GPU rich versus poor, multimodality, and RAG/ops tooling. He says agents were less hot than at the start of the year ([Four Wars](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-four-wars-of-the-ai-stack-dec-2023-audio-recap/)).
- After Mixtral's release, prices fell sharply. Using Dylan Patel's analysis, swyx puts break-even at about 50-75 cents per million tokens: “anyone charging under 50, definitely losing money” ([Four Wars](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-four-wars-of-the-ai-stack-dec-2023-audio-recap/)).
- He argues production buyers optimise for latency, uptime and throughput, not just cost, and sees Apple's MLX as a sign of local inference on device ([Four Wars](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-four-wars-of-the-ai-stack-dec-2023-audio-recap/)).

### Data: lawsuits, walled gardens and synthetic data

- swyx expects the New York Times case against OpenAI to define fair use for AI. He calls Reddit and Stack Overflow locking up data to train their own models locally optimal but globally suboptimal ([Four Wars](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-four-wars-of-the-ai-stack-dec-2023-audio-recap/)).
- Fanelli borrows Jeff Huber's idea of low background tokens: pre-ChatGPT web text, uncontaminated by model output ([Four Wars](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-four-wars-of-the-ai-stack-dec-2023-audio-recap/)).
- Lambert calls GPT-4 labelling very cheap next to humans, but warns about contamination and terms of service. The M4 team released WebSight, a synthetic image-to-HTML dataset ([RLHF 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/rlhf-201-with-nathan-lambert-of-ai2-and-interconnects/), [Multimodal](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/how-to-train-your-own-large-multimodal-model-with-hugo-lauren-on-leo-t/)).

### RLHF is mostly preference data

- Lambert argues the RL part of RLHF is small; preference data, reward models and infrastructure matter more. He estimates Llama 2's preference data at about $6-8 million, comparable to compute ([RLHF 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/rlhf-201-with-nathan-lambert-of-ai2-and-interconnects/)).
- He says “DPO is closer to RLHF than RLHF is to RL”, expects DPO models to dominate open releases for six months, and notes most open DPO work uses the UltraFeedback dataset ([RLHF 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/rlhf-201-with-nathan-lambert-of-ai2-and-interconnects/)).
- Lambert says AI2 under Ali Farhadi is moving from papers only to releasing open models with data and code, and being active in policy ([RLHF 201](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/rlhf-201-with-nathan-lambert-of-ai2-and-interconnects/)).

### Open multimodal models and canvas interfaces

- Hugo Laurençon and Leo Tronchon built OBELICS and IDEFICS, an open 80B reproduction of Flamingo. They say data curation, resolution and good backbones matter more than architecture ([Multimodal](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/how-to-train-your-own-large-multimodal-model-with-hugo-lauren-on-leo-t/)).
- IDEFICS 2 moves to Mistral and SigLIP with a commercial-friendly licence, and matched the 80B model's performance at 9B. They expect only multimodal models within two years ([Multimodal](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/how-to-train-your-own-large-multimodal-model-with-hugo-lauren-on-leo-t/)).
- Steve Ruiz's Make Real sends wireframes, text and prior HTML to GPT-4 with Vision and drew about 22 million views in 30 days. He predicts collaborative whiteboards will become a commodity feature, like Kanban boards ([tldraw](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/01/the-accidental-ai-canvas-with-steve-ruiz-of-tldraw/)).
