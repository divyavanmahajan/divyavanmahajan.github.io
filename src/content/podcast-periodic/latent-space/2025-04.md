## Overview

April on Latent Space had three episodes with three separate subjects: a protocol, a market and a model. The creators of the Model Context Protocol explained its design, Evan Conrad of SF Compute laid out the economics of GPU clouds, and two OpenAI post-training researchers presented GPT-4.1.

## Main themes

### MCP as a protocol for AI applications

- Justin Spahr-Summers and David Soria Parra describe MCP as a way to extend AI applications, not models, with an ecosystem of integrations. The idea came from Soria Parra's frustration at copying code between Claude Desktop and his IDE in July 2024 ([MCP creators](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/the-creators-of-model-context-protocol/)).
- They argue that tool calling is “necessary but very insufficient” for application developers. MCP adds resources and user-initiated prompts, although tools make up about 95% of integrations ([MCP creators](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/the-creators-of-model-context-protocol/)).
- A useful server takes about half an hour, often 100-200 lines. The new streamable HTTP transport replaces long-lived SSE connections, and the draft spec uses OAuth 2.1 ([MCP creators](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/the-creators-of-model-context-protocol/)).
- The guests want to keep the protocol conservative and are wary of a standards body. Pydantic, Microsoft, JetBrains and Spring AI already hold commit rights on SDKs ([MCP creators](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/the-creators-of-model-context-protocol/)).

### The economics of GPU clouds

- Evan Conrad argues GPU buyers are extremely price sensitive because every extra GPU earns revenue. A 10% premium on a billion dollars of hardware is a hundred million dollars, so customers will not pay for cloud software ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/sf-compute-commoditizing-compute-to-solve-the-gpu-bubble-forever/)).
- He says CoreWeave succeeded with locked-in long-term contracts to low-credit-risk customers and cheap borrowing against them. In his words, “It's a bank. It's a bank. It's, it's a real estate company.” ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/sf-compute-commoditizing-compute-to-solve-the-gpu-bubble-forever/)).
- SF Compute runs a spot market where unsold blocks fall in price before they expire. Conrad's goal is an index price that supports a cash-settled future, which he sees as risk reduction rather than speculation ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/sf-compute-commoditizing-compute-to-solve-the-gpu-bubble-forever/)).
- He attributes the H100 glut to delayed clusters coming online together, not falling demand, and expects test-time inference to push back towards shortage ([SF Compute](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/sf-compute-commoditizing-compute-to-solve-the-gpu-bubble-forever/)).

### GPT-4.1 and post-training

- Michelle Pokrass and Josh McGrath present GPT-4.1, Mini and Nano, built for developers, with better instruction following and coding and OpenAI's first million-token context models. The model was tested on OpenRouter as Quasar Alpha ([GPT-4.1](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/gpt-4-1-the-new-openai-workhorse/)).
- The guests say “we're able to squeeze a lot more out of post-training now”, while the vision gains came from pre-training ([GPT-4.1](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/gpt-4-1-the-new-openai-workhorse/)).
- Josh says single needle-in-a-haystack tests saturated quickly, so most work went into harder long-context reasoning evals that OpenAI open-sourced. OpenAI expects developers to upload full context instead of using RAG for smaller tasks ([GPT-4.1](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/gpt-4-1-the-new-openai-workhorse/)).
- 4.1 scores 55 on SWE-bench against about 41 for o1, and extraneous code edits fell from 9% with 4o to 2% ([GPT-4.1](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/04/gpt-4-1-the-new-openai-workhorse/)).
