## Overview

November on Latent Space was dominated by OpenAI's first DevDay, covered in a recap episode (published twice, once with cleaned audio) with builders reacting to GPT-4 Turbo, the Assistants API and GPTs. Around it, three guests looked at retrieval, open models and compute: Phind on beating GPT-4 for programmers, Hex on treating RAG as a recommender, and SemiAnalysis on who has the GPUs.

## Main themes

### DevDay: more than expected, rougher than it looked

- Simon Willison and Alex Volkov walked through GPT-4 Turbo with a 128k context window, the Assistants API with stateful threads, GPTs, vision, text-to-speech and JSON mode ([DevDay Recap](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-openai-devday-w-simon-willison-ale/)).
- Willison judged the releases at about 80% of what the keynote implied. Assistants accept 20 files of up to 512 MB, and the hosts cited Jeremy Howard's figure of 20 cents per gigabyte per assistant per day for storage, far above S3 ([DevDay Recap, cleaned audio](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-devday-recap-cleaned-audio/)).
- On security, Willison said the Zapier and browsing demo was, in his words, “a prompt injection nightmare”. The only safeguard he found was marking an action as consequential so the user must confirm it ([DevDay Recap, cleaned audio](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-devday-recap-cleaned-audio/)).
- Jim Fan estimated $180 to run all eight Harry Potter films through GPT-4V at one frame per second, and framed the year's theme as customization by API, dialogue and data ([DevDay Recap](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-openai-devday-w-simon-willison-ale/)).

### Startups in OpenAI's path

- swyx argued OpenAI is “Sherlocking a lot of the startup features”, comparing the launch to a red wedding for RAG and agent startups ([DevDay Recap, cleaned audio](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-devday-recap-cleaned-audio/)).
- Plugin developer Surya Dantuluri saw plugins vanish from the new UI in favour of GPTs. Rahul of Julius said he launched after Code Interpreter and still reached 100,000 users ([DevDay Recap](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-openai-devday-w-simon-willison-ale/)).
- Raza Habib said OpenAI's repeated price cuts build developer confidence, and make self-hosting even small fine-tuned models less obviously economical ([DevDay Recap, cleaned audio](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-devday-recap-cleaned-audio/)).

### Retrieval is the product

- Michael Royzen says models do not know what they do not know, so they must always search: “any model plus web search is just significantly better than that model itself” ([Phind](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/beating-gpt-4-with-open-source-llms-with-michael-royzen-of-phind/)).
- Habib called OpenAI's drop-in document retrieval gimmicky for enterprises, which need control over chunking and per-user permissions. Willison wanted OpenAI to document how its built-in RAG works ([DevDay Recap, cleaned audio](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-devday-recap-cleaned-audio/)).
- Bryan Bischof maps retrieval to the multi-stage recommender of retrieval, ranking and serving. Hex builds warehouse-aware retrieval because models hallucinate tables and miss joins ([Hex Magic](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/notebooks-chat-and-rag-recsys-with-bryan-bischof-of-hex-magic/)).

### Open models, fine-tuning and evals

- Royzen expects the gap between proprietary and open models to shrink in 2024. Phind's Code Llama 34B model tops the BigCode leaderboard, and he considers HumanEval contaminated ([Phind](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/beating-gpt-4-with-open-source-llms-with-michael-royzen-of-phind/)).
- Bischof and Habib both treat fine-tuning as a late step. Bischof says most gains come from evals, retrieval and post-processing, and tracks auxiliary metrics such as hallucinated tables ([Hex Magic](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/notebooks-chat-and-rag-recsys-with-bryan-bischof-of-hex-magic/), [DevDay Recap](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/agi-is-being-achieved-incrementally-openai-devday-w-simon-willison-ale/)).

### Compute and the GPU poor

- Dylan Patel argues everyone is GPU poor relative to scaling ambitions, and that efficient infrastructure is becoming the main advantage ([SemiAnalysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/the-state-of-silicon-and-the-gpu-poors-with-dylan-patel-of-semianalysi/)).
- He says memory bandwidth utilisation matters more than MFU for inference, expects Nvidia and Google TPUs to stay dominant, and calls the chip supply chain fragile given expectations about Taiwan ([SemiAnalysis](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/11/the-state-of-silicon-and-the-gpu-poors-with-dylan-patel-of-semianalysi/)).

- Rising: **GPT-4**: 3 of 5 episodes (0 of 6 before); **Open-weight models**: 3 of 5 episodes (0 of 6 before); **Fine-tuning**: 4 of 5 episodes (2 of 6 before).
- Fading: **LangChain**: 0 of 5 episodes (4 of 6 before); **Contextual AI**: 0 of 5 episodes (2 of 6 before); **Llama 2**: 0 of 5 episodes (2 of 6 before).
