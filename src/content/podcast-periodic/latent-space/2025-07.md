## Overview

July on Latent Space spread across six separate subjects with no shared thread. Two episodes went deep on research: how much information embeddings and weights hold, and where reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards came from. The others covered AI search, an open source coding agent, AI video creators and an AI language tutor.

## Main themes

### AI search and the agent experience

- Robert of Scrunch AI argues that AI is replacing web browsing, not just search, so businesses must make their content useful to agents inside ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude ([AI is Eating Search](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-is-eating-search/)).
- He says most AI search still runs traditional retrieval such as BM25 and PageRank, then re-ranks. Pages with clickbait metadata often get re-ranked out, and most AI retrievers do not execute JavaScript ([AI is Eating Search](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-is-eating-search/)).
- Robert calls ChatGPT by far the strongest consumer AI platform and says many current behaviours “work that way sort of by accident” ([AI is Eating Search](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-is-eating-search/)).
- His advice is clear writing, structured content and FAQs. He cites Clerk's sixfold rise in ChatGPT traffic and warns that prompt injection on product pages will be penalised ([AI is Eating Search](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-is-eating-search/)).

### An open source coding agent that spends on context

- Saoud Rizwan and Pash describe Cline as an open source, bring-your-own-key agent with no margin on inference, so its incentive is to build the best agent rather than cut costs ([Cline](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/cline-the-open-source-coding-agent-that-doesn-t-cut-costs/)).
- They call RAG a distraction for code and say fast-apply models are fading, since diff-edit failures with Claude Sonnet 4 fell to about four percent ([Cline](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/cline-the-open-source-coding-agent-that-doesn-t-cut-costs/)).
- Cline uses summarisation plus a carried-forward to-do list instead of truncation. Revenue is to come from enterprise governance, and the project has around 6,000 forks ([Cline](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/cline-the-open-source-coding-agent-that-doesn-t-cut-costs/)).

### Measuring what models know and how they learn

- Jack Morris argues deep learning lacks an equivalent of Shannon's bit. His work showed embeddings can be inverted back to text, and that GPT-style transformers store about 3.6 to 3.9 bits per parameter ([Information Theory](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/information-theory-for-language-models-jack-morris/)).
- Morris also aligns embeddings from different models without paired data, and argues that AI's paradigm shifts came from new datasets more than new methods ([Information Theory](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/information-theory-for-language-models-jack-morris/)).
- Nathan Lambert traces RLVR to the Tulu 3 effort at AI2 and says data and environment design matter more than the algorithm. He names planning, tool use, calibration and reward hacking in code as the open problems ([RLVR Revolution](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/the-rlvr-revolution-with-nathan-lambert-ai2-interconnects-ai/)).
- Lambert says academics must move from papers to artifacts such as models and evals, while admitting a nonprofit struggles to fund a frontier open model ([RLVR Revolution](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/the-rlvr-revolution-with-nathan-lambert-ai2-interconnects-ai/)).

### Consumer AI: video creators and language tutors

- Olivia and Justine Moore of a16z say AI video has gone mainstream since Veo 3, with viral content now starting on TikTok and Instagram ([AI Video](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-video-is-eating-the-world-olivia-and-justine-moore-a16z/)).
- They find making it pay is hard: generation is costly and creators depend on platform payouts, ads, courses and consulting ([AI Video](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/ai-video-is-eating-the-world-olivia-and-justine-moore-a16z/)).
- Andrew Hsu of Speak describes an AI-native third generation of language learning built on speaking drills. He believes “eighty to ninety percent of the tech is here now” for a superhuman tutor ([Speak](/podcasts/latent-space/2025/07/personalized-ai-language-education-with-andrew-hsu-speak/)).
