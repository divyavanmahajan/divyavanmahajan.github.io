## Overview

April 2024 on Latent Space was about how to build AI products that hold up: narrow agents, checkable steps and typed outputs. A compilation of crossover appearances set out the hosts' four wars framework and a sceptical look at Groq. The month ended with simulators that use a model to generate whole worlds instead of answering as an assistant.

## Main themes

### The four wars and hardware economics

- swyx explains he calls them wars because the companies' stories “collectively, they cannot all be true”. The four are data, GPU rich/poor, multimodality, and RAG/ops ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/)).
- The hosts read Inflection's move to Microsoft and departures from Stability AI as consolidation, not an AI winter. Alessio says narrow agents do better than generic ones such as Auto-GPT, which plateaued after its early growth ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/)).
- Dylan Patel of SemiAnalysis estimates Groq's API runs seven to ten times below break-even on 576 chips with no off-chip memory. He also argues edge inference makes little economic sense next to the cloud ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/)).
- Milind Naphade says Capital One built an applied-research job family because finance has a low tolerance for error ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/)).

### Supervising the process, not just outcomes

- Jungwon Byun and Andreas Stuhlmüller describe Elicit as an AI research assistant that breaks expert workflows into small steps, each easy for humans to check. Stuhlmüller started its predecessor, Ought, as a nonprofit research lab ([Elicit](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/supervise-the-process-of-ai-research-with-jungwon-byun-and-andreas-stu/)).
- The guests want models to “earn their right to the expertise” step by step. Because the product is built around the workflow, new models improve it without changing its shape ([Elicit](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/supervise-the-process-of-ai-research-with-jungwon-byun-and-andreas-stu/)).
- Stuhlmüller argues the field undervalues ways to turn willingness to pay into accuracy, which is behind Elicit's credits pricing and high accuracy mode. Byun expects users to shift from doing work to checking it ([Elicit](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/supervise-the-process-of-ai-research-with-jungwon-byun-and-andreas-stu/)).
- swyx argues long context will not replace RAG because of cost, latency and debuggability ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/)).

### Structured outputs over frameworks

- Jason Liu sums up his library: “LLMs give you strings and Instructor gives you data structures.” He sees it as a thin wrapper, like Requests, not a framework ([Instructor](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/high-agency-pydantic-vc-backed-frameworks-with-jason-liu-of-instructor/)).
- Liu prefers function calling to JSON mode because it defines the schema. With dozens of tools he would store specs in a vector database and retrieve the top few ([Instructor](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/high-agency-pydantic-vc-backed-frameworks-with-jason-liu-of-instructor/)).
- He favours planned workflows of steps over ReAct-style loops, since each component can be fixed and fine-tuned. He chose consulting over venture funding ([Instructor](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/high-agency-pydantic-vc-backed-frameworks-with-jason-liu-of-instructor/)).
- Claude 3 came up in three episodes: Alessio found Opus better than GPT-4 for summarisation, and Liu praises Sonnet and Haiku on cost ([Latent Space Chats](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/latent-space-chats-nlw-four-wars-gpt5-josh-albrecht-ali-rohde-tnai-dyl/), [Instructor](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/high-agency-pydantic-vc-backed-frameworks-with-jason-liu-of-instructor/)).

### Simulators instead of assistants

- Karan Malhotra of Nous Research says Claude 3 is easier to steer than GPT-4, and uses a fake command line to have it simulate universes in WorldSim ([WebSim, WorldSim](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/websim-worldsim-and-the-summer-of-simulative-ai-with-joscha-bach-of-li/)).
- Rob Haisfield describes WebSim as a browser that makes up any URL on the fly; users have built Figma and slide decks inside it ([WebSim, WorldSim](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/websim-worldsim-and-the-summer-of-simulative-ai-with-joscha-bach-of-li/)).
- Joscha Bach of Liquid AI sees a shift from chatbot agents to environments people can explore, and worries the big labs may not keep allowing it ([WebSim, WorldSim](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/04/websim-worldsim-and-the-summer-of-simulative-ai-with-joscha-bach-of-li/)).
