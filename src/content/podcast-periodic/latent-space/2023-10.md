## Overview

October on Latent Space led up to the first AI Engineer Summit, with two crossposts in which swyx made the case for the AI engineer as a new role. The guest episodes debated how to give models knowledge, through retrieval or through more training, and why agents still fail. Cube's Artem Keydunov closed the month on natural-language queries over company data.

## Main themes

### The AI engineer and the Summit

- swyx defines the AI engineer as a software engineer building on foundation models through APIs: “I draw a line at the API layer.” He calls the job maybe 90% software engineering ([Software 3.0](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-1-swyx-on-software-3-0-and-the-rise-of-the-ai-engin/)).
- He grounds the prediction in labour supply, not technology. With perhaps 5,000 good LLM researchers worldwide, most companies must hire software engineers instead ([Software 3.0](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-1-swyx-on-software-3-0-and-the-rise-of-the-ai-engin/), [AI Horcrux](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-2-the-ai-horcrux-swyx-on-cognitive-revolution/)).
- swyx describes the Summit as a two-day single-track event and a ten-year effort to build an ICML for engineers. His rule for teams is to buy tools such as LangChain first, then rip out what they outgrow ([AI Horcrux](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-2-the-ai-horcrux-swyx-on-cognitive-revolution/)).
- swyx says OpenAI has promised not to compete with developers but doubts the promise is ironclad. Nathan Labenz worries it will absorb adjacent products ([AI Horcrux](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-2-the-ai-horcrux-swyx-on-cognitive-revolution/)).

### Why agents do not work yet

- swyx says his most useful agent, the SavvyCal or Calendly scheduler, uses no LLM, and that “we might be permanently, for a while at least, five to ten years away from full self-driving in agents” ([AI Horcrux](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/aie-summit-preview-2-the-ai-horcrux-swyx-on-cognitive-revolution/)).
- Kanjun Qiu says reasoning is the biggest blocker, so Imbue trains foundation models optimised for it. Its internal agents succeed only on very small tasks, and 50% reliability is not enough for users ([Imbue](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/why-ai-agents-don-t-work-yet-with-kanjun-qiu-of-imbue/)).
- Qiu calls chat interfaces skeuomorphic. Imbue's tools let users inspect, fork and edit an agent's plan mid-run, and she argues users must trust an agent's choices before relying on it ([Imbue](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/why-ai-agents-don-t-work-yet-with-kanjun-qiu-of-imbue/)).

### Retrieval versus fine-tuning

- Jerry Liu calls RAG a hack, but a good one: easy to adopt, with source transparency and access control. He says larger context windows do not remove the need when enterprise data runs to gigabytes ([LlamaIndex](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/rag-is-a-hack-with-jerry-liu-from-llamaindex/)).
- Liu sees retrieval as the bottleneck and wants every AI engineer to build RAG from scratch once. He believes knowledge can be fine-tuned in but has not made it work yet ([LlamaIndex](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/rag-is-a-hack-with-jerry-liu-from-llamaindex/)).
- Jeremy Howard, who pioneered the pretrain-then-fine-tune recipe with ULMFiT, now wants to “throw away the idea of fine-tuning” in favour of continued pre-training on a mix that keeps all data types ([End of Finetuning](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/the-end-of-finetuning-with-jeremy-howard-of-fast-ai/)).
- Howard blames catastrophic forgetting, citing Code Llama getting worse at everything except code. He says it is fixable with a good data mix and that fine-tuning is still underused ([End of Finetuning](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/the-end-of-finetuning-with-jeremy-howard-of-fast-ai/)).

### Copilots for data

- Artem Keydunov argues LLMs should query a semantic layer that defines metrics once, rather than write complex warehouse SQL with many joins ([Cube](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/powering-your-copilot-for-data-with-artem-keydunov-of-cube-dev/)).
- In Cube, the semantic layer is a code base of YAML and Python files that teams should debate in pull requests. Most builders he sees use GPT-3.5 or GPT-4 ([Cube](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/10/powering-your-copilot-for-data-with-artem-keydunov-of-cube-dev/)).
