## Overview

August on Latent Space opened with a Q2 recap arguing that OpenAI was no longer the default and that synthetic data had become an open recipe. The guests then went deep on practice: fine-tuning GPT-4o into a coding agent, writing your own benchmarks, running a lab with no managers and building SAM 2 around its demo. OpenAI came up in four of the five episodes.

## Main themes

### A non-OpenAI-centric world

- swyx and Alessio Fanelli say Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads the hard and coding benchmarks on LMSYS, and both hosts have cancelled ChatGPT. They argue it “marks the beginning of a non-OpenAI-centric world” ([Winds of AI Winter](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/the-winds-of-ai-winter-q2-four-wars-recap-chatgpt-voice-mode-preview/)).
- swyx estimates the cost of the same intelligence falls by about an order of magnitude every four months. Alessio notes Fortune 500 groups save $10-20 million a year with models, so prices must keep falling ([Winds of AI Winter](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/the-winds-of-ai-winter-q2-four-wars-recap-chatgpt-voice-mode-preview/)).
- Alessio says buyers want labour, not tools, citing Dropzone triaging security alerts at about $6 each versus $35 for a human analyst. A bonus segment tests ChatGPT Advanced Voice Mode on accents and emotion ([Winds of AI Winter](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/the-winds-of-ai-winter-q2-four-wars-recap-chatgpt-voice-mode-preview/)).

### Synthetic data and fine-tuning for coding agents

- swyx calls Llama 3 the synthetic data model: its paper spells out the recipe and its license now permits distillation ([Winds of AI Winter](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/the-winds-of-ai-winter-q2-four-wars-recap-chatgpt-voice-mode-preview/)).
- Alistair Pullen says Cosine fine-tuned GPT-4o on billions of tokens of largely synthetic data showing how engineers reason, because a PR is a lossy artifact. He frames it as “the difference between a model writing code and a model doing software engineering” ([Cosine Genie](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/is-finetuning-gpt4o-worth-it-with-alistair-pullen-cosine-genie/)).
- Cosine injected synthetic runtime errors into ASTs to teach iteration, and OpenAI gave it larger LoRA adapters than are public. Genie scored 43.8% on SWE-bench Verified, and success drops as context grows past about 60K tokens ([Cosine Genie](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/is-finetuning-gpt4o-worth-it-with-alistair-pullen-cosine-genie/)).

### Benchmarks you can trust, and attacks on models

- Nicholas Carlini complains that model releases cherry-pick a benchmark to claim state of the art. He built a small language for turning his own past requests into tests ([Carlini](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/why-you-should-write-your-own-llm-benchmarks-with-nicholas-carlini-goo/)).
- He uses models for boilerplate, Docker, FFmpeg flags and decompiling binaries, and argues most of novel research is work done many times before ([Carlini](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/why-you-should-write-your-own-llm-benchmarks-with-nicholas-carlini-goo/)).
- Carlini describes practical attacks on deployed systems: buying expired domains behind LAION-400M images, stealing a layer of production OpenAI models with permission, and extracting training data from GPT-3.5 Turbo ([Carlini](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/why-you-should-write-your-own-llm-benchmarks-with-nicholas-carlini-goo/)).

### Small teams and demo-first models

- Jeremy Howard wants Answer.AI to ship thousands of commercially successful products with about twelve people and no managers. Investors came in through a vehicle that commits them to long-term value ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/ai-magic-shipping-1000s-of-successful-products-with-no-managers-and-a/)).
- Howard treats pre-training, instruction tuning and task training as one continuum, argues encoder models are underused, and presents FastHTML as a one-file Python web framework ([Jeremy Howard](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/ai-magic-shipping-1000s-of-successful-products-with-no-managers-and-a/)).
- Nikhil Ravi says Meta built the SAM 2 demo alongside the model, and the demo doubles as the annotation tool. SAM 2 is about a third the size of SAM 1 and adds a memory bank for video ([SAM 2](/podcasts/latent-space/2024/08/segment-anything-2-demo-first-model-development/)).
