## Overview

May 2023 on Latent Space was about who can build capable models now that LLaMA had opened the field. Replit and MosaicML explained how they trained their own models cheaply, Simon Willison reacted to the leaked No Moat memo, and three founders looked at agents and at making LLM output reliable enough to use.

## Main themes

### Open models and the moat debate

- Willison says open models built on LLaMA, Alpaca and Vicuna caught up in about two months. He thinks they are not yet at GPT-3.5 level but “within spitting distance in some respects” ([No Moat](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/no-moat-closed-ai-gets-its-open-source-wakeup-call-ft-simon-willison/)).
- He argues stackable LoRA fine-tunes that train in hours could outrun companies that retrain giant models. Alessio points instead to GPUs and first-party data loops as the real moats ([No Moat](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/no-moat-closed-ai-gets-its-open-source-wakeup-call-ft-simon-willison/)).
- Sharif Shameem thinks people underestimate open models and says fine-tuning small ones such as Flan-T5 on user data can be 10 to 100 times cheaper than an API ([Lexica](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/the-ai-founder-gene-being-early-building-fast-and-believing-in-greatne/)).
- Abhinav Venigalla says Meta's LLaMA release inspired MosaicML to publish MPT-7B under a commercial licence ([MPT-7B](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/mpt-7b-and-the-beginning-of-context-infinity-with-jonathan-frankle-and/)).

### Training your own model, past Chinchilla

- Reza Shabani says Replit trained a 2.7B code model in about a week only because it had spent months on data infrastructure. It saw 525 billion tokens over multiple epochs ([Replit](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/training-a-sota-code-llm-in-1-week-and-quantifying-the-vibes-with-reza/)).
- Shabani says the results suggest “we've really been under-training these, these models”, possibly even LLaMA ([Replit](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/training-a-sota-code-llm-in-1-week-and-quantifying-the-vibes-with-reza/)).
- Jonathan Frankle says MPT-7B trained in 9.5 days for about $200K with no human intervention. Venigalla says it saw a trillion tokens, about seven times the Chinchilla-optimal amount ([MPT-7B](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/mpt-7b-and-the-beginning-of-context-infinity-with-jonathan-frankle-and/)).
- Frankle frames MosaicML's business as customers training their own models, and Shabani credits MosaicML's infrastructure for Replit's run ([MPT-7B](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/mpt-7b-and-the-beginning-of-context-infinity-with-jonathan-frankle-and/), [Replit](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/training-a-sota-code-llm-in-1-week-and-quantifying-the-vibes-with-reza/)).

### Vibes versus benchmarks

- Shabani describes a model with good HumanEval scores whose vibes were terrible, so Replit relies on CEO Amjad Masad's informal testing of latency and usefulness ([Replit](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/training-a-sota-code-llm-in-1-week-and-quantifying-the-vibes-with-reza/)).
- Frankle says “you're only as good as your evaluation”, admits the team knows little about data mixes, and ran vibe checks on fixed prompts during training ([MPT-7B](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/mpt-7b-and-the-beginning-of-context-infinity-with-jonathan-frankle-and/)).
- Itamar Friedman proposes level-based, property-based benchmarks for generative systems ([CodiumAI](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/debugging-the-internet-with-ai-agents-with-itamar-friedman-of-codium-a/)).

### Agents and reliable output

- Shameem's 2021 browsing-agent demo cost about $100 for 60 seconds. He now sees web agents as an engineering problem of representing pages and targeting actions ([Lexica](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/the-ai-founder-gene-being-early-building-fast-and-believing-in-greatne/)).
- Friedman argues LLMs and classic testing must be combined to verify code logic. He contrasts CodiumAI's narrow agents with AutoGPT's general swarm ([CodiumAI](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/debugging-the-internet-with-ai-agents-with-itamar-friedman-of-codium-a/)).
- Shreya Rajpal says LLMs will never be deterministic, so Guardrails AI validates every output against a spec and corrects failures. She sees this as essential for agents ([Guardrails](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/guaranteed-quality-and-structure-in-llm-outputs-with-shreya-rajpal-of/)).
- Shabani wants Replit's next model to drive the IDE and build whole projects from operational-transform data ([Replit](/podcasts/latent-space/2023/05/training-a-sota-code-llm-in-1-week-and-quantifying-the-vibes-with-reza/)).
