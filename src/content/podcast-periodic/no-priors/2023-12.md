## Overview

December on No Priors looked at who benefits from generative AI, from the largest enterprises to corner shops to individual workers. Salesforce AI's CEO and Square's former CEO described adoption at the two ends of the business market, and Reid Hoffman closed the year with an argument for optimism over caution.

## Main themes

### AI as the new enterprise interface

- Clara Shih says “AI is a new UI or maybe slack is a new UI for AI”. She expects software teams to describe goals and let AI work out the steps, except where workflows must stay deterministic ([Clara Shih](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/ai-is-the-new-enterprise-ui-with-clara-shih-ceo-salesforce-ai/)).
- She puts enterprise adoption at roughly the second or third inning, with most customers still getting their data in order. Elad Gil outlines the stages: data, internal prototypes, then customer-facing products ([Clara Shih](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/ai-is-the-new-enterprise-ui-with-clara-shih-ceo-salesforce-ai/)).
- Salesforce mixes in-house, customer fine-tuned and third-party models from Anthropic, Cohere, OpenAI and Google Vertex behind Einstein Copilot and Copilot Studio. Shih cites shorter handle times for Gucci service advisors ([Clara Shih](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/ai-is-the-new-enterprise-ui-with-clara-shih-ceo-salesforce-ai/)).
- Shih calls AI pricing difficult because customers should not have to predict token usage; Sarah Guo expects falling model costs to help ([Clara Shih](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/ai-is-the-new-enterprise-ui-with-clara-shih-ceo-salesforce-ai/)).

### Small businesses and latent demand

- Alyssa Henry argues much AI-enabled work is work that never got done, not jobs taken away. Owners know they should market but lack time; as her cupcake baker puts it, “I love cupcakes not because I like writing email marketing” ([Small businesses](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/how-ai-can-help-small-businesses/)).
- Guo says demand for generated content at a hundredth to a thousandth of the cost surprised many tech people. Henry says in-store digitisation lags online and expects generative AI to make it much faster and cheaper ([Small businesses](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/how-ai-can-help-small-businesses/)).
- Henry predicts a shift from point products to bundles, and sees each computing wave settling on a clear number one and two. She does not expect Nvidia to hold a monopoly ([Small businesses](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/how-ai-can-help-small-businesses/)).

### Optimism, jobs and regulation

- Reid Hoffman calls AI a steam engine of the mind and wants more focus on people plus AI. He argues doomers misread exponential curves and that critics who only warn may do more harm than good ([AI optimism](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/the-case-for-ai-optimism/)).
- He expects mid-level white-collar jobs to change first, but the transition to be slower than feared: half the trucks on the road would not be autonomous for 10 years. His advice is to use AI on tasks that matter to you ([AI optimism](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/the-case-for-ai-optimism/)).
- Hoffman says “regulation is enshrining the past”, criticises the EU AI Act and argues AI cannot be built by committee ([AI optimism](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/the-case-for-ai-optimism/)).
- He describes Inflection's Pi as a compassionate personal intelligence that steers lonely users towards human friends ([AI optimism](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/12/the-case-for-ai-optimism/)).
