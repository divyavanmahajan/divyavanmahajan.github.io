## Overview

April centred on agents and the training that makes them work. Isa Fulford explained how OpenAI built Deep Research, and Brendan Foody argued that writing evals will become a major knowledge-work job. Sarah Guo and Elad Gil looked at converging models and specialised ones, and Josh Goldman showed AI applied to finding critical minerals.

## Main themes

### Building research agents

- Isa Fulford says the Deep Research team started with read-only synthesis rather than actions like ordering food. Runs take 5 to 30 minutes for work experts rate as many hours ([Deep Research](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/inside-deep-research-with-isa-fulford-building-the-future-of-ai-agents/)).
- She credits datasets, graders and tools more than the algorithm, and sees literature review as a step towards OpenAI's goal of new scientific discovery ([Deep Research](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/inside-deep-research-with-isa-fulford-building-the-future-of-ai-agents/)).
- Fulford notes that as models improve, “the level of abstraction of the human becomes higher”. She wants a unified agent that acts like a remote coworker, with trust and safety as the binding constraints ([Deep Research](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/inside-deep-research-with-isa-fulford-building-the-future-of-ai-agents/)).

### Reinforcement fine-tuning and evals

- Fulford advises reinforcement fine-tuning for tasks far outside the training distribution, or where an extra 10-15% is business-critical; skip it if each new base model already improves on the task ([Deep Research](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/inside-deep-research-with-isa-fulford-building-the-future-of-ai-agents/)).
- Brendan Foody is bullish on RFT because it needs hundreds to thousands of examples rather than tens of thousands, which makes customising models at the application layer worthwhile ([Brendan Foody](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/from-job-displacement-to-ai-trainers-brendan-foody-on-work-in-the-ai-a/)).
- He says the human data market moved from crowdsourcing to vetting top experts. Of writing evals, he says “it would not surprise me if that becomes the most common knowledge work job in the world” ([Brendan Foody](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/from-job-displacement-to-ai-trainers-brendan-foody-on-work-in-the-ai-a/)).

### AI, hiring and jobs

- Foody says models already beat human hiring managers at assessing talent on most of Mercor's evals, especially from interview transcripts ([Brendan Foody](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/from-job-displacement-to-ai-trainers-brendan-foody-on-work-in-the-ai-a/)).
- He sees displacement already in customer support and recruiting and expects physical-world automation to lag. Gil argues parts of government, academia and big tech already work like a form of UBI ([Brendan Foody](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/from-job-displacement-to-ai-trainers-brendan-foody-on-work-in-the-ai-a/)).

### AI for critical minerals

- Josh Goldman frames mining as an information problem. Geoscience data sits in tens of thousands of repositories, and “There's nowhere you can go where this is all aggregated in one place” ([KoBold Metals](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/predicting-the-earth-with-josh-goldman-how-kobold-uses-ai-to-find-crit/)).
- KoBold runs its own sensors, a unified data system and dozens of models retrained daily, and targets $50 to 100 million of exploration per discovery ([KoBold Metals](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/predicting-the-earth-with-josh-goldman-how-kobold-uses-ai-to-find-crit/)).
- Goldman says the world must mine more copper in the next 25 years than in all of history, driven by batteries and data centres ([KoBold Metals](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/predicting-the-earth-with-josh-goldman-how-kobold-uses-ai-to-find-crit/)).

### Converging models, specialised opportunities

- Guo says frontier models are converging on the same product surfaces, so consumer surplus and distribution become the question. Gil cites x.ai and Google's Gemini as signs the frontier is crowded ([Public Markets and Image Gen](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/public-markets-image-gen-and-specialized-models-with-sarah-and-elad/)).
- Gil sees under-funded openings in physics, materials, robotics and health; Guo says the key is a data collection engine where training data does not yet exist ([Public Markets and Image Gen](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/04/public-markets-image-gen-and-specialized-models-with-sarah-and-elad/)).
