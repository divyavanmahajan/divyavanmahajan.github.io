## Overview

April on No Priors covered the stack from chips to consumer products. Jensen Huang explained NVIDIA's bet on accelerated computing, Noam Shazeer argued scaling still has room to run, and Matei Zaharia argued the opposite case for small, cheap models. The hosts closed the month with a listener Q&A on hype, regulation and open source.

## Main themes

### Small models and commoditisation

- Matei Zaharia says Databricks' Dolly copied Stanford's Alpaca recipe on a 6-billion-parameter open model and “actually worked pretty well” ([Databricks](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-future-is-small-models-with-matei-zaharia-cto-of-databricks/)).
- Zaharia argues the core LLM technology is commoditising quickly, and that ChatGPT-level capability may one day run locally on a phone ([Databricks](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-future-is-small-models-with-matei-zaharia-cto-of-databricks/)).
- He says extra parameters mainly add memorisation, which retrieval handles better, and that enterprise value will come from data, retrieval and systems around models ([Databricks](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-future-is-small-models-with-matei-zaharia-cto-of-databricks/)).
- Sarah Guo bets on an open GPT-3.5-level model within a year. Elad Gil notes that would still leave open source a generation or two behind ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/listener-q-a-ai-investment-hype-foundation-models-regulation-opportuni/)).

### Transformers, scaling and AI companions

- Noam Shazeer says transformers won because they process a whole sequence at once on parallel hardware. Jensen Huang makes the same point about RNNs and LSTMs ([Noam Shazeer](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/your-ai-friends-have-awoken-with-noam-shazeer/), [Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-computing-platform-underlying-ai-with-jensen-huang-founder-ceo-of/)).
- Shazeer sees no scaling wall yet, expecting gains from algorithms, chips and quantization plus much more spending ([Noam Shazeer](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/your-ai-friends-have-awoken-with-noam-shazeer/)).
- He argues a single assistant persona ends up boring, so Character.AI lets users create many. He notes that many people use it “cuz they're lonely or troubled and need someone to talk to” ([Noam Shazeer](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/your-ai-friends-have-awoken-with-noam-shazeer/)).

### NVIDIA and accelerated computing

- Huang says NVIDIA exists to solve problems normal computers can't, and believes everything will eventually be accelerated as the CPU runs its course ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-computing-platform-underlying-ai-with-jensen-huang-founder-ceo-of/)).
- NVIDIA made every chip CUDA-compatible for years with few customers. Gil says CUDA and interconnect scaling are why practitioners prefer NVIDIA GPUs ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-computing-platform-underlying-ai-with-jensen-huang-founder-ceo-of/)).
- Huang calls AI a platform shift where, for the first time, “the language of programming a computer is human”. He says NVIDIA is not trying to be a model company ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/the-computing-platform-underlying-ai-with-jensen-huang-founder-ceo-of/)).

### Hype, regulation and where to build

- Gil compares the moment to the mobile photo-app wave, where most things failed and only a few, like Instagram, lasted ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/listener-q-a-ai-investment-hype-foundation-models-regulation-opportuni/)).
- Gil argues regulation tends to lock in incumbents and would limit it for now to export controls and defence. Guo notes alignment research is tied to capability research ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/listener-q-a-ai-investment-hype-foundation-models-regulation-opportuni/)).
- Both hosts like voice synthesis, dubbing and compliance as opportunity areas, and Gil expects incumbents to capture most of the value ([Listener Q&A](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/04/listener-q-a-ai-investment-hype-foundation-models-regulation-opportuni/)).
