## Overview

July on No Priors covered how models get trained and judged, how coding agents should work inside large companies, and how the US government wants to win the AI race. The one thread shared by two episodes was RL environments: simulated copies of real software used to train agents. Rippling's Parker Conrad added a sceptical view on AI and headcount.

## Main themes

### Training data, RL environments and benchmarks

- Edwin Chen says Surge passed a billion in revenue with a little over 100 people and no venture money. He argues human feedback will never run out because models need an external signal tied to real objectives ([Surge](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/the-power-of-quality-human-data-with-surgeai-founder-and-ceo-edwin-che/)).
- Chen describes RL environments that simulate a salesperson's whole world across Salesforce, Gmail, Slack and calendars, and sees no ceiling on useful realism. Misha Laskin is also bullish on cloned-software environments for agent training ([Surge](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/the-power-of-quality-human-data-with-surgeai-founder-and-ceo-edwin-che/), [Asimov](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/asimov-building-an-omniscient-rl-oracle-with-reflectionai-s-misha-lask/)).
- Chen calls LMArena a plague on AI: raters spend seconds per comparison and longer answers win. Labs chasing rank, he says, do not realise “all they're doing is training their models to produce better clickbait.” ([Surge](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/the-power-of-quality-human-data-with-surgeai-founder-and-ceo-edwin-che/)).
- He adds that customers who generated millions of synthetic examples found 99% unusable ([Surge](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/the-power-of-quality-human-data-with-surgeai-founder-and-ceo-edwin-che/)).

### Comprehension before generation in coding agents

- Laskin argues tools are weighted the wrong way: “So 80% code generation, 20% comprehension. The actual problem is exactly the opposite.” Reflection AI's Asimov is a code research agent for large codebases ([Asimov](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/asimov-building-an-omniscient-rl-oracle-with-reflectionai-s-misha-lask/)).
- Asimov keeps a team-wide memory that senior engineers gate, which Laskin calls a GitHub++ for versioning the knowledge around code ([Asimov](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/asimov-building-an-omniscient-rl-oracle-with-reflectionai-s-misha-lask/)).
- Laskin says a lab can skip pre-training and compete with about two orders of magnitude less compute. He sees RL as limited by noisy, hackable rewards and argues evals should come from customer pain points ([Asimov](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/asimov-building-an-omniscient-rl-oracle-with-reflectionai-s-misha-lask/)).

### Platforms, AI and headcount

- Conrad argues shared capabilities such as permissions, reports, analytics and workflow automations make platforms win, and every dollar of platform R&D at Rippling pays off 35x ([Rippling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/why-platforms-win-and-point-solutions-fail-with-rippling-ceo-parker-co/)).
- He says most large engineering organisations have not seen big efficiencies from coding assistants. His view: “if you make it easier to build software I think the demand for software will actually go way up.” ([Rippling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/why-platforms-win-and-point-solutions-fail-with-rippling-ceo-parker-co/)).
- Conrad also argues AI agents must inherit the permissions of the person they work for ([Rippling](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/why-platforms-win-and-point-solutions-fail-with-rippling-ceo-parker-co/)).

### America's AI Action Plan

- Sriram Krishnan says DeepSeek showed the US lead over China was small. The 28-page plan rests on three pillars: build infrastructure, cut red tape with federal-level rules and open source, and export the American stack ([Sriram Krishnan](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/america-s-plan-to-dominate-the-full-ai-stack-with-sriram-krishnan/)).
- He proposes measuring the race by the share of the world's inferenced tokens running on American hardware and models, and cites partnerships in the Gulf ([Sriram Krishnan](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/america-s-plan-to-dominate-the-full-ai-stack-with-sriram-krishnan/)).
- Elad Gil argues that removing open source centralises power in a few companies. Krishnan cites Linus's law and says DeepSeek showed China builds models fine on its own ([Sriram Krishnan](/podcasts/no-priors/2025/07/america-s-plan-to-dominate-the-full-ai-stack-with-sriram-krishnan/)).
