## Overview

July on No Priors moved from models to the physical and service economy. Booking.com and DoorDash explained how agents change the way customers buy, Netic described agents running service businesses end to end, and Valar Atomics made the case for building nuclear reactors fast.

## Main themes

### Agentic commerce

- Andy Fang says about 50% of restaurant sessions in Ask DoorDash end in an order from a place the user has never tried, and grocery baskets are about 40% larger. Voice was the first bet and did not land; conversational text did ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).
- Fang adds: “I think we're all going to need to think about like what does the agent first experience look like.” ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).
- Glenn Fogel rejects the outside view that AI would make travel companies worthless. He wants Booking's agents to predict disruptions and fix the knock-on effects, while Penny adoption has doubled monthly for several months ([Booking.com](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/travel-through-the-lens-of-ai-with-with-booking-com-ceo-glenn-fogel/)).
- Fogel is not yet pushing Penny hard while Booking works out cost per booking and lifetime value against $186 billion of travel last year ([Booking.com](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/travel-through-the-lens-of-ai-with-with-booking-com-ceo-glenn-fogel/)).

### Autonomous delivery, built from the use case

- Stanley Tang says DoorDash has worked on autonomy since 2018 and built its own robot, Dot, after sidewalk robots proved too slow and robotaxis too large for 3-5 mile deliveries ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).
- Dot weighs 300 lb, travels up to 20 mph and went fully autonomous (L4) in Phoenix last year. Data from 10 billion past deliveries helps with pickup and drop-off ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).
- Tang says “the problem 5 years ago was autonomy. Now it's increasingly becoming more about operations, commercialization, hardware, manufacturing.” ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).

### Agents that run service businesses

- Melisa Tokmak describes Netic as an autonomous enterprise: agents sit between essential-service companies and their customers and handle everything except the labour itself ([Netic](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-enterprise-for-real-world-services-with-netic-f/)).
- Over 70% of Netic's customers are now Netic first, and she says it has generated over $600 million for customers from AI-handled interactions ([Netic](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-enterprise-for-real-world-services-with-netic-f/)).
- She prefers a reusable product to an AI roll-up and sees frontier labs, OpenAI among them, as partners rather than threats. Fogel also discussed OpenAI's place in travel ([Netic](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-enterprise-for-real-world-services-with-netic-f/), [Booking.com](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/travel-through-the-lens-of-ai-with-with-booking-com-ceo-glenn-fogel/)).

### AI and jobs

- Fogel says machine translation eliminated Booking.com's human translation and multilingual service roles. He worries job losses may outpace job creation and favours upskilling staff over government retraining ([Booking.com](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/travel-through-the-lens-of-ai-with-with-booking-com-ceo-glenn-fogel/)).
- Tang takes the opposite view for his own workforce, predicting more Dashers in ten years because the business grows 25% a year ([DoorDash](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/building-an-autonomous-delivery-experience-with-doordash-co-founders-a/)).

### Nuclear as a hardware problem

- Isaiah Taylor argues most of the nuclear industry produces paper reactors. Valar measures progress by the time between successive reactors splitting atoms, and its Ward 250 is the first startup-built advanced reactor to make power ([Valar Atomics](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/how-nuclear-will-unlock-energy-abundance-with-valar-atomics-founder-is/)).
- He says the design reduces risk by reducing consequence, removing decay heat without active cooling. Valar also powered an NVIDIA Blackwell chip directly from the reactor ([Valar Atomics](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/07/how-nuclear-will-unlock-energy-abundance-with-valar-atomics-founder-is/)).
