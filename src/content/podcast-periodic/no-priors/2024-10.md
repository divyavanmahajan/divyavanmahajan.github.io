## Overview

October on No Priors had a strong regulatory thread: the FTC chair on antitrust and AI, and Kalshi's founder on suing the CFTC to list election markets. Alongside that, Braintrust explained why evals stay central to AI products, Waymo's co-CEO described how full autonomy was reached, and the hosts reviewed o1, the AI Nobel Prizes and which businesses AI puts at risk.

## Main themes

### Regulators, markets and AI

- Lina Khan argues two decades of hands-off enforcement assumed digital markets would self-correct, when in fact they can tip towards dominance. She stresses that “merger enforcement is inherently a predictive exercise” ([Lina Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/the-sheriff-of-silicon-valley-lina-khan-s-ftc-agenda-for-m-a-ai-acquis/)).
- Khan says fewer than 3% of deals reported to the FTC and DOJ are investigated. Her advice to founders: selling to the incumbent monopolist draws the most scrutiny, and eight to ten potential buyers beat one or two ([Lina Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/the-sheriff-of-silicon-valley-lina-khan-s-ftc-agenda-for-m-a-ai-acquis/)).
- She favours simple rules over complex ones that suit incumbents with many lawyers, citing the FTC's ban on most non-competes, and has spoken for open-weight models as a way to lower startup costs ([Lina Khan](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/the-sheriff-of-silicon-valley-lina-khan-s-ftc-agenda-for-m-a-ai-acquis/)).
- Tarek Mansour says Kalshi spent close to three years being regulated by the CFTC before launching, then sued it over election contracts and won. He argues prediction markets are risk transfer, not gambling ([Kalshi](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/forecasting-the-future-with-kalshi-america-s-first-regulated-predictio/)).

### Evals and shipping AI products

- Ankur Goyal says the core problem of AI products, uncertainty about quality, has not changed with LLMs: “AI is an inherently non-deterministic thing and so I think evals are are still going to be there” ([Braintrust](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/launching-ai-products-with-braintrust-s-ceo-ankur-goyal/)).
- He reports that about half of production use cases involve RAG, most customers moved from fine-tuned to instruction-tuned models, and early free-form agents gave way to code-controlled flows ([Braintrust](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/launching-ai-products-with-braintrust-s-ceo-ankur-goyal/)).
- Goyal argues TypeScript suits AI product engineering, and says Braintrust grew from evals into observability after Airtable used it to log production traffic ([Braintrust](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/launching-ai-products-with-braintrust-s-ceo-ankur-goyal/)).

### Full autonomy at Waymo

- Dmitri Dolgov says the gap between driver assistance and full autonomy is “the number of nines”, and that building the driver and evaluating it must go hand in hand ([Waymo](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/waymo-s-journey-to-full-autonomy-ai-breakthroughs-safety-and-scaling/)).
- He cites 22 million rider-only miles with about 2x fewer low-severity collisions than human benchmarks and about 6x fewer airbag-deployment crashes ([Waymo](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/waymo-s-journey-to-full-autonomy-ai-breakthroughs-safety-and-scaling/)).
- Dolgov says a fine-tuned vision-language model can drive well for hundreds of miles, but imitation learning alone plateaus too early; full autonomy also needs synthetic data and closed-loop simulation ([Waymo](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/waymo-s-journey-to-full-autonomy-ai-breakthroughs-safety-and-scaling/)).

### The hosts on o1, Nobel Prizes and AI-exposed businesses

- Sarah Guo sees o1 as a new scaling dimension through test-time compute, though not better on every axis. Elad Gil likens it to GPT-2 for this approach ([Gaming and Nobels](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/gaming-nobel-prizes-and-at-risk-businesses-in-the-ai-era/)).
- Both hosts see the chemistry Nobel for AI work on protein folding as deserved, while Guo questions whether the physics prize advanced physics ([Gaming and Nobels](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/gaming-nobel-prizes-and-at-risk-businesses-in-the-ai-era/)).
- Gil points to customer support and voice agents as most exposed; Guo adds thin vertical software and web hosting. Guo also argues NotebookLM shows how building on users' existing artifacts solves cold start ([Gaming and Nobels](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/10/gaming-nobel-prizes-and-at-risk-businesses-in-the-ai-era/)).
