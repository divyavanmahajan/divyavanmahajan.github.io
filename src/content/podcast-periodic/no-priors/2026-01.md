## Overview

January on No Priors featured four leaders arguing against a pessimistic narrative in their field. Jensen Huang pushed back on bubble and job-loss fears, Emil Michael set out how the Department of War plans to move at startup speed, and CJ Desai defended incumbent software platforms. Laura Deming closed the month with a business case for cryopreservation.

## Main themes

### No bubble, says Nvidia

- Jensen Huang frames AI as a five-layer stack of energy, chips, infrastructure, models and applications, not as one chatbot ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).
- He argues there is a global, multi-industry compute shortage and that roughly $2 trillion of annual R&D is moving to AI. He says Nvidia “would be a multiundred billion dollar company” even without chatbots ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).
- He notes that Nvidia's autonomous vehicle business is nearing $10 billion and that its self-driving stack just received the top safety rating ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).

### AI, jobs and open models

- Huang separates a job's tasks from its purpose: AI reads scans, yet demand for radiologists has grown because their purpose is diagnosing disease ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).
- He points to doubling paychecks for electricians building AI factories, and expects robots to fill labour shortages made worse by ageing populations ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).
- He says AI work at startups and industrial firms would be suffocated without open source, and that no country contributes more to it than China ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-reasoning-models-robotics-and-refuting-the-ai/)).

### AI and autonomy in defence

- Emil Michael says a 60-day tiger team built GenAI.mil on Gemini 2.5, with Grok to follow, and that no department data flows back to model makers. It had over a million unique users in 30 days ([Emil Michael](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/ai-and-the-future-of-warfare-with-us-under-secretary-of-war-emil-micha/)).
- He describes “robots as the new front line” after the Russia-Ukraine war, and would not be surprised if autonomous systems took 20-30% of the budget within 10 years ([Emil Michael](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/ai-and-the-future-of-warfare-with-us-under-secretary-of-war-emil-micha/)).
- He notes that about 50 defence contractors in the 1980s merged into roughly five. He wants startups in procurement but asks them to prove they can manufacture at scale, and names supply dependencies on China ([Emil Michael](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/ai-and-the-future-of-warfare-with-us-under-secretary-of-war-emil-micha/)).

### Durable software and enterprise adoption

- CJ Desai argues that products are replaceable but platforms are sticky. One London bank runs 300 of its 9,000 applications on MongoDB ([MongoDB](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/no-priors-live-building-durable-software-in-the-ai-age-with-mongodb-pr/)).
- He says messy, unstructured AI data suits MongoDB, which he calls the only next-generation database to pass $2 billion ([MongoDB](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/no-priors-live-building-durable-software-in-the-ai-age-with-mongodb-pr/)).
- Fortune 500 buyers report weak value from office copilots, strong results from coding assistants, and customer support that is not there yet ([MongoDB](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/no-priors-live-building-durable-software-in-the-ai-age-with-mongodb-pr/)).

### A startup bet on cryopreservation

- Laura Deming argues reversible cryopreservation is a scaling problem rather than a question of possibility. Until's first product targets transplant organs, where timing and travel now limit supply ([Until](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/why-cryopreservation-is-no-longer-science-fiction-with-until-co-founde/)).
- Its long-term goal is medical hibernation for seriously ill patients waiting for new therapies. Deming names the brain as the main open question ([Until](/podcasts/no-priors/2026/01/why-cryopreservation-is-no-longer-science-fiction-with-until-co-founde/)).
