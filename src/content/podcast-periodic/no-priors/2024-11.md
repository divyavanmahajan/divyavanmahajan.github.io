## Overview

November on No Priors had three long interviews, each on a different layer of the stack. Jensen Huang described the data center as the new unit of computing, DeepMind's AlphaProof team explained how reinforcement learning on formal proofs reached IMO silver level, and Aidan Gomez argued that enterprises have years of work ahead even if models stop improving.

## Main themes

### Data centers as AI factories

- Jensen Huang says the new data centers are single-tenant and produce tokens rather than store files. In his words, “we don't build computers anymore we build factories” ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-ai-chip-design-scaling-data-centers-and-his-1/)).
- Huang says Dennard and VLSI scaling have run out of steam, and full-stack co-design (lower precision, the network as compute fabric) can double or triple performance at scale each year ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-ai-chip-design-scaling-data-centers-and-his-1/)).
- He describes Nvidia building every data center configuration in-house, then selling the parts so they fit into GCP, AWS, Azure and OCI. He credits a digital twin for bringing up xAI's 100,000-GPU cluster within weeks ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-ai-chip-design-scaling-data-centers-and-his-1/)).
- Huang expects to rent AI engineers from Synopsys and Cadence, and sees SaaS platforms such as SAP and ServiceNow as rich ground for specialised agents ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/nvidia-s-jensen-huang-on-ai-chip-design-scaling-data-centers-and-his-1/)).

### Reinforcement learning meets formal mathematics

- Thomas Hubert, Rishi Mehta and Laurent Sartran explain that formal proofs in Lean give a machine-checkable reward, which suits AlphaZero-style learning. AlphaProof solved four of six problems at the 2024 IMO ([AlphaProof](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/ai-and-the-future-of-math-with-deepmind-s-alphaproof-team/)).
- Mehta says it is strongest at algebra and number theory and weaker at combinatorics. When stuck, it generates and solves variations of a problem at test time ([AlphaProof](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/ai-and-the-future-of-math-with-deepmind-s-alphaproof-team/)).
- The team names theory building as the main gap. Mehta expects specialist human data to seed models, after which “the RL is going to take you from there to like something that's like super human” ([AlphaProof](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/ai-and-the-future-of-math-with-deepmind-s-alphaproof-team/)).

### Plateaus, reasoning and enterprise adoption

- Aidan Gomez says model capability is entering a flatter part of the curve as it reaches the limits of human expert knowledge, but argues today's models already cover most business tasks ([Cohere](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/model-plateaus-and-enterprise-ai-adoption-with-cohere-s-aidan-gomez/)).
- He says a GPT-4-class model for enterprise tasks now costs $10-20 million to build, so Cohere chooses to stay about six months behind the frontier and will not build a ChatGPT competitor ([Cohere](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/model-plateaus-and-enterprise-ai-adoption-with-cohere-s-aidan-gomez/)).
- Gomez names trust, security and privacy as the first barrier and developer know-how as the second. He sees “a half decade of work to go integrate this into the economy” even without new models ([Cohere](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/model-plateaus-and-enterprise-ai-adoption-with-cohere-s-aidan-gomez/)).
- He calls inference-time reasoning a second lever: customers can buy more compute now rather than wait for a bigger training run. He thinks falling model prices reflect price dumping, not commoditisation ([Cohere](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/11/model-plateaus-and-enterprise-ai-adoption-with-cohere-s-aidan-gomez/)).
