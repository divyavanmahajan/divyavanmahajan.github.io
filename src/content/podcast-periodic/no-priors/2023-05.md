## Overview

May on No Priors asked how general models get adapted to people, companies and regulated fields. Mustafa Suleyman made the case for a personal AI on each user's side, Kelvin Guu covered retrieval and model editing, and Kevin Scott explained Microsoft's copilot platform. Google's Med-PaLM work brought the same questions to healthcare.

## Main themes

### Personal AI and the end of ten blue links

- Mustafa Suleyman argues “most computing is going to become a conversation”, with each person wanting an AI on their own side. Inflection's Pi starts with companionship rather than information retrieval ([Mustafa Suleyman](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/will-everyone-have-a-personal-ai-with-mustafa-suleyman-founder-of-deep/)).
- He says Google search is already a dialogue run through 1980s Yellow Pages-style tools, and that ad incentives have shaped web content around SEO ([Mustafa Suleyman](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/will-everyone-have-a-personal-ai-with-mustafa-suleyman-founder-of-deep/)).
- Inflection has about 30 technical staff, ships every six weeks and follows each release with an in-person hackathon week ([Mustafa Suleyman](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/will-everyone-have-a-personal-ai-with-mustafa-suleyman-founder-of-deep/)).

### Retrieval and personalisation

- Kelvin Guu names three motives for retrieval: more memorisation capacity, modularity and timely updates without retraining. He says its benefits now lie mostly in personal and enterprise data ([Kelvin Guu](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/personalizing-ai-models-with-kelvin-guu-senior-staff-research-scientis/)).
- Guu says retrieval suits precise facts like a wifi password, while expert models suit a company's specialised code. He expects providers to push adaptation into prompting for the broadest reach ([Kelvin Guu](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/personalizing-ai-models-with-kelvin-guu-senior-staff-research-scientis/)).
- Kevin Scott lists retrieval-augmented generation as one layer of Microsoft's copilot stack ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/going-full-send-on-ai-and-the-positive-impact-of-ai-on-jobs-with-kevin/)).

### Microsoft's platform bet and AI's effect on jobs

- Scott says Microsoft concentrated its GPU budget and partnered with OpenAI as a no-regrets bet. It now treats AI as a platform used mainly through assistive tools rather than fully autonomous ones ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/going-full-send-on-ai-and-the-positive-impact-of-ai-on-jobs-with-kevin/)).
- He says the best products come “where you've made the phase change from impossible to hard”, and that no single company builds all the useful things on a platform ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/going-full-send-on-ai-and-the-positive-impact-of-ai-on-jobs-with-kevin/)).
- Scott argues we have a cognitive exponential but not a robotics one, so demand for surgeons, nurses and electricians will rise and creative work stays human-centred ([Kevin Scott](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/going-full-send-on-ai-and-the-positive-impact-of-ai-on-jobs-with-kevin/)).

### Healthcare as a high-stakes testbed

- Karan Singhal describes Med-PaLM as a general Google model aligned to the medical domain and built around careful evaluation by physicians and lay people ([Med-PaLM](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/the-ai-will-see-you-now-exploring-biomedical-ai-and-google-s-med-palm2/)).
- Singhal says big health-information providers are risk averse. Sarah Guo argues for a reasonable safety case rather than an unlimited one, and Elad Gil notes healthcare is about 20% of GDP ([Med-PaLM](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/the-ai-will-see-you-now-exploring-biomedical-ai-and-google-s-med-palm2/)).
- Singhal expects general models to land first in documentation and billing, and sees overseeing near-expert models as an unsolved alignment problem ([Med-PaLM](/podcasts/no-priors/2023/05/the-ai-will-see-you-now-exploring-biomedical-ai-and-google-s-med-palm2/)).
