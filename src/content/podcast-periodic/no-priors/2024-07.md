## Overview

July on No Priors centred on enterprise adoption. A hosts-only episode argued that the big wave has not started and that AI skeptics misread the technology, while Airtable's Howie Liu said the gap is imagination rather than capability. A mid-year compilation of clips added views on evaluation, design and hardware.

## Main themes

### Enterprise adoption is early

- Elad Gil says “the big wave of Enterprise adoption hasn't even happened yet”, and that most activity so far has been buying vendors such as Decagon and Harvey ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).
- Howie Liu argues the gap is “in imagination and knowhow”. Airtable runs day-long AI workshops and teaches patterns such as translate, self-critique, then review ([Airtable](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/low-code-in-the-age-of-ai-and-going-enterprise-with-howie-liu-from-air/)).
- Airtable's AI beta had a thousand customers, including a top-five law firm automating parts of its contracting workflows. Liu sees structured recurring processes with humans in the loop as a bigger prize than chat over internal data ([Airtable](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/low-code-in-the-age-of-ai-and-going-enterprise-with-howie-liu-from-air/)).

### Answering the bubble talk

- Sarah Guo and Gil push back on a widely read Goldman Sachs report that questions the payoff of roughly a trillion dollars of AI capex ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).
- Gil says many critics still treat AI like old-school ML, and recalls IBM's CEO calling internet companies “fireflies before the storm” ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).

### Services, buyouts and incubations

- Gil sets about half a trillion dollars of US software spend against $5 trillion in services headcount costs that AI could transform. Corporate AI mandates are opening markets ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).
- He has backed firms that buy companies and rework them with AI, unlike older tech-enabled roll-ups that were mostly valuation arbitrage. Guo says such buyouts shortcut change management, and that services founders tell her distribution, not automation, is the problem ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).
- Guo sees founders aiming for high revenue per employee. Gil thinks real headcount cuts are a couple of years away, with SDRs and support first ([New markets](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/how-ai-is-opening-up-new-markets-and-impacting-the-startup-status-quo/)).

### No-code and code generation

- Liu argues code generation will struggle to produce sophisticated end-to-end process-automation apps. Generated code suits developers because they can inspect and steer it ([Airtable](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/low-code-in-the-age-of-ai-and-going-enterprise-with-howie-liu-from-air/)).
- Guo adds that if professional developers cannot zero-shot applications, non-developers are unlikely to. Liu concludes that generated apps should come out as no-code a non-technical owner can edit ([Airtable](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/low-code-in-the-age-of-ai-and-going-enterprise-with-howie-liu-from-air/)).
- In the mid-year compilation, Dylan Field says it is not clear humans will write every line of code in three to five years, but expects more and better-designed software rather than fewer designers ([Best of 2024](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/the-best-of-2024-so-far-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-gil/)).

### Mid-year clips: evals and hardware

- Alexandr Wang says academic benchmarks often leak into training data and calls for public leaderboards. Brett Adcock explains why hardware development at Figure is more methodical than software ([Best of 2024](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/07/the-best-of-2024-so-far-with-sarah-guo-and-elad-gil/)).
