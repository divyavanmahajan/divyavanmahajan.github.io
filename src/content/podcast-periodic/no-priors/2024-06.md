## Overview

June on No Priors was four separate founder conversations with no shared thread. Each guest argued against a default assumption of the time: that long context will replace retrieval, that scaling language models leads to AGI, that video should be generated end to end, and that Transformers are the only architecture worth running. Together they make a month about alternatives to the mainstream path.

## Main themes

### Retrieval over long context

- Tengyu Ma of Voyage AI says retrieval quality, not the LLM, is the main bottleneck in RAG for enterprise data. His view: “my prediction is that rag will be much cheaper than long context going forward” ([RAG with Tengyu Ma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/the-evolution-and-promise-of-rag-architecture-with-tengyu-ma-from-voya/)).
- Ma frames context as short-term memory and RAG as long-term memory. Many companies hold 100 million tokens, far beyond a million-token window ([RAG with Tengyu Ma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/the-evolution-and-promise-of-rag-architecture-with-tengyu-ma-from-voya/)).
- Voyage fine-tuned embeddings on code and legal data, reporting 15-20% gains for code. Latency budgets of 50 ms to 1 second keep embedding models below about 10 billion parameters, and longer-context embeddings reduce the need for chunking ([RAG with Tengyu Ma](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/the-evolution-and-promise-of-rag-architecture-with-tengyu-ma-from-voya/)).

### Measuring AGI differently

- Mike Knoop rejects the economically-useful-work definition of AGI, which he credits to Vinod: “I think it says more about what many humans do for work than it does about actual general intelligence” ([ARC Prize](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/how-the-arc-prize-is-democratizing-the-race-to-agi-with-mike-knoop-fro/)).
- He follows François Chollet in defining general intelligence as efficiently acquiring new skills. On ARC-AGI, state of the art rose only from 20% to 34% in four years, and he says language models do not work to beat it ([ARC Prize](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/how-the-arc-prize-is-democratizing-the-race-to-agi-with-mike-knoop-fro/)).
- The ARC Prize is a $1M+ nonprofit challenge. About 300 mostly small teams entered the 2023 contest, and Knoop expects an outsider to find the solution, possibly in under 10,000 lines of code ([ARC Prize](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/how-the-arc-prize-is-democratizing-the-race-to-agi-with-mike-knoop-fro/)).

### AI video built from components

- Joshua Xu says filming, not editing, is the costly part of video, so HeyGen started with avatars. A CEO shoot can need two weeks of scheduling ([HeyGen](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/can-ai-replace-the-camera-with-joshua-xu-from-heygen/)).
- HeyGen splits video into avatar A-roll, B-roll and voice, assembled by an orchestration engine, rather than generating clips end to end. Xu says logos and fonts should not be generated at all ([HeyGen](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/can-ai-replace-the-camera-with-joshua-xu-from-heygen/)).
- Lower loss does not mean better visuals, so HeyGen relies on in-product A/B tests. It has just over 40 staff serving over 40,000 paying customers ([HeyGen](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/can-ai-replace-the-camera-with-joshua-xu-from-heygen/)).

### State space models and the edge

- Albert Gu describes SSMs such as Mamba as fuzzy compressors with constant time per token. Hybrids of about 10 SSM layers to one attention layer work best ([Cartesia](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/state-space-models-and-real-time-intelligence-with-karan-goel-and-albe/)).
- Karan Goel wants capabilities that cost a million dollars in the data center to run for $10 at the edge. Cartesia starts with Sonic, a real-time text-to-speech engine ([Cartesia](/podcasts/no-priors/2024/06/state-space-models-and-real-time-intelligence-with-karan-goel-and-albe/)).
