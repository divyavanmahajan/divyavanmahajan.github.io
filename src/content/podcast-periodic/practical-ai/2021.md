## Overview

Practical AI's 2021 was about getting models out of notebooks and into production, and about the tools that make that easier. GPT-3 and Codex reached developers, with GitHub Copilot as the year's most discussed product. Data quality, labeling and privacy-preserving training became recurring threads. Several guests also argued that new hardware and compilers are needed to keep AI efficient.

## Main themes

### Large language models reach developers

- The hosts present GitHub Copilot as an AI pair programmer, and Chris Benson calls it not a strategic threat: “I think that this is a tactical goodie” ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).
- Natalie Pistunovich finds Codex a fast pair programmer for code, tests and documentation, but not for configuration or keys ([OpenAI Codex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/ai-generated-code-with-openai-codex/)).
- Nicholas Mohnacky builds bundleIQ on GPT-3 but sees relying on it as a black box as a business risk ([Eureka moments with NLP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/eureka-moments-with-natural-language-processing/)).
- By December OpenAI's API had no waitlist and Hugging Face was becoming a hub for models and datasets ([OpenAI and Hugging Face tooling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/openai-and-hugging-face-tooling/)).

### From notebook to production

- Nhung Ho warns against an obsession with real-time models and advises starting with batch ([Intuit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/04/data-science-for-intuitive-user-experiences/)).
- Tuhin Srivastava says most model value is lost between notebook and production ([BaseTen](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/06/the-fastest-way-to-build-ml-powered-apps/)); Ville Tuulos wants infrastructure taken off data scientists' hands ([Metaflow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/from-notebooks-to-netflix-scale-with-metaflow/)).
- Roey Mechrez blames drifting data for enterprise AI failures in production ([Towards stability and robustness](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/towards-stability-and-robustness/)).

### Data as the strategic asset

- Michael Malyuk argues “the infrastructure for ML kind of becomes a commodity”, so labeling and data preparation are the advantage ([Trends in data labeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/trends-in-data-labeling/)).
- Gretel's John Myers engineers privacy in at the source with synthetic data ([Gretel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/02/cooking-up-synthetic-data-with-gretel/)); Jeff Adams says data, not algorithms, limits speech technology for low-resource languages ([25 years of speech technology](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/05/25-years-of-speech-technology-innovation/)).
- The hosts argue human and machine together beat either alone ([Balancing human intelligence with AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/09/balancing-human-intelligence-with-ai/)).

### Federated learning becomes practical

- Benson says implementation technology has finally caught up with the idea of training a central model on decentralised data ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/10/federated-learning/)).
- Daniel Beutel says Flower lets teams take an existing workload and “federate it in less than 20 lines of code” ([Friendly federated learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/friendly-federated-learning/)).

### Hardware, efficiency and responsibility

- Roy Schwartz and Jesse Dodge want compute costs reported to curb carbon costs and research inequality ([Green AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/03/green-ai/)).
- Graphcore co-designs chips with their software stack ([Graphcore](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/04/going-full-bore-with-graphcore/)), Luis Ceze compiles models for any hardware ([Apache TVM](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/05/apache-tvm-and-octoml/)), and Lightmatter bets on photonics ([Photonic computing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/photonic-computing-for-ai-acceleration/)).
- Paula Goldman says AI ethics is maturing into a standard discipline, as security did ([Technology as a force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).
