## Overview

May on Practical AI moved from protocols to products to privacy. The hosts gave a full episode to the Model Context Protocol, Superhuman's head of engineering explained how LLMs change what product quality means, and Intel's Patrick Foley opened a two-part series on federated learning in production.

## Main themes

### MCP as AI middleware

- Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson explain that before MCP everyone wired tools into models with custom code and prompts, so sharing tools meant rewriting them. MCP gives a common interface, much as HTTP did for the web ([MCP Deep Dive](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/model-context-protocol-deep-dive/)).
- Whitenack describes hosts, clients and servers, and says “an MCP server within the specification of the protocol can expose tools, resources, or prompts” ([MCP Deep Dive](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/model-context-protocol-deep-dive/)).
- He turned Prediction Guard's FastAPI text-to-SQL service into an MCP server with about three lines of code, and tells listeners to vet third-party servers like any web server ([MCP Deep Dive](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/model-context-protocol-deep-dive/)).
- Benson credits Anthropic with shipping good examples and SDKs from the start. Whitenack says open models can use MCP if prompted with examples, and Loïc Houssier lists MCP among the fast-arriving changes that reshape roadmaps ([MCP Deep Dive](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/model-context-protocol-deep-dive/), [Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).

### Product quality when users write the prompts

- Houssier says Superhuman once held a zero-bug standard, but “now, with LLMs, a bunch of the perceived quality depends on your prompt” ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).
- His response is system prompts around user input, prompt guidance and shared libraries, plus being explicit about quality risk at launch ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).
- Auto draft, which writes ready-to-send replies in the user's tone, is the feature users talk about most ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).

### Best model first, cheaper model later

- Superhuman launches features on the most expensive model so weak feedback is not caused by the model, then moves to cheaper or fine-tuned models once a feature has traction ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).
- Houssier says BERT-type classifiers can match LLM labelling quality at a fraction of the cost, which requires easy model switching, A/B tests and golden-dataset evals ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).
- He also notes that Fortune 500 executives now push their security teams to approve tools from young AI startups ([Superhuman](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/emailing-like-a-superhuman/)).

### Federated learning for privacy and governance

- Patrick Foley explains that federated learning sends the model to the data, with only model weights crossing the network. OpenFL limits that traffic to NumPy bytes to reduce exfiltration risk ([Federated learning, part 1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/federated-learning-in-production-part-1/)).
- OpenFL began with Intel and the University of Pennsylvania deploying federated training across hospitals from around 2018. It was open sourced around 2020 and now sits under the Linux Foundation ([Federated learning, part 1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/federated-learning-in-production-part-1/)).
- Foley says most real deployments so far use non-generative models, while PEFT and quantisation make federated training of large models feasible ([Federated learning, part 1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/federated-learning-in-production-part-1/)).
- He agrees with Benson that privacy and governance are the real drivers, and describes Intel's service for vetting and identity records between parties that do not trust each other ([Federated learning, part 1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/05/federated-learning-in-production-part-1/)).
