## Overview

June on Practical AI was about trusting AI agents. Two episodes covered how to secure and certify agents in the enterprise, one through Anthropic's zero trust framework and one through the AIUC-1 standard. The month opened with the hosts reading the 2026 Stanford AI Index.

## Main themes

### Zero trust for agents

- Daniel Whitenack argues organisations will have to adopt autonomous agents, partly because attackers already use agentic tools, and that most enterprises do not yet operate this way ([Zero trust](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/zero-trust-for-ai-agents/)).
- Anthropic's framework lists threats such as indirect prompt injection, tool misuse through MCP, identity abuse and memory poisoning. Each control has foundation, enterprise and advanced tiers, starting with “unique cryptographic identifiers for each agent instance.” ([Zero trust](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/zero-trust-for-ai-agents/)).
- As exploit timelines shrink, Whitenack says “You can't just, like, rely on waking up the CISO in the middle of the night” to approve containment ([Zero trust](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/zero-trust-for-ai-agents/)).
- Chris Benson, who works in defence, says rolling back an agent in a critical function is hard because the function still has to run ([Zero trust](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/zero-trust-for-ai-agents/)).

### Certification, audits and insurance

- Emil Lassen of AIUC argues agents need the trust layer that earlier technologies got from standards, third-party audits and insurance. He says enterprises will not trust a vendor's own safety claims ([AIUC-1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/aiuc-1-building-trust-in-ai-agents/)).
- AIUC-1 covers the agent layer that ISO 42001 and SOC 2 leave out. Six of its 40 mandatory requirements concern red teaming, and about 250 security leaders update it quarterly ([AIUC-1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/aiuc-1-building-trust-in-ai-agents/)).
- AIUC attacks each agent with roughly 1,000 to 5,000 scenarios and retests every quarter. No P0 or P1 findings are allowed, and certified companies can buy insurance for their agents ([AIUC-1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/aiuc-1-building-trust-in-ai-agents/)).
- Lassen says agentic systems can always be jailbroken under enough pressure, so a realistic report is worth more than a spotless one ([AIUC-1](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/aiuc-1-building-trust-in-ai-agents/)).

### The state of AI in numbers

- The Stanford AI Index says capability is accelerating, and over 90% of notable frontier models were produced in 2025 ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/breaking-down-the-2026-stanford-ai-index-report/)).
- The report finds the US-China performance gap has closed. Whitenack says China clearly leads on open models, and the US hosts the most AI data centres while most chips come from one Taiwanese foundry ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/breaking-down-the-2026-stanford-ai-index-report/)).
- On the jagged frontier, models “can win a gold medal at the International Mathematical Olympiad but cannot reliably tell time”. Whitenack argues they need a harness around them ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/breaking-down-the-2026-stanford-ai-index-report/)).

### Jobs and learning

- Productivity gains appear in the same fields where entry-level jobs decline. Whitenack says junior SQL-writing roles are gone, but shared skills repos can help new hires level up faster ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/breaking-down-the-2026-stanford-ai-index-report/)).
- Most students use AI for school while few teachers have a policy. Both hosts urge using AI to learn rather than only to finish tasks ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/06/breaking-down-the-2026-stanford-ai-index-report/)).
