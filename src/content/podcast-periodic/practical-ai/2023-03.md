## Overview

March on Practical AI had three founder interviews, each on a different layer of the stack. Erik Bernhardsson of Modal covered cloud compute for data teams. Bryan McCann of You.com and Beth Rudden of Bast AI both argued that generated answers need grounding and clear attribution, a few months after ChatGPT arrived.

## Main themes

### Cloud compute in the developer's feedback loop

- Bernhardsson says developer productivity comes down to feedback loops, and cloud deployment is the slowest of them. Modal built its own container runtime, file system and container builder so that cached code starts in the cloud in about a second ([Cloud compute](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/end-to-end-cloud-compute-for-ai-ml/)).
- He describes infrastructure defined in Python code, with zero config and no YAML. He credits front-end hot reloading for the live redeploy that host Daniel Whitenack, who has 129 Modal apps deployed, praises ([Cloud compute](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/end-to-end-cloud-compute-for-ai-ml/)).
- Online GPU inference was a surprise hit, driven by the cost of idle endpoints and slow autoscaling. He notes that “some people even say modal is faster than running things locally, even though it's running in the cloud” ([Cloud compute](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/end-to-end-cloud-compute-for-ai-ml/)).

### Search moves from links to grounded answers

- McCann says two things are new: models understand context far better, and they can generate answers. YouChat sits on a traditional search engine and cites its results to reduce hallucination ([You.com](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/ai-search-at-you-com/)).
- You.com's open platform lets partners build an app in a couple of hours and keep any monetisation from it. McCann sees this as a response to answers replacing link clicks ([You.com](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/ai-search-at-you-com/)).
- His advice to practitioners is to “try to anticipate the hallucinations, try to ground things, try to provide clear attribution as much as possible” and to measure outputs ([You.com](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/ai-search-at-you-com/)).

### Explainable AI through ontologies and provenance

- Rudden's Bast AI ingests a user's document into an ontology, keeping each entity's source paragraph and page so answers can cite where they came from. About 50 models run through its orchestration ([Explainable AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/explainable-ai-that-is-accessible-for-all-humans/)).
- She wants the black-box model to do the generating, not the answering. Of generative transformers she says “It doesn't understand what you're saying. It's a stochastic parrot. It mimics language.” ([Explainable AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/explainable-ai-that-is-accessible-for-all-humans/)).
- Rudden argues the semantic side of NLP was neglected once GPUs made statistical models dominant. She hopes attribution becomes the valued feature across text, images and video ([Explainable AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/explainable-ai-that-is-accessible-for-all-humans/)).
- McCann and Rudden both mention ChatGPT, and both warn against treating chat models as people ([You.com](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/ai-search-at-you-com/), [Explainable AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/03/explainable-ai-that-is-accessible-for-all-humans/)).
