## Overview

December on Practical AI was three guest interviews about AI built for a specific job. A small Paris lab explained its real-time speech model, Shopify described how it picks models for its merchant assistant, and a startup founder pitched music listening data as an early warning for workplace burnout.

## Main themes

### Real-time speech from a small open lab

- Alexandre Défossez describes Kyutai as a Paris non-profit, launched about a year earlier and funded by Xavier Niel, Rodolphe Saadé and Eric Schmidt. It chose not to compete on text-only LLMs ([Kyutai](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/full-duplex-real-time-dialogue-with-kyutai/)).
- Moshi is a 7 billion parameter model that is “completely full duplex, meaning that the model can both listen and speak at any time”, with about 200 ms latency ([Kyutai](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/full-duplex-real-time-dialogue-with-kyutai/)).
- Separate audio and text streams let the same framework act as text-to-speech or speech recognition, depending on whether text runs ahead of or behind the audio ([Kyutai](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/full-duplex-real-time-dialogue-with-kyutai/)).
- Défossez says the non-profit setup made it easier to release Moshi under a commercially friendly licence. He now wants smaller on-device models that hand harder questions to larger ones ([Kyutai](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/full-duplex-real-time-dialogue-with-kyutai/)).

### Pragmatic model choice at Shopify

- Mike Tamir says Shopify came late to ML but redirected heavily towards it from 2022. He calls its approach to model choice unembarrassedly pragmatic ([Sidekick](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/sidekick-is-an-ai-shopify-expert/)).
- Matt Colyer pitches Sidekick as the always-available co-founder merchants wish they had, and says its core has been replaced many times as better approaches appeared ([Sidekick](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/sidekick-is-an-ai-shopify-expert/)).
- Tamir says pragmatism needs a definition of working: “you have to have your eval system dialed in.” Colyer treats evals as a compass that shows whether A beats B ([Sidekick](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/sidekick-is-an-ai-shopify-expert/)).
- Colyer expects a hybrid of commercial and open models, and predicts agents will replace clicking through forms within about ten years. Tamir expects bumps first, as with self-driving ([Sidekick](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/sidekick-is-an-ai-shopify-expert/)).

### Music as a wellbeing signal

- Jeff Smith argues pulse surveys fail because people skip them or answer cautiously. One CHRO told him a single survey cost $250,000 and took 90 days ([CHRP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/mozart-to-megadeth-at-chrp/)).
- CHRP taps users' existing streaming on an opt-in basis and builds a personal baseline over three to four weeks. Smith treats music as a mirror of mood ([CHRP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/mozart-to-megadeth-at-chrp/)).
- Organisations see only anonymised, team-level dashboards of motivation, stress and balance. Smith presents it as screening, not diagnosis, and says CHRP has turned away executives who wanted it to fix their culture ([CHRP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/12/mozart-to-megadeth-at-chrp/)).
