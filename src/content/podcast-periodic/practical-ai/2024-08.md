## Overview

August on Practical AI was about making LLM applications trustworthy in production. Two guests covered security and evaluation of the application rather than the model, and the hosts broke down what data AI actually needs while reviewing the newly active EU AI Act. Intel opened the month with its case for cheaper, available hardware.

## Main themes

### Security is a property of the application

- Donato Capitella of WithSecure argues the threat model of the use case matters more than whether a model is secure. His first rule: “we don't trust anything the LLM produces” ([Threat modeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/threat-modeling-llm-apps/)).
- His LLM security canvas is a set of controls, starting with output validation, since rendered links or Markdown images can leak user data ([Threat modeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/threat-modeling-llm-apps/)).
- Capitella says prompt injection has no known fix. He warns that an LLM-as-judge is itself open to injection, while Daniel Whitenack favours small classifiers that run fast on CPUs ([Threat modeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/threat-modeling-llm-apps/)).
- Browser agents raise the stakes: a malicious page could redirect one into the user's mailbox, so he recommends human approval for tool calls and alerts into the SIEM ([Threat modeling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/threat-modeling-llm-apps/)).

### Evaluating applications, not models

- Shahul Es of Ragas says assuming a model's published accuracy carries over to your app would be “a wild assumption to make” ([Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/)).
- Metrics-driven development borrows from test-driven development: score the pipeline before and after swapping a prompt or retriever, and think in scores rather than pass/fail ([Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/)).
- Most Ragas metrics are LLM-based because they track human judgment better; a planned feature aligns judges with a team's own strictness ([Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/)).
- He recommends 100 to 500 test samples with good coverage, generated synthetically from internal documents and pruned by hand ([Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/)).

### What data, for what purpose

- Whitenack splits the slogan that AI is only as good as the data into pre-training, evaluation, fine-tuning and runtime augmentation data, using Llama 3.1 as the worked example ([Only as good as the data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/only-as-good-as-the-data/)).
- He argues most companies should fine-tune an existing model rather than train from scratch, and stresses that RAG changes no model parameters ([Only as good as the data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/only-as-good-as-the-data/)).
- RAG came up across three of the four episodes, from Intel's enterprise platform to Ragas metrics ([Gaudi](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/gaudi-processors-intel-s-ai-portfolio/), [Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/)).

### The EU AI Act takes effect

- Chris Benson traces the Act from its April 2021 proposal to entry into force on 1 August 2024, with provisions phasing in over 6 to 36 months ([Only as good as the data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/only-as-good-as-the-data/)).
- He walks the risk tiers from social scoring to spam filters and notes military and national security uses are exempt. Whitenack expects European rules to spread to the US and beyond ([Only as good as the data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/only-as-good-as-the-data/)).

### Hardware beyond NVIDIA

- Greg Serochi pitches Gaudi, a dedicated AI processor rather than a GPU, as a low-cost alternative driven by customer demand around cost and availability ([Gaudi](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/gaudi-processors-intel-s-ai-portfolio/)).
- Benjamin Consolvo lays out Xeon, the AI PC and the future Falcon Shores GPU; Whitenack, whose Prediction Guard runs on Gaudi, backs the easy migration through PyTorch and Hugging Face ([Gaudi](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/gaudi-processors-intel-s-ai-portfolio/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Threat modeling LLM apps](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/threat-modeling-llm-apps/): a practical, control-by-control approach to LLM security from a working penetration tester.
- [Metrics Driven Development](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/metrics-driven-development/): a clear method for evaluating LLM applications as engineers rather than model builders.
- [Only as good as the data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/only-as-good-as-the-data/): a back-to-basics map of data types plus a concise EU AI Act briefing.
- [Gaudi processors & Intel's AI portfolio](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/08/gaudi-processors-intel-s-ai-portfolio/): Intel's hardware and software roadmap as an NVIDIA alternative.
