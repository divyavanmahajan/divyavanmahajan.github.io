## Overview

November on Practical AI kept returning to how models are released, licensed and trusted. Two host episodes covered licensing and the month's controversies around GitHub Copilot and Meta's Galactica. Two guest interviews covered contextual AI for harmful content and quantum processors as accelerators for classical computing.

## Main themes

### Licensing and releasing models

- Daniel Whitenack explains how his SIL team wrote a Responsible AI License for benchmark models. He says models fit into a weird space between software and data, so neither licence type fits cleanly ([Releasing models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/the-practicalities-of-releasing-models/)).
- The team restricted commercial use because, in Whitenack's words, “big companies could use language data from language communities to make money without any real benefit going back to those language communities.” They also matched the Creative Commons restrictions of the training data in good faith ([Releasing models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/the-practicalities-of-releasing-models/)).
- Whitenack contrasts OpenAI's controlled API, Stability's open release under OpenRAIL and overclaiming. He argues that the expectations set at release drive how people perceive and adopt a model ([Copilot and Galactica](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/copilot-lawsuits-galactica-science/)).

### Copilot in court, Galactica in trouble

- Whitenack describes the 3 November class action against GitHub, Microsoft and OpenAI, which claims Copilot suggestions lack the attribution open source licences require ([Copilot and Galactica](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/copilot-lawsuits-galactica-science/)).
- He judges that advocacy groups would target large organisations rather than small teams, and still uses Copilot. Chris Benson says he likes it but is never quite comfortable with it, and expects years of litigation ([Copilot and Galactica](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/copilot-lawsuits-galactica-science/)).
- Meta's Galactica was trained on 48 million papers, textbooks and reference materials, yet invented giraffes on lunar missions in Janelle Shane's test. Benson calls it a misstep in the race to release, after which “no one's going to trust that model.” ([Copilot and Galactica](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/copilot-lawsuits-galactica-science/)).

### Contextual AI against online harm

- Matar Haller of ActiveFence says keyword bans are evaded with emojis, leetspeak and coded numbers, so keywords and n-grams fall short and models must read context ([Database of Evil](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/protecting-us-with-the-database-of-evil/)).
- Her examples include an ISIS logo next to a CNN logo and a chef demonstrating knives, where titles, comments and platform policy change the verdict ([Database of Evil](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/protecting-us-with-the-database-of-evil/)).
- Content gets a risk score, analysts verify it, and confirmed violations go into the database of evil for matching and retraining. Clients can define their own version of evil, and domain experts' knowledge becomes engineered features ([Database of Evil](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/protecting-us-with-the-database-of-evil/)).

### Quantum processors beside CPUs and GPUs

- Yonatan Cohen, CTO of Quantum Machines, argues early quantum advantage will come from QPUs accelerating certain subroutines, not from replacing classical machines ([Quantum](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/hybrid-computing-with-quantum-processors/)).
- He says high error rates are the main problem today. His company builds the classical control hardware that drives qubits, plus a workflow tool called Entropy for mixing CPU, GPU and QPU steps ([Quantum](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/hybrid-computing-with-quantum-processors/)).
- Cohen expects most AI developers to reach quantum subroutines through libraries wrapped by frameworks such as TensorFlow or PyTorch ([Quantum](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/11/hybrid-computing-with-quantum-processors/)).
