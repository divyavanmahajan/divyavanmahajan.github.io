## Overview

May on Practical AI kept returning to what it takes to run machine learning in production. A TensorFlow Dev Summit recap and an interview with O'Reilly's Ben Lorica both stressed pipelines, governance and tooling over model training. The month also covered quantum machine learning and how practitioners can join the AI community through meetups and talks.

## Main themes

### Production ML beyond the model

- Daniel Whitenack says training is a small part of production work. He walks through TFX components, a shared metadata store, lineage tracking and orchestration via Airflow or Kubeflow ([TensorFlow Dev Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/tensorflow-dev-summit-2019/)).
- Chris Benson calls TFX one of the summit's most important announcements, because teams he worked with used TensorFlow in very different, unstandardised ways ([TensorFlow Dev Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/tensorflow-dev-summit-2019/)).
- Ben Lorica expects models to become assets that need governance: “We'll have to have tools for model governance, model operations, right? So monitoring, tracking.” He notes MLflow reached over 200 user companies in 10 months ([Enterprise adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/ai-adoption-in-the-enterprise/)).

### Enterprise adoption: leaders pull ahead

- Drawing on O'Reilly's survey, Lorica says companies with mature practices plan to invest far more than explorers, so “the gap between the leaders and the laggards may widen” ([Enterprise adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/ai-adoption-in-the-enterprise/)).
- Explorers struggle to identify use cases and with company culture, while mature firms cite lack of data and skilled people. Early successes tended to build on existing analytics infrastructure and familiar KPIs ([Enterprise adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/ai-adoption-in-the-enterprise/)).
- Lorica sees the data scientist role splitting, with many moving to the machine learning engineer title. He lists fairness, privacy, security, safety and explainability as risks beyond accuracy ([Enterprise adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/ai-adoption-in-the-enterprise/)).

### TensorFlow 2.0 gets easier

- Benson describes an 80-20 split, with most work going through Keras and the lower-level API kept for edge cases. He thinks the user experience is now probably competitive with PyTorch ([TensorFlow Dev Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/tensorflow-dev-summit-2019/)).
- Whitenack says the deferred graph execution of TensorFlow 1.x was his main blocker for debugging, so eager execution by default is welcome ([TensorFlow Dev Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/tensorflow-dev-summit-2019/)).
- The hosts also cover TensorFlow Federated, TensorFlow Privacy, TensorFlow Lite, TensorFlow.js and Google's Coral Edge TPU hardware ([TensorFlow Dev Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/tensorflow-dev-summit-2019/)).

### Quantum computing and machine learning

- Shohini Ghose argues quantum computing offers new rules for computation rather than more speed. She says the area of quantum machine learning is growing fast, with Waterloo and Toronto as hubs ([Quantum mechanics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/when-ai-meets-quantum-mechanics/)).
- Marcus Edwards says current machines from D-Wave and IBM are still too small for large optimisation or ML gains. Both guests expect cloud access and hybrid algorithms rather than quantum laptops ([Quantum mechanics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/when-ai-meets-quantum-mechanics/)).
- Ghose explains that tensor networks from quantum physics map onto neural network structures. Whitenack recalls ML methods outperforming his grad-school physics methods ([Quantum mechanics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/when-ai-meets-quantum-mechanics/)).

### Getting plugged into the community

- Whitenack sorts events into meetups, large industry conferences, focused tool events and research conferences. He suggests a focused event such as spaCy IRL may beat a broad $2,000-3,000 conference ([AI community](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/how-to-get-plugged-into-the-ai-community/)).
- Benson advises promising a short meetup talk as a way to learn a topic. He relays Lorica's advice that abstracts should describe how you solve the problem ([AI community](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/05/how-to-get-plugged-into-the-ai-community/)).
