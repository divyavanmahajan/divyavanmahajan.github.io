## Overview

September on Practical AI was about how machine learning gets built and run in practice. Sherol Chen explained AutoML at Google, Chris Benson set out an HPC strategy for AI at Lockheed Martin, and Samuel Taylor of Indeed made the case for open source side projects. The month closed with AI talent outside the US and Europe and with Hugging Face's DistilBERT.

## Main themes

### AutoML and Google's AI organisation

- Chen describes AutoML as the middle ground between pre-trained APIs and building models from scratch. Its aim is the best prediction model for a labelled dataset, even if that is a couple of if statements ([AutoML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/automl-and-ai-at-google/)).
- She says AutoML's purpose “should really be able to extend the data scientist's abilities and intuitions”, because preparing data and judging results still need expertise ([AutoML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/automl-and-ai-at-google/)).
- AutoML Tables, launched in April 2019, hides hyperparameters and feature analytics, trading control and interpretability for simplicity ([AutoML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/automl-and-ai-at-google/)).
- Chen explains how Google's research and product groups relate, with TensorFlow developed inside Google Brain and the company's move to AI first at Google I/O 2017 ([AutoML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/automl-and-ai-at-google/)).

### AI and high performance computing

- Benson says AI workloads were not part of HPC until recently. Mass hyperparameter exploration and large-scale self-play can absorb all available compute ([HPC](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/worlds-are-colliding-ai-and-hpc/), [AutoML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/automl-and-ai-at-google/)).
- He argues an HPC strategy must plan for hardware refresh in phases and an extensible software stack. Lockheed Martin already has substantial HPC capability but is reassessing how it provides it ([HPC](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/worlds-are-colliding-ai-and-hpc/)).
- User experience decides whether an expensive cluster gets used. Benson names Google Colab as his favourite workflow and expects cloud providers to move towards clustering as a service ([HPC](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/worlds-are-colliding-ai-and-hpc/)).

### Open source and learning by building

- Taylor describes full-stack data science at Indeed, where one person owns a model from idea through deployment and monitoring, with uses such as salary estimation and job fit ([Open source](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/the-influence-of-open-source-on-ai-development/)).
- Benson suggests Google open-sourced TensorFlow to capture mindshare. Taylor agrees, and says some models, such as fraud models, should never be released ([Open source](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/the-influence-of-open-source-on-ai-development/)).
- Taylor recommends side projects that are tangible or scratch a personal itch, vetting libraries through trusted colleagues, stars and forks, and testing competing libraries behind a feature flag ([Open source](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/the-influence-of-open-source-on-ai-development/)).

### AI in the majority world and smaller models

- Whitenack points to ICLR in Ethiopia, Google AI offices in Bangalore and Ghana and the Deep Learning Indaba. Benson thinks big-tech sponsorship is partly because “it's really hard to find great talent” ([Majority world](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/ai-in-the-majority-world-and-model-distillation/)).
- Whitenack describes AI Singapore's competitive nine-month apprenticeship on real company problems and hopes other countries copy it ([Majority world](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/ai-in-the-majority-world-and-model-distillation/)).
- Hugging Face used teacher-student distillation to build DistilBERT, with 66 million parameters and 95% of BERT's GLUE performance, aimed at latency, phones and lower environmental cost ([Majority world](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/ai-in-the-majority-world-and-model-distillation/)).
- Whitenack calls for monitoring live voice systems for bias across accents and dialects. AI bias also came up in the open source episode ([Majority world](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/ai-in-the-majority-world-and-model-distillation/), [Open source](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/09/the-influence-of-open-source-on-ai-development/)).
