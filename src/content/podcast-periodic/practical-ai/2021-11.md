## Overview

November on Practical AI moved from the physical limits of AI hardware to the state of the field and two new kinds of language model use. Lightmatter's CEO made the case for photonic chips, the hosts read through the 2021 AI Index, and two episodes looked at what GPT-3's successors could do: zero-shot prompting with T0 and code generation with Codex.

## Main themes

### The energy wall and photonic chips

- Nick Harris says the end of Dennard scaling around 2005 means transistors no longer use less energy as they shrink, so chips run hotter. He warns that “progress in AI will slow because of the heat problem” ([Photonic computing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/photonic-computing-for-ai-acceleration/)).
- He cites a Department of Energy estimate that compute and interconnect will use 10% of the planet's energy by 2030, with chips such as NVIDIA's A100 at about 450 watts ([Photonic computing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/photonic-computing-for-ai-acceleration/)).
- Lightmatter's Envise is a quad-core photonic matrix processor quoted at about 80 watts. Harris says it scales through higher clock rates and several colours of light rather than smaller devices, and targets inference because deployment is where most AI energy goes ([Photonic computing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/photonic-computing-for-ai-acceleration/)).

### What the 2021 AI Index says

- Daniel Whitenack cites more than $13.8 billion invested in AI drug design, four and a half times the 2019 level ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/)).
- Chris Benson notes that 65% of North American AI PhDs went into industry, up from 44.4% a decade earlier. Whitenack links this to scarce academic posts and higher salaries ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/)).
- ImageNet training time fell from 6.2 minutes in 2018 to 47 seconds in 2020, though Whitenack warns this may just mean more runs and more energy ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/)).
- Benson asks why the 222-page report says little about automation and jobs, a topic the hosts returned to later in the month with a keynote's human-to-automated task scale ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/), [Zero-shot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/zero-shot-multitask-learning/)).

### Ethics and generative risk

- The Allen Institute's Delphi, trained on 1.7 million ethical judgements, was rated 92.1% accurate by Mechanical Turk workers, above GPT-3, but stumbled on time-of-day questions and reflects today's status quo ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/)).
- Benson says “the generative stuff worries me the most in the short term”, far more than sentient AI, because convincing targeted content can erode a shared sense of reality ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/)).

### Language models beyond GPT-3

- Whitenack describes BigScience as a CERN-style collaboration of 600 researchers from 50 countries and more than 250 institutions. Its first model, T0, beats GPT-3 on some tasks while being 16 times smaller ([Zero-shot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/zero-shot-multitask-learning/)).
- T0 was trained on many labelled NLP tasks rewritten as prompts, which Whitenack says targets the zero-shot, custom-prompt use people want ([Zero-shot](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/zero-shot-multitask-learning/)).
- Natalie Pistunovich explains that Codex, the model behind GitHub Copilot, writes library calls, unit tests and explanations in seconds. She calls it “sort of like pair programming with a computer” ([Codex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/ai-generated-code-with-openai-codex/)).
- She expects configuration and infrastructure work to stay manual, since a generated SSH key looks right but is not valid. She argues Go suits model-serving infrastructure ([Codex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/ai-generated-code-with-openai-codex/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Photonic computing for AI acceleration](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/photonic-computing-for-ai-acceleration/): a clear argument that power and heat, not ideas, will limit AI scale.
- [Analyzing the 2021 AI Index Report](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/analyzing-the-2021-ai-index-report/): a compact tour of investment, talent, training speed and generative risk.
- [Zero-shot multitask learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/zero-shot-multitask-learning/): explains why prompted multitask training made a much smaller model competitive.
- [AI-generated code with OpenAI Codex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/11/ai-generated-code-with-openai-codex/): an early, practical view of AI coding assistants and their limits.
