## Overview

January on Practical AI had two episodes, and open-weight models ran through both. Genmo's Paras Jain explained why his company open-sourced its Mochi 1 video model, and the hosts closed the month with a deep dive into DeepSeek R1 that separated the downloadable model from the hosted app.

## Main themes

### DeepSeek: the model versus the product

- Daniel Whitenack argues the privacy concern attaches to DeepSeek's app and its terms, which keep user data for training on servers in China. The weights can run “in complete isolation where no data goes to DeepSeek, no data goes to China” ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).
- Chris Benson found the app refused a question about Tiananmen Square in 1989. Whitenack notes it first generated an answer and then withdrew it, which points to a product filter rather than the weights ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).
- Whitenack cites a study finding R1 more sensitive to prompt injection than other leading models, so self-hosting still needs monitoring ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).

### Cost claims and model optionality

- DeepSeek published a training figure of roughly $5-6 million. Citing Philipp Schmid, Whitenack says it covers only the final run and excludes RL, smaller runs and data generation; the team has access to tens of thousands of GPUs ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).
- R1 is a mixture-of-experts model of about 700 billion parameters needing around 16 H100s at full precision, while distilled versions from 1.5B to 70B parameters run on a laptop or one GPU ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).
- Whitenack calls DeepSeek “a shock to the general public's perception that there is model optionality out there.” Benson wonders whether investors will now question $100 million rounds ([DeepSeek](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/deep-dive-into-deepseek/)).

### Open video models and realistic motion

- Paras Jain says Genmo open-sourced Mochi 1 because no good open video model existed and that was holding the field back. The model has 10-11 billion parameters and runs on a consumer-grade GPU ([Video generation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/video-generation-with-realistic-motion/)).
- Jain argues the goal is to learn physics and subject motion. Most internet video barely moves, and he says walking does not emerge at one or two billion parameters ([Video generation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/video-generation-with-realistic-motion/)).
- Mochi leads open models on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard, but Jain says human raters favour detail over motion, so Genmo adds functional test cases ([Video generation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/01/video-generation-with-realistic-motion/)).
