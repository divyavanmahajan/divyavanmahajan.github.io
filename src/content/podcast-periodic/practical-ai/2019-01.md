## Overview

January on Practical AI was a turn-of-the-year month. The hosts reviewed 2018 and made predictions, then shared their learning plans for 2019. Between those, IBM researchers described using speech analysis to support clinicians.

## Main themes

### NLP takes the spotlight

- Daniel Whitenack says 2018 brought “a shift to a lot of focus on natural language processing”, pointing to BERT ([2018 in review](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/2018-in-review-and-bold-predictions-for-2019/)).
- Chris Benson describes a former employer moving its NLP work from legacy techniques to deep learning during the year ([2018 in review](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/2018-in-review-and-bold-predictions-for-2019/)).
- Whitenack's 2019 side project is to try Facebook's unsupervised machine translation code, possibly with BERT embeddings, on low-resource languages ([Dive into deep learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/new-year-s-resolution-dive-into-deep-learning/)).

### Trust, regulation and the shift to products

- Both hosts name GDPR and Cambridge Analytica as the year's two big shocks. Benson calls GDPR the first large-scale regulation of data and AI, and Whitenack expects it to push interpretability ([2018 in review](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/2018-in-review-and-bold-predictions-for-2019/)).
- Whitenack predicts pressure on data scientists to build scalable, versioned systems with tools such as Pachyderm and Kubeflow. Benson says “inferencing APIs, prediction APIs are going to become so standard in our stacks” ([2018 in review](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/2018-in-review-and-bold-predictions-for-2019/)).
- Benson backs Google's AutoML while Whitenack bets on transfer learning. Benson also sees AI augmenting workers rather than replacing them, and new roles such as chief AI officers ([2018 in review](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/2018-in-review-and-bold-predictions-for-2019/)).

### AI as an aid to clinicians

- Guillermo Cecchi and Ajay Royyuru of IBM Research argue that speech analysis can give psychiatrists and neurologists measurable signals they now judge by ear ([IBM](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/ibm-s-ai-for-detecting-neurological-state/)).
- Royyuru stresses consent by design: participants start each short phone session and decide what the clinician sees. The system surfaces relevant features rather than issuing diagnoses ([IBM](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/ibm-s-ai-for-detecting-neurological-state/)).

### Learning deep learning

- The hosts say you do not need a PhD to work in AI unless you want research, and Whitenack lists industry research routes such as the OpenAI Fellows Program and the Google AI Residency ([Dive into deep learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/new-year-s-resolution-dive-into-deep-learning/)).
- Whitenack suggests developers pick resources that stretch their maths and scientists pick ones with hands-on coding ([Dive into deep learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/new-year-s-resolution-dive-into-deep-learning/)).
- He argues that books only get you so far and learning sinks in through a side project. Benson values his Atlanta Deep Learning Meetup for the conversations ([Dive into deep learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/01/new-year-s-resolution-dive-into-deep-learning/)).
