## Overview

July on Practical AI was about the distance between AI research and AI that works inside a company. The hosts reacted to GitHub Copilot and the Bengio, Hinton and LeCun article on deep learning, a BeyondMinds CTO explained why most enterprise models fail once they meet real data, and the month closed with advice on building a data team.

## Main themes

### AI pair programmers arrive

- Daniel Whitenack describes GitHub Copilot as a collaboration between GitHub and OpenAI that can turn comments into code, closer to machine translation than autocomplete ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).
- Chris Benson welcomes it and calls Copilot “not a strategic threat to us. I think that this is a tactical goodie” ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).
- Whitenack sees the risk in over-reliance: generated code can introduce breaking changes that are hard to debug. He also worries that public training code may skew towards side projects ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).

### Deep learning defends its path

- Benson reads the Communications of the ACM article by the three Turing Award winners as a joint statement that the current deep learning path is the right one, a view he is sceptical of ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).
- The article's open problems include too much labelled data for supervised learning, too many trials for model-free reinforcement learning, weak robustness to change and immature reasoning ([From symbols to AI pair programmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/from-symbols-to-ai-pair-programmers/)).

### Why enterprise AI fails in production

- Roey Mechrez says enterprise AI faces a 90% failure rate outside the big tech firms, and the causes “are not organizational. They are technical problems.” ([Towards stability and robustness](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/towards-stability-and-robustness/)).
- His giraffe problem: a cat-and-dog classifier calls a giraffe a dog with full confidence, and AI usually fails silently. Out-of-distribution detection on inputs and confidence estimation on outputs are his two defences ([Towards stability and robustness](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/towards-stability-and-robustness/)).
- Low-confidence predictions go to a human, which matters most for mission-critical work such as insurance claims, anti-money laundering and fraud detection ([Towards stability and robustness](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/towards-stability-and-robustness/)).
- BeyondMinds wraps models in model-agnostic components such as an input gate and aims to cut the typical 12 to 14 months to production to about 12 weeks ([Towards stability and robustness](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/towards-stability-and-robustness/)).

### Building a data team

- Starting from Erik Bernhardsson's parable about a mid-stage startup, Benson says there is no standard idea of what a data team is. He now hires for the whole workflow, not only deep learning maths ([Building a data team](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/building-a-data-team/)).
- Whitenack notes that the first need is often SQL-style metrics, which can frustrate ML hires. Benson adds that useful telemetry is not always the right data for training models ([Building a data team](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/building-a-data-team/)).
- Both argue for a data-driven culture over self-proclaimed experts, and for working prototypes (for example in Streamlit) rather than accuracy figures from a GPU server ([Building a data team](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/07/building-a-data-team/)).
