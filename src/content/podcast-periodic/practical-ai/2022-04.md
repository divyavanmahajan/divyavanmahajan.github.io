## Overview

April on Practical AI looked at the gap between building a model and getting it in front of people. Gradio's Abubakar Abid made the case for interactive demos and OctoML's Luis Ceze argued that deployment should be ordinary DevOps. In between, three African researchers described AI for smallholder farmers built on local, open data.

## Main themes

### Demos that put models in front of people

- Abubakar Abid describes Gradio, now part of Hugging Face, as a Python library that wraps any model or function in a web GUI. He claims its components cover “95% of machine learning use cases” ([Gradio](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/quick-beautiful-web-uis-for-ml-apps/)).
- Abid argues demos build trust in good models and expose bias in bad ones, citing a clinician who altered an input image and watched the prediction change, and a flag button that collects failures for retraining ([Gradio](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/quick-beautiful-web-uis-for-ml-apps/)).
- Gradio targets prototyping rather than production traffic, and Hugging Face Spaces hosts demos for free. Whitenack and Benson say a live demo sets job candidates apart more than a notebook on GitHub ([Gradio](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/quick-beautiful-web-uis-for-ml-apps/)).

### AI for African smallholder farmers

- Leo Mutuku says subsistence households go hungry when rain-fed crops fail, and that AI “can really support those subsistence farmers at their household level” rather than only regionally ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/ai-in-africa-agriculture/)).
- Mutuku's localised early warning system combines phone-collected ground truth with Earth observation data for intercropped plots of 0.5 to 3 hectares. He sees yield estimates also feeding insurance and financial services ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/ai-in-africa-agriculture/)).
- Mutuku frames open data and citizen science as the answer to Africa's data sparsity, and Godliver Owomugisha stresses publishing collection protocols. Joyce Nabende's Makerere lab collects crop images and builds speech recognition for farmer radio shows ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/ai-in-africa-agriculture/)).
- With one extension officer per 10,000 farmers, LDRI trains peer farmer champions and treats county governments as co-designers ([AI in Africa](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/ai-in-africa-agriculture/)).

### MLOps narrowed, deployment automated

- Luis Ceze quips that asking 10 people what MLOps is yields 12 answers. He wants MLOps to cover data and model creation, with packaging, serving and monitoring left to DevOps ([MLOps is NOT Real](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/mlops-is-not-real/)).
- Ceze argues models are “treated as this special thing that's just hard to deploy”, which slows intelligent applications that really run ensembles of models ([MLOps is NOT Real](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/mlops-is-not-real/)).
- He sees a shift from end-to-end platforms such as SageMaker or Azure ML towards best-in-class tools. OctoML uses Apache TVM to automate turning a trained model into a hardware-optimised artifact, and has grown to about 130 people ([MLOps is NOT Real](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/04/mlops-is-not-real/)).
