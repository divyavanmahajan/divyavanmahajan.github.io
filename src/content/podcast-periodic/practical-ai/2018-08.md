## Overview

August on Practical AI leaned towards the organisational side of AI: how companies pick decisions to automate, how enterprise teams build models, and how to get people to use them. Two news episodes covered AI ethics and Henry Kissinger's warnings, and one technical interview explained Mask R-CNN for robots.

## Main themes

### Decisions, culture and adoption

- Mike Bugembe, former chief analytics officer of JustGiving, argues the main use case for data “whether you're playing with data science or AI, is for decision making”, and that getting value is a people problem ([Behavioral economics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/behavioral-economics-and-ai-driven-decision-making/)).
- He looks for decisions that are repeatable, complex and measurable, such as who should receive a campaign email after the Haiti earthquake. Strategy comes first, reduced to one sentence with numeric goals ([Behavioral economics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/behavioral-economics-and-ai-driven-decision-making/)).
- JustGiving taught staff behavioural economics to show human bias, used BJ Fogg's behaviour model for adoption, and trained the whole data team to communicate. His main regret is not educating the whole organisation early ([Behavioral economics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/behavioral-economics-and-ai-driven-decision-making/)).

### Enterprise ML and open source tools

- Whitenack reads O'Reilly's enterprise adoption report as showing that embedded data science teams build most models and only about 3% use cloud ML services. Benson expects that to change as companies settle their AI strategy ([Enterprise ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/open-source-tools-ai-for-dota-and-enterprise-ml-adoption/)).
- The hosts suggest open source tools such as AutoKeras and Netron may explain part of the gap with paid platforms like Google's AutoML ([Enterprise ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/open-source-tools-ai-for-dota-and-enterprise-ml-adoption/)).
- They also cover OpenAI Five beating strong Dota players, Julia 1.0 and Ian Hogarth's argument that AI policy may become a central area of government policy ([Enterprise ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/open-source-tools-ai-for-dota-and-enterprise-ml-adoption/)).

### Ethics, hype and long-term risk

- Benson summarises Henry Kissinger's Atlantic essay, which ends with a call to be careful about “how we implement that AI”. He says current deep learning does not feel threatening, while leaving a door open for distant developments ([Kissinger on AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/eye-tracking-henry-kissinger-on-ai-vim/)).
- Benson finds a study linking eye movements to personality slightly creepy. Whitenack highlights a sign-language-to-Alexa accessibility project and urges listeners to choose AI-for-good work ([Kissinger on AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/eye-tracking-henry-kissinger-on-ai-vim/)).
- Whitenack, drawing on his faith, says builders shape the morality of AI, and Benson discusses a Harvard Business Review piece on AI and corporate purpose ([Enterprise ML](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/open-source-tools-ai-for-dota-and-enterprise-ml-adoption/)).

### Robot perception with Mask R-CNN

- Chris DeBellis of Honeywell explains that convolutional layers build up from simple lines and curves to whole objects, and that Mask R-CNN adds pixel-level masks on top of detection ([Mask R-CNN](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/robot-perception-and-mask-r-cnn/)).
- Masks reveal orientation, such as a toothpaste tube rotated 45 degrees, which a robot arm needs. For a self-driving car, a YOLO bounding box is usually enough ([Mask R-CNN](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/robot-perception-and-mask-r-cnn/)).
- Annotation is the bottleneck: his team spent 15 to 20 minutes per image. Compute is the other cost, with a robot GPU managing perhaps two to ten images per second ([Mask R-CNN](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/robot-perception-and-mask-r-cnn/)).
