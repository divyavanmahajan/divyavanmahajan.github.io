## Overview

April on Practical AI centred on coding agents, with two episodes asking how they fit real production work and real teams. Another episode went down the stack to Groq's inference hardware, and the month closed with the practical limits of AI in hospital imaging.

## Main themes

### Coding agents on production code

- Robert Brennan of All Hands AI says agents first handled tasks of about 10 steps and now run hundreds. He places their open source OpenHands agent at the production, agentic end of the market ([Open source AI for your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/)).
- Graham Neubig says a single agent beat an early multi-agent design, and that OpenHands made 209 commits to its own repository in three months ([Open source AI for your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/)).
- Brennan warns that code bases rot without review as agents duplicate or bloat functions. He likens adopting agents to a new manager learning to delegate while still checking the work ([Open source AI for your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/)).
- Both episodes referred to SWE-bench. Neubig says their harness covers 20 benchmarks beyond it ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/), [Open source AI for your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/)).

### The human side of working with agents

- Pavel Veller of EPAM says the reviewer is now the bottleneck, because he must reverse-engineer how the agent reached its output ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/)).
- He wants agent code to match team conventions: “correct by itself is not good enough. I want it to be aesthetically the same” ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/)).
- Veller scrapped a filmed productivity demo after overprompting, and worries about rising entry barriers for junior engineers ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/)).

### MCP and enterprise orchestration

- EPAM's open source DIAL started as a model-agnostic proxy that logs every prompt, became a ChatGPT-like front door, and is adding MCP at 20-something clients ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/)).
- Veller compares MCP to what HTTP did for people, groups tools into a tree rather than offering a thousand, and finds it pays to delegate more planning to the model ([Orchestrating agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/)).
- All Hands keeps enterprise features such as multi-tenancy and compliance closed, and supports self-hosting on AWS with Bedrock ([Open source AI for your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/)).

### Fast inference through hardware and compilers

- DJ Singh says Groq built its compiler before its chip and removed components such as network switches that add non-determinism, so every operation is scheduled in advance ([Groq](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/software-and-hardware-acceleration-with-groq/)).
- Groq uses no hand-written kernels; Singh says “all these enhancements just reflect onto all of the models that we end up supporting” ([Groq](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/software-and-hardware-acceleration-with-groq/)).
- He argues speed improves quality, since reasoning models can produce more tokens in the same wait, and says switching from OpenAI's API is a one- or two-line change ([Groq](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/software-and-hardware-acceleration-with-groq/)).

### AI in clinical imaging

- Gavin Winston sees value in triaging growing scan volumes and spotting patterns people miss, but says the gap to clinical use remains wide ([Neuroimaging](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/seeing-beyond-the-scan-in-neuroimaging/)).
- He names scarce labelled data, explainability, privacy and unclear liability as the barriers, and expects AI to augment physicians rather than replace them ([Neuroimaging](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/seeing-beyond-the-scan-in-neuroimaging/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Open source AI to tackle your backlog](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/open-source-ai-to-tackle-your-backlog/): an agent that became the top committer to its own repository, and frank advice on review.
- [Orchestrating agents, APIs, and MCP servers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/orchestrating-agents-apis-and-mcp-servers/): an honest account of where agents still slow developers down.
- [Software and hardware acceleration with Groq](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/software-and-hardware-acceleration-with-groq/): a clear explanation of compiler-first, deterministic inference hardware.
- [Seeing beyond the scan in neuroimaging](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/04/seeing-beyond-the-scan-in-neuroimaging/): a researcher's view of why clinical adoption lags the technology.
