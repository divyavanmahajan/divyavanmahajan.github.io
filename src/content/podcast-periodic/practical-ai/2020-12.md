## Overview

December on Practical AI kept returning to data: who opens it, who labels it and how to use it fairly. Unsplash, two public-sector data practitioners and Snorkel AI each took a different angle, while Microsoft's Bharat Sandhu explained how research becomes product in Azure AI.

## Main themes

### Opening up data

- Luke Chesser and Timothy Carbone explain why Unsplash released metadata for roughly 1.3 million human-reviewed photos, shipped as CSV files with download links rather than images ([Open library dataset](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/the-world-s-largest-open-library-dataset/)).
- Chesser hopes companies will treat data like open source software and says of Unsplash, “we're happy to be the guinea pigs” ([Open library dataset](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/the-world-s-largest-open-library-dataset/)).
- Emily Martinez points to New York City's open data platform, which carries datasets from several agencies including the health department, while Danya Murali stresses government's ability to collect and mandate survey data ([AI for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/engaging-with-governments-on-ai-for-good/)).

### From research to product at Microsoft

- Bharat Sandhu says AI projects should start from a business goal with stakeholders committed up front, and warns that today's deep neural networks will be “quite passe in a few years time” ([Azure AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/from-research-to-product-at-azure-ai/)).
- He describes Azure AI as Azure Machine Learning for data scientists plus pre-built Cognitive Services, used inside Teams transcription and Xbox's Personalizer-driven dashboards ([Azure AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/from-research-to-product-at-azure-ai/)).
- Microsoft's eight research centres feed Azure AI through a link Sandhu calls loosely coupled. An image captioning model pre-trained on a visual vocabulary reached human parity on a benchmark and now helps Seeing AI users ([Azure AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/from-research-to-product-at-azure-ai/)).
- He also frames mixed reality, spanning virtual and augmented reality, as a good example of applied AI, citing HoloLens hand tracking ([Azure AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/from-research-to-product-at-azure-ai/)).

### Equitable data work in the public sector

- Murali defines AI for good as equitable use across all communities. At Arcadia Power she combines Energy Information Administration data with customer usage to estimate each customer's CO2 footprint ([AI for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/engaging-with-governments-on-ai-for-good/)).
- She argues for race-conscious rather than race-blind analysis, noting that zip codes “can very, very easily be a proxy to race and socioeconomic status” ([AI for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/engaging-with-governments-on-ai-for-good/)).
- Martinez describes a new system for pop-up COVID vaccination clinics and the health department's move from SAS to an R server ([AI for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/engaging-with-governments-on-ai-for-good/)).

### Programmatic labeling

- Braden Hancock argues training data, not model architecture, is the bottleneck. Experts write noisy labeling functions whose votes are aggregated, giving 100,000 good-enough labels in the time of 100 perfect ones ([Snorkel AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/getting-in-the-flow-with-snorkel-ai/)).
- He treats training data as a depreciating asset with a half-life, and cites a COVID-related task where three engineers passed 99% accuracy in a day ([Snorkel AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/getting-in-the-flow-with-snorkel-ai/)).

## Notable episodes
