## Overview

Practical AI launched in July 2018 with hosts Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson promising to make AI usable for ordinary developers. The first half-year covered the tools of the day, with transfer learning, PyTorch and TensorFlow at the centre. Ethics and regulation ran alongside: GDPR, government facial recognition and bias in hiring. By December the focus had moved from training models to running them, in the cloud, on new chips and at the edge.

## Main themes

### Transfer learning and the framework race

- Chris argues “transfer learning is really almost the default way” data scientists and AI engineers reach their goals, and Daniel says most companies apply existing architectures to their own data ([PyTorch 1.0 vs TensorFlow 2.0](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/pytorch-1-0-vs-tensorflow-2-0/)).
- The same episode sees the frameworks converge: PyTorch 1.0 pushes into production, and TensorFlow 2.0 makes eager execution its primary mode.
- Google's pre-trained BERT makes strong NLP cheap to apply through fine-tuning ([BERT](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/11/bert-one-nlp-model-to-rule-them-all/)).
- PlantVillage retrained a TensorFlow model to run offline on phones so Tanzanian cassava farmers can spot crop disease ([Helping African farmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/helping-african-farmers-with-tensorflow/)).

### Regulation, ethics and bias

- Immuta's Andrew Burt and Matthew Carroll describe GDPR as a real compliance and explainability burden, while France and the UK publish strategies to promote AI ([Data management, regulation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/data-management-regulation-the-future-of-ai/)).
- The hosts argue government facial recognition needs public ethics frameworks such as Google's AI principles ([Government use of facial recognition](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/government-use-of-facial-recognition-and-ai-at-google/)).
- Lindsey Zuloaga warns that “algorithms are really good at stereotyping”, but says monitored models can reduce bias that hiring already has ([Fighting bias in AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/fighting-bias-in-ai-and-in-hiring/)).
- Brett Gaylor uses art and film to show AI systems as human design choices that can be changed ([AI's impact through art and film](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/11/analyzing-ai-s-impact-on-society-through-art-and-film/)).

### GPUs, chips and the edge

- NVIDIA chief scientist Bill Dally argues GPUs ignited deep learning and that tensor cores keep flexibility a hardwired ASIC lacks ([AI at NVIDIA](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/artificial-intelligence-at-nvidia/)).
- A news episode notes a wave of chip startups and an article betting on ASICs over commodity hardware ([Hardware acceleration](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/09/ai-in-healthcare-synthesizing-dance-moves-hardware-acceleration/)).
- Intel's Cormac Brick says edge models need pruning and quantization to stay portable across silicon ([Uber and Intel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/11/uber-and-intel-s-machine-learning-platforms/)).

### From model to production

- An Intel panel says the hard part of AI is now production and problem definition, not accuracy ([Quora questions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/09/answering-recent-ai-questions-from-quora/)).
- Uber's Mike Del Balso explains why production ML at scale needs a shared platform with a feature store ([Uber and Intel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/11/uber-and-intel-s-machine-learning-platforms/)); Pachyderm's Joe Doliner offers versioned data on Kubernetes for small teams ([Pachyderm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/pachyderm-s-kubernetes-based-infrastructure-for-ai/)).
- The hosts say a model only creates value once it is served, with inference split between cloud and edge ([So you have an AI model](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).

### Enterprise adoption and safety

- Daniel finds embedded data science teams build most enterprise models, rather than cloud ML services ([Enterprise ML adoption](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/08/open-source-tools-ai-for-dota-and-enterprise-ml-adoption/)).
- Susan Etlinger says success means moving from rules to probabilities and from models to products ([AI in the enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).
- Wojciech Zaremba of OpenAI sees reinforcement learning moving to robots through randomised simulation, but warns single-objective systems carry misspecification risks ([OpenAI, reinforcement learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/10/openai-reinforcement-learning-robots-safety/)).
