## Overview

August on Practical AI had two interviews at opposite ends of the field. Pieter Cailliau of Redis Labs explained how RedisAI serves models from inside the database, and David Yakobovitch of Galvanize talked about data science training, automation and human-centred design.

## Main themes

### Model serving inside the database

- Cailliau describes Redis as an in-memory key-value database and says a common misconception “is that Redis is purely a cache. It's effectively a database.” ([RedisAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/serving-deep-learning-models-with-redisai/)).
- RedisAI merges earlier efforts (Neural Redis, Redis ML and Redis DL) and adds three data types: tensor, script and model. It supports PyTorch, TensorFlow and ONNX Runtime behind one client API ([RedisAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/serving-deep-learning-models-with-redisai/)).
- His pitch is to “Run your inference where your data lives”, rather than wrapping a model in a Flask app and scaling it yourself. He sees transaction classification and fraud detection as good fits ([RedisAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/serving-deep-learning-models-with-redisai/)).
- The roadmap includes RedisGears integration, batching, performance statistics and model versioning for A/B tests, developed with Tensorwerk ([RedisAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/serving-deep-learning-models-with-redisai/)).

### Training data scientists and the jobs they get

- Yakobovitch says many bootcamps and university programmes rush people into industry without enough preparation. Bootcamp students with the most success are already software engineers or have PhDs ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).
- He separates ML engineers and data engineers from data scientists, predicts a data-science-as-a-service segment, and advises applicants to meet about 75% of listed requirements ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).
- He argues automation is already removing jobs, citing media and copywriting cuts in New York City, self-checkout and automated customer service. In his view efficiency pressure will reach every industry ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).

### Human-centred AI

- Yakobovitch wants diverse views and attention to bias early in AI projects, and warns that pre-built cloud AI services bring their bias into your product ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).
- Whitenack notes Google Translate covers about 100 of more than 7,000 spoken languages. Yakobovitch adds that over 20% of New York City residents do not speak English ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).
- His practical steps: write down who your customers are, pair engineers with business strategists, and read Amy Webb's trend report and Matt Turck's data and AI landscape ([humAIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/08/on-being-humain/)).
