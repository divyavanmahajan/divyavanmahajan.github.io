## Overview

January on Practical AI moved from a large enterprise to an industry consortium to a niche sensor company. American Express described machine learning over a trillion dollars of transactions, MLCommons made the case for shared standards, and Aryballe showed how smell can become data.

## Main themes

### Machine learning at enterprise scale

- Madhurima Khandelwal, head of American Express AI Labs, says Amex has more than $1.2 trillion in annual transactions and wants a fraud decision within milliseconds. Online fraud is harder because it rarely happens at the cardholder's physical address ([Amex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-1-trillion-dollar-ml-model/)).
- She notes that “fraud prevention is really one of the first areas where we deployed machine learning models”, back in 2010 ([Amex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-1-trillion-dollar-ml-model/)).
- AI Labs does research and builds horizontal tools, such as a duplicate-invoice detector and a natural-language interface to complex reports, while business units keep their own ML teams ([Amex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-1-trillion-dollar-ml-model/)).
- Khandelwal says extra model complexity has to be earned, and describes fair lending reviews, a separate oversight team and tools for explaining model scores ([Amex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-1-trillion-dollar-ml-model/)).

### Benchmarks, datasets and portability

- David Kanter says MLCommons, formally launched in December 2020, rests on three pillars: benchmarks and metrics, datasets and models, and best practices ([MLCommons](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/accelerating-ml-innovation-at-mlcommons/)).
- He calls a benchmark “a barometer on progress”. MLPerf measures full-system training and inference speed and drew 70 or 80 companies ([MLCommons](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/accelerating-ml-innovation-at-mlcommons/)).
- Kanter describes People's Speech as about 87,000 hours of audio, far larger than any public speech set, and names model portability as the top friction to remove. MLCube offers conventions, distinct from Docker containers, for running models anywhere ([MLCommons](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/accelerating-ml-innovation-at-mlcommons/)).

### Capturing smell as data

- Terri Jordan defines digital olfaction as the digital capture and production of aromas, and notes that odor has nothing like RGB standards for colour ([The nose knows](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-nose-knows/)).
- Aryballe grafts short-chain peptides onto a silicon photonics chip. Jordan calls the company “hardware-enabled software dataware” and lists uses in raw material checks, fragrance batches and car interiors ([The nose knows](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-nose-knows/)).
- Yanis Caritu says each sample is fewer than 100 numbers, normalised for concentration. His team uses scikit-learn, and logistic regression sometimes beats neural networks; the hard part is reproducible capture, not the AI ([The nose knows](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/01/the-nose-knows/)).
