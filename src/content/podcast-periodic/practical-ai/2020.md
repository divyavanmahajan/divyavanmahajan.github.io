## Overview

Practical AI spent 2020 treating AI as a normal layer of the software stack and asking how to run it responsibly. Ethics moved from principles to governance and incident response, and MLOps tooling recurred. Reinforcement learning, transformers and GPT-3 covered research. The pandemic brought CORD-19 and a run of open data episodes.

## Main themes

### From ethics principles to regulation

- Greg Allen explains how the Joint AI Center adopts commercial AI under the DoD's law-of-war and AI ethics principles ([How the U.S. military thinks about AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/how-the-u-s-military-thinks-about-ai/)).
- The hosts argue published principles are only the starting line until they map to policy, governance and developer tooling ([Practical AI Ethics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/practical-ai-ethics/)).
- Andrew Burt says existing discrimination, privacy and liability laws already apply to AI, and Patrick Hall adds “I do think sort of regulation is imminent” ([When AI goes wrong](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/09/when-ai-goes-wrong/)).

### MLOps and production discipline

- Nir Bar-Lev notes “MLOps itself as a term is not something that is set already” and wants experiment tracking integrated ([Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/)).
- Lukas Biewald calls ML tooling broken at almost every step ([Killer developer tools](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/11/killer-developer-tools-for-machine-learning/)).
- Suju Rajan stresses monitoring, feature lineage and technical debt at LinkedIn ([Productionizing AI at LinkedIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/productionizing-ai-at-linkedin/)).
- Braden Hancock argues training data is the bottleneck and labeling should be programmatic ([Snorkel AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/getting-in-the-flow-with-snorkel-ai/)).

### Reinforcement learning in practice

- Costas Boulis pairs reinforcement learning with digital twins so robots can explore in simulation ([AI-driven automation in manufacturing](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/ai-driven-automation-in-manufacturing/)).
- Google Brain's Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini treat chip floor planning as a game, using edge-focused graph embeddings ([RL for chip design](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/reinforcement-learning-for-chip-design/)).
- Hamish Ogilvy prefers lightweight Bayesian bandits built into search indexes over heavy per-customer models ([RL for search](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/reinforcement-learning-for-search/)).

### Transformers, GPT-3 and the road to AGI

- Daniel Whitenack credits Hugging Face with much of 2019's transformer momentum ([2019's AI top 5](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/01/2019-s-ai-top-5/)); Sasha Rush describes its shared open source ecosystem ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/all-things-transformers-with-hugging-face/)).
- At episode 100 Whitenack calls GPT-3 an achievement but not a paradigm shift, noting OpenAI is “releasing this via API and they're running it internally” ([Practical AI turns 100](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/08/practical-ai-turns-100/)).
- The hosts see System 2 deep learning and DARPA's third wave as next steps ([The long road to AGI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/06/the-long-road-to-agi/)).

### Open data and AI for good

- CORD-19 turned coronavirus papers into machine-readable text, released on Kaggle by AI2 and partners ([COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/), [Exploring CORD-19](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/exploring-the-covid-19-open-research-dataset/)).
- Daniel argues open data is scattered compared with open source code ([Open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/)); Unsplash opened its photo dataset to researchers ([Open library dataset](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/12/the-world-s-largest-open-library-dataset/)).
- DataRobot predicted failing water points in Sierra Leone ([Clean water access](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/05/ai-for-good-clean-water-access-in-africa/)).
