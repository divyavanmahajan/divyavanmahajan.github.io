## Overview

February on Practical AI was mostly recorded at Applied Machine Learning Days in Lausanne, where Chris Benson spoke with Microsoft, Intel and the founders of GirlsCoding.org about AI for good and access to computing. The month closed with the hosts discussing OpenAI's GPT-2 and its decision to withhold the full model.

## Main themes

### GPT-2 and responsible release

- Benson cites OpenAI's description of GPT-2: a transformer with 1.5 billion parameters trained on 8 million web pages to predict the next word ([GPT-2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/openai-s-new-dangerous-gpt-2-language-model/)).
- Daniel Whitenack says “the task isn't really new. The transformer idea isn't really new.” The gains come from scale, which few teams can match ([GPT-2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/openai-s-new-dangerous-gpt-2-language-model/)).
- OpenAI listed misuse risks such as misleading news, impersonation and spam. Benson sees the danger in people misusing narrow tools, not conscious AI, and warns generated text could become a weapon in conflicts ([GPT-2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/openai-s-new-dangerous-gpt-2-language-model/)).
- Benson says there is no guidebook for responsible disclosure but the staged release buys time. Whitenack notes critics' objections and expects big labs to stop releasing everything openly ([GPT-2](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/openai-s-new-dangerous-gpt-2-language-model/)).

### AI for the environment

- Jennifer Marsman says Microsoft committed $50 million over five years to AI for Earth grants in agriculture, water, climate change and biodiversity, plus Azure compute and data labelling grants ([Microsoft](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/how-microsoft-is-using-ai-to-help-the-earth/)).
- She argues the world often asks “the people with the least resources to solve the world's hardest problems”, so her team builds shared APIs such as land cover mapping ([Microsoft](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/how-microsoft-is-using-ai-to-help-the-earth/)).
- FarmBeats found connectivity drove sensor costs to about $1,000 each. It uses fewer sensors, drone imagery and links over unused TV channels ([Microsoft](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/how-microsoft-is-using-ai-to-help-the-earth/)).

### AI for social good inside a chipmaker

- Anna Bethke leads AI for Social Good at Intel and names three business benefits: marketing, hiring and retention, and insight into what data its hardware must support ([Intel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/ai-for-social-good-at-intel/)).
- TrailGuard AI runs detection on a Movidius chip inside anti-poaching cameras, and the Wheelie lets quadriplegic users steer a wheelchair with facial gestures ([Intel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/ai-for-social-good-at-intel/)).
- She suggests volunteering with groups like Delta Analytics or DataKind and networking heavily to find who has the problems and who has the solutions ([Intel](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/ai-for-social-good-at-intel/)).

### Getting girls into computing

- Marta Martinez-Cámara and Miranda Kreković of EPFL say girls avoid computing for lack of exposure and role models, not talent ([GirlsCoding.org](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/girlscoding-org-empowers-young-women-to-embrace-computer-science/)).
- Their workshops for ages 9-16 now spend more than half the time on talks about mentors' jobs, and use Scratch and Python in Google Colab. Volunteers have grown to more than 50 mentors ([GirlsCoding.org](/podcasts/practical-ai/2019/02/girlscoding-org-empowers-young-women-to-embrace-computer-science/)).
