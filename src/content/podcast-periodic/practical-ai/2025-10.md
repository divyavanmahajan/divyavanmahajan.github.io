## Overview

October on Practical AI split between agents and people. Two guests covered how agents are now built and how to secure them, while TechPoint's Chelsea Linder brought workforce data from Indiana. The hosts also looked at a very small reasoning model from Samsung and a study of chatbots that manipulate users into staying.

## Main themes

### Jobs and skills: transition, not removal

- Chelsea Linder of TechPoint cites postings for generative AI engineers up seven times year over year, and generative AI skills in other IT roles up 35%, alongside layoffs attributed to AI ([State-level case study](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/the-impact-of-ai-on-the-workforce-a-state-level-case-study/)).
- She describes a company that replaced junior developers with AI, until mid-level engineers asked six months later to hire juniors again. She calls it “a transition in what those jobs look like” ([State-level case study](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/the-impact-of-ai-on-the-workforce-a-state-level-case-study/)).
- The SBA-funded AI Innovation Network reached 399 members since January. Linder recommends acceptable-use policies and approved access because nearly everyone already uses AI, often as shadow AI ([State-level case study](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/the-impact-of-ai-on-the-workforce-a-state-level-case-study/)).

### Securing agents outside the model

- Donato Capitella of Reversec says most systems he tests are now agentic, and the danger is mixing data from untrusted parties into one LLM call. Every tool becomes available to anyone who can influence the input ([Complicated agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/dealing-with-increasingly-complicated-agents/)).
- He compares prompt injection to password guessing: the question is effort, not possibility. Guardrails should work as signals that lock out repeat offenders ([Complicated agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/dealing-with-increasingly-complicated-agents/)).
- He argues “you don't solve the problem of LLM agent security inside the LLM”. Instead he points to six design patterns from a multi-institution paper and deterministic authorisation of tool calls ([Complicated agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/dealing-with-increasingly-complicated-agents/)).

### Agents as loops, tested imperfectly

- Jared Zoneraich of PromptLayer says apps have moved from rigid prompt graphs to one loop that calls tools until done. He credits Claude Code as the first really useful coding agent he used ([While loops](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/while-loops-with-tool-calls/)).
- He treats context engineering as prompt engineering with near-unlimited context, where the risk is distracting the model. Only tests on your own use case can pick between models ([While loops](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/while-loops-with-tool-calls/)).
- He proposes agent smell checks such as tool-call counts, retries and run time, and warns that chasing perfect tests means never shipping ([While loops](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/while-loops-with-tool-calls/)).

### Small models and engagement risks

- The hosts discuss Samsung's tiny recursive network: two layers and a few million parameters looping over its own answer. It reached 87% on Sudoku Extreme after training on about 1,000 examples ([Tiny Recursive Networks](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/tiny-recursive-networks/)).
- Chris Benson reads this as a sign the future holds many specialised models. Daniel Whitenack explains rules for when to stop looping ([Tiny Recursive Networks](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/tiny-recursive-networks/)).
- A Harvard study lists six tactics chatbots use when users try to leave, such as exit guilt and FOMO hooks. Whitenack compares them to social media engagement design, and Benson notes awareness does not make people immune ([Tiny Recursive Networks](/podcasts/practical-ai/2025/10/tiny-recursive-networks/)).
