## Overview

March on Practical AI moved between the plumbing of machine learning and the arrival of very large models. Greeneye and ClearML showed MLOps and Kubernetes running on tractors, and Chris Benson laid out data fabrics as the logistics layer for data. The hosts then unpacked Stanford's foundation model report and a news week dominated by BigScience and Nvidia, with one guest episode on how companies organise for innovation.

## Main themes

### MLOps from the lab to the tractor

- Alon Klein Orback says Greeneye went from about 10 hand-converted models a year on tractors to thousands, with one click handling conversion, metric checks and delivery ([Tractors](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/deploying-models-to-tractors/)).
- Greeneye's sprayers spot weeds with cameras and use about 10% of the chemicals. Klein Orback stresses that “Everything is done in real time, no connectivity at all to the cloud” ([Tractors](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/deploying-models-to-tractors/)).
- Greeneye runs K3s on the tractor. Moses Guttmann of ClearML says a failed training job should be dropped rather than restarted, so ClearML uses Kubernetes mainly as a scheduler, and Greeneye's requests drove that integration ([Tractors](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/deploying-models-to-tractors/)).
- Klein Orback keeps a human decision in the loop rather than automating the full cycle for every model, and runs a team spanning MLOps, DevOps, IoT Ops and FinOps ([Tractors](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/deploying-models-to-tractors/)).

### Data fabrics as logistics

- Benson defines a data fabric narrowly: a large distributed system that moves data from producers to consumers with delivery guarantees, like a trucking network. Analytics and AI are consumers, not part of it ([Data fabric](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/clothing-ai-in-a-data-fabric/)).
- He says the fabric acts on metadata such as security, routing and priority, so a critical-care alert can beat records headed to an S3 bucket ([Data fabric](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/clothing-ai-in-a-data-fabric/)).
- The cost is not trivial, so a fabric only pays off at the scale of thousands of producers and consumers. Benson expects many consumers to be MLOps pipelines and Kubernetes to run even on small devices ([Data fabric](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/clothing-ai-in-a-data-fabric/)).

### Foundation models and homogenisation

- Daniel Whitenack frames Stanford's foundation model idea on two pillars, transfer learning and scale, and equates it with what NLP calls large language models ([Foundation models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/foundation-models/)).
- He warns that a bias in a base model flows into a huge number of downstream tasks “because no one's retraining that foundation model”. Benson compares it to a bug in a ubiquitous open-source package ([Foundation models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/foundation-models/)).
- Whitenack, who works at SIL International, says minority languages are largely missing from these models. He recommends behavioural testing and explains grounding as learning concepts across several modalities ([Foundation models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/foundation-models/)).

### Scale in the news: BigScience and Nvidia

- BigScience, a CERN-style collaboration of hundreds of researchers, is training a 176 billion parameter multilingual model on 416 A100 GPUs in 46 languages, with data from SIL International ([Big week](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/it-s-been-a-big-week-in-ai-news/)).
- Benson relays Nvidia's claim that the H100 trains large language models 30 times faster than the A100. Whitenack worries that hardware tuned for transformers penalises other architectures ([Big week](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/it-s-been-a-big-week-in-ai-news/)).
- The AI Index 2022 shows more capable but more biased language models, falling training costs and private investment that is soaring but concentrating ([Big week](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/it-s-been-a-big-week-in-ai-news/)).

### Organising for innovation

- SAP's Lukas Egger argues innovation fails on organisational setup and incentives, and wants desirability, viability and feasibility worked in parallel ([Innovation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/creating-a-culture-of-innovation/)).
- His team scores ideas on four phases against four risk categories, giving owners “an a la carte menu of de-risking activities based on a scorecard” ([Innovation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2022/03/creating-a-culture-of-innovation/)).
