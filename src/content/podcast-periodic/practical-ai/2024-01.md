## Overview

January on Practical AI was about building with LLMs now that the models were commodities. Retrieval-augmented generation came up in three of four episodes, as the first thing teams build and as the better way to bring in company data. The month opened with predictions for 2024 and closed with the Rabbit R1 and its large action model.

## Main themes

### Retrieval first

- Daniel Whitenack says RAG systems are “the first thing that everybody is building” among Prediction Guard's clients, before any fine-tuning ([Hackathon recap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/advent-of-genai-hackathon-recap/)).
- Raza Habib of Humanloop says RAG has proved better than fine-tuning for giving models private knowledge ([Collaboration & evaluation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/collaboration-evaluation-for-llm-apps/)).
- At the Advent of GenAI hackathon, Rahul Nair highlights RAG over YouTube audio and a code explainer that cited its sources. Ryan Metz argues that sourced answers give a practical form of explainable AI ([Hackathon recap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/advent-of-genai-hackathon-recap/)).
- Whitenack's consensus predictions for the year start with continued RAG improvements ([AI predictions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/ai-predictions-for-2024/)).

### Prompts, fine-tuning and evaluation

- Habib argues prompts let domain experts build LLM apps alongside engineers. One ed tech customer lets only its linguists edit prompts ([Collaboration & evaluation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/collaboration-evaluation-for-llm-apps/)).
- He advises pushing prompt engineering first and sees fine-tuning mainly as an optimisation for cost, latency or tone, comparing it to compilation ([Collaboration & evaluation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/collaboration-evaluation-for-llm-apps/)).
- Habib separates three stages of evaluation: interactive testing, regression tests against gold sets and production monitoring. A model upgrade should be treated like any other change ([Collaboration & evaluation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/collaboration-evaluation-for-llm-apps/)).

### Commoditised models and the year ahead

- The hackathon run by Intel Liftoff and Prediction Guard closed registrations at 2,000, twice the stretch goal. Nair compares the results to MVPs that startups spend months on ([Hackathon recap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/advent-of-genai-hackathon-recap/)).
- Open models such as Intel's Neural Chat came up in three episodes. Habib says the gap to GPT-4 is closing with Mistral and Llama 2, but self-hosting mostly suits privacy, edge or high-volume uses ([Hackathon recap](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/advent-of-genai-hackathon-recap/), [Collaboration & evaluation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/collaboration-evaluation-for-llm-apps/)).
- Whitenack predicts winners will combine traditional machine learning with generative AI, and expects AI to enhance work rather than replace it. Chris Benson says “I would be very surprised if they don't all come true this year.” ([AI predictions](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/ai-predictions-for-2024/)).

### Large action models

- Whitenack explains tool use today: the model emits JSON that ordinary code sends to an API. Many apps lack a usable API, which is the gap Rabbit targets ([LAMs & Rabbits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/large-action-models-lams-rabbits/)).
- He interprets Rabbit's LAM as neuro-symbolic, running programs synthesised from human demonstrations ([LAMs & Rabbits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/large-action-models-lams-rabbits/)).
- Benson says “Maybe the phone is no longer the central device in your life going forward”. Whitenack expects smartphones to become more like the R1 instead ([LAMs & Rabbits](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/01/large-action-models-lams-rabbits/)).
