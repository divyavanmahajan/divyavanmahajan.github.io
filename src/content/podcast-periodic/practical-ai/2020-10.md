## Overview

October on Practical AI focused on the less glamorous side of data science: learning the basics, keeping production models healthy and avoiding misleading results. LinkedIn came up twice, once as the guest's employer and once as a source of search libraries. The month closed with a search startup using reinforcement learning instead of custom models.

## Main themes

### Teaching data science basics

- Daniel Chen argues that knowing the steps to tidy a messy dataset matters more than whether you learn R or Python first ([R and data science](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/r-data-science-computational-biology/)).
- Chen says many biomedical researchers work from spreadsheets because they were never taught programming. His DS4 Biomed material starts there, and he would be happy if a first workshop only taught better spreadsheet structure ([R and data science](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/r-data-science-computational-biology/)).
- He recommends a project layout where the same command runs on any machine, and keeping raw data intact so analyses can be rerun ([R and data science](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/r-data-science-computational-biology/)).
- Rajiv Shah tells newcomers that most useful enterprise data science is more than three years old and suggests end-to-end passion projects ([Data leakage](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/when-data-leakage-turns-into-a-flood-of-trouble/)).

### Production AI at LinkedIn

- Suju Rajan says LinkedIn uses AI on almost every product surface, with 700 million plus members and 14 to 20 million job postings on a given day ([LinkedIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/productionizing-ai-at-linkedin/)).
- Member and job embeddings, built on LinkedIn's economic graph, drive search, job alerts and recommended matches that help recruiters narrow large candidate pools ([LinkedIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/productionizing-ai-at-linkedin/)).
- Rajan says AI is still far from an engineering discipline. She describes how “a team deploys a feature and then there are 20 other teams” using it unknowingly, and wants model maintenance taught in schools ([LinkedIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/productionizing-ai-at-linkedin/)).

### Target leakage

- Shah defines target leakage as using information from the future when making a prediction. His example is a salary model that uses monthly salary as a feature ([Data leakage](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/when-data-leakage-turns-into-a-flood-of-trouble/)).
- He warns: “Often when you have target leakage, the performance of your model looks really good.” He guesses about 75% of models leak at some point ([Data leakage](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/when-data-leakage-turns-into-a-flood-of-trouble/)).
- Shah recommends group partitioning for clustered data, snapshots of changing records, and nested cross-validation as a default for beginners ([Data leakage](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/when-data-leakage-turns-into-a-flood-of-trouble/)).

### Reinforcement learning for search

- Hamish Ogilvy describes Sajari as fast ML-based search as a service, and cites Amazon and Google studies showing delays cost revenue and clicks ([RL for search](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/reinforcement-learning-for-search/)).
- He says custom models for customers paying by credit card are not economical, so Sajari uses Bayesian bandits. During COVID, purchases moved results for face masks towards N95 respirators ([RL for search](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/reinforcement-learning-for-search/)).
- Sajari puts reinforcement signals into a mutable index, an approach Ogilvy notes LinkedIn has also published libraries for ([RL for search](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/reinforcement-learning-for-search/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Productionizing AI at LinkedIn](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/productionizing-ai-at-linkedin/): a frank account of feature lineage, drift and technical debt at large scale.
- [When data leakage turns into a flood of trouble](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/when-data-leakage-turns-into-a-flood-of-trouble/): concrete cases of leakage, including a published paper, and how to catch it.
- [Reinforcement Learning for search](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/reinforcement-learning-for-search/): a practical alternative to custom models when serving hundreds of customers.
- [R, Data Science, & Computational Biology](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/10/r-data-science-computational-biology/): teaching advice that starts from where researchers already are.
