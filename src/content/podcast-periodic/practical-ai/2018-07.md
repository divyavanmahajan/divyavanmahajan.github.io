## Overview

Practical AI launched in July 2018 with hosts Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack and a promise to make AI usable by ordinary developers. The first month mixed basics (what AI and deep learning actually are) with applied stories on cassava disease and exoplanets. Regulation and ethics, from the GDPR to government facial recognition, ran alongside.

## Main themes

### What AI is, and who can use it

- Benson treats AI as a moving label, “almost a holding term for whatever is the current technology”, which in 2018 mostly meant deep learning. He puts the revival down to compute, data and algorithm expertise now being widely available ([Meet your hosts](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/meet-your-practical-ai-hosts/)).
- Jared Lander places AI on a spectrum from scripted automation through regression and boosted trees to deep learning, which he calls extreme nonlinear modelling. He claims much of what is sold as AI is an if-else statement or logistic regression ([AI landscape](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/understanding-the-landscape-of-ai-techniques/)).
- Lander also explains why nonlinear activation functions matter: without them a stacked network would collapse into one linear model ([AI landscape](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/understanding-the-landscape-of-ai-techniques/)).

### Packaging models for developers

- Mat Ryer and David Hernandez of MachineBox ship models as Docker containers with small HTTP APIs. Ryer says they care about people “without any kind of machine learning experience being able to use these powerful technologies” ([MachineBox](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/putting-ai-in-a-box-at-machinebox/)).
- Facebox supports one-shot teaching from a single image. Hernandez says the hardest production problem is people: customers file bugs that are really false positives ([MachineBox](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/putting-ai-in-a-box-at-machinebox/)).
- Lander points to Plumber and Algorithmia for turning models into REST APIs, which Whitenack sees as a new layer in the software stack for developers ([AI landscape](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/understanding-the-landscape-of-ai-techniques/)).

### Regulation, governance and ethics

- Andrew Burt and Matthew Carroll of Immuta call the GDPR the first major regulation with parts aimed at AI. Burt thinks the "right to explainability" is overstated, while Carroll cites fines of 4% of revenue or 20 million euros ([Data and regulation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/data-management-regulation-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Carroll recommends “early and often bringing governance in” and using only the data a model needs. He worries more about biased models than about Skynet ([Data and regulation](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/data-management-regulation-the-future-of-ai/)).
- The hosts discuss government facial recognition, the ACLU's call for a moratorium and Amazon's work with US law enforcement. Benson walks through Google's AI principles as a model for other companies ([Facial recognition](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/government-use-of-facial-recognition-and-ai-at-google/)).

### Deep learning in the field and in space

- Penn State's PlantVillage team put an offline TensorFlow object-detection model on Android phones to spot cassava disease in Tanzania. Data collection took about two years and yielded only 2,200-2,500 usable images ([African farmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/helping-african-farmers-with-tensorflow/)).
- The team moved from Inception v4 to MobileNet to stop phones overheating, prefers false negatives to false positives, and is mentored by Google engineers including Pete Warden ([African farmers](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/helping-african-farmers-with-tensorflow/)).
- Chris Shallue and Andrew Vanderburg trained a basic convolutional network on about 30,000 labelled Kepler signals, treating light curves as 1D images. It found two missed planets, including Kepler-90i ([Planets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/detecting-planets-with-deep-learning/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Detecting planets with deep learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/detecting-planets-with-deep-learning/): a simple model, cheap training and open code produce a real astronomical result.
- [Helping African farmers with TensorFlow](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/helping-african-farmers-with-tensorflow/): an honest account of how hard data collection is and how to design for phones in the field.
- [Data management, regulation, the future of AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/data-management-regulation-the-future-of-ai/): a practical guide to what the GDPR means for data science teams.
- [Putting AI in a box at MachineBox](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/07/putting-ai-in-a-box-at-machinebox/): an early take on models as developer-friendly containers.
