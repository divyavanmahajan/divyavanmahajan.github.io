## Overview

November on Practical AI so far is about how organisations decide what AI should do and how they keep control of it. A federated learning researcher, a bank's AI leader and three Midwest AI Summit speakers all treat agents as useful but in need of limits, private data handling and human accountability.

## Main themes

### Private data and agent trajectories

- Lorenzo Sani of Flower Labs explains that federated learning moves training to where data is collected, so raw data never leaves its device or silo ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).
- Sani says agent trajectories hold user prompts, tool calls, MCP servers and RAG results, and gather more personal context as an agent runs. He calls federated learning “a natural way to train in a distribution these AI systems.” ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).
- He expects enterprises to analyse their own trajectories to find failure modes instead of relying on providers that often do not disclose full traces. For privacy-heavy work he would start from an open-weight model ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/)).

### Value first, people at the centre

- Gautham Pallapa of Scotiabank starts from what value means for the organisation, then quantifies success, and only then asks how AI could help. His book Invaluable sets this out as an eight-part value transform ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- He says technology only accelerates and people create the value. He describes current models as nondeterministic probabilistic models that lack human context and intent ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- Pallapa sees coding, documentation and tests as repeatable work for agents, but tells his teams “we're not here to replace people.” ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).

### Enterprise adoption in regulated settings

- Pallapa calls banking risk-averse for good reason. He would not yet let an agent run transactions on personal accounts and starts with customer service, chat assistance, compliance and adviser support ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
- Mark Lay, CIO at Noblis, weighs each AI task by reward against the risk of error, and warns of a new kind of technical debt from department-built apps ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Pallapa suggests mirroring a balanced human team with BA, design and testing agents under an orchestrator, and warns that swarms will not solve everything ([Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).

### Boundaries before building

- Don Barger of the International Mission Board says eight people wrote an AI charter nearly four years ago, before any code. It rules out AI posing as human or making final decisions about people ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Barger says the limits leave him able to do more, not less ([Midwest AI Summit](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/abundant-intelligence-at-the-midwest-ai-summit-part-2/)).
- Both Sani and Pallapa touched on vibe coding: Sani's flat vibe-coded an agent-judged costume contest, while Pallapa bans vibe coding on premises ([Federated Learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/implementing-federated-learning-to-enhance-agentic-ai/), [Human-Centered AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/11/building-human-centered-ai/)).
