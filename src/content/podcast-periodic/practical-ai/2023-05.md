## Overview

May on Practical AI was about making LLMs usable in real products. Guests covered output you can parse and test, private data in the prompt, cheaper inference and better training data. Agents, LangChain and evaluation came up across several episodes for the first time.

## Main themes

### From demo to production

- Travis Fischer frames LLM engineering around quality, cost, latency and reliability. He warns that “every time you take a step down that ladder of complexity, it adds engineering complexity”, so start with hosted models and prompting ([Last mile](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/the-last-mile-of-ai-app-development/)).
- Fischer advises building an evaluation set for the job and tracking it over time. LlamaIndex's Jerry Liu describes eval modules that use another LLM call instead of ground-truth labels ([Last mile](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/the-last-mile-of-ai-app-development/), [LlamaIndex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/data-augmentation-with-llamaindex/)).
- Fischer describes LLMs as a new CPU and agents as programs, and Liu names agent flexibility versus constraints as a focus ([Last mile](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/the-last-mile-of-ai-app-development/), [LlamaIndex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/data-augmentation-with-llamaindex/)).

### Structured output and compliant hosting

- Fischer says narrow tasks with typed outputs can be unit tested, and he is building a TypeScript framework with self-healing JSON ([Last mile](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/the-last-mile-of-ai-app-development/)).
- In an episode where Chris Benson interviews him, co-host Daniel Whitenack wants a sentiment model limited to fixed tags. He says prompt engineering helps, “but ultimately it doesn't solve the problem” ([Prediction Guard](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/controlled-and-compliant-ai-applications/)).
- His company Prediction Guard wraps open models with typed output, factuality and toxicity checks, and compliant hosting. He argues this brings open models closer to commercial output while keeping data private ([Prediction Guard](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/controlled-and-compliant-ai-applications/)).

### Private data in the prompt

- Liu argues you do not need to retrain a model to use it on private data. LlamaIndex orchestrates ingestion, indexing and querying on top of existing storage ([LlamaIndex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/data-augmentation-with-llamaindex/)).
- Context limits drive the design: an Uber 10-K is far longer than early context windows. For comparing Uber and Lyft filings, LlamaIndex splits the question into sub-questions before synthesising ([LlamaIndex](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/data-augmentation-with-llamaindex/)).

### Cheaper inference on CPUs

- Mark Kurtz of Neural Magic says up to 95-97% of weights can be zeroed through pruning, with quantization and distillation as further tools ([Large models on CPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/large-models-on-cpus/)).
- He says Neural Magic's MLPerf results show commodity CPUs beating T4 and A40 GPUs. For enterprises at scale, 80-90% of costs are in deployment ([Large models on CPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/large-models-on-cpus/)).
- Kurtz adds that a 175-billion-parameter model run locally on CPUs would remove data privacy concerns ([Large models on CPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/large-models-on-cpus/)).

### Human feedback and labelling

- Erin Mikail Staples of Label Studio walks through RLHF and says “The details that you give your annotation team can really directly influence the model.” ([Instruction tuning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/creating-instruction-tuned-models/)).
- She argues the problem's context should decide model size, and wants data and its processing disclosed. Whitenack urges data scientists to take part in labelling themselves ([Instruction tuning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/05/creating-instruction-tuned-models/)).
