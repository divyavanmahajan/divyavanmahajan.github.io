## Overview

April on Practical AI was shaped by the pandemic: two of four episodes covered the CORD-19 paper dataset and the NLP tools built on it. The other two went in different directions, with Stuart Russell on the AI control problem and Google Brain researchers on reinforcement learning for chip design.

## Main themes

### CORD-19 and mining the pandemic literature

- Lucy Lu Wang of the Allen Institute for AI explains that CORD-19 was possible because Semantic Scholar already had a pipeline for harmonising metadata and parsing PDFs into structured text ([Exploring CORD-19](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/exploring-the-covid-19-open-research-dataset/)).
- Wang puts the dataset at more than 50,000 papers, about 40,000 with full text. On Kaggle it saw millions of page views and more than 70,000 downloads ([Exploring CORD-19](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/exploring-the-covid-19-open-research-dataset/)).
- Tony Reina of Intel described an earlier release of roughly 25,000 PubMed articles and warned that “the data is always going to be dirty” ([COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/)).

### Open, volunteer-built question answering

- Timo Möeller of deepset says COVID-QA began at a German government hackathon, with about 25 developers pulling together answers spread across official pages ([COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/)).
- For the public it matches questions to official FAQs, starting from an Elasticsearch baseline. For researchers, deepset's Haystack retrieves candidate papers before a BERT reader extracts answers ([COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/)).
- Möeller says extractive QA is hard to scale to many users, and that he had never heard of an open source labelling process before this one ([COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/)).
- Both episodes stress expert reviewers. Wang notes that simpler methods such as Lucene and Elasticsearch often beat transformers on the Kaggle questions ([Exploring CORD-19](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/exploring-the-covid-19-open-research-dataset/), [COVID-19 Q&A](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/)).

### Human-compatible AI

- Stuart Russell argues the standard model of giving a machine a fixed objective becomes dangerous as systems grow more capable: “there's no examples of a dumb species controlling a more intelligent species forever” ([Human-compatible AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/achieving-provably-beneficial-human-compatible-ai/)).
- His alternative keeps preferences with the human. The machine knows it is uncertain about them, asks permission and treats interference as information ([Human-compatible AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/achieving-provably-beneficial-human-compatible-ai/)).
- Russell says deep learning is not AGI, urges practitioners to think about their loss functions, and wants public-facing algorithms regulated in a way analogous to pharmaceuticals ([Human-compatible AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/achieving-provably-beneficial-human-compatible-ai/)).

### Chip placement as a game

- Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini of Google Brain treat placing a netlist on a 2D grid as a reinforcement learning game, with a reward mixing wire length, congestion and timing ([Chip design](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/reinforcement-learning-for-chip-design/)).
- Goldie says their graph embeddings focus on edges, because connections between components matter most. A policy pre-trained on 20 netlists and fine-tuned for 12 hours beat one trained from scratch for 24 hours or more ([Chip design](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/reinforcement-learning-for-chip-design/)).
- Goldie notes chip design takes nearly two years, so faster design could make new ML architectures viable ([Chip design](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/reinforcement-learning-for-chip-design/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Achieving provably beneficial, human-compatible AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/achieving-provably-beneficial-human-compatible-ai/): a leading AI researcher sets out the control problem and his proposed fix.
- [Reinforcement learning for chip design](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/reinforcement-learning-for-chip-design/): a clear account of how Google applied RL to a decades-old optimisation problem.
- [Exploring the COVID-19 Open Research Dataset](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/exploring-the-covid-19-open-research-dataset/): how a large research dataset was assembled in days, and why expert judgement is the bottleneck.
- [COVID-19 Q&A and CORD-19](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/04/covid-19-q-a-and-cord-19/): a practical view of building a QA system quickly with volunteers.
