## Overview

February on Practical AI moved between small open models and the big labs. Nous Research's Karan Malhotra explained how synthetic data makes small models competitive, and the hosts compared Google's Gemini with GPT-4. Retired Lt. General Jack Shanahan described what it took to field AI in the US Department of Defense, and the month ended with a look at steering models through their hidden states.

## Main themes

### Synthetic data and small open models

- Malhotra says the first Hermes came from GPT-4-only instruction data in the Alpaca style, trained on Llama, with roughly 50,000 to 100,000 examples ([Data synthesis](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/data-synthesis-for-sota-llms/)).
- He compares distillation to an expert passing on “compressed tidbits that'll help them just get up and running”. Members with laptops or a single gaming GPU aimed to make 7-billion-parameter models useful on edge devices ([Data synthesis](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/data-synthesis-for-sota-llms/)).
- Malhotra says Nous does not profit from models built on GPT-4 outputs, and calls big companies hypocritical for training on copyrighted data while forbidding training on their outputs ([Data synthesis](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/data-synthesis-for-sota-llms/)).
- Nous grew from about 40 people to some 5,000 on Discord and became a company after a $5.2 million seed round, still focused on local, offline use ([Data synthesis](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/data-synthesis-for-sota-llms/)).

### Google against OpenAI

- Chris Benson explains the rebrand from Bard: Gemini Pro roughly matches free GPT-3.5, while Gemini Advanced runs Ultra against GPT-4. Most reviews he has seen still favour GPT-4 ([Gemini vs OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/gemini-vs-openai/)).
- The hosts argue the ecosystem around GPT-4 keeps it ahead more than the model does. Benson calls Gemini “very, very rough around the edges” and notes Google had only about 100 private beta testers ([Gemini vs OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/gemini-vs-openai/)).
- Benson welcomes the FCC ruling against AI voices in robocalls after a Biden voice clone, while noting most synthetic voice uses are legitimate ([Gemini vs OpenAI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/gemini-vs-openai/)).
- Google's open Gemma topped Hugging Face trends but carries custom terms of use. Benson argues small open models are where practical work happens ([Representation engineering](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/representation-engineering-activation-hacking/)).

### AI in national security

- Shanahan says Project Maven began because analysts could not keep up with drone video, and it put models into combat operations within a year. The JAIC then started with low-risk uses such as predictive maintenance ([National security](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/leading-the-charge-on-ai-in-national-security/)).
- He lists eight non-technical enablers, from mandate to talent management, and says “culture eats strategy for breakfast”. Data most often stopped DoD AI projects ([National security](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/leading-the-charge-on-ai-in-national-security/)).
- He describes most decisions as shared between humans and machines, cites the 737 MAX as integration gone wrong, and points to Ukraine's software-defined adaptation ([National security](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/leading-the-charge-on-ai-in-national-security/)).

### Steering models without retraining

- Daniel Whitenack presents control vectors as a middle ground between unreliable prompting and costly fine-tuning. They are built from contrasting prompt pairs, hidden-state differences and PCA per layer ([Representation engineering](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/representation-engineering-activation-hacking/)).
- With a happy vector on or reversed, Mistral answers the same prompt joyfully or gloomily. Whitenack notes vectors could both jailbreak models and help prevent jailbreaks ([Representation engineering](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/02/representation-engineering-activation-hacking/)).
