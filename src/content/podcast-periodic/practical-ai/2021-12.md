## Overview

December on Practical AI was about the practices and tools that surround models. Salesforce's Paula Goldman described AI ethics becoming a normal professional discipline, Flower's Daniel Beutel made the case for federated learning, and the hosts argued that AI tooling was maturing as OpenAI opened its API and Hugging Face expanded.

## Main themes

### Responsible AI as a discipline

- Paula Goldman argues AI ethics is going the way security went in the 1990s, from an immature field to a standard discipline that combines technology, human judgement and process ([Force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).
- She says that to address it at scale “you actually need to automate the solution as well”. Salesforce tools warn users, for example, that a zip code field can correlate with race ([Force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).
- Salesforce's consequence scanning asks scrum teams about intended and unintended consequences and adds the results to the backlog. Goldman sees Europe's draft AI law and civil society as part of a largely healthy ecosystem ([Force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).

### Bias in data and decisions

- Goldman says of AI that “it's as good as the data that it gets fed”, and cites research by Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru on facial recognition being less accurate on darker-skinned people ([Force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).
- She notes that human loan and hiring decisions are also biased, so the answer is AI combined with human judgement and guardrails ([Force for good](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/technology-as-a-force-for-good/)).
- Beutel points to algorithmic approaches such as q-fair federated learning, and access to more representative data, as ways federated learning can address bias ([Federated learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/friendly-federated-learning/)).

### Federated learning made practical

- Beutel argues federated learning solves a data access problem: organisations that cannot pool data can still train together, and the alternative is often no model at all ([Federated learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/friendly-federated-learning/)).
- He gives the example of manufacturers who would not share sensor data because it could reveal how many parts they produce. The main cost versus centralised training is longer convergence time ([Federated learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/friendly-federated-learning/)).
- Flower can federate existing TensorFlow, PyTorch or JAX code “in less than 20 lines of code”, and its Virtual Client Engine ran 15 million clients on average hardware ([Federated learning](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/friendly-federated-learning/)).

### AI tooling grows up

- Daniel Whitenack explains that GPT-3 access was tightly controlled over misuse and bias concerns before OpenAI dropped its waitlist. Chris Benson lists the nine prohibited content categories ([Tooling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/openai-and-hugging-face-tooling/)).
- Whitenack describes Hugging Face as a one-stop shop with model and dataset hubs, Spaces, an inference API, a first reinforcement learning environment and a Data Measurements Tool for bias metrics ([Tooling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/openai-and-hugging-face-tooling/)).
- His SIL team of about 15 combines Colab, an on-prem A100 server in Dallas, ClearML for experiment tracking and job queues, S3 for versioned artefacts and the Hugging Face hubs. He says working out that stack took about a year ([Tooling](/podcasts/practical-ai/2021/12/openai-and-hugging-face-tooling/)).
