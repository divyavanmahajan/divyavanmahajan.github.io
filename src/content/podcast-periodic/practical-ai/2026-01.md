## Overview

Practical AI opened 2026 with a look back at the year of agents, then turned to two questions about control: how to make models safe from the inside, and how AI changes the balance of power in democracies. Across the three episodes, the model itself was rarely the bottleneck. Integration, power, safety economics and politics were.

## Main themes

### Agents and coding cross a threshold

- Daniel Whitenack calls 2025 the year the conversation moved to AI agents. Chris Benson says Opus 4.5 and OpenAI's 5.2 now write senior-level code, even in Rust ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).
- Benson describes a prompt that, “in the matter of six minutes, it produced what I would have at least six weeks of work”, after many hundreds of earlier prompts ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).
- Whitenack cites Gartner figures on agentic AI in production and blames failures on missing integration expertise and on automating processes that were bad to begin with ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).

### Models are commodities; systems and power are not

- Whitenack argues reasoning models are mislabelled and that their tokens add latency and cost in tuned business workflows ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).
- He sees the multiplier in an LLM orchestrating SQL, forecasting tools and other predictive models. Benson links this to Yann LeCun leaving Meta to pursue world models ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).
- Benson says power, not GPUs, is now the limiting factor. Whitenack notes speculators buying rights to decommissioned power plants and says AI now drives key policy decisions ([Year of agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/)).

### Safety from inside the model

- Alizishaan Khatri of Wrynx compares today's guardrails to checking IDs at the gate of a thousand-condo building: prompt and response filters cannot see what happens inside ([Controlling AI models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/controlling-ai-models-from-the-inside/)).
- He says vendors ship weakly protected audio and video models because guarding expensive outputs is uneconomic, “like paying someone a thousand dollars to guard a $100 bill” ([Controlling AI models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/controlling-ai-models-from-the-inside/)).
- Wrynx watches which internal subspaces activate during bad generation. Khatri claims a module of about 20 million parameters replaces about 160 billion parameters of guard inference, which makes safety feasible on edge devices ([Controlling AI models](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/controlling-ai-models-from-the-inside/)).

### AI, democracy and work

- Bruce Schneier says deepfakes are the least interesting part of AI and democracy. His book with Nathan Sanders covers elections, legislation, administration, courts and citizens ([AI and democracy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/how-is-ai-shaping-democracy/)).
- He advocates public AI built outside the profit motive, pointing to the Swiss model from ETH Zurich and Singapore's Sea Lion, and argues AI mostly exacerbates problems whose solutions are political ([AI and democracy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/how-is-ai-shaping-democracy/)).
- Schneier expects disruption on par with the industrial revolution, with apprentice professions losing their junior tiers, and calls for decoupling healthcare and income from employment ([AI and democracy](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/how-is-ai-shaping-democracy/)).

## Notable episodes

- [2025 was the year of agents, what's coming in 2026?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/2025-was-the-year-of-agents-what-s-coming-in-2026/): the hosts' candid review of agent hype versus real productivity, with 2026 predictions on fragmentation and consolidation.
- [Controlling AI Models from the Inside](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/controlling-ai-models-from-the-inside/): a concrete cost argument for reading model internals instead of stacking guard models.
- [How is AI shaping democracy?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2026/01/how-is-ai-shaping-democracy/): Schneier's case for public AI, with examples from Japan, Brazil, Germany and Taiwan.
