## Overview

May on Practical AI covered four separate subjects with little overlap: agent reliability, OpenAI's GPT-4o, AI flying aircraft, and AI policy in the US Congress. Education came up in three episodes as a hopeful use case. The common question was trust, whether in agents, in voice assistants that listen all the time, or in autonomous machines.

## Main themes

### Agents that can be trusted to act

- Josh Albrecht of Imbue defines agents as AI systems acting on your behalf. He says “60, 70, 80% isn't really enough for like deploying this”, and closing the gap to 99.99% takes most of the work ([Full-stack agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/full-stack-approach-for-effective-ai-agents/)).
- Imbue runs its whole stack, from its own hardware through pre-training, RL and evaluation to UI. Albrecht admits this research-first approach is why there is no product yet ([Full-stack agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/full-stack-approach-for-effective-ai-agents/)).
- He advises hand-labelling at least about 100 evaluation examples. He also wants separate systems that audit agent actions after training, and prefers interactive coding tools over large automated pull requests ([Full-stack agents](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/full-stack-approach-for-effective-ai-agents/)).

### GPT-4o and the voice interface

- Chris Benson says GPT-4o became the most-opened app on his phone. Its speed across modalities makes the assistant part of the conversation rather than a separate task ([GPT-4o](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/first-impressions-of-gpt-4o/)).
- Daniel Whitenack notes millisecond voice responses and fewer token penalties for non-English languages. His live demo on air gave fluent answers but cut off twice ([GPT-4o](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/first-impressions-of-gpt-4o/)).
- Benson asks how always-on voice use fits US state recording laws. Whitenack compares the unease to when Alexa and Google Home first entered homes ([GPT-4o](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/first-impressions-of-gpt-4o/)).
- Both hosts conclude that thin wrappers around an LLM API can become obsolete after one announcement, and that value comes from domain expertise ([GPT-4o](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/first-impressions-of-gpt-4o/)).

### AI moves into physical machines

- Benson describes the X-62A VISTA, an F-16 flown by AI in a simulated dogfight. He says the Boeing 737 MAX problems “really shaken the public's trust in automation in aircraft and airliners”, even though that was not AI ([Autonomous fighter jets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/autonomous-fighter-jets/)).
- Whitenack cites an investor view that the opportunity has moved from foundation models to embedding AI in retail, manufacturing and agriculture ([Autonomous fighter jets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/autonomous-fighter-jets/)).
- He also covers the Linux Foundation's OPEA, which offers enterprise RAG blueprints and evaluation scorecards, and compares the video generation boom to the DALL-E era ([Autonomous fighter jets](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/autonomous-fighter-jets/)).

### Congress and AI regulation

- Representative Don Beyer, studying for a machine learning master's, says Congress “moves glacially”. A task force is narrowing about 200 AI bills to the few that matter ([AI in the U.S. Congress](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/ai-in-the-u-s-congress/)).
- Beyer groups the issues as deepfakes and copyright, reliability of large language models, and safety. His list of risks runs from job loss and misinformation to misuse and existential risk ([AI in the U.S. Congress](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/ai-in-the-u-s-congress/)).
- He contrasts the EU's regulatory stance with the US focus on innovation, and wants AI aimed at problems such as suicide prevention and education ([AI in the U.S. Congress](/podcasts/practical-ai/2024/05/ai-in-the-u-s-congress/)).
