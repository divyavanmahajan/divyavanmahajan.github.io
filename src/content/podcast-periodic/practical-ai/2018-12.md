## Overview

December on Practical AI was about what happens after a model is trained. Pachyderm's founder made the case for versioned, containerised data pipelines, the hosts walked through inference and deployment targets, and Altimeter analyst Susan Etlinger described how enterprises get from experiments to products while managing bias.

## Main themes

### Data infrastructure for production pipelines

- Joe Doliner (JD) describes Pachyderm as production data infrastructure in a box: a file system that can version-control terabyte-scale datasets, with processing in Docker containers orchestrated by Kubernetes ([Pachyderm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/pachyderm-s-kubernetes-based-infrastructure-for-ai/)).
- Provenance links data versions to outputs, so a model can be traced to its data and code. The enterprise tier uses it for permissions so derived models do not leak restricted data ([Pachyderm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/pachyderm-s-kubernetes-based-infrastructure-for-ai/)).
- To move a notebook to production, JD says “the first step to do is just to extract the code from Jupyter”, then split pre-processing, training and post-processing into separate pipelines ([Pachyderm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/pachyderm-s-kubernetes-based-infrastructure-for-ai/)).
- He says the hard part is DevOps rather than software skill, and that Pachyderm plans a hosted version after its Series A ([Pachyderm](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/pachyderm-s-kubernetes-based-infrastructure-for-ai/)).

### Inference and model serving

- Daniel Whitenack describes a trained model as a saved function loaded and called for inference. Chris Benson says “you need to wrap your model up as a software component”, often deployed in C++, Java or Go ([Now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).
- Benson notes that deployment involves software and systems engineering many data scientists have never dealt with ([Now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).
- They discuss NVIDIA open-sourcing its TensorRT inference server and Amazon's own ML chip, which Benson reads as pricing leverage over NVIDIA and Intel. Whitenack worries about the number of architectures to support and finds ONNX still rough ([Now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).

### Inference at the edge and in the browser

- Benson says inference is now distributed between cloud and edge, though he has not seen training on the edge in production ([Now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).
- Latency in speech recognition, connectivity and battery limits, and privacy all push models onto devices. ONNX.js and TensorFlow.js bring them to the browser, though shipping a model to clients exposes it ([Now what?](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/so-you-have-an-ai-model-now-what/)).

### Scaling AI in the enterprise

- Susan Etlinger presents the AI Maturity Playbook, which she calls a playbook for building a playbook, with five pillars from business strategy to ethics and governance ([Enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).
- She sees the move from if-then rules to probabilistic decisions as the biggest shift, and says success needs a culture with “the willingness to fail and fail fast” ([Enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).
- Whitenack notes job titles moving from data scientist to ML engineer. Etlinger hears that enterprises struggle to scale from models to products ([Enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).

### Bias and explainability controls

- Etlinger stresses that algorithms are only as good as their data. She notes facial recognition is less accurate for people of color, yet collecting more of their face data raises its own problems ([Enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).
- She cites algorithmic impact assessments, IBM's supplier declaration of conformity and bias dashboards, and argues interfaces should signal low-confidence output ([Enterprise](/podcasts/practical-ai/2018/12/finding-success-with-ai-in-the-enterprise/)).
