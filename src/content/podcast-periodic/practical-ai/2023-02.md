## Overview

February on Practical AI was about making generative models useful in practice. Cohere came up in two episodes, through Jay Alammar's applied NLP work and the hosts' look at prompting. The other two episodes covered the operations side: whether MLOps survives foundation models, and how Banana serves models on serverless GPUs.

## Main themes

### Prompting and realistic expectations

- Daniel Whitenack says generative models can be expected to be creative and coherent, but not factually correct or logical. The hosts cite Bing's chatbot, known as Sydney, gaslighting users and Galactica's fabricated papers ([Prompting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/success-and-failure-in-prompting/)).
- Whitenack argues “the biggest thing that's under our control that guides the utility or acceptability of the output is the prompt.” ([Prompting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/success-and-failure-in-prompting/)).
- He summarises Cohere's prompting guide and Elvis Saravia's split into instructions, context, input data and an output indicator. Chris Benson compares prompt engineering to UI and UX design ([Prompting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/success-and-failure-in-prompting/)).
- Benson argues models trained on internet-scale data mirror human bias and error, so businesses must reset their users' expectations ([Prompting](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/success-and-failure-in-prompting/)).

### Building on pre-trained models

- Jay Alammar of Cohere sees a frontier forming between training models and using them, with most applied work above it: picking a model, then adding few-shot prompts, embeddings, retrieval and fine-tuning ([Applied NLP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/applied-nlp-solutions-ai-education/)).
- He argues prompt engineering alone is not a competitive advantage, so a product playbook needs proprietary data and fine-tunes. He starts with three to five examples and fine-tunes once he has 500 or 1,000 labelled ones ([Applied NLP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/applied-nlp-solutions-ai-education/)).
- Alammar urges people to ask “is this a cherry-picked example or is this reliably something that the model is able to do?” He sees semantic search with embeddings as a mature use case ([Applied NLP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/applied-nlp-solutions-ai-education/)).
- Cohere's latest release was a multilingual embedding model covering over 100 languages. Alammar's advice to new educators is to start now and emulate writers they admire ([Applied NLP](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/applied-nlp-solutions-ai-education/)).

### Serverless GPUs for inference

- Erik Dunteman of Banana says loading a model from disk to CPU to GPU can take 10 minutes, which held back serverless GPUs. The aim is to keep weights as close to GPU RAM as possible without occupying it ([Serverless GPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/serverless-gpus/)).
- Replicas stay up for a configurable 10 seconds after a call. Dunteman is surprised that most customers are more price sensitive than latency sensitive ([Serverless GPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/serverless-gpus/)).
- About 80% of deployments come from custom repos rather than templates. Dunteman expects each user to have a fleet of models fine-tuned on their own data, which needs scale-to-zero infrastructure ([Serverless GPUs](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/serverless-gpus/)).

### MLOps after foundation models

- In a joint episode with the MLOps Community podcast, Mihail Eric describes MLOps as taking machine learning from research into the real world, and says it is here to stay ([MLOps](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/mlops-is-alive-and-well/)).
- Eric and Whitenack say systems such as GPT-3 and ChatGPT were themselves operations achievements, and chaining models only adds operational burden ([MLOps](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/mlops-is-alive-and-well/)).
- Demetrios Brinkmann says teams fail by copying Google's maturity levels. Eric says the right approach depends on budget, time and organisational maturity ([MLOps](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/02/mlops-is-alive-and-well/)).
