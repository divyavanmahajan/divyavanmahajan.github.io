## Overview

July on Practical AI was about the working habits around models rather than the models themselves. Two host-only episodes covered open source norms and how to turn AI ethics principles into practice. Two guest episodes looked at MLOps tooling from Allegro AI and at Hugging Face's open source transformer ecosystem.

## Main themes

### Open source as the base of AI work

- Daniel Whitenack and Chris Benson note that most AI software is open source, but code, data and models each carry licences. Whitenack cites Mozilla's Common Voice terms, which forbid re-identifying speakers ([Open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/)).
- Benson suggests contributing documentation, examples or data, and asking maintainers what they need. He points out that “the majority of maintainers out there are maintaining it for free” ([Open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/)).
- For safe experiments, Benson builds a Docker container per tutorial and Whitenack uses several Google Colab notebooks to compare approaches ([Open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/)).

### From AI principles to practice

- Benson, who leads AI ethics at Lockheed Martin, says writing principles is the easy part. The work is mapping them to existing policies, governance, compliance and developer tooling ([Practical AI Ethics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/practical-ai-ethics/)).
- He recommends a cross-functional group (product, legal, HR) to draft principles, tied to documented values so they do not become a separate “beast unto themselves” ([Practical AI Ethics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/practical-ai-ethics/)).
- Benson separates compliance, ideally automated, from governance, which handles failures. Whitenack argues traceability also helps engineering and uses a Swedish self-driving car meeting a kangaroo in Australia to show data bias ([Practical AI Ethics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/practical-ai-ethics/)).

### MLOps, experiment tracking and data versioning

- Nir Bar-Lev of Allegro AI says MLOps differs from DevOps because teams run many heavy, untested experiments on large machines from day one ([Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/)).
- He says tracking usually starts when an engineer on the team says “stop, this is crazy.” He has seen teams of tens of data scientists without it ([Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/)).
- Bar-Lev calls data the holy grail and argues teams must version datasets and their metadata, not just files ([Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/)).
- Allegro first built a large data platform before most companies needed it, then open sourced Trains for experiment management, which had just joined the PyTorch ecosystem ([Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/)).

### Hugging Face and transformers

- Sasha Rush says Hugging Face moved from chatbots to open source after quickly porting BERT to PyTorch. The Transformers library grew from no users to about 30,000 ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/all-things-transformers-with-hugging-face/)).
- Rush sees pre-training on large text corpora as the more important half of the transformer story. He splits users into architects, trainers and end users ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/all-things-transformers-with-hugging-face/)).
- Hugging Face also appeared in the open source episode, where Whitenack cited Clem's tweet about 25 team members and 400 contributors ([Open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Practical AI Ethics](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/practical-ai-ethics/): a practitioner's guide to moving from published principles to policies, compliance and tooling.
- [MLOps and tracking experiments with Allegro AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/mlops-and-tracking-experiments-with-allegro-ai/): a clear case for why ML needs its own operations discipline, with data treated like code.
- [🤗 All things transformers with Hugging Face](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/all-things-transformers-with-hugging-face/): attention and pre-training explained, plus how ICLR 2020 went fully virtual in about six weeks.
- [The ins and outs of open source for AI](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/07/the-ins-and-outs-of-open-source-for-ai/): practical advice on licences, finding tools and contributing back.
