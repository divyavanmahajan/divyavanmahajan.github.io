## Overview

February on Practical AI began with a data-driven look at the state of AI through Stanford's AI Index, then turned to applied language technology. Guests covered AI for reading scientific literature, classifying phone calls for marketers and bringing NLP to local languages. AI for good ran through two of the four episodes.

## Main themes

### The state of AI in numbers

- Chris Benson and Daniel Whitenack walk through the 291-page AI Index 2019 from Stanford's Human-Centered AI Institute and partners. Benson notes China has passed Europe in publication volume, though US papers still have higher citation impact ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/insights-from-the-ai-index-2019-annual-report/)).
- The report cites “AI investment was over 70 billion with AI related startup investments over 37 billion”. Benson says startup investment keeps growing around 50% a year ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/insights-from-the-ai-index-2019-annual-report/)).
- Whitenack says benchmarks keep getting harder as models reach human level on many tasks, with GLUE surpassed and replaced by SuperGLUE ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/insights-from-the-ai-index-2019-annual-report/)).
- Both hosts describe PhDs and faculty leaving academia for industry. Benson says this has driven AI compensation through the roof ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/insights-from-the-ai-index-2019-annual-report/)).

### AI that reads science

- Doug Raymond of the Allen Institute for AI cites about 3.5 million new publications a year, while the average scholar reads around 250. Whitenack calculates one year's output would take about 300 years to read ([Semantic Scholar](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/ai-powered-scientific-exploration-and-discovery/)).
- Raymond argues AI2's non-profit status lets it tackle general discovery where commercial tools have little incentive. Semantic Scholar's knowledge graph covers over 180 million papers ([Semantic Scholar](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/ai-powered-scientific-exploration-and-discovery/)).
- To look beyond citation counts, AI2 built SciBERT on 3 billion words of scientific text. Combined with the citation graph, it powers a personalised paper feed ([Semantic Scholar](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/ai-powered-scientific-exploration-and-discovery/)).

### Insights from phone calls

- Mike McCourt says Invoca closes the attribution gap when buyers pick up the phone, and notes that most AI research is not focused on phone calls ([Phone call data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/real-time-conversational-insights-from-phone-call-data/)).
- Calls vary from under a minute to two hours, and McCourt says hold messages “can sound just like a purchase”, so Invoca strips them out ([Phone call data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/real-time-conversational-insights-from-phone-call-data/)).
- Each customer gets one model trained only on its own data, kept parsimonious. McCourt also built an interpretable hierarchical topic model constrained by Zipf's law ([Phone call data](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/real-time-conversational-insights-from-phone-call-data/)).

### Language technology for the long tail

- Speaking for SIL International, Whitenack says there are 7,111 living languages and over half the world speaks about 23 of them. He links language to education and the UN Sustainable Development Goals ([7000 languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/nlp-for-the-world-s-7000-languages/)).
- Dan Jeffries describes Pachyderm as version control for data science, and cites a customer's language preprocessing falling from about ten weeks to six or seven days once parallelised ([7000 languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/nlp-for-the-world-s-7000-languages/)).
- Whitenack walks through a Singlish text-to-speech pipeline published as a reusable template. Jeffries frames AI as a lever that scales scarce experts, not a path to AGI ([7000 languages](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/nlp-for-the-world-s-7000-languages/)).
- AI for good also surfaced in the AI Index episode, where Whitenack recommends IBM's AI Fairness 360 toolkit ([AI Index](/podcasts/practical-ai/2020/02/insights-from-the-ai-index-2019-annual-report/)).
