## Overview

July on Practical AI was about open models catching up with closed ones, from a flood of new releases to Meta's commercially licensed Llama 2. Codeium's founders made the case for fine-tuned, self-hosted coding models, and lawyer Damien Riehl looked at what generated content means for copyright, patents and jobs.

## Main themes

### Open models catch up

- Daniel Whitenack says the foundation model “is no longer this sort of moat that separates you because now there's open source models” ([Cambrian explosion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/cambrian-explosion-of-generative-models/)).
- Chris Benson compares the shift to how software moved from proprietary languages to open source. Whitenack notes that open models lack the maintained APIs closed providers offer, which leaves engineering work to the user ([Cambrian explosion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/cambrian-explosion-of-generative-models/)).
- Whitenack reads Databricks' $1.3 billion purchase of MosaicML as a bet on enterprises running open models on private data ([Cambrian explosion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/cambrian-explosion-of-generative-models/)).
- Llama 2 allows commercial use unless a company had more than 700 million monthly users. Whitenack calls it commercially available but not open source in the official sense, and Benson calls the ban on using its outputs to improve other LLMs anti-competitive ([New Llama](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/there-s-a-new-llama-in-town/)).

### Choosing and tuning a model

- Whitenack filters models first by commercial licence, then by inference speed and context length. Models up to 7B fit on a single GPU, while 40-60B models need multi-GPU setups ([Cambrian explosion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/cambrian-explosion-of-generative-models/)).
- He recommends PEFT and LoRA fine-tuning of the smaller Llama 2 models for business tasks, and says 7B runs on a Colab T4 with 4-bit quantisation ([New Llama](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/there-s-a-new-llama-in-town/)).
- In both news episodes he advises building a test set of your own examples and trying several models on it rather than trusting leaderboards ([Cambrian explosion](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/cambrian-explosion-of-generative-models/), [New Llama](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/there-s-a-new-llama-in-town/)).
- The hosts compare Claude 2's 100,000-token context with ChatGPT's Code Interpreter, which writes and shows pandas code over uploaded data ([New Llama](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/there-s-a-new-llama-in-town/)).

### AI coding beyond autocomplete

- Varun Mohan and Anshul Ramachandran say their GPU virtualization work at Exafunction led them to see a Copilot-style product as one of the largest GPU workloads ([Codeium](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/a-developer-s-toolkit-for-sota-ai/)).
- Codeium adds in-IDE chat, code lenses and codebase search to autocomplete. Mohan cites over 100,000 active developers and more than 10 billion tokens of code a day, with the individual plan free ([Codeium](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/a-developer-s-toolkit-for-sota-ai/)).
- Low-latency autocomplete sees only about 150 lines of context, so Ramachandran argues for fine-tuning on a company's own code. Codeium does this on serving GPUs during idle time, fully air-gapped ([Codeium](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/a-developer-s-toolkit-for-sota-ai/)).

### Copyright, patents and work

- Damien Riehl and Noah Rubin generated 471 billion melodies and placed them in the public domain. Riehl says defendants in melody cases have won using his arguments ([Legal consequences](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/legal-consequences-of-generated-content/)).
- He supports the US Copyright Office view that fully machine-generated work is not copyrightable; a human who adds a little gets only thin copyright. He expects AI to speed the decline of patents ([Legal consequences](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/legal-consequences-of-generated-content/)).
- On jobs, Riehl sketches a path to layoffs once employers capture 10x gains, but notes that after the spreadsheet “we have more accountants than ever because the tools are actually a force multiplier” ([Legal consequences](/podcasts/practical-ai/2023/07/legal-consequences-of-generated-content/)).
