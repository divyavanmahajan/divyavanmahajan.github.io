## Overview

September opened with an experiment: Peter Diamandis questioned two AI personas, one optimistic and one pessimistic, about public fears of AI, with jobs the most common thread. Two episodes with Salim Ismail then promoted their book Exponential Organizations 2.0. The month closed with Keith Ferrazzi on public speaking and Jewel on using technology to scale mental health support.

## Main themes

### Talking to AI personas about AI fears

- PeterBot, built by Steve Brown's team from Diamandis's books and blogs, answers questions on jobs, deepfakes and superintelligence with steady optimism. Diamandis says the voice exchange was unscripted and that the topic needs more digging ([AI Clone](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/a-conversation-with-my-ai-clone-on-the-future-of-ai-ep-62/)).
- Nigel, trained on pessimistic news, says “people have every reason to be scared of AI” and predicts elite control and a dangerous superintelligence ([Doomsday AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/1-on-1-with-a-doomsday-ai-ais-worst-case-scenario-ep-63/)).
- On alignment, Diamandis cites Mo Gawdat and Emad Mostaque on the importance of training data. PeterBot agrees curated data can help, while noting values differ across 8 billion people ([AI Clone](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/a-conversation-with-my-ai-clone-on-the-future-of-ai-ep-62/)).
- PeterBot imagines a 2024 election marred by fabricated scandals and advises fact-checking before sharing. Diamandis notes the bot is itself a kind of deepfake ([AI Clone](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/a-conversation-with-my-ai-clone-on-the-future-of-ai-ep-62/)).

### AI and jobs

- Nigel says AI is already taking jobs in manufacturing, customer service and journalism, and doubts retraining will be enough. Diamandis counters that automation has historically created more jobs than it destroyed ([Doomsday AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/1-on-1-with-a-doomsday-ai-ais-worst-case-scenario-ep-63/)).
- PeterBot argues AI will augment screenwriters, artists and physicians rather than replace them, and that schools should teach critical thinking and creativity ([AI Clone](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/a-conversation-with-my-ai-clone-on-the-future-of-ai-ep-62/)).
- Salim Ismail cites the gig economy and ATMs, which did not reduce bank teller numbers, to argue automation does not destroy jobs ([ExO Q&A](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/ai-tech-and-exponential-organizations-q-a-exponential-organizations-2/)).

### The Exponential Organizations playbook

- Diamandis and Ismail present a 12-step method that starts with a massive transformative purpose (MTP), then community and a four-person team, before choosing technology or business model ([Don't Fall Behind](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/dont-fall-behind-in-the-ai-and-tech-revolution-exponential-organizatio/)).
- They say a tech company that cost about $5 million to start 20 years ago can now be started for around $500. Founders should define the problem first rather than build around a shiny widget like LLMs ([ExO Q&A](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/ai-tech-and-exponential-organizations-q-a-exponential-organizations-2/)).
- Ismail warns large companies: “do not Implement EXO inside your organization you'll have an immune system response”. He contrasts GE's failed Lean Startup push with Nespresso, walled off from Nestle ([ExO Q&A](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/ai-tech-and-exponential-organizations-q-a-exponential-organizations-2/)).
- The hosts cite Amazon's institutional yes, XPRIZE crowdsourcing and platforms such as GitHub as models ([Don't Fall Behind](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/dont-fall-behind-in-the-ai-and-tech-revolution-exponential-organizatio/)).

### Skills and support that scale

- Diamandis and Ferrazzi say fear of speaking yields to practice. They treat keynotes as a business built on a book, a clear topic and relationships with individual agents ([Public Speaker](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/how-anyone-can-become-a-great-public-speaker-w-keith-ferrazzi-ep-65/)).
- Jewel says the US is about 500,000 therapists short and that one-to-one care cannot close the gap. Her for-profit Innerworld uses trained lay guides in anonymous virtual groups, with AI oversight, for $8 a month or free ([Jewel](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/solving-the-mental-health-crisis-with-technology-w-jewel-ep-66/)).
- She cites an NIH grant for a randomised trial and plans a seed round. Diamandis estimates the model is about a thousand times cheaper than therapy ([Jewel](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/09/solving-the-mental-health-crisis-with-technology-w-jewel-ep-66/)).
