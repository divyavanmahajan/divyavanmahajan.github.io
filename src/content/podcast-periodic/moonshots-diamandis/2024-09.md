## Overview

September was a short month of three episodes with no shared thread. Neil deGrasse Tyson debated space, Mars and robots with Peter Diamandis. The other two episodes looked at health and longevity companies, OneSkin and Viome, from a business and market angle.

## Main themes

### Who pays for space

- Tyson argues big space programmes are funded by fear of a rival, not love of exploration. On the Moon return he asks “How come we have Artemis? Because well, it's China.” ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).
- He says Mars would cost about a trillion dollars, which no venture investor will fund, so the US goes only if China makes it a priority. Diamandis counters that entrepreneurs like Elon Musk can now take on projects that once needed governments ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).
- Diamandis prefers O'Neill-style orbital habitats built by robots from asteroid material. Tyson thinks finding and deflecting dangerous asteroids is a more realistic insurance policy than moving people to Mars ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).
- Tyson puts the odds of microbial life under the Martian surface at about 50/50, and says fuzzy Navy footage cannot turn an unknown object into proof of aliens ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).

### AI and robots, seen by a sceptic

- Tyson says “I'm not into humanoid robots. I think it's a waste of time.” He sees the self-driving car as proof that purpose-built machines do the job better ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).
- Diamandis holds his line, citing forecasts of 1 to 10 billion humanoids by 2040 at prices heading towards $20,000 ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).
- Tyson supports informed AI safety rules but calls California's call-centre job protections naive, and expects deepfakes to wreck trust in the internet within about five years ([Neil deGrasse Tyson](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/neil-degrasse-tyson-are-we-alone-on-earth-the-truth-about-aliens-ufos/)).

### Longevity and prevention as business

- Carolina Reis Oliveira says OneSkin, a team of about 30 in San Francisco, spent its first five years on research before launching a product. She argues the anti-aging skincare market, which she sizes at around $80 billion, has not kept up with longevity science ([OneSkin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/how-i-am-reversing-my-skins-aging-with-peptides-w-carolina-reis-olivei/)).
- She describes a measurement-first lab platform and partnerships with AI companies that propose candidate molecules for OneSkin to test. Diamandis discloses he advises the company and that it sponsors the show ([OneSkin](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/how-i-am-reversing-my-skins-aging-with-peptides-w-carolina-reis-olivei/)).
- Naveen Jain says the two biggest changes AI brings “is going to be in education and Healthcare”. He and Diamandis argue US healthcare pays for sickness rather than staying healthy ([Naveen Jain](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/billionaire-naveen-jain-reveals-how-ai-will-change-medicine-forever-ep/)).
- Diamandis lists seven ways AI will reshape healthcare, from health coaches to robot surgeons. Jain predicts India may leapfrog the US and pitches Viome to employers as a way to lower insurance costs ([Naveen Jain](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/09/billionaire-naveen-jain-reveals-how-ai-will-change-medicine-forever-ep/)).
