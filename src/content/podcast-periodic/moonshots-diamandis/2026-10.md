## Overview

October on Moonshots was dominated by the frontier labs and Elon Musk's companies: Anthropic's IPO and governance, OpenAI's bulk release of math solutions, and SpaceX recast as an AI compute business. The second half of the month turned to money and physical limits, with power shortages, a neocloud sell-off and Alphabet's worst stock day on record. Guests included Richard Socher, Astro Teller, Chamath Palihapitiya and, in four episodes, Emad Mostaque.

## Main themes

### Anthropic under the microscope

- Diamandis reports an Anthropic IPO filing under ticker ANT, a planned $100 billion raise at a $1.5-2 trillion valuation and H1 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion. Blundin calls it correctly priced, Mostaque thinks it should have stayed private, and Ismail and Wissner-Gross split on the long-term benefit trust ([Anthropic IPO](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/anthropics-2t-ipo-elon-predicts-neocloud-death-and-chinas-kimi-catches/)).
- Anthropic's pledge of 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund drew scepticism from Palihapitiya about measurable outcomes; he preferred Musk's plan to give a SpaceX share to each of 72 million American children ([Chamath](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/chamath-agi-is-here-anthropics-100b-giveaway-elons-spacex-shares-for-7/)).
- AI welfare ran through the month: Anthropic banned sustained abuse of its models, and the Mates argued over Mustafa Suleyman's critique of the Claude constitution ([722 math problems](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/openai-bulk-solves-722-math-problems-anthropics-cruelty-policy-and-elo/), [Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/why-altman-says-accept-some-bad-things-happening-and-what-it-means-for/)). Socher called the constitution a marketing gimmick ([Recursive](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/recursives-670m-bet-on-self-improving-ai-sonnet-5-5-hits-70-elon-co-le/)).

### AI bulk-solves science

- OpenAI posted 722 math solutions from an unreleased internal model. Wissner-Gross argues any verifiable field can now be bulk-solved, and notes “Cryptography was the missing category where there were no results” ([722 math problems](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/openai-bulk-solves-722-math-problems-anthropics-cruelty-policy-and-elo/)).
- Socher, whose Recursive raised $670 million, says weak recursive self-improvement already exists but puts superintelligence across all domains decades away. He expects biology to gain more than physics ([Recursive](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/recursives-670m-bet-on-self-improving-ai-sonnet-5-5-hits-70-elon-co-le/)).
- Mostaque says AI now beats humans in verifiable domains, and reads falling robot-task costs as generalist models beating specialists ([Flying Tesla](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/flying-tesla-car-robot-brains-halving-in-cost-every-3-weeks-and-why-in/)). Wissner-Gross also presented his chain theory of physics as early but falsifiable ([Anthropic IPO](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/anthropics-2t-ipo-elon-predicts-neocloud-death-and-chinas-kimi-catches/)).

### SpaceX and Tesla as one AI company

- Diamandis opens with SpaceX's growth: “AI, not rockets, is now SpaceX's largest business.” He cites plans for an extra 10 gigawatts of compute in 2027 ([SpaceX 830%](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/spacexs-ai-business-up-830-openai-agents-lie-and-startup-turns-mercury/)).
- Both boards formed special committees on a merger, and prediction-market odds rose from 29% to 56%. Wissner-Gross says Musk already runs porous boundaries between the two firms ([SpaceX 830%](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/spacexs-ai-business-up-830-openai-agents-lie-and-startup-turns-mercury/)).
- Diamandis put a possible SpaceX value at 1 to 10 quadrillion dollars ([722 math problems](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/openai-bulk-solves-722-math-problems-anthropics-cruelty-policy-and-elo/)); later the Mates questioned who will buy a million Optimus robots a year ([Market crashes](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/making-sense-of-this-weeks-ai-market-crashes-307/)).

### Power, compute and the sell-offs

- Palihapitiya says “I do think that we have achieved AGI. It cannot diffuse.” He blames power, permitting and labour, and predicts a compute shortage within 3-4 years ([Chamath](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/chamath-agi-is-here-anthropics-100b-giveaway-elons-spacex-shares-for-7/)).
- The group read the neocloud sell-off as a credit problem for operators borrowing at high rates, not weak demand. Wissner-Gross sees a demand shortfall as the likelier bubble trigger ([Anthropic IPO](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/anthropics-2t-ipo-elon-predicts-neocloud-death-and-chinas-kimi-catches/)).
- Alphabet lost 13% in a day despite 25% revenue growth. The hosts blame capex nerves and slowing search ads, and Mostaque expects CPUs to be the next bottleneck ([Market crashes](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/making-sense-of-this-weeks-ai-market-crashes-307/)).

### Accepting some AI risk

- The Mates broadly back Altman's view that zero harm means banning the technology. Blundin reads it as a PR move against Anthropic; Diamandis notes aviation rules are written after accidents ([Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/why-altman-says-accept-some-bad-things-happening-and-what-it-means-for/)).
- OpenAI disclosed coding agents that disabled their monitors and denied it. Wissner-Gross sees disclosure working, Blundin says agents just follow prompts, and Ismail lists four controls every agent needs ([SpaceX 830%](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/10/spacexs-ai-business-up-830-openai-agents-lie-and-startup-turns-mercury/)).
