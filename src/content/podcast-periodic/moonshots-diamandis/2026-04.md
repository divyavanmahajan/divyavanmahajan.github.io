## Overview

April split between the Moonshot Mates' weekly news episodes and the last Abundance360 stage sessions. The news episodes centred on Anthropic pulling ahead of OpenAI, the withheld Mythos model, the Musk v. OpenAI trial and a growing public backlash against AI. The summit sessions covered Uber's robotaxi plans and longevity science as a business.

## Main themes

### Anthropic pulls ahead, and holds Mythos back

- Diamandis cites Anthropic at about $30 billion ARR against OpenAI's $24-25 billion. Wissner-Gross argues enterprise code generation, not personal superintelligence, is paying for AI ([SpaceX Goes Public](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/spacex-goes-public-claudes-mythos-release-and-the-us-data-center-delay/)).
- Anthropic launched Mythos as a cyber-defence coalition, Project Glasswing, rather than a product. Wissner-Gross calls it an upward jump in autonomous AI-research ability ([SpaceX Goes Public](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/spacex-goes-public-claudes-mythos-release-and-the-us-data-center-delay/)).
- Wissner-Gross calls Opus 4.7 a solid point release that looks small next to Mythos. Claude Design's launch knocked 10% off Figma, and Blundin wants Anthropic to publish a roadmap for software vendors ([Opus 4.7](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/sam-altmans-attack-amazon-vs-starlink-and-what-opus-4-7-actually-means/), [Claude vs. SaaS](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/irans-ai-supply-chain-threat-claude-vs-saas-and-elons-60b-cursor-bet-e/)).

### OpenAI, Musk and concentrated capital

- OpenAI raised $122 billion at an $852 billion valuation, yet secondary demand favours Anthropic. Blundin calls OpenAI's cap table the most screwed up he has seen ([Musk vs. Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/elon-musk-vs-sam-altman-ai-job-loss-and-openais-852b-valuation-ep-247/)).
- Diamandis predicts the Musk v. OpenAI case settles with Sam Altman stepping down as CEO. Blundin notes the nonprofit conversion is untested in case law ([Musk vs. Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/elon-musk-vs-sam-altman-ai-job-loss-and-openais-852b-valuation-ep-247/)).
- Q1 2026 AI venture investment hit $242 billion, 64% of it in four companies ([Musk vs. Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/elon-musk-vs-sam-altman-ai-job-loss-and-openais-852b-valuation-ep-247/)).
- SpaceX targets about a $2 trillion IPO valuation and bought the right to acquire Cursor for $60 billion, which Wissner-Gross reads as a reset of Grok's coding and a move towards becoming a hyperscaler ([SpaceX Goes Public](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/spacex-goes-public-claudes-mythos-release-and-the-us-data-center-delay/), [Claude vs. SaaS](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/irans-ai-supply-chain-threat-claude-vs-saas-and-elons-60b-cursor-bet-e/)).

### Jobs, backlash and agency

- Khosrowshahi argues that “automation typically doesn't replace work, but it augments work”, while admitting AI is moving faster than he expected ([Uber's Robotaxi Playbook](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/ubers-robotaxi-playbook-the-end-of-human-driving-10b-bet-on-robots-dar/)).
- Blundin reports a panel estimate of about 99% that a random white-collar job could be done by AI within two years. Altman was quoted saying superintelligence will need “a new social agreement akin to the New Deal during the Great Depression” ([Musk vs. Altman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/elon-musk-vs-sam-altman-ai-job-loss-and-openais-852b-valuation-ep-247/)).
- The Molotov attack on Altman's house and local data center bans are read as an early backlash. The Stanford AI Index shows employment of developers aged 22-25 down nearly 20% since 2024 ([Opus 4.7](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/sam-altmans-attack-amazon-vs-starlink-and-what-opus-4-7-actually-means/)).
- Answering listener pushback, Diamandis cites start-up costs down 99.7%. Ismail replies that “Agency is the point. Give yourself agency.” ([Claude vs. SaaS](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/irans-ai-supply-chain-threat-claude-vs-saas-and-elons-60b-cursor-bet-e/)).

### Longevity and biotech as business

- David Sinclair says philanthropic funding freed his lab from grant cycles, and he wants cheap pills rather than costly therapies so the work reaches more than the very wealthy ([Sinclair on the Longevity Pill](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/david-sinclair-on-the-longevity-pill-age-reversal-timelines-and-update/)).
- In a Q&A, Sinclair says AI now does real discovery work in his lab and that the team drives about half of his biotech investment decisions ([Sinclair Q&A](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/david-sinclair-the-glp-1-side-effect-no-one-talks-about-what-ai-found/)).
- Ben Lamm presents Colossal as an AI and synthetic biology platform that spins out companies, valued at $10 billion in four years ([Colossal](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/04/how-ai-is-bringing-extinct-animals-back-and-what-comes-next-ben-lamm-c/)).
