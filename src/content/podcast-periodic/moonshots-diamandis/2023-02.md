## Overview

February on Moonshots opened with two episodes featuring NASA astrophysicist Amber Straughn on the James Webb Space Telescope and what NASA plans next. The rest of the month moved from space to Earth: a live AMA with Anthony Scaramucci on abundance and FTX, a conversation on longevity with Mark Hyman, and a look at deep-tech venture capital with Dakin Sloss.

## Main themes

### Webb as a model moonshot

- Straughn says “Engineers had to invent 10 brand new technologies just to make the telescope a reality”, and Congress cancelled the mission at one point. Peter Diamandis notes it was conceived in 1996 and cost about $10 billion ([Webb discoveries](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-27-black-holes-exoplanets-and-other-webb-telescope-discoveries-w-am/)).
- The two-week deployment had more than 300 single points of failure. Straughn rates the telescope about 100 times more powerful than Hubble, and Diamandis calls it quintessential NASA despite his criticism of the agency ([Webb discoveries](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-27-black-holes-exoplanets-and-other-webb-telescope-discoveries-w-am/)).
- Early galaxies turn out more numerous, brighter and more evolved than theory predicted. Straughn rejects claims that this disproves the Big Bang. Webb also made the first definitive detection of carbon dioxide on an exoplanet ([Webb discoveries](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-27-black-holes-exoplanets-and-other-webb-telescope-discoveries-w-am/)).

### NASA's next steps and space questions

- In her AMA, Straughn says wormholes are allowed by physics but not usable for travel. She gives about a 90% chance of a return to the Moon by 2030 and agrees with Diamandis that it could be Chinese ([Wormholes AMA](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-26-amber-straughns-ama-are-wormholes-real-w-nasa-astrophysicist/)).
- She says NASA takes near-Earth asteroids more seriously than a decade ago and calls the DART mission a successful first proof of concept ([Wormholes AMA](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-26-amber-straughns-ama-are-wormholes-real-w-nasa-astrophysicist/)).
- The Nancy Grace Roman telescope (due 2027) and a planned Habitable Worlds Observatory aim at exoplanets and life beyond Earth. Straughn expects more data than astronomers can analyse, which Diamandis sees as an opening for AI and citizen-science prizes ([Wormholes AMA](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-26-amber-straughns-ama-are-wormholes-real-w-nasa-astrophysicist/), [Webb discoveries](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-27-black-holes-exoplanets-and-other-webb-telescope-discoveries-w-am/)).

### Abundance, pessimism and FTX

- Diamandis argues living standards have risen sharply thanks to technology and blames the gloom on brain wiring and negative news. Scaramucci cites Charlie Munger's sixfold rise in living standards over a century and calls Diamandis the anti-Malthusian ([State of America](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-28-the-state-of-america-live-twitter-spaces-ama-w-anthony-scaramucc/)).
- Scaramucci says “I thought Sam was building the architecture for the Next Generation everything exchange”. He blames the fraud on a handful of insiders and accounting back doors ([State of America](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-28-the-state-of-america-live-twitter-spaces-ama-w-anthony-scaramucc/)).

### The longevity debate

- Diamandis says he spends most of his time on adding 20 to 30 healthy years to a life. Scaramucci worries the very wealthy would gain access first and concentrate wealth further ([State of America](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-28-the-state-of-america-live-twitter-spaces-ama-w-anthony-scaramucc/)).
- Mark Hyman describes a shift in medicine from treating single diseases to targeting the shared processes of ageing, helped by billions in new research funding. Both he and Diamandis put lifestyle basics ahead of experimental therapies, and Diamandis cites a 17-year lag before breakthroughs reach clinics ([Mark Hyman](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-29-the-secret-to-a-long-healthy-life-w-dr-mark-hyman/)).

### Deep-tech venture capital in 2023

- Dakin Sloss says Prime Movers Lab likes to enter “right at that inflection point between science risk and engineering risk”. He and Diamandis agree hardware is at least ten times harder than software ([Dakin Sloss](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-30-venture-capital-in-2023-w-dakin-sloss/)).
- Sloss says Series B/C valuations are down 70-80% and predicts many new funds will close. Diamandis tells founders to raise enough to survive two or three years ([Dakin Sloss](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2023/02/ep-30-venture-capital-in-2023-w-dakin-sloss/)).
