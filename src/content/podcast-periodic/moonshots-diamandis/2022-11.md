## Overview

November had four episodes. The busiest was a conversation with Salim Ismail, recorded the day after the US midterms, on Twitter, FTX, democracy and AI in government. The other three were about purpose and mindset: Marc Benioff on running a company for all stakeholders, Jane McGonigal on games, and Naveen Jain on picking moonshots.

## Main themes

### Institutions too slow for technology

- Salim Ismail argues that “the metabolism of decision-making and change in a democracy is too slow for the change of technology” and expects city-state micro-democracies to outperform large nation-states ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).
- Peter Diamandis says he would prefer an incorruptible AI decision-maker. Ismail expects AI governance to start as a guide by the side and gives it about a year before a Jarvis-style assistant handles basic tasks ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).
- Both expect AI and robotics to make universal basic income affordable, though Ismail thinks general household robots are further off than people expect ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).

### Musk's Twitter, FTX and crypto

- Ismail cites ex-Twitter engineers who value the company at $10-12 billion, against the $45 billion Elon Musk paid. He calls public discourse a cultural rather than engineering problem ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).
- He says crypto contacts had warned for about 18 months that FTX's interest rates looked like a Ponzi scheme, and predicts a new crypto bull market in January ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).
- The hosts agree Bitcoin will either reach a million dollars or go to zero, which they call the biggest asymmetric bet ([Salim Ismail](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-11-the-future-of-twitter-american-democracy-and-ai-w-salim-ismail/)).

### Business as a platform for change

- Marc Benioff describes Salesforce's three founding bets: the cloud, subscriptions and the 1-1-1 philanthropy model. He ranks its values as trust, customer success, innovation and equality ([Marc Benioff](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-10-the-multi-billion-dollar-purpose-behind-salesforce-w-marc-beniof/)).
- He backed San Francisco's Proposition C, which he says funds $300 million a year for homelessness. He calls homelessness “a very solvable problem” ([Marc Benioff](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-10-the-multi-billion-dollar-purpose-behind-salesforce-w-marc-beniof/)).

### Games, abundance and moonshot mindsets

- Jane McGonigal argues future-simulation games leave players with urgent optimism rather than anxiety. She says a 2008 pandemic simulation led her to imagine game releases keeping people at home, which happened in 2020 ([Jane McGonigal](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-12-gaming-will-save-the-world-w-jane-mcgonigal/)).
- Diamandis links this to abundance: instead of slicing the pie thinner, we can bake more pies ([Jane McGonigal](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-12-gaming-will-save-the-world-w-jane-mcgonigal/)).
- Naveen Jain argues bigger problems are easier to solve because audacious goals attract talent and then capital. His test asks why this, why now and why me ([Naveen Jain](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-13-how-to-think-like-a-billionaire-w-naveen-jain/)).
- Jain explains how he raised three founder children by setting an example, and says outsiders such as Musk tend to disrupt industries ([Naveen Jain](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/11/ep-13-how-to-think-like-a-billionaire-w-naveen-jain/)).
