## Overview

The show launched in October 2022 with five episodes on one day and nine in total. Peter Diamandis used them to set out his recurring interests: space, longevity, prizes for big problems and the pace of AI. Two Eric Schmidt sessions supplied the month's most detailed discussion of AI, while Michael Saylor closed the month on Bitcoin.

## Main themes

### Eric Schmidt on AI, speed and Google

- Schmidt names April 2042 for AGI, based on a median expert forecast of 20 years, and expects five to ten such systems controlled by nation states because they are so expensive to run ([How to Run a Tech Giant](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-7-eric-schmidt-ex-google-ceo-how-to-run-a-tech-giant/)).
- He worries about the compression of time: defensive AI may have to act without humans in the loop. He suggests a US-China treaty on proportionate automated responses, and argues software is harder to control than plutonium ([How to Run a Tech Giant](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-7-eric-schmidt-ex-google-ceo-how-to-run-a-tech-giant/)).
- His main lesson from Google is that decisions must be made within days. He cites buying YouTube after it overtook Google Video, and an ad-targeting system built over a weekend that he calls a $150 billion business ([Eric Schmidt's AMA](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-8-eric-schmidts-ama-ai-national-security-and-cyborgs/)).
- He expects each country to regulate AI differently and the US and China to stay on an oppositional path ([Eric Schmidt's AMA](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-8-eric-schmidts-ama-ai-national-security-and-cyborgs/), [How to Run a Tech Giant](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-7-eric-schmidt-ex-google-ceo-how-to-run-a-tech-giant/)).

### Space and a multiplanetary future

- Diamandis tells how an 11-year fight with the FAA preceded Zero G and the flight that took Stephen Hawking into weightlessness. He calls becoming multiplanetary “a moral and ethical obligation that we have to the future of the human species” ([Flying Stephen Hawking](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-2-i-almost-killed-stephen-hawking-true-story-of-flying-stephen-hawk/)).
- He notes US government space spending fell from 2% at the height of Apollo to about a quarter of a percent ([Flying Stephen Hawking](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-2-i-almost-killed-stephen-hawking-true-story-of-flying-stephen-hawk/)).
- Sian Proctor traces her route from a NASA rejection to piloting Inspiration4, crediting curiosity and persistence ([Sian Proctor](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-5-becoming-the-first-black-female-space-pilot-w-dr-sian-proctor/)).

### Climate and prizes for big problems

- Elon Musk and Diamandis launch the $100 million carbon removal XPRIZE. Musk says “there currently does not exist technologies that can scale to the gigaton level at a reasonable cost” ([How to Save the World](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-1-how-to-save-the-world-w-elon-musk/)).
- Musk favours a carbon tax with rebates and defends rocket emissions as the price of becoming multiplanetary ([How to Save the World](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-1-how-to-save-the-world-w-elon-musk/)).
- XPRIZE came up in five episodes, with guests proposing their own prizes: cryonics from Tim Urban, plastic packaging from Proctor, and a dollar-and-Bitcoin mobile wallet from Saylor ([Tim Urban](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-3-are-humans-headed-toward-a-utopian-or-dystopian-future-w-tim-urba/), [Sian Proctor](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-5-becoming-the-first-black-female-space-pilot-w-dr-sian-proctor/), [Michael Saylor](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-9-the-future-is-bitcoin-w-michael-saylor/)).

### Longevity, regulation and purpose

- Tony Robbins argues people care more about quality of life than its length, and reports being near one billion meals in his hunger pledge ([Tony Robbins](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-4-how-to-create-a-successful-life-w-tony-robbins/)).
- Balaji Srinivasan argues the FDA and wider US regulatory state hold back medical and longevity innovation, and that building alternatives such as network states beats reform ([Balaji Srinivasan](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-6-abuses-of-the-american-regulatory-state-w-balaji-srinivasan/)).
- Tim Urban argues curiosity can be learned once you have a basic trunk of knowledge, and that it is trained out of children by school ([Tim Urban](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-3-are-humans-headed-toward-a-utopian-or-dystopian-future-w-tim-urba/)).

### Bitcoin as digital property

- Saylor says replacing gold would put Bitcoin at $500,000 a coin and $100 trillion as digital property would mean $5 million, framed as scenarios ([Michael Saylor](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-9-the-future-is-bitcoin-w-michael-saylor/)).
- He sees politics, not engineering, as the main brake on abundance ([Michael Saylor](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2022/10/ep-9-the-future-is-bitcoin-w-michael-saylor/)).
