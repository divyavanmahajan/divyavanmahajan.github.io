## Overview

August on Moonshots centred on what cheap AI and robots do to work. Emad Mostaque, Salim Ismail and Brett Adcock each took a different angle on jobs and humanoids. The rest of the month covered Bitcoin and crypto politics with Bill Barhydt, and two founder stories: John Mackey's Love.Life and Keller Rinaudo Cliffton's Zipline.

## Main themes

### AI and jobs

- Emad Mostaque says “what's a danger initially is the Outsource jobs and the entry level jobs”. Mid-level workers gain productivity and leaders who direct AI become multipliers ([Emad Mostaque](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/humanoid-robots-the-job-market-mass-automation-the-current-state-of-ai/)).
- His AI Atlantis framing, a phrase he credits to Nat Friedman, treats models as a new continent of cheap interns. He says GPT-4-class costs fell about a thousandfold and cites 38% of the current IIT batch without job placements ([Emad Mostaque](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/humanoid-robots-the-job-market-mass-automation-the-current-state-of-ai/)).
- Salim Ismail pushes back on Diamandis: jobs are bundles of tasks, and AI automates some of them (say 10 of 27 for a financial advisor) rather than whole roles. He also argues organisational design matters more than raw intelligence ([AI Hype vs. Reality](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/ai-hype-vs-reality-2024-the-state-of-emerging-technologies-w-salim-ism/)).
- Both episodes touched on AGI timelines and the power needed for data centres. Diamandis backs Gen 4 nuclear, while Ismail expects data centres to move to stranded renewables ([Emad Mostaque](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/humanoid-robots-the-job-market-mass-automation-the-current-state-of-ai/), [AI Hype vs. Reality](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/ai-hype-vs-reality-2024-the-state-of-emerging-technologies-w-salim-ism/)).

### Humanoid robots

- Brett Adcock says Figure 02 triples onboard compute, nearly doubles the battery to about 2.3 kWh and adds six cameras. Figure raised $675 million at a $2.6 billion valuation and calls itself an AI company that happens to do robotics ([Brett Adcock](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/the-man-taking-on-tesla-in-the-race-for-humanoid-robots-w-brett-adcock/)).
- Adcock says reliability, not volume, is the hard problem. He expects costs “like sub $20,000 a unit” at volume and refuses all defence work ([Brett Adcock](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/the-man-taking-on-tesla-in-the-race-for-humanoid-robots-w-brett-adcock/)).
- Diamandis repeatedly cites projections of up to 10 billion humanoids by 2040. Mostaque says robot control is largely solved for most use cases, while Ismail points to the Roomba as a reason to expect slow progress in homes ([Emad Mostaque](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/humanoid-robots-the-job-market-mass-automation-the-current-state-of-ai/), [AI Hype vs. Reality](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/ai-hype-vs-reality-2024-the-state-of-emerging-technologies-w-salim-ism/)).

### Bitcoin, DeFi and crypto politics

- Bill Barhydt treats the drop below $50,000 as liquidity noise from the yen carry-trade unwind. He says Bitcoin's 15-year log chart “looks like a straight line up and to the right” ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/bitcoin-the-us-election-and-ai-w-bill-barhydt-ep-113/)).
- He describes the Nashville Bitcoin conference, where Donald Trump promised to fire SEC chair Gary Gensler, and argues US politics is turning friendlier to crypto ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/bitcoin-the-us-election-and-ai-w-bill-barhydt-ep-113/)).
- Barhydt sees DeFi as a banking stack with no off switch, offers Bitcoin-backed loans through Abra, and expects Bitcoin yields of about 4 to 6%. Diamandis discloses he holds his Bitcoin on Abra ([Bill Barhydt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/bitcoin-the-us-election-and-ai-w-bill-barhydt-ep-113/)).
- Both Diamandis and Ismail say they are bullish, citing Morgan Stanley advisors pitching Bitcoin ETFs ([AI Hype vs. Reality](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/ai-hype-vs-reality-2024-the-state-of-emerging-technologies-w-salim-ism/)).

### Logistics and founder lessons

- Keller Rinaudo Cliffton is sure instant automated delivery will reach every city. Zipline has made about 1.1 million deliveries, and he says the aircraft is only about 15% of the complexity ([Zipline](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/the-company-competing-with-amazon-for-instant-delivery-w-keller-rinaud/)).
- He advises starting with one narrow paid use case, expecting everything to take ten times longer, and preferring early revenue to unpaid pilots ([Zipline](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/the-company-competing-with-amazon-for-instant-delivery-w-keller-rinaud/)).
- John Mackey argues outsiders disrupt industries, founders should keep control of their capital, and behaviour change needs physical community. He is sceptical of the VC model ([John Mackey](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/whole-foods-founder-on-american-health-care-ai-and-lack-of-quality-of/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Humanoid Robots, the Job Market & Mass Automation](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/humanoid-robots-the-job-market-mass-automation-the-current-state-of-ai/): Mostaque's clearest case for open, sovereign AI and his view of which jobs go first.

- [The Company Competing With Amazon for Instant Delivery](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2024/08/the-company-competing-with-amazon-for-instant-delivery-w-keller-rinaud/): Zipline's path from one use case in Rwanda to home delivery, with practical founder lessons.
