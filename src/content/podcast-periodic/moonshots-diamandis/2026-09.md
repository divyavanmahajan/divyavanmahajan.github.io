## Overview

September was the show's busiest month, with 14 episodes including two listener AMAs and a run of recordings from the first Moonshots Live. The news episodes centered on OpenAI and Anthropic trading the frontier, and on a wave of calls to slow AI down that the Moonshot Mates mostly rejected. The live stage added guests on defense, agent payments, investing and the XPRIZE competitions.

## Main themes

### OpenAI and Anthropic trade the frontier

- Peter Diamandis read OpenAI's claims for GPT-6 Astra: 98% on FrontierMath Tier 4, 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 and a hallucination rate down from 92% to 51%. Emad Mostaque noted that Artificial Analysis still ranks it behind Claude Fable 5.1 and Meta Muse Spark ([GPT-6 Astra](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/gpt-6-astra-saturates-arc-agi-3-teslas-30k-cybercab-floods-austin-anth/)).
- Dave Blundin argues a 30-day lead only matters if a lab uses it to lock in partners, chips and governments. Mostaque reported Anthropic formalizing Fermat's Last Theorem in 13 million lines of code ([GPT-6 Astra](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/gpt-6-astra-saturates-arc-agi-3-teslas-30k-cybercab-floods-austin-anth/)).
- After OpenAI cut Cursor off following the SpaceX acquisition, Anthropic stepped in. The hosts also expect AI to be priced by outcomes, as OpenAI now does for some large customers ([Star Probe](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/humanitys-first-star-probe-architect-labs-beats-nvidia-3-4x-musk-wants/)).
- Altman said OpenAI will not go public in 2026, citing safety. Blundin pointed to Anthropic's simpler business model and 80% gross margins ([Frontier Labs](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/frontier-labs-want-to-slow-down-openai-delays-its-2026-ipo-anthropic-f/)).

### Pushback against slowing down

- Diamandis reported Dario Amodei's essay calling for pacing the frontier, with Altman, Hassabis and Musk agreeing. Alexander Wissner-Gross and Salim Ismail called it a safety cartel, and Ismail dismissed Anthropic's bioweapon-risk report as badly timed theater ([Frontier Labs](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/frontier-labs-want-to-slow-down-openai-delays-its-2026-ipo-anthropic-f/)).
- The Mates criticized the Sanders/Casar bill to ban superintelligence and contrasted it with the G20 Carolina principles, where Musk argued that “new things must be default legal as opposed to default illegal” ([GPT-6 Astra](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/gpt-6-astra-saturates-arc-agi-3-teslas-30k-cybercab-floods-austin-anth/)).
- On stage, Diamandis played clips of Jensen Huang and Mark Zuckerberg rejecting doom talk. Blundin warned that states without data centers will never catch up ([Doomers](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/why-jensen-and-zuck-think-the-doomers-are-wrong-plus-ai-gets-a-rebrand/)).

### AGI claims and AI doing science

- Huang posted that AGI has arrived after Astra. The hosts treated the label as semantics and focused on OpenAI's reported Navier-Stokes result: 10,000 agents, 88 hours and about $6.5 million of inference ([Jensen AGI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/why-jensen-huang-believes-weve-reached-agi-and-inside-openais-german-w/)).
- Matt Pines says his company PSI found the trajectory for a $15 million Alpha Centauri probe in a week, after six months of failed work. Architect Labs claims an AI-designed chip with 3.4 times Nvidia Jetson's performance per watt ([Star Probe](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/humanitys-first-star-probe-architect-labs-beats-nvidia-3-4x-musk-wants/)).
- Ben Lamm and Martine Rothblatt brought a business view of biology: proprietary data over models, government biobank partnerships, and organ supply as a commercial operation ([Ben Lamm](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/why-were-living-in-a-biological-singularity-with-ben-lamm-moonshots-li/), [Rothblatt](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/martine-rothblatt-growing-unlimited-organs-ai-consciousness-digital-pe/)).

### Agents, ownership and builders

- Circle's Nikhil Chandhok says “every one of us has thousands of agents working for us” is easy to imagine, and that agents need identity and cheap stablecoin settlement ([Cathie Wood](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/cathie-wood-on-tesla-spacex-merger-1m-bitcoin-more-ais-than-humans-ep/)).
- Vlad Tenev argues broad ownership and tokenized assets will build support for AI, and expects AI code to ship with formal correctness certificates ([Vlad Tenev](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/robinhoods-vlad-tenev-on-tokenizing-everything-openais-6-misalignment/)).
- The Build with Gemini XPRIZE gave teams 90 days to build a profitable company; Polyfork won $500,000 ([XPRIZE](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/we-gave-away-2-000-000-to-5-ai-builders-moonshots-live/)).

### Defense needs specialized machines

- Palmer Luckey argues autonomous weapons are ancient and that defense will remain a field of “exotic highly specialized robotic systems” ([Palmer Luckey](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/palmer-luckey-autonomous-weapons-are-ancient-and-why-anduril-wont-buil/)).
- Anduril skips humanoids, but Luckey sees them operating legacy systems such as radar sites ([Palmer Luckey](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2026/09/palmer-luckey-autonomous-weapons-are-ancient-and-why-anduril-wont-buil/)).
