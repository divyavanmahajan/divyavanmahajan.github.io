## Overview

March was shaped by the 2025 Abundance Summit, with stage interviews of Brett Adcock and Palmer Luckey and a news recap with Salim Ismail. Humanoid robots were the month's main thread, appearing in four of five episodes. The other episodes asked what AI means for education, jobs, purpose and communities that are usually left out of technology shifts.

## Main themes

### Humanoid robots reach real work

- Brett Adcock argues that AGI needs a humanoid body to act in the physical world, and that general-purpose robots are “a neural net problem not a control problem”. He puts the addressable market at roughly half of global GDP ([Figure](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/a-humanoid-robot-in-every-home-its-closer-than-you-think-w-brett-adcoc/)).
- Figure shipped its first robot 31 months after a cold start and builds most parts itself because no humanoid supply chain exists. Robots already work at BMW's Spartanburg plant, and Adcock targets $20,000 to $30,000 per robot at volume ([Figure](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/a-humanoid-robot-in-every-home-its-closer-than-you-think-w-brett-adcoc/)).
- In the news recap, Diamandis reports Figure's factory can build 12,000 robots a year, and Ismail says he is revising his scepticism because robots share what they learn ([State of AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/the-state-of-ai-humanoid-robots-ai-copyright-wars-chinas-growing-influ/)).

### Education, purpose and jobs

- In an El Salvador Q&A, Diamandis calls school systems massively broken and says “schools should not fear use of ai”. He sees purpose, not jobs, as the hardest problem AI creates ([Hard Questions](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/our-lives-after-the-ai-revolution-answering-the-hard-questions-ep-155/)).
- Diamandis and Ismail contrast Estonia and Beijing, which are rolling out AI in schools, with US teens at the summit who were told not to use it ([State of AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/the-state-of-ai-humanoid-robots-ai-copyright-wars-chinas-growing-influ/)).
- Citing Ray Dalio, Diamandis worries that cheaper money will go into agents and robots rather than hiring. will.i.am predicts that one lawyer or financier with AI will replace whole firms ([will.i.am and Van Jones](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/an-ai-conversation-for-everyday-people-w-will-i-am-van-jones-ep-154/)).

### AI for overlooked communities

- will.i.am and Van Jones say almost nobody understands AI yet, so everyone starts roughly equal. Jones calls the remaining gap wetware, meaning mindset ([will.i.am and Van Jones](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/an-ai-conversation-for-everyday-people-w-will-i-am-van-jones-ep-154/)).
- will.i.am notes that most of the world's young people live in Africa, which has no GPUs, and says his company FYI is working with Cassava on a GPU cluster. He also argues that people should own their AI and data ([will.i.am and Van Jones](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/an-ai-conversation-for-everyday-people-w-will-i-am-van-jones-ep-154/)).

### Copyright and the China question

- The hosts discuss Sam Altman calling DeepSeek state-controlled and OpenAI and Google asking to train on copyrighted content. Ismail says copyright has been stretched for decades but creators still need compensation, and proposes a Bretton Woods-style global convening on AI ([State of AI](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/the-state-of-ai-humanoid-robots-ai-copyright-wars-chinas-growing-influ/)).

### Defence as a product business

- Palmer Luckey argues cost-plus contracting rewards slow work and expensive parts. He says about 80% of major programmes go to five companies, so Anduril spends its own money on working systems and then sells them ([Palmer Luckey](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/palmer-luckey-on-the-next-wave-of-military-tech-powered-by-ai-ep-158/)).
- Luckey describes taking over the Army's IVAS headset programme from Microsoft. He argues against fully autonomous warfare, saying “we need to avoid Outsourcing responsibility for violence to machines” ([Palmer Luckey](/podcasts/moonshots-diamandis/2025/03/palmer-luckey-on-the-next-wave-of-military-tech-powered-by-ai-ep-158/)).
