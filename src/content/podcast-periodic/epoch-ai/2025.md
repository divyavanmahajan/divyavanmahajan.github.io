## Overview

Epoch After Hours spent its first year on one question: what happens to the economy if AI can substitute for human labour. Explosive growth was central in all five episodes. Epoch researchers debated compute trends, timelines and the 2030s, and two economists, Phil Trammell and Luis Garicano, tested those ideas against growth theory, GDP measurement and European policy.

## Main themes

### Explosive growth from automated labour

- Tamay Besiroglu argues AI removes the labour bottleneck, so output can be reinvested in an accelerating growth cycle. Ege Erdil puts about 50% on a tenfold growth increase if AI can do all remote and physical tasks ([AI in 2030](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/01/ai-in-2030-scaling-bottlenecks-and-explosive-growth/)).
- Matthew Barnett says AI's value will come from ordinary labour across the economy rather than a country of geniuses in a data center. Erdil agrees the scientific gains will come from scale effects ([AGI Timelines](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/03/is-it-3-years-or-3-decades-away-disagreements-on-agi-timelines/)).
- Jaime Sevilla and Yafah Edelman's modal story has AI doing every cognitive task around 2035 with about 10% annual growth. Sevilla notes that going further needs robots, since only about 30% of US work time is remote-only ([Forecasting to 2040](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/09/forecasting-ai-progress-until-2040/)).

### Timelines and what is missing

- Erdil estimates his and Barnett's timelines differ by “maybe a factor of three or something”. He now puts AI able to do any remote job about 30 years out, down from 40 in 2023 ([AGI Timelines](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/03/is-it-3-years-or-3-decades-away-disagreements-on-agi-timelines/)).
- Both trace the gap to agency, common sense and transfer learning. Erdil says progress on agency is nowhere near the pace seen in math, and Barnett invokes Moravec's paradox: the important tasks are the ordinary ones ([AGI Timelines](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/03/is-it-3-years-or-3-decades-away-disagreements-on-agi-timelines/)).
- Edelman gives 2035 for full cognitive automation and Sevilla 2034, though Sevilla's median is up to a decade later than the modal story ([Forecasting to 2040](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/09/forecasting-ai-progress-until-2040/)).

### Compute scaling and its bottlenecks

- Besiroglu says training compute has grown about 4-5x per year since deep learning, and that over half of model performance variation comes from pre-training compute. Erdil adds that algorithms slightly more than halve compute needs each year ([AI in 2030](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/01/ai-in-2030-scaling-bottlenecks-and-explosive-growth/)).
- The January episode judged a 10,000x scale-up feasible by 2030. Sevilla calls a 5 gigawatt data center possible but unprecedented, and argues the data wall is further off than feared ([AI in 2030](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/01/ai-in-2030-scaling-bottlenecks-and-explosive-growth/)).
- By September Edelman expected compute growth to slow to 2.5-3x a year, with runs near 1e29 FLOP still likely by 2030. She would not be surprised if people stop writing code by then; Sevilla expects engineers to keep reviewing it ([Forecasting to 2040](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/09/forecasting-ai-progress-until-2040/)).

### What economics can and cannot tell us

- Phil Trammell questions the Jones growth model, task-based models such as Epoch's GATE, and real GDP as a guide to AGI's impact ([Trammell](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/10/what-does-economics-actually-tell-us-about-agi-phil-trammell/)).
- He calls a rising capital share “the most robust one” of the signs of approaching full automation, but finds Nordhaus's indicators and capital shares still flat ([Trammell](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/10/what-does-economics-actually-tell-us-about-agi-phil-trammell/)).

### Jobs, Europe and macroeconomics

- Luis Garicano says gains hinge on autonomy: “As long as the AI needs your supervision because it makes lots of mistakes, then the bottleneck is the human” ([Garicano](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/12/the-eu-and-the-not-so-simple-macroeconomics-of-ai-luis-garicano/)).
- He warns AI threatens junior jobs and training ladders, citing a consultant who says deep research does a junior researcher's work ([Garicano](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/12/the-eu-and-the-not-so-simple-macroeconomics-of-ai-luis-garicano/)).
- He argues an overregulated EU risks higher interest rates without higher growth, while the US and China capture the hardware layer of the value chain ([Garicano](/podcasts/epoch-ai/2025/12/the-eu-and-the-not-so-simple-macroeconomics-of-ai-luis-garicano/)).
