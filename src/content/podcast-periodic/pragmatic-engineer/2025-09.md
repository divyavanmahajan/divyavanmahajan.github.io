## Overview

September had two long conversations with experienced engineers. Steve McConnell looked back on writing Code Complete and on building a career that adds up, and Charles-Axel Dein described engineering through hypergrowth at Uber and now at CloudKitchens. Both treated AI as useful but limited, with code review becoming more important.

## Main themes

### Software construction and deliberate careers

- McConnell says that before Code Complete there “wasn't a book on the main thing that programmers do, which is software construction”. He planned 250 pages and his first real estimate came to 900 ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).
- He wrote the book inductively, from specifics to general statements, because most programmers are detail-oriented ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).
- McConnell offers his career pyramid as the antidote to lily pad hopping between projects that never add up. He calls going all-in on technology alone the most career-limiting choice ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).
- He says focused personal energy makes up for many deficiencies, citing a core Windows team of under 15 people against IBM's much larger OS/2 staff ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).

### Engineering through hypergrowth

- Dein describes hypergrowth at Uber as productive chaos: weekly onboarding, poor on-call and Friday-evening firefighting. He thinks the era of zero-interest-rate hyperscale startups is probably over ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).
- CloudKitchens tracks time to detection, mitigation and resolution for every incident, with mitigation the most important to Dein. He calls feature flags the simplest stability fix ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).
- He treats hiring as a funnel, works closely with recruiters and pairs interviewers so they shadow, then reverse-shadow ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).
- Dein favours weekly updates that always include ETAs, saying “It's incredible how just sending a weekly update drives a perception of movement.” ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).

### AI as a helper that still needs review

- McConnell sees AI as useful for pseudocode, design alternatives and test-case ideas. He says “I worry about it being subtly off” rather than glaringly wrong ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).
- He argues the happy path was always the easy part, so edge cases and off-by-one errors remain the programmer's job. Orosz adds that code review grows in importance ([Code Complete](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/code-complete-with-steve-mcconnell/)).
- Dein says CloudKitchens' own study found AI coding tools moderately useful, with migrations driven by a shared prompt the best use so far and code still reviewed ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).
- Dein uses agents to multitask around meetings, but says complex work still fails. He found 25 of 30 recent resumes were AI-generated ([Hypergrowth](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/09/hypergrowth-startups-uber-and-cloudkitchens-with-charles-axel-dein/)).
