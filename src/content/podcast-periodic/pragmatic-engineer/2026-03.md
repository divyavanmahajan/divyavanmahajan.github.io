## Overview

March on The Pragmatic Engineer centred on Anthropic. Boris Cherny explained how Claude Code is built and used inside the company, and Steve Yegge described where he thinks agent-driven engineering is heading. Jean Lee's account of WhatsApp offered a counterpoint: a very small team that got far through scope discipline rather than tools.

## Main themes

### Claude Code inside Anthropic

- Boris Cherny says Claude Code began as a terminal chat app and now writes about 80% of Anthropic's code on average. Anthropic released it so that it could study safety in the wild ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).
- Cherny runs five parallel checkouts, starts each agent in plan mode and ships 20-30 pull requests a day. Every pull request gets a first review from Claude in CI, which he says catches about 80% of bugs, before a human pass ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).
- The architecture is deliberately simple. A local vector database for RAG was dropped because indexes drifted; Cherny says “agentic search just outperformed everything”, meaning model-driven glob and grep ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).

### Prototypes and Cowork

- Cherny says the team rarely writes specs and prototypes instead. Cowork came from non-engineers going out of their way to use Claude Code, and was built in about 10 days with Claude Code ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).
- Most of Cowork's complexity is safety: backend classifiers, a shipped virtual machine and operating-system integrations for non-technical users ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).
- Yegge cites the same 10-day Cowork launch as evidence that Anthropic builds many prototypes and ships the best one ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).

### Orchestration and the abstraction ladder

- Yegge argues that what engineers need to know keeps moving up, from assembly to higher layers, and that managing agents is the next rung. He describes eight levels of AI adoption ending in orchestration ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).
- His orchestrator, Gas Town, has a mayor, long-context crew for design and short-lived polecats for well-specified tasks. He says most people should not use it yet and that it needs Opus 4.5 at minimum ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).
- Yegge puts the limit of what agents can handle at roughly half a million to a few million lines, so large monoliths will need to be broken up or rewritten ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).

### AI, jobs and burnout

- Yegge calls AI an augmentation rather than a replacement, yet expects big companies to lose about half their engineers while small teams rival their output ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).
- He describes a vampiric effect: with the easy work gone, people may only get three productive hours a day. How the value is split between engineer and employer becomes the new work-life balance ([Steve Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/from-ides-to-ai-agents-with-steve-yegge/)).
- Cherny expects generalists to gain: methodical, curious people who switch context well, rather than those with strong opinions on code style ([Boris Cherny](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-claude-code-with-boris-cherny/)).

### Small teams without process

- Jean Lee says WhatsApp served 450 million monthly users with under 30 engineers, no code reviews after the first commit, and dogfooding instead of canaries or feature flags ([WhatsApp](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-whatsapp-with-jean-lee/)).
- She credits Jan Koum rejecting about 99% of feature requests, and suggests that some efficiency now credited to AI may simply come from staying lean. She concedes that at 1,000 engineers formal process is needed ([WhatsApp](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/03/building-whatsapp-with-jean-lee/)).
