## Overview

December's three episodes had no shared thread. Grady Booch covered decades of software architecture and gave a sceptical view of LLMs. Notion's mobile engineers explained a four-year move to native code, and Sean Goedecke of GitHub described what it really takes to ship a project in a large company.

## Main themes

### Software architecture as rising abstraction

- Booch says the history of software engineering is one of rising levels of abstraction, with many architectural decisions now built into frameworks and cloud services ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).
- He describes golden ages: first algorithmic decomposition of monoliths, when machines cost more than humans, then distributed systems as a systems engineering problem ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).
- The Booch method decomposed systems into classes and objects rather than algorithms. He now says it overemphasised inheritance ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).
- He recounts founding Rational in 1982 to build an Ada environment. IBM bought it for about $2.7 billion in 2003, outbidding Microsoft ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).

### Scepticism about LLMs

- Booch says LLMs are, by their architecture, “unreliable narrators” that do not reason or understand but produce coherent results ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).
- He argues LLMs are the wrong architecture for AGI, citing their energy needs, and favours neuro-symbolic, embodied systems ([Booch](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/software-architecture-with-grady-booch/)).
- Goedecke is more practical. He uses Copilot and ChatGPT daily and finds them most useful in unfamiliar codebases ([Shipping](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/shipping-projects-at-big-tech-with-sean-goedecke/)).

### Notion goes native, one tab at a time

- Austin Louden says loading the whole web app gave launch times of 1.5 to 2 seconds. The team tracks initial home render, from tapping the icon to visible home items ([Notion](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/notion-going-native-on-ios-and-android/)).
- Karn Saheb says a late-2021 offsite settled on making everything except the editor native, moving incrementally to avoid a multi-year rewrite ([Notion](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/notion-going-native-on-ios-and-android/)).
- The native home tab took about nine months and blocked other mobile work. The full migration took about four years for an 11-engineer team ([Notion](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/notion-going-native-on-ios-and-android/)).
- Builds are cut on Wednesdays and rolled out from 1% on Fridays, behind hundreds of feature flags ([Notion](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/notion-going-native-on-ios-and-android/)).

### Shipping projects and working remotely

- Goedecke says a project ships when management believes it has. Closing every JIRA ticket or deploying to production is not the same thing ([Shipping](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/shipping-projects-at-big-tech-with-sean-goedecke/)).
- With 5 to 25 teams involved, he says “projects ship because one single person has the technical context in their head” ([Shipping](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/shipping-projects-at-big-tech-with-sean-goedecke/)).
- He ranks technical skill above political skill and calls slowness the main project killer ([Shipping](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/shipping-projects-at-big-tech-with-sean-goedecke/)).
- As the only APAC engineer on his team for nine months, he found afternoons free for deep work. He says follow-the-sun handoffs can roughly double speed on bug investigations if the culture is async-first ([Shipping](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2024/12/shipping-projects-at-big-tech-with-sean-goedecke/)).
