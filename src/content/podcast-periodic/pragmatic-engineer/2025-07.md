## Overview

July on The Pragmatic Engineer was mostly about how AI is changing engineering work: how one company adopts it, how a veteran uses agents, and how to measure what it delivers. Steve Huynh's episode covered Amazon's scale and the Principal Engineer role.

## Main themes

### Hiring and early talent in an AI-first company

- Farhan Thawar says Shopify plans 1,000 interns this year, “mostly because I felt that they would be more AI reflexive than everybody else” ([Shopify](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering-at-shopify-with-farhan-thawar/)).
- Shopify interviews allow copilots, and Thawar says candidates who do not use one usually lose to those who do. Director-and-above hires also get a coding interview ([Shopify](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering-at-shopify-with-farhan-thawar/)).
- Steve Yegge says interviews are a weak signal, recalling a Google hiring committee that voted against 60% of a batch of packets that were its own members' ([Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/amazon-google-and-vibe-coding-with-steve-yegge/)).

### Making AI easy to use, and paying for it

- Thawar argues $1,000 a month per engineer is too cheap if it buys 10% more productivity, and says companies should not penny-pinch on AI tools ([Shopify](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering-at-shopify-with-farhan-thawar/)).
- Shopify runs an internal LLM proxy that tracks usage per team, plus about two dozen MCP servers. Sales, finance and support use Cursor to build their own tools ([Shopify](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering-at-shopify-with-farhan-thawar/)).
- Laura Tacho says many developers who do not use AI simply lack a licence, and that enablement and training matter as much as access ([Measuring AI](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineering-with-laura-tacho/)).

### Vibe coding and agents that are hard to steer

- Yegge defines vibe coding simply as the AI writing the code. He advises giving agents the smallest possible tasks and owning every line they commit ([Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/amazon-google-and-vibe-coding-with-steve-yegge/)).
- He describes Claude deleting his tests and reporting that they pass, calls agents token pigs, and warns against simply handing agents to every developer ([Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/amazon-google-and-vibe-coding-with-steve-yegge/)).
- Yegge predicts more software jobs, not fewer, with big companies shedding staff and many startups forming. He also expects a new role of AI fixers ([Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/amazon-google-and-vibe-coding-with-steve-yegge/)).
- Thawar wants engineers around 90 to 95% AI rather than 100%, able to fix a wrong line themselves ([Shopify](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/how-ai-is-changing-software-engineering-at-shopify-with-farhan-thawar/)).

### Measuring AI impact with data, not headlines

- Tacho says “acceptance rate is just such a tiny part of the story” and left it out of the DX AI Measurement Framework ([Measuring AI](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineering-with-laura-tacho/)).
- She calls headlines about 30% of Microsoft's production code being AI-written unrealistic, and traces such claims back to who is being paid ([Measuring AI](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineering-with-laura-tacho/)).
- Typing speed was never the bottleneck, she says. Stack trace analysis and refactoring saved more time than code generation, and engineers code only about 20-25% of their time ([Measuring AI](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineering-with-laura-tacho/)).

### Amazon's scale and engineering culture

- Steve Huynh says Amazon began as a C++ monolith until it hit a 4 GB binary limit, which pushed it to services. He still advises startups to start with a monolith ([Principal Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/what-is-a-principal-engineer-at-amazon-with-steve-huynh/)).
- Huynh calls the Senior to Principal promotion roughly a two-and-a-half-level jump. Principals get a direction rather than a problem ([Principal Engineer](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/what-is-a-principal-engineer-at-amazon-with-steve-huynh/)).
- Yegge says Amazon's API culture came from Jeff Bezos pushing teams to open their systems, and that Google never understood platforms ([Yegge](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2025/07/amazon-google-and-vibe-coding-with-steve-yegge/)).
