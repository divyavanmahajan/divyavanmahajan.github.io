## Overview

April on The Pragmatic Engineer was split between two views of coding agents: DHH's enthusiasm for working agent-first, and the Pi team's warnings about control and code quality. The other two episodes went back to fundamentals, with Martin Kleppmann on data systems in the cloud and Thuan Pham on how Uber survived hypergrowth.

## Main themes

### Working agent-first

- DHH says his views on AI did not change, the tools did. Opus 4.5, released in late November, produced code he wanted to merge with little or no alteration, so he now starts every project with agents ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).
- He uses OpenCode as his main harness and some Claude Code, and compares running many agents to stepping into a mech suit rather than managing a team. He says the cost of exploring a hunch has dropped a thousandfold ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).
- After an OpenClaw agent signed itself up for HEY and Fizzy, 37signals started building CLIs for all its products. DHH sees this as proof of the Unix idea of small tools joined by pipes ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).
- Thuan Pham says Faire uses orchestrated swarm coding and its best engineers doubled their output, but adding features to large old codebases is still unsolved ([Uber](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/scaling-uber-with-thuan-pham-ubers-first-cto/)).

### Control, friction and code quality

- Mario Zechner built Pi after Claude Code grew unpredictable and injected hidden context. Pi ships only read, write, edit and bash tools, users extend it by asking it to modify itself, and OpenClaw builds on it ([Pi](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying-software-so-fascinating/)).
- Armin Ronacher says that in over half of about 30 companies he spoke to, agent use exploded after Christmas and quality dropped. Zechner tells companies that claim agents write all their code: “Quality is garbage. We feel it in our bones when we use your product.” ([Pi](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying-software-so-fascinating/)).
- Zechner warns that good agent output lulls engineers into automation bias, and that agents feel no pain from bad code, so they do not fix its causes. Ronacher argues that friction such as approvals is often deliberate ([Pi](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/building-pi-and-what-makes-self-modifying-software-so-fascinating/)).

### Who gains, and the jobs question

- DHH says senior engineers gain most because they can judge whether agent output is fit for production ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).
- He bets “we've seen peak programmer” and expects price pressure wherever software is a cost centre, even if more software gets built ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).
- At 37signals, about 10 designers act as product managers and implementers in a 60-person company, and DHH expects agents to push the industry towards that model ([DHH](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/dhhs-new-way-of-writing-code/)).

### Data systems and scaling fundamentals

- Kleppmann says the second edition of his book replaces the assumption of machines with local disks with databases built on object stores. He adds that higher-level cloud services are fine for business logic, but “Somebody's got to implement the cloud services.” ([Kleppmann](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/designing-data-intensive-applications-with-martin-kleppmann/)).
- He treats multi-region and multi-cloud as business trade-offs between risk and cost, and expects LLMs to make formal verification practical ([Kleppmann](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/designing-data-intensive-applications-with-martin-kleppmann/)).
- Pham describes Uber's rewrites, microservices and program/platform team split as survival tactics: find the next thing that will break and fix it just enough ([Uber](/podcasts/pragmatic-engineer/2026/04/scaling-uber-with-thuan-pham-ubers-first-cto/)).
