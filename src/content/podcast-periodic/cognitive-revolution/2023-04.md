## Overview

April was dominated by the Future of Life Institute letter calling for a six-month pause on training systems beyond GPT-4. Two episodes debated it head on, and GPT-4 ran through six of the eight. The rest of the month was practical: how to prompt and break models, what GPT-4 means for jobs, and what it takes to ship agents and LLM products.

## Main themes

### The pause letter and existential risk

- Flo Crivello thinks the danger is real but says a pause will not help, because “China is not going to pause it.” Anton Troynikov doubts the danger is well argued ([Open letter debate](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/ai-open-letter-debate-pausers-what-scares-us-w-anton-troynikov-flo-cri/)).
- Both say they could accept a pause backed by a concrete safety roadmap. Nathan Labenz wants the few leaders able to scale further to choose to wait, not to be regulated into it ([Open letter debate](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/ai-open-letter-debate-pausers-what-scares-us-w-anton-troynikov-flo-cri/)).
- Jaan Tallinn estimates a 1 to 50% risk of catastrophe per 10x jump in training compute. He reads the reported Anthropic fundraising plan as evidence of a dangerous race among US labs ([Jaan Tallinn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/pausing-the-ai-revolution-with-technologist-jaan-tallinn/)).
- Tallinn favours compute governance and says a pause would buy time for interpretability work and evals ([Jaan Tallinn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/pausing-the-ai-revolution-with-technologist-jaan-tallinn/)).

### GPT-4 and the economy

- Labenz notes GPT-4 went from the 10th to the 90th percentile on the bar exam in one generation, yet is “not crushing human genius in anything” ([Open letter debate](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/ai-open-letter-debate-pausers-what-scares-us-w-anton-troynikov-flo-cri/)).
- He expects GPT-4 to unbundle jobs into tasks and drive a new Taylorism of knowledge work, with cost reductions of 10-to-1 to 100-to-1 ([Economic Transformation](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/will-gpt-4-cause-economic-transformation/)).
- Labenz argues this time is different because there is no obvious place to shift workers. Erik Torenberg predicts a split between regulated and unregulated sectors ([Economic Transformation](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/will-gpt-4-cause-economic-transformation/)).

### Prompting, RLHF and jailbreaks

- Riley Goodside traces models from pretraining to instruction tuning to RLHF, and says RLHF makes models more capable as well as safer ([Riley Goodside](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-art-of-prompting-chatgpt-with-riley-goodside/)).
- Goodside describes prompting as constraining the generation space to documents that contain correct answers. A page of instructions now replaces a custom dataset ([Riley Goodside](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-art-of-prompting-chatgpt-with-riley-goodside/)).
- Alex Albert argues publishing jailbreaks exposes how shallow safeguards are. He criticises OpenAI's bug bounty for leaving jailbreaks out of scope ([Alex Albert](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/why-jailbreaking-chatgpt-is-a-public-service-with-alex-albert-of-the-p/)).
- Ought's founders argue for reasoning built from small, human-checkable steps rather than end-to-end optimisation, and apply it in Elicit ([Ought](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-ai-reasoning-revolution-with-ought-s-jungwon-byun-and-andreas-stuh/)).

### Agents and production LLMs

- Matt Welsh says models lack the appendages into company systems. He expects enterprises to build their own agents and stresses knowing where data flows ([Fixie.ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/how-ai-agents-will-change-how-we-work-with-matt-welsh-of-fixie-ai/)).
- Raza Habib says reliable products depend on evaluation, user feedback and fast iteration. He recommends prompt engineering first, then fine-tuning, and RLHF only for large problems ([Humanloop](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-great-ai-implementation-with-raza-habib-of-humanloop/)).
- Habib notes chained agent steps compound errors, and customers report agents working about 80% of the time, not enough for production ([Humanloop](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/04/the-great-ai-implementation-with-raza-habib-of-humanloop/)).
