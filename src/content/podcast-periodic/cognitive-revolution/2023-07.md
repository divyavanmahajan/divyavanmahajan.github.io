## Overview

July was the month of Nathan Labenz's three-part AI Scouting Report, which stepped back from daily news to cover fundamentals, implementation trends and impact. AI safety was the strongest thread, from Zvi Mowshowitz's map of the debate to Arthur Conmy's work on automating interpretability. Guests also covered browser agents, drone autonomy, AI interface design and the GPU shortage.

## Main themes

### The AI safety debate

- Zvi Mowshowitz calls today's LLMs almost entirely beneficial, but argues that superhuman optimisers given maximalist goals will seek power and resources by default. He says many people wrongly treat alignment as a yes-or-no property ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-safety-debates-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)).
- He thinks the factions share more than their fights suggest, and that existential and near-term concerns complement each other. On capacity he says “There are only a few hundred alignment researchers on the planet. This is orders of magnitude wrong” ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-safety-debates-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)).
- In Part 3 of his report Labenz calls OpenAI's alignment plan thin, though the lab pledged 20% of its compute to it. He argues for deploying AI tools widely now while being very careful about further scaling ([Scouting Report 3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/ai-scouting-report-part-3-impact-fallout-and-outlook/)).

### Capabilities, moats and jobs

- Labenz says GPT-4 doubles the human success rate on hard LeetCode problems with five attempts and scores 86% on MMLU against 32% for the best truly open model ([Scouting Report 3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/ai-scouting-report-part-3-impact-fallout-and-outlook/)).
- He argues the leaders' advantages hold: “So I think these moats are very real.” He notes OpenAI cut prices about 98% in a year and Databricks bought MosaicML ([Scouting Report 3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/ai-scouting-report-part-3-impact-fallout-and-outlook/)).
- Citing an OpenAI and University of Pennsylvania paper, he says the most qualified jobs are the most exposed. Hayk Martiros expects inspection workers to become managers of drone fleets ([Scouting Report 3](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/ai-scouting-report-part-3-impact-fallout-and-outlook/), [Skydio](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-autonomy-revolution-with-hayk-martiros-of-skydio/)).
- Parts 1 and 2 cover fundamentals such as data, compute, scaling and emergence, then practical techniques for using models like GPT-4 ([Scouting Report 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/ai-scouting-report-part-1-of-3-fundamentals/), [Scouting Report 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/e46-ai-scouting-report-part-2-of-3-implementation-trends-bonus-youtube/)).

### Agents and the interfaces around them

- Div Garg says most agents are early and the main problem is reliability across repeated runs. MultiOn uses a GPT-4 planner inside a Chrome extension that lets the user step in, then turns those corrections into training data ([MultiOn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-copilot-revolution-with-div-garg-of-multi-on/)).
- Linus Lee of Notion AI argues good AI interfaces add the right constraints around flexible models. He frames agent design as trust versus control ([Linus Lee](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/e41-bonus-episode-latent-space-building-the-ai-x-ux-scenius-with-linus/)).

### GPUs and compute governance

- Ronen Dar says GPU demand after ChatGPT grew faster than Nvidia foresaw. He notes “People say that NVIDIA has 87% of the market” and credits its software ecosystem, starting with CUDA ([Run:ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/gpumaxing-with-dr-ronen-dar-of-run-ai/)).
- Mowshowitz argues the only control that does not badly impinge on liberty targets chips, large training runs and frontier models. Dar doubts hardware controls can govern AI once capable open models exist ([Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-safety-debates-with-zvi-mowshowitz/), [Run:ai](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/gpumaxing-with-dr-ronen-dar-of-run-ai/)).

### Automating interpretability

- Arthur Conmy describes ACDC, which automates the most laborious step of circuit discovery: the intervention experiments. It keeps only the edges whose removal hurts the task metric ([Arthur Conmy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/demystifying-llms-with-mechanistic-interpretability-researcher-arthur/)).
- He says models often solve tasks unlike humans, and that ACDC cannot yet scale much beyond GPT-2 small. Mowshowitz calls interpretability dual-use but worth funding ([Arthur Conmy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/demystifying-llms-with-mechanistic-interpretability-researcher-arthur/), [Zvi Mowshowitz](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/07/the-ai-safety-debates-with-zvi-mowshowitz/)).
