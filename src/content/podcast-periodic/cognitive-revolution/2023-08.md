## Overview

August was about builders. Two Replit guests made the case for AI-assisted software development, and Khan Academy and Gamma explained how they shipped products on OpenAI models. Around them, Nathan Labenz and Zvi Mowshowitz weighed Meta's open release of Llama 2, OpenAI's new fine-tuning offer and Hugging Face's valuation, while Google's guests covered PaLM 2 and a generalist medical model.

## Main themes

### Replit and the future of coding

- Tyler Angert argues that code is the most general medium for AI to build tools. He says assistants roughly double speed today, but 100x gains will need many agents working in parallel ([Tyler Angert](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/e50-the-future-of-software-development-with-tyler-angert-of-replit/)).
- Michele Catasta says “I would say the 2x has been already kind of accomplished by code completion.” He thinks 10x is reachable in about a year through better debugging and agent-like behaviour ([Michele Catasta](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/replit-s-vp-of-ai-michele-catasta-on-artificial-developer-intelligence/)).
- Catasta frames Replit's goal as artificial developer intelligence and points to its keystroke-level telemetry and a custom 3B code model built with MosaicML. Labenz pressed Angert on safety after getting Generate to write denial-of-service code ([Michele Catasta](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/replit-s-vp-of-ai-michele-catasta-on-artificial-developer-intelligence/), [Tyler Angert](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/e50-the-future-of-software-development-with-tyler-angert-of-replit/)).

### Llama 2, open weights and the live players

- Labenz says Llama 2 is roughly on par with GPT-3.5, which no other open model had matched. Mowshowitz says “Creating frontier models and open sourcing them is the worst thing you can do in the world.” ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/openai-anthropic-and-meta-analyzing-the-ai-frontier-with-zvi/)).
- Mowshowitz credits Anthropic's safety culture but says it turned a two-horse race into three. His live-player list becomes tier 1 in Labenz's updated scouting report, with Stability, Replit and Character in tier 2 ([Zvi](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/openai-anthropic-and-meta-analyzing-the-ai-frontier-with-zvi/), [Scouting Report update](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/advancements-in-ai-updating-the-scouting-report-task-automation-and-go/)).
- Labenz and Erik Torenberg argue Hugging Face owns AI's discovery layer, but its open-source stance limits lock-in. They see a strategic sale as the likeliest outcome ([Hugging Face](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/how-hugging-face-raised-235m/)).

### Fine-tuning and model moats

- Labenz says GPT-3.5 Turbo fine-tuning costs roughly 90% less than the old option and beats self-hosting a fine-tuned Llama 2 for most teams. He crossed that task off his Waymark list ([GPT-3.5 Turbo](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/e56-openai-s-gpt3-5-turbo-and-the-state-of-ai-in-coding-healthcare-and/)).
- Jon Noronha says few-shot prompting beat fine-tuning while Gamma had little data, and expects that to change now it has scale ([Gamma](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/the-presentation-revolution-with-jon-noronha-co-founder-of-gamma/)).

### GPT-4 products in education and work

- Shawn Jansepar says GPT-4 was the first model that stayed Socratic instead of handing out answers, so Khanmigo uses it for all tutoring. He stresses “our intent is not to replace teachers by any stretch of the imagination.” ([Khan Academy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/the-ai-revolution-in-education-with-shawn-jansepar-director-of-enginee/)).
- Noronha explains how Gamma's AI drafts began as an onboarding fix and became the core product, with HTML as the output format models handle best ([Gamma](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/the-presentation-revolution-with-jon-noronha-co-founder-of-gamma/)).
- Labenz says routine, high-volume tasks with an explicit rubric are the best automation targets, and that defining good output is most of the work ([Scouting Report update](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/advancements-in-ai-updating-the-scouting-report-task-automation-and-go/)).

### Google's frontier and medical models

- Bailey describes product-managing PaLM 2, now in more than 38 products, and warns that apparent reasoning often breaks under small changes ([PaLM 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/google-s-palm-2-with-paige-bailey/)).
- Vivek Natarajan and Tao Tu present Med-PaLM M as a proof of concept: one set of weights across many medical tasks. Natarajan says medical-device rules assume single-task systems and do not fit generalist models ([Med-PaLM](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/08/google-s-multimodal-med-palm-with-vivek-natarajan-and-tao-tu/)).
