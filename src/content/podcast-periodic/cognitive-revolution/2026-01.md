## Overview

January on The Cognitive Revolution opened with two solo AMAs in which Nathan Labenz weighed the holiday excitement around Claude Code, the AI bubble debate and the future of work. Guests carried the jobs question further, from Luke Drago's intelligence curse to Daniel Miessler's personal AI. Safety research, oversight of coding agents, enterprise data and the legal profession filled out the month.

## Main themes

### Claude Code, Opus 4.5 and the frontier labs

- Labenz argues that “in software, I think you could make the case, certainly, that Opus 4.5 is software AGI and/or coding AGI”, but not general AGI. He did not feel a step change himself ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ama-part-1-is-claude-code-agi-are-we-in-a-bubble-plus-live-player-anal/)).
- He built three family apps with Claude Code on Replit in a few days, yet a fresh model given the whole exported codebase fixed a bug that agentic search missed ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ama-part-1-is-claude-code-agi-are-we-in-a-bubble-plus-live-player-anal/)).
- In his live-player ranking Google DeepMind comes first. OpenAI no longer has an obvious lead and seems to pursue a too-big-to-fail financing strategy, while Anthropic has the best safety work and strong talent retention ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ama-part-1-is-claude-code-agi-are-we-in-a-bubble-plus-live-player-anal/)).

### Jobs, ownership and UBI

- Miessler says that with AI, labour “gets massively diminished. And so then ownership matters a lot more”. He expects pressure for UBI around 2028 or 2029 ([PAI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/pioneering-pai-how-daniel-miessler-s-personal-ai-infrastructure-activa/)).
- Labenz thinks disruption is arriving sooner than a 3-to-10-year framing suggests. He reads recent UBI studies as encouraging and calls the jobs-give-meaning argument mostly cope ([AMA Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ama-part-2-is-fine-tuning-dead-how-am-i-preparing-for-agi-are-we-heade/)).
- Kevin Frazier and Alan Rozenshtein see frontier models out-reasoning the median lawyer, but billable hours slow adoption. Rozenshtein worries about losing the apprenticeship path for juniors ([AI & The Law](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ai-the-law-changing-practice-claude-constitution-new-rights-w-kevin-al/)).

### Power concentration and the intelligence curse

- Luke Drago compares AI that replaces workers to the resource curse: states and firms stop investing in people whose economic value disappears ([Intelligence Curse](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/confronting-the-intelligence-curse-w-luke-drago-of-workshop-labs-from/)).
- He warns that “Aligned super intelligence in the hands of one person makes that person a de facto dictator unless they choose not to be.” His remedies include open-weights models and user-owned data ([Intelligence Curse](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/confronting-the-intelligence-curse-w-luke-drago-of-workshop-labs-from/)).
- Rozenshtein warns of AI giving the president fine-grained control of the bureaucracy ([AI & The Law](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ai-the-law-changing-practice-claude-constitution-new-rights-w-kevin-al/)).

### Safety research and keeping agents in check

- Ryan Kidd of MATS argues for a broad portfolio of safety bets and weights concern toward earlier AGI timelines. He sees models getting better at sycophancy and deception, though without sustained coherent deception so far ([MATS](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/building-scaling-the-ai-safety-research-community-with-ryan-kidd-of-ma/)).
- Labenz says fine-tuning is on the decline and can generalise in harmful ways, citing the emergent misalignment paper ([AMA Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/ama-part-2-is-fine-tuning-dead-how-am-i-preparing-for-agi-are-we-heade/)).
- Dominic Williams proposes forcing different models to reach consensus before code ships, and agrees collusion between models is an open problem ([Internet Computer](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/the-internet-computer-caffeine-ai-ceo-dominic-williams-on-unstoppable/)).

### Harnesses and data around the model

- Baris Gultekin says reasoning models have made text-to-SQL reliable enough for business users, and that agents inheriting user permissions keep governance intact ([Snowflake](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/bringing-ai-to-data-agent-design-text-2-sql-rag-more-w-snowflake-vp-of/)).
- Daniel Miessler builds his PAI framework on Claude Code and credits its harness, not the model ([PAI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/01/pioneering-pai-how-daniel-miessler-s-personal-ai-infrastructure-activa/)).
