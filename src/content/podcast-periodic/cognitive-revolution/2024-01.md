## Overview

January on The Cognitive Revolution was spread across applied AI: web agents, enterprise data, contact centres, image generation and AI-run lab experiments. Two threads ran through several episodes: what makes agents reliable and safe, and how copyright law will treat generative models. Rebroadcasts with Shane Legg and Nita Farahany added AGI timelines and the governance of brain data.

## Main themes

### Agents, and keeping them safe

- Div Garg says GPT-4 was overhyped for agents. MultiOn handles short single-site tasks, keeps prompts under 5,000 tokens and gives the model memory operations ([MultiOn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/the-quest-for-autonomous-web-agents-with-div-garg-cofounder-and-ceo-of/)).
- Sander Schulhoff's HackAPrompt results show instructions in a prompt cannot stop hijacking. He says “Prompt-based defenses do not work, period” and recommends restricting permissions and fine-tuning ([Schulhoff](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/ignore-previous-instructions-listen-to-this-interview-sander-schulhoff/)).
- Gabe Gomes describes Coscientist, a GPT-4 planner directing search, code execution and cloud-lab hardware to run real chemistry experiments. He withheld the riskiest parts of the system ([Coscientist](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/breaking-boundaries-ai-coscientist-to-accelerate-science-research-with/)).
- Juan Sequeda reports GPT-4 answered enterprise questions three times more accurately when querying a knowledge graph than SQL schemas alone, and calls naive RAG the simplest kind of agent ([data.world](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/unlocking-enterprise-data-with-knowledge-graphs-juan-sequeda-head-of-a/)).

### Copyright and the image stack

- Cecilia Ziniti calls verbatim regurgitation the New York Times' strongest claim against OpenAI, but says training under fair use is the real fight. She expects settlements and licensing deals rather than destroyed models ([NYT vs OpenAI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/nytimes-vs-openai-generative-ai-and-the-law-with-cecilia-ziniti-founde/)).
- She predicts self-service tools for rights holders and watermarking startups bought by a major player to become a marketplace ([NYT vs OpenAI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/nytimes-vs-openai-generative-ai-and-the-law-with-cecilia-ziniti-founde/)).
- Suhail Doshi says image generation is still near a GPT-2 level of usefulness and that community fixes for hands and eyes are “all just patches”. Playground favours rules on commercial use over rules on training data ([Playground](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/the-pixel-revolution-part-2-with-suhail-doshi-founder-of-playground-ai/)).

### AGI timelines and alignment

- Shane Legg keeps a 50% chance of AGI by 2028 and sees memory, factuality and search as research problems rather than walls ([Shane Legg](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/2028-agi-new-architectures-and-aligning-superhuman-models-with-shane-l/)).
- Legg argues containment is not a winning strategy. He wants System 2 reasoning over a world model and explicit ethics that humans can audit ([Shane Legg](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/2028-agi-new-architectures-and-aligning-superhuman-models-with-shane-l/)).

### AI employees in business communications

- Dan O'Connell explains why Dialpad owns its stack, training its own language model on 5 billion minutes of calls, and ranks latency above capacity and cost ([Dialpad](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/dialpad-s-chief-strategy-officer-dan-o-connell-on-ai-powered-business/)).
- Labenz expects AI employees within a few years; O'Connell agrees on support chatbots but doubts indistinguishable voice agents in 2024. Garg expects agents to start replacing some professional help from 2025 ([Dialpad](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/dialpad-s-chief-strategy-officer-dan-o-connell-on-ai-powered-business/), [MultiOn](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/the-quest-for-autonomous-web-agents-with-div-garg-cofounder-and-ceo-of/)).

### Neurotechnology and cognitive liberty

- Nita Farahany argues brain-reading technology is further along than people realise and that generative AI is speeding up decoding. She calls for a right to cognitive liberty and business models that keep brain data on the device ([Farahany](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/cutting-edge-neurotechnology-with-nita-farahany-in-conversation-with-l/)).
- She points to workplace fatigue monitoring already sold to thousands of companies, and says the US lags on governance ([Farahany](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/01/cutting-edge-neurotechnology-with-nita-farahany-in-conversation-with-l/)).
