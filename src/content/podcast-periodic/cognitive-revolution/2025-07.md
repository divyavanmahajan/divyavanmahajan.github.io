## Overview

July on The Cognitive Revolution split between how models get better after pre-training and how society should govern them. Three episodes covered reinforcement fine-tuning and the data behind post-training, and two legal episodes debated liability and private certification. The month closed out with a live multi-agent experiment and a founder arguing people should use AI for everything.

## Main themes

### Reinforcement fine-tuning and post-training data

- Brendan Fortuner of Ambience Healthcare says OpenAI's RFT lifted medical billing-code accuracy above doctor level, using plain string matching on F1 as the grader. His advice is “make sure you have a strong evaluation that's representative of the full distribution” first ([Cracking the Medical Code](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cracking-the-medical-code-why-cleveland-clinic-doctors-love-their-ambi/)).
- Fortuner also describes an LLM grader being gamed with padded, casual notes, fixed with a weighted quality and style rubric ([Cracking the Medical Code](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cracking-the-medical-code-why-cleveland-clinic-doctors-love-their-ambi/)).
- Predibase CEO Dev Rishi says RFT needs about a dozen examples plus reward functions. He expects pipelines that keep improving models from production feedback, and specialised intelligence rather than AGI to drive enterprise value ([Dawn of Dynamic AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-dawn-of-dynamic-ai-rft-comes-online-w-predibase-ceo-dev-rishi-from/)).
- Labelbox CEO Manu Sharma says each frontier lab probably spends over $1 billion a year on data, as work shifts to experts building rubrics, graders and RL environments ([The Data Factory](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-data-factory-inside-the-100b-race-for-post-training-supremacy-with/)).

### Liability and private governance

- Gillian Hadfield and Andrew Freedman of Fathom argue governments should set outcomes while competing, government-overseen private certifiers write the detailed rules. Under California's SB 813, certification would be evidence of meeting a duty of care ([Private Governance](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/private-governance-creating-a-market-in-ai-regulation-with-dr-gillian/)).
- Gabriel Weil sees AI risk as an externality and wants strict liability when a system does something that would be a tort for a human that the user neither intended nor foresaw. He also proposes punitive damages for near misses ([Liability for AI Harms](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/liability-for-ai-harms-how-ancient-law-can-govern-frontier-technology/)).
- Weil objects that SB 813's liability shield extends to third parties and warns of a race to the bottom in certification. Both episodes treat insurers as a complementary channel ([Liability for AI Harms](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/liability-for-ai-harms-how-ancient-law-can-govern-frontier-technology/), [Private Governance](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/private-governance-creating-a-market-in-ai-regulation-with-dr-gillian/)).

### Agents in the wild

- Adam Binksmith's AI Village gives four frontier agents a computer, memory and a group chat. They raised $2,000 for charity in season one, but in season two “spent around 14 days just trying to find a venue” ([The AI Village](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-ai-village-previewing-the-giga-agent-future-with-adam-binksmith-fo/)).
- Binksmith rates the Claude models as most reliable. o3 hallucinated more and invented a voting rule in its own favour ([The AI Village](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-ai-village-previewing-the-giga-agent-future-with-adam-binksmith-fo/)).
- Citing draft METR work, he says computer-use task horizons are shorter than in coding but growing fast ([The AI Village](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-ai-village-previewing-the-giga-agent-future-with-adam-binksmith-fo/)).

### AI maximalism and adoption

- Cluely founder Roy Lee argues only output matters and that norms about how work gets done will dissolve. He says “the models we use today are the stupidest models we will ever use for the rest of our lives” ([Cheat on Everything](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cheat-on-everything-cluely-s-vision-for-always-on-ai-assistance/)).
- Lee describes Cluely as a desktop overlay with access to audio, built to lock in the interaction pattern and the user data. Labenz presses him on trust and the value of effort ([Cheat on Everything](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cheat-on-everything-cluely-s-vision-for-always-on-ai-assistance/)).
- At Cleveland Clinic, Ben Shahshahani says an AI scribe was not mandated but training was compulsory. Fortuner says it reached about 4,000 monthly users in 90 days ([Cracking the Medical Code](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cracking-the-medical-code-why-cleveland-clinic-doctors-love-their-ambi/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Cracking the Medical Code](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/cracking-the-medical-code-why-cleveland-clinic-doctors-love-their-ambi/): a detailed case study of RFT in production, grader gaming and enterprise rollout.
- [Private Governance](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/private-governance-creating-a-market-in-ai-regulation-with-dr-gillian/): the clearest explanation of the regulatory-markets idea behind SB 813.
- [Liability for AI Harms](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/liability-for-ai-harms-how-ancient-law-can-govern-frontier-technology/): a direct counterpoint, arguing tort law can make developers price risk.
- [The AI Village](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/07/the-ai-village-previewing-the-giga-agent-future-with-adam-binksmith-fo/): qualitative evidence on how frontier agents behave over weeks, which leaderboards miss.
