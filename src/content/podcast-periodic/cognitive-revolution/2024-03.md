## Overview

March on The Cognitive Revolution centred on GPT-4: how builders turn it into reliable products for lawyers and developers, and how easily its safety training can be undone. Around that, Nathan Labenz covered a large survey of AI researchers on timelines and risk, a search engine designed for AI consumers, and the first 90 days of Mamba research.

## Main themes

### GPT-4 as the product foundation

- Pablo Arredondo says Casetext pivoted the whole company within 12 hours of seeing a GPT-4 demo, and that for law the choice has been GPT-4 or nothing. The model's lead meant “the difference between we can put this in lawyers' hands and we can't” ([CoCounsel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/ai-and-the-practice-of-law-from-casetext-to-cocounsel-with-pablo-arred/)).
- Arredondo describes CoCounsel as heavily tested skills built on GPT-4 without fine-tuning, priced at about $200 a month, which solo lawyers compare with hiring a paralegal. He thinks existing bar rules already cover most AI misuse ([CoCounsel](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/ai-and-the-practice-of-law-from-casetext-to-cocounsel-with-pablo-arred/)).
- Itamar Friedman argues that code integrity (testing, review and checking code against a spec) matters more than code generation, because writing code takes well under 30% of developer time ([Codium AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/flow-engineering-and-code-integrity-at-scale-with-itamar-friedman-ceo/)).
- His AlphaCodium research uses about 100 model calls per problem in a structured flow of steps, solving roughly 30% of CodeContests test problems. He says prompt engineering shrank to a small share of the work ([Codium AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/flow-engineering-and-code-integrity-at-scale-with-itamar-friedman-ceo/)).

### Exploitable by default

- Adam Gleave of FAR AI reports that fine-tuning GPT-4 on about 100 harmless examples or public domain books erodes its safety training, which he calls accidental jailbreaking ([Exploitable by Default](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/exploitable-by-default-vulnerabilities-in-gpt-4-apis-superhuman-go-ais/)).
- A handful of planted examples produced politically biased models or leaked email addresses, and the Assistants API could be hijacked through function calls or uploaded documents ([Exploitable by Default](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/exploitable-by-default-vulnerabilities-in-gpt-4-apis-superhuman-go-ais/)).
- An adversary with under 10% of KataGo's training compute beat the superhuman Go engine in over 90% of games. Gleave estimates that “capabilities are improving an order of magnitude faster than robustness” ([Exploitable by Default](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/exploitable-by-default-vulnerabilities-in-gpt-4-apis-superhuman-go-ais/)).

### What 2,700 researchers expect

- Katja Grace's AI Impacts survey gives high-level machine intelligence a 10% chance by 2027 and 50% by 2047, down from 2060 a year earlier ([AI Impacts survey](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/surveying-2-700-ai-researchers-on-the-industry-s-future-with-katja-gra/)).
- For 35 of 39 narrow tasks respondents gave a 50% chance within 10 years; maths results were furthest out ([AI Impacts survey](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/surveying-2-700-ai-researchers-on-the-industry-s-future-with-katja-gra/)).
- Median extinction-risk answers were 5% to 10%. Labenz reads the result as radical uncertainty; Grace sees a consensus that very bad outcomes deserve attention ([AI Impacts survey](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/surveying-2-700-ai-researchers-on-the-industry-s-future-with-katja-gra/)).

### New architectures for search and sequences

- Will Bryk says AIs write long queries, read 100 results at once and want full page content, so Exa predicts which documents people link to instead of matching keywords ([Exa](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/the-example-engine-how-exa-is-creating-the-ai-librarian-for-the-web-wi/)).
- Jason Meaux and Labenz review Mamba papers that turn images, video and graphs into sequences scanned in several directions. Labenz warns that an unbounded state will degrade without a way to clean it ([Mamba-Palooza](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/mamba-palooza-90-days-of-mamba-inspired-research-with-jason-meaux-part/)).
- Tim Reutemann's novel imagines AI-assisted liquid democracy, which he says stays humane only with locally controlled identity and data ([Liquid Reign](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2024/03/envisioning-a-positive-ai-future-with-tim-reutemann-author-of-liquid-r/)).
