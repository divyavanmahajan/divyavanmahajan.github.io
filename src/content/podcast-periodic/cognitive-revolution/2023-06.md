## Overview

June opened with Nathan Labenz asking where the moats in AI are, and much of the month returned to that question from different sides: OpenAI's lead, open models, cheap custom training and venture returns. Regulation came up in four episodes. The rest was research: tiny models, interpretable networks, tokenizer-free transformers and medical imaging work built on pretrained models.

## Main themes

### OpenAI's moats and the open-source gap

- Labenz expects inference to split like the cloud market, with a few leaders holding about half. He puts OpenAI 18 months to 2 years ahead of open source and lists nine moats, including the feedback loop: “nobody has the volume of LLM usage that OpenAI has, that ChatGPT has” ([Moats](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/where-are-the-moats-in-ai-with-nathan-labenz-and-erik-torenberg/)).
- He says Athena starts every new automation with GPT-4, and rates network effects as the weakest moat because switching to Claude takes a few lines of code. He expects the open-closed gap to go back and forth ([Moats](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/where-are-the-moats-in-ai-with-nathan-labenz-and-erik-torenberg/)).
- Nathaniel Whittemore covers OpenAI's process-supervision research, which rewards each reasoning step and improved maths results while making reasoning easier to inspect. Sam Altman reportedly told developers OpenAI is heavily GPU-limited ([AI Breakdown](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/e32-bonus-episode-the-ai-breakdown-can-openai-s-new-gpt-training-model/)).

### Own the model, or invest in the incumbents

- Jonathan Frankle and Abhi Venigalla argue that proprietary data should be the moat again. Venigalla says Mosaic is “making ML training boring again”, and Frankle calls GPT-4 versus your own model a false choice ([MosaicML](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/your-model-your-weights-with-mosaicml-s-abhi-venigalla-and-jonathan-fr/)).
- Sam Lessin calls generative AI a sustaining innovation that mostly helps incumbents with data and distribution. Emad Mostaque points to regulated sectors and the global South, where Stability builds national models with local partners ([Investing debate](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/stability-ai-s-emad-mostaque-and-slow-ventures-sam-lessin-debate-inves/)).

### Regulating the giants

- Joshua Browder wants AI banned in debt collection and sentencing, and liability placed on large platforms, as robocall rules did with carriers. On startups he says “No one should have to hire a lawyer or a lobbyist to innovate” ([DoNotPay](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-consumer-rights-revolution-with-joshua-browder-of-donotpay/)).
- Browder argues consumers deserve AI because companies already use it against them. He says a GPT-4 bot negotiating bills pushes back harder than GPT-3 did ([DoNotPay](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-consumer-rights-revolution-with-joshua-browder-of-donotpay/)).

### Small models and readable networks

- Ronen Eldan and Yuanzhi Li show that models of 1 to 33 million parameters trained on TinyStories, about 1.5 million GPT-4-written stories, learn grammar and basic reasoning. Their single-layer models contain readable attention heads ([TinyStories](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-tiny-model-revolution-with-ronen-eldan-and-yuanzhi-li-of-microsoft/)).
- Ziming Liu's Brain-Inspired Modular Training penalises long connections so networks become sparse and modular. Labenz sees mechanistic interpretability as one of the most promising paths to safety ([Ziming Liu](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/seeing-is-believing-with-mit-s-ziming-liu/)).
- Yu's MEGABYTE drops tokenizers by modelling bytes in patches, with a large global model and small local ones ([MEGABYTE](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/meta-s-megabyte-revolution-with-lili-yu-of-meta-ai/)).

### Medical AI on modest budgets

- Tanishq Mathew Abraham, founder of MedARC, argues that mapping new data into the latent spaces of existing pretrained models gives strong results with small datasets and modest compute ([Mind-Reading](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-mind-reading-revolution-with-dr-tanishq-mathew-abraham-part-1-of-2/)).
- In part two he shows an older model and careful data preparation speeding up a slow diagnostic workflow, with clinical trials still ahead. MedARC works in the open, with compute from Stability AI ([Virtual Biopsy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-virtual-biopsy-revolution-with-dr-tanishq-mathew-abraham-part-2-of/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Where Are the Moats in AI?](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/where-are-the-moats-in-ai-with-nathan-labenz-and-erik-torenberg/): a structured list of nine moats and a view of how the market will split.
- [Your Model, Your Weights](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/your-model-your-weights-with-mosaicml-s-abhi-venigalla-and-jonathan-fr/): the enterprise case for owning model weights, with concrete pricing and long-context detail.
- [The Consumer Rights Revolution](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-consumer-rights-revolution-with-joshua-browder-of-donotpay/): a founder's specific regulatory proposals and blunt job-loss estimates.
- [The Tiny Model Revolution](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2023/06/the-tiny-model-revolution-with-ronen-eldan-and-yuanzhi-li-of-microsoft/): small synthetic data producing big-model behaviours that can be studied.
