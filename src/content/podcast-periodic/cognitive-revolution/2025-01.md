## Overview

January on The Cognitive Revolution was about reasoning models. Nathan Labenz unpacked OpenAI's o3 results in a two-part listener AMA, then recorded an emergency episode on DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi k1.5. Other episodes covered enterprise adoption at Box, AI in materials science and medical research, vision language models and attacks on activation-based safety monitors.

## Main themes

### o3 and the reasoning shift

- Labenz notes o3 scored 75% on ARC-AGI in low-effort mode, about 15 points ahead of anything else, and 87.5% with high compute. He reads this as a move to expensive, inference-heavy reasoning ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-1-openai-s-o3-deliberative-alignment-and-ai-surprises-of-2/)).
- He expects “the AIs are gonna run away with anything where there is already easily accessible reward signal”, such as maths and programming ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-1-openai-s-o3-deliberative-alignment-and-ai-surprises-of-2/)).
- Reasoning models are still weird: his tic-tac-toe test tripped up o1 pro, Gemini Flash Thinking and DeepSeek. He calls deliberative alignment useful but silent on deception and self-formed goals ([AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-1-openai-s-o3-deliberative-alignment-and-ai-surprises-of-2/)).

### DeepSeek-R1 and Chinese reasoning models

- Labenz explains that R1-Zero was trained with only accuracy and format rewards on DeepSeek V3. Thinking length grew from about 500 to about 10,000 tokens and reflection emerged untrained ([RL Works](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/emergency-pod-reinforcement-learning-works-reflecting-on-chinese-model/)).
- He concludes DeepSeek “has joined the top tier of global AI developers”. R1 costs $2 per million tokens against $60 for o1 ([RL Works](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/emergency-pod-reinforcement-learning-works-reflecting-on-chinese-model/)).
- Moonshot's Kimi k1.5 reached similar results by a similar path. Labenz takes this as a sign the West's lead is small, and questions both the AI war framing and what chip export controls are for ([RL Works](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/emergency-pod-reinforcement-learning-works-reflecting-on-chinese-model/)).

### Enterprise adoption and agents

- Aaron Levie says CIOs want more AI use cases than they ever wanted cloud, but at-scale deployments are in very early innings ([Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/aaron-levie-ceo-of-box-on-box-ai-enterprise-enthusiasm-and-the-evoluti/)).
- He stresses reliability: “you can't have an enterprise workflow in a particularly a regulated industry that works 98% of the time”. Curated Hubs content, he claims, makes Box's RAG far better ([Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/aaron-levie-ceo-of-box-on-box-ai-enterprise-enthusiasm-and-the-evoluti/)).
- Levie sees IT becoming the HR department of AI agents. Labenz thinks fine-tuned existing models could automate far more work than they do today ([Box](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/aaron-levie-ceo-of-box-on-box-ai-enterprise-enthusiasm-and-the-evoluti/), [AMA Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-1-openai-s-o3-deliberative-alignment-and-ai-surprises-of-2/)).

### Abundance, work and consciousness

- Labenz finds it hard to imagine the technology for abundance not arriving by about 2030. The risk he sees is human: unions or doctors keeping AI out, as with nuclear power ([AMA Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-2-ai-utopia-consciousness-and-the-future-of-work/)).
- He is not worried about lost meaning if needs are met, and puts AI consciousness in the same bucket as the octopus. His p(doom) is 10-90%, depending on how AI is built ([AMA Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/ai-ama-part-2-ai-utopia-consciousness-and-the-future-of-work/)).

### Science, perception and robustness

- Jonathan Godwin and Tim Duignan say Orbital Materials' models learn physics from small simulations and run far faster than numerical methods, with a focus on data-centre materials ([Orbital Materials](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/material-progress-developing-ai-s-scientific-intuition-with-orbital-ma/)).
- Catherine Brownstein says cheap sequencing moved the rare-disease bottleneck to reading the literature, where o1 saves her hours, while data sharing needs policy change ([Gene Hunting](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/gene-hunting-with-o1-pro-reasoning-about-rare-diseases-with-chatgpt-pr/)).
- Will Hardman says vision language model progress comes from training recipes and data, but benchmarks like BLINK show perception still lags ([Vision Language Models](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/teaching-ai-to-see-a-technical-deep-dive-on-vision-language-models-wit/)).
- Luke Bailey, Erik Jenner and Scott Emmons show attackers can hide harmful behaviour from probes and sparse autoencoders, so latent monitors are useful but not robust ([Obfuscated Activations](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/01/dodging-latent-space-detectors-obfuscated-activation-attacks-with-luke/)).
