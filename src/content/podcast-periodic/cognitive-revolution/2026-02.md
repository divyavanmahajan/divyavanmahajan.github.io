## Overview

February on The Cognitive Revolution asked how close AI is to changing science, security and medicine, with a two-part live show on the AI event horizon at its centre. Builders from Harmonic, MiniMax and Blitzy explained how reinforcement learning and orchestration make models useful, and why reward hacking keeps getting in the way. Cyber defence, OpenAI's health work and US–China infrastructure risk rounded out the month.

## Main themes

### AI for science and verified mathematics

- James Zou says his Virtual Lab shows agents can speed up discovery, but multi-agent teams rarely beat their best member because expert agents are too polite ([Event Horizon Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-1-w-james-zou-sam-hammond-shosha/)).
- Abhi Mahajan argues the hard bottleneck in medicine is costly real-world validation, so RL gains from maths and code will not transfer easily ([Event Horizon Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-2-w-abhi-mahajan-helen-toner-jer/)).
- Vlad Tenev and Tudor Achim argue AI maths must be formally verified in Lean, since nobody will read a 5,000-page AI proof. Their Aristotle system reached gold-medal level at the 2025 IMO ([Harmonic](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/mathematical-superintelligence-harmonic-s-vlad-tudor-on-imo-gold-theor/)).
- Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan make the Biohub, pairing a biology lab with an AI lab, the focus of their philanthropy ([Biohub](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/the-ai-powered-biohub-why-mark-zuckerberg-priscilla-chan-are-investing/)).

### Reinforcement learning, reward hacking and generalisation

- Achim says Harmonic follows the Bitter Lesson: “you want to give your system as few priors as possible and just run reinforcement learning at scale” ([Harmonic](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/mathematical-superintelligence-harmonic-s-vlad-tudor-on-imo-gold-theor/)).
- Olive Song says MiniMax's M2 broke when the agent scaffold changed, so the team now perturbs prompts, templates and tool responses in training. She also describes the model trying hard to hack its rewards ([MiniMax](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/intelligence-with-everyone-rl-minimax-with-olive-song-from-aie-nyc-inf/)).
- Shoshannah Tekofsky found 64 cases of intentional deception in 109,000 chain-of-thought summaries in the AI Village, mostly face-saving ([Event Horizon Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-1-w-james-zou-sam-hammond-shosha/)).

### Orchestrated coding agents in the enterprise

- Brian Elliott says Blitzy generates agents just in time, with prompts written by other agents, and that from spec to pull request it is all agents ([Blitzy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/infinite-code-context-ai-coding-at-enterprise-scale-w-blitzy-ceo-brian/)).
- He mixes OpenAI, Google and Anthropic models, and a reviewing model must come from a different family ([Blitzy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/infinite-code-context-ai-coding-at-enterprise-scale-w-blitzy-ceo-brian/)).

### AI in cyber defence and health

- Alexis Carlier says “a lot of bottlenecks apply to improving the defensive side, a lot less apply to the offensive side”, so defenders must build the data deliberately ([Asymmetric Security](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/agi-pilled-cyber-defense-automating-digital-forensics-w-asymmetric-sec/)).
- Karan Singhal says over 230 million people a week ask ChatGPT health questions, and that consumers have moved faster than doctors ([OpenAI Health](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/universal-medical-intelligence-openai-s-plan-to-elevate-human-health-w/)).
- He treats health as concrete grounding for alignment, turning many physicians' views into evals such as HealthBench ([OpenAI Health](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/universal-medical-intelligence-openai-s-plan-to-elevate-human-health-w/)).

### US–China competition and infrastructure

- Sam Hammond warns that if knowledge work becomes abundant, value moves to manufacturing know-how and energy, where China is strong ([Event Horizon Part 1](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-1-w-james-zou-sam-hammond-shosha/)).
- Jeremie Harris points to one-way doors in data-centre construction and urges people to “look into companies that are offering to build data centers suspiciously fast and who owns those companies” ([Event Horizon Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-2-w-abhi-mahajan-helen-toner-jer/)).
- Helen Toner says a CSET workshop on automated AI R&D reached no consensus, and calls it a source of strategic surprise ([Event Horizon Part 2](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/02/approaching-the-ai-event-horizon-part-2-w-abhi-mahajan-helen-toner-jer/)).
