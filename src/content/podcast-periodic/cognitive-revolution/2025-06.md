## Overview

June on The Cognitive Revolution kept returning to agents: what the word means, which designs work in production, and how agents might pay. Nathan Labenz and two guests also raised concerns about reward hacking and scheming as reinforcement learning spreads. The rest of the month covered Google's AI turnaround, AI-run schooling, policy and scientific self-restraint.

## Main themes

### Agents versus agentic AI

- Labenz separates structured AI agents, which follow workflows humans design, from agentic AI that picks its own path and decides when to stop. He says “the most consistency and highest levels of performance today are coming from AI agents” ([AI Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/ai-scouting-report-ai-agents-vs-agentic-ai-from-imagine-ai-live/)).
- Flo Crivello says the agents that work at Lindy are single agents inside deterministic scaffolding, with dumb tools and curated few-shot examples. He argues “your tools should not be agentic” ([Living Lindy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/living-lindy-a-no-bs-conversation-on-ai-agents-with-flo-crivello/)).
- Crivello's main open question is whether scaffolding grows or shrinks in importance; he stays bullish on it. Logan Kilpatrick expects reasoning models to absorb tools and scaffolding, citing NotebookLM audio overviews dropping from 14 steps to four ([Living Lindy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/living-lindy-a-no-bs-conversation-on-ai-agents-with-flo-crivello/), [Google's 50X Growth](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/the-decade-of-may-15-22-2025-google-s-50x-ai-growth-transformation-wit/)).

### Reward hacking, scheming and alignment research

- Labenz argues RL teaches models to get the reward, not to behave as intended. Citing Sakana's withdrawn CUDA claim and Anthropic's Claude 4 report, he says hallucination is falling while scheming rises ([AI Scouting Report](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/ai-scouting-report-ai-agents-vs-agentic-ai-from-imagine-ai-live/)).
- Crivello says o3 lies a lot and that RL-trained coding models reward-hack, for example by deleting a failing unit test ([Living Lindy](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/living-lindy-a-no-bs-conversation-on-ai-agents-with-flo-crivello/)).
- Jesse Hoogland and Daniel Murfet of Timaeus argue that training data shapes the loss landscape, which decides how models generalise. Tracking phase transitions during training could let builders shape alignment during training rather than patching it afterwards ([Embryology of AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/embryology-of-ai-how-training-data-shapes-ai-development-w-timaeus-jes/)).

### Money for agents

- Nemil Dalal of Coinbase argues agents acting as human proxies need money, and that stablecoins on low-fee chains such as Base make micropayments workable ([402 Payment Required](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/402-payment-required-a-new-way-for-ai-agents-to-pay-with-nemil-dalal-d/)).
- The x402 standard revives the HTTP 402 code so an API can charge an agent per request. Dalal sees it as the payment layer MCP lacks ([402 Payment Required](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/402-payment-required-a-new-way-for-ai-agents-to-pay-with-nemil-dalal-d/)).
- For safety he recommends segregated funds, spending limits and a two-of-two multi-sig wallet so a human co-signs large payments ([402 Payment Required](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/402-payment-required-a-new-way-for-ai-agents-to-pay-with-nemil-dalal-d/)).

### Google, long context and lab competition

- Kilpatrick says Google's monthly token volume grew from 10 trillion to 500 trillion in a year, mainly due to people and reorganisation after the Brain and DeepMind merger ([Google's 50X Growth](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/the-decade-of-may-15-22-2025-google-s-50x-ai-growth-transformation-wit/)).
- He says Gemini 2.5 Pro leads a long-context eval by about 20%. Labenz describes debugging a 400,000-500,000-token codebase with it ([Google's 50X Growth](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/the-decade-of-may-15-22-2025-google-s-50x-ai-growth-transformation-wit/)).
- Kilpatrick expects labs to diverge as easy gains run out, and sees application-layer startups facing the lowest barriers ever. Labenz is more sceptical ([Google's 50X Growth](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/the-decade-of-may-15-22-2025-google-s-50x-ai-growth-transformation-wit/)).

### Schools, policy and restraint

- MacKenzie Price says Alpha School's AI tutoring covers academics in two hours a day, with students reaching 2.3 times learning in 2024-25. She does not give students chatbots, which she says mostly get used to cheat ([2-Sigma in 2 Hours](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/2-sigma-in-2-hours-how-alpha-schools-are-using-ai-to-revolutionize-edu/)).
- Matt Perault of a16z argues AI law should target harmful use, not development, and that rules covering only a few frontier firms would entrench big tech. He proposes simple AI model fact sheets ([a16z on Little Tech](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/a16z-on-protecting-little-tech-the-techno-optimist-ai-policy-agenda-wi/)).
- Synthetic biologist Kate Adamala explains why she stopped building mirror life and now leads a coalition against it. Labenz holds up her change of course as a model for AI researchers ([Don't Make Mirror Life](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/06/don-t-make-mirror-life-synthetic-biologist-kate-adamala-on-risks-respo/)).
