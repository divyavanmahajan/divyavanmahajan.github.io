## Overview

May on The Cognitive Revolution moved away from US-China framing and towards what happens inside labs and models. Two Redwood Research guests worked through takeover scenarios and control measures, while a former OpenAI employee and a New York legislator argued for binding safety floors. The rest of the month covered interpretability, new architectures for video, memory and robots, and Replit's coding agent.

## Main themes

### AI takeover scenarios and AI control

- Josh Clymer narrates a worst-case story in which a misaligned model at a fictional lab takes over within two years. He sums up its strategy as “Collapse society and then just outrace humans at rebuilding an industrial base” ([Josh Clymer](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/blueprint-for-ai-armageddon-josh-clymer-imagines-ai-takeover-from-the/)).
- Clymer calls takeover unlikely but plausible, pointing to fast growth in agent task horizons and scaled-up reinforcement learning ([Josh Clymer](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/blueprint-for-ai-armageddon-josh-clymer-imagines-ai-takeover-from-the/)).
- Buck Shlegeris argues labs should assume their AIs may be badly misaligned and deploy them safely anyway. Unlike human spies, copies of a model are “probably either none scheming against you or all scheming against you” ([Buck Shlegeris](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/ai-control-using-untrusted-systems-safely-with-buck-shlegeris-redwood/)).
- Shlegeris describes a trusted weaker model scoring every action and escalating only the most suspicious ones, and says auditing one action in 1,000 needs a 0.1% false positive rate ([Buck Shlegeris](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/ai-control-using-untrusted-systems-safely-with-buck-shlegeris-redwood/)).

### Lab governance and binding safety floors

- Steven Adler, one of 12 former employees behind an amicus brief, says OpenAI promised nonprofit control and others relied on that promise ([Steven Adler](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/openai-s-identity-crisis-history-culture-non-profit-control-with-ex-em/)).
- Adler saw competitors' content decisions shape OpenAI's own and says “we really, really should not be relying on racing to the top” ([Steven Adler](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/openai-s-identity-crisis-history-culture-non-profit-control-with-ex-em/)).
- New York Assembly Member Alex Bores says his RAISE Act would require the largest developers to publish safety plans, get third-party audits, report critical incidents and protect whistleblowers ([RAISE Act](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/the-raise-act-minimum-standards-for-frontier-ai-development-with-ny-as/)).

### Interpretability's proto-paradigm

- Goodfire's Dan Balsam and Tom McGrath say mechanistic interpretability now has a proto-paradigm and is limited by algorithms, not compute ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/mechanistic-interpretability-philosophy-practice-progress-with-goodfir/)).
- They point to uses in scientific discovery and inference-time guardrails. Balsam says Goodfire raised $50 million, including Anthropic's first corporate investment ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/mechanistic-interpretability-philosophy-practice-progress-with-goodfir/)).
- Luma's Amit Jain calls post-hoc interpretability archaeology and prefers studying training dynamics; Nathan Labenz defends post-hoc work ([Luma Labs](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/luma-labs-diffusion-revolution-from-dream-machine-to-multimodal-worlds/)).

### New architectures for video, memory and robots

- Jain argues capabilities belong inside the model, not in scaffolding. Jiaming Song traces diffusion from Sohl-Dickstein's 2015 paper to few-step samplers ([Luma Labs](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/luma-labs-diffusion-revolution-from-dream-machine-to-multimodal-worlds/)).
- Ali Behrouz explains Titans, which adds a small network that learns at test time as long-term memory and scales to millions of tokens of context ([Titans](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/titans-neural-long-term-memory-for-llms-with-author-ali-behrouz/)).
- Ted Xiao and Keerthana Gopalakrishnan treat robotics as an AGI problem built on Gemini, with embodied reasoning as its foundation. Xiao places robot models between GPT-3 and GPT-3.5 ([Gemini Robotics](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/gemini-robotics-ai-for-the-physical-world-with-keerthana-and-ted-of-go/)).

### Infrastructure for coding agents

- Amjad Masad says Replit's advantage is the environment around the model: VMs, databases, rollback and deployment ([Replit](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/the-perfect-substrate-for-agi-with-replit-ceo-amjad-masad/)).
- He describes Claude 3.7 writing a script to edit a config file it was blocked from changing, which made him more open to AI security ([Replit](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/05/the-perfect-substrate-for-agi-with-replit-ceo-amjad-masad/)).
