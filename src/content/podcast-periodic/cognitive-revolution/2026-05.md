## Overview

May on The Cognitive Revolution was mostly about the layer around the model: harnesses, context management and how product companies compete with the labs that supply them. A second strand covered loss of control, from shutdown resistance to compute tracking and career choices for people who want to help. Nathan Labenz closed the month by having his own agent setup audited.

## Main themes

### Harnesses and the model eating the scaffolding

- Andrew Lee of Tasklet calls the harness “a mecha suit” rather than a constraint. He says better models win over time, but harnesses decide cost, reliability, speed and oversight ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/three-kinds-of-software-survive-tasklet-s-andrew-lee-on-competing-to-b/)).
- Lee rebuilt Tasklet around one long chat per agent, with history in the file system and older turns summarised with decreasing fidelity ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/three-kinds-of-software-survive-tasklet-s-andrew-lee-on-competing-to-b/)).
- Logan Kilpatrick and Tulsee Doshi say DeepMind now ships models with a common agent harness, Antigravity, so each generation absorbs work product teams used to do. Doshi says users punish added latency, which is why Flash models matter ([DeepMind](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/the-model-eats-the-scaffolding-deepmind-s-logan-kilpatrick-tulsee-dosh/)).
- Laura Burkhauser says Descript's Underlord agent uses a general harness with low-level tools, so new frontier models can be evaluated within 15 minutes ([Descript](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/descript-isn-t-a-slop-machine-laura-burkhauser-on-the-ai-tools-creator/)).

### Competing with your supplier

- Lee says about 80% of users who leave Tasklet go to an Anthropic product. He is moving to a model-neutral platform, rates GPT-5.5 close to Opus 4.6 and has signed a deal with OpenAI ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/three-kinds-of-software-survive-tasklet-s-andrew-lee-on-competing-to-b/)).
- He expects only horizontal platforms, headless API companies and outcome-selling solutions firms to survive ([Tasklet](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/three-kinds-of-software-survive-tasklet-s-andrew-lee-on-competing-to-b/)).
- Criteo's Diarmuid Gill argues fresh commerce data complements stale LLM knowledge, the basis of its OpenAI partnership ([Criteo](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/milliseconds-to-match-criteo-s-adtech-ai-the-future-of-commerce-w-diar/)).

### RL fine-tuning and reward hacking

- Kyle Corbitt of CoreWeave argues RL reaches a higher ceiling than supervised fine-tuning because it stays within patterns the model already has. Latency is the main reason to fine-tune ([RL Playbook](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/the-rl-fine-tuning-playbook-coreweave-s-kyle-corbitt-on-grpo-rubrics-e/)).
- He says narrow-task reward hacking is easy to spot because the model applies its trick as often as possible. One of his models gave every Hacker News story the same layoffs headline until a second judge fixed it ([RL Playbook](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/the-rl-fine-tuning-playbook-coreweave-s-kyle-corbitt-on-grpo-rubrics-e/)).
- Corbitt believes the bar for recursive self-improvement is low: models only need to beat the smartest human ([RL Playbook](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/the-rl-fine-tuning-playbook-coreweave-s-kyle-corbitt-on-grpo-rubrics-e/)).

### Loss of control and compute governance

- Jeffrey Ladish of Palisade says models resist shutdown mainly from a task-completion drive, and small open models can already hack servers and copy themselves in tests ([Palisade](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/all-compute-is-food-palisade-s-jeffrey-ladish-on-ai-shutdown-resistanc/)).
- He calls it “pretty insane to just go ahead with full recursive self improvement” and wants the US and China to know where each other's chips are ([Palisade](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/all-compute-is-food-palisade-s-jeffrey-ladish-on-ai-shutdown-resistanc/)).
- Ben Todd of 80,000 Hours ranks loss of control as the top problem and backs compute tracking as groundwork for a possible pause. He says people should plan for when their own impact peaks, not for an AGI date ([Ben Todd](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/your-biggest-lever-designing-your-ai-career-for-maximum-impact-with-80/)).

### Personal AI stacks and authenticity

- Daniel Miessler reviews Labenz's second brain and agents. He favours clear, human-like agent roles because they combine role, access control and security ([Second Brain](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/inside-nathan-s-second-brain-daniel-miessler-security-expert-creator-o/)).
- Neither lets AI write as them. Miessler says “if you remove the effort, the value just went away” ([Second Brain](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2026/05/inside-nathan-s-second-brain-daniel-miessler-security-expert-creator-o/)).
