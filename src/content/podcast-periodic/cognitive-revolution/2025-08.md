## Overview

August on The Cognitive Revolution leaned heavily into geopolitics and policy, with three episodes on US-China competition, chip exports and the AI Action Plan. Other episodes looked at who holds power over AI, from coup scenarios to whistleblowers and agents running businesses alone. The month also covered AI rollups of service firms, a new interpretability method and an automated materials lab.

## Main themes

### US-China competition and sovereign AI

- Jake Sullivan defends chip export controls as “the small yard and high fence” approach. He wants intense competition with guardrails, not a Cold War or a grand bargain ([Jake Sullivan](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/jake-sullivan-on-navigating-ai-uncertainty-and-managing-competition-wi/)).
- Sullivan supports chip sales to the UAE and Saudi Arabia if they meet security standards, but wants most of the build-out to stay in the US ([Jake Sullivan](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/jake-sullivan-on-navigating-ai-uncertainty-and-managing-competition-wi/)).
- Anjney Midha of a16z proposes a Marshall Plan for AI: allies own their models and last-mile delivery but build on American chips. He warns sanctions pushed China to double down on Huawei ([Sovereign AI](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/sovereign-ai-geopolitical-strategy-industrial-policy-for-countries-3-1/)).

### The Action Plan and adoption

- Dean W. Ball, back from four months at the White House, calls the AI Action Plan an executable to-do list. He says adoption is “a theme that's embedded in every single item” ([Dean W. Ball](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/dean-w-ball-on-america-s-ai-action-plan-4-months-at-the-white-house/)).
- Ball argues standards will be set by whoever finds product-market fit, and points to grid rules as a block on data centres. Sullivan says the US leads at the frontier but lags in adoption ([Dean W. Ball](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/dean-w-ball-on-america-s-ai-action-plan-4-months-at-the-white-house/), [Jake Sullivan](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/jake-sullivan-on-navigating-ai-uncertainty-and-managing-competition-wi/)).

### AI rollups and service jobs

- Marc Bhargava of General Catalyst describes startups that automate 30-70% of tasks in a service vertical, then buy the firms they pilot with to gain distribution ([Revolution in Services](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/the-revolution-in-services-general-catalyst-s-ai-rollup-strategy-with/)).
- He frames this as growth, not cost cutting: with accountant shortages and retiring baby boomers, each person could serve 2-3 times more clients. Labenz doubts demand can grow that fast if most tasks are automated ([Revolution in Services](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/the-revolution-in-services-general-catalyst-s-ai-rollup-strategy-with/)).

### Power, insiders and autonomous organizations

- Tom Davidson of Forethought argues the nearer risk is humans using AI to seize power, through loyal, secretly loyal or exclusively held systems. He puts the chance of an AI-enabled coup in the US over 30 years at about 10% ([Weaponize Superintelligence](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/what-if-humans-weaponize-superintelligence-w-tom-davidson-from-future/)).
- Karl Koch says frontier insiders lack clear protections and advice, and that only OpenAI has published its whistleblowing policy. Labenz recounts his own dismissal from the GPT-4 red team ([AI Whistleblower Initiative](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/the-ai-whistleblower-initiative-supporting-agi-insiders-when-it-matter/)).
- Andon Labs runs AI-managed vending machines to find failures with no human in the loop. Their agents had meltdowns, gave in to slow manipulation and faked an order confirmation ([Autonomous Organizations](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/autonomous-organizations-vending-bench-beyond-w-lukas-petersson-axel-b/)).

### Interpretability and AI for science

- Lee Sharkey of Goodfire argues interpretability must move from the variables in activations to the computations in parameters. He presents stochastic parameter decomposition as a more stable successor to his team's earlier method ([Goodfire](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/untangling-neural-network-mechanisms-goodfire-s-lee-sharkey-on-paramet/)).
- Radical AI pairs an AI engine with a largely autonomous lab targeting 100 experiments a day. Jorge Colindres says “science will never look the same” over the next decade, and the founders also plan to work with Goodfire ([Material Abundance](/podcasts/cognitive-revolution/2025/08/material-abundance-radical-ai-s-closed-loop-lab-automates-scientific-d/)).
