## Overview

In 2022 the Dwarkesh Podcast was a broad interview show more than an AI show. Effective altruism and FTX bracket the year: Sam Bankman-Fried in July, then Bethany McLean on corporate collapse in December. In between came longtermism, cheap energy, construction and cities, and a run of economists and historians. AI appears mostly as a long-run risk, through David Deutsch and Will MacAskill.

## Main themes

### Effective altruism and FTX

- Then FTX CEO Sam Bankman-Fried says the scarce input for altruistic and for-profit projects alike is “the right person or team to take the lead on it” ([Sam Bankman-Fried](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/07/sam-bankman-fried-crypto-altruism-and-leadership/)).
- He also says EA can get too narrow about plotting courses of impact, and describes FTX as a company that keeps taking on new things ([Sam Bankman-Fried](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/07/sam-bankman-fried-crypto-altruism-and-leadership/)).
- After the collapse, Bethany McLean argues failures from Enron to FTX come from self-delusion and culture rather than deliberate villainy ([Bethany McLean](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/12/bethany-mclean-enron-ftx-2008-musk-frauds-visionaries/)).

### Longtermism, contingency and risk

- Will MacAskill argues technological progress is largely inevitable while moral values are contingent, so the future depends on avoiding premature value lock-in, especially through AI ([Will MacAskill](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/will-macaskill-longtermism-altruism-history-technology/)).
- Charles C. Mann says contact between the hemispheres was bound to happen but its outcome turned on chance and agency ([Charles C. Mann](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/09/charles-c-mann-americas-before-columbus-scientific-wizardry/)).
- Tyler Cowen expects long-run collapse, says existential risk matters more than almost anyone thinks, but that EA people overvalue it a bit ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/09/tyler-cowen-talent-collapse-pessimism-of-sex/)). David Deutsch rejects the idea that AGI could fundamentally surpass humans as universal explainers ([David Deutsch](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/01/david-deutsch-ai-america-fun-bayes/)).

### Energy and building things

- Austin Vernon expects very cheap solar energy to remake industry, and argues US nuclear plants stalled mainly because market conditions changed, not only because of regulation ([Austin Vernon](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/09/austin-vernon-energy-superabundance-starship-missiles-finding-alpha/)).
- Brian Potter explains why construction resists industrialisation: tightly coupled building systems, thin margins and NEPA's open-ended litigation ([Brian Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).

### Cities and Robert Moses

- Kenneth T. Jackson says Moses saved New York from Detroit-style decline and calls him “the greatest builder in American history” ([Kenneth T. Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- Jackson finds The Power Broker right in outline but full of small errors, and blames NIMBYism for blocking building now ([Kenneth T. Jackson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/kenneth-t-jackson-robert-moses-hero-of-new-york/)).
- Edward Glaeser argues cities survive terrorism, pandemics and remote work because face-to-face contact matters, and that restrictive housing rules are their main self-inflicted wound ([Edward Glaeser](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/11/edward-glaeser-cities-terrorism-housing-remote-work/)).

### Economists on talent and incentives

- Cowen treats talent spotting as an art of context and intuition, and his book Talent doubles as a critique of Bryan Caplan's case against education ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/09/tyler-cowen-talent-collapse-pessimism-of-sex/)).
- Caplan defends large rewards for billionaires through tournament theory and points to his record of 23 resolved bets, all won ([Bryan Caplan](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/bryan-caplan-feminists-billionaires-and-demagogues/)).
- Steve Hsu frames genomic prediction as a machine learning problem whose main bottleneck is data access ([Steve Hsu](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/08/steve-hsu-intelligence-embryo-selection-the-future-of-humanity/)).
