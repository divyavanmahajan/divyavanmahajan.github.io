## Overview

October had two guests and no shared thread. Brian Potter explained why construction stays slow and expensive and how environmental review adds cost and uncertainty. Bryan Caplan, on his third appearance, defended contrarian positions on feminism, refugees, education and billionaire wealth.

## Main themes

### Why construction does not get cheaper

- Potter says buildings are highly coupled systems: “Every single part affects every single other part.” Unlike software, a finished building cannot be deployed to a million users ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).
- He says a single-family home is roughly 50% labour and 50% materials. A factory cuts labour but not materials and adds transport, which is why prefabrication bets such as Katerra's did not work out ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).
- Real gains, in his view, need components redesigned for machines, such as insulated metal panels. He is sceptical of 3D printing, which only prints walls worth perhaps 7-8% of a building's value ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).

### Environmental review and NEPA

- Potter says environmental impact statements take about four and a half years on average, and run to around 660 pages against guidance of about 150 ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).
- He argues NEPA is purely procedural and enforced through lawsuits, so its main effect is uncertainty that deters projects. Timeline caps could backfire by inviting more litigation ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).
- His preferred reform is to give wind, solar and transmission the categorical exclusions that oil and gas drilling already enjoy ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).

### Plainer modern buildings

- Potter reframes the ugliness debate as a question of why modern buildings have less ornamentation. He credits air conditioning, float glass and steel, which let owners optimise light-filled interiors over exteriors ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).
- He notes glass curtain walls are expensive, and that most architects must satisfy paying owners rather than impose their own taste ([Potter](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/brian-potter-future-of-construction-ugly-modernism-environmental-revie/)).

### Caplan's contrarian positions

- Caplan defines feminism as the view that society treats women less fairly than men. He calls the US measures close to a wash and says false accusations tip the balance, while conceding women fare worse in countries such as Saudi Arabia ([Caplan](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/bryan-caplan-feminists-billionaires-and-demagogues/)).
- He calls open borders the most doable route to hundreds of trillions of dollars of extra wealth this century, and says Poland's Ukrainian intake would equal the US taking 30-33 million refugees in a month ([Caplan](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/bryan-caplan-feminists-billionaires-and-demagogues/)).
- Against heavy taxes on billionaires he cites tournament theory, saying they are “inspiring a generation of movers and shakers” ([Caplan](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/bryan-caplan-feminists-billionaires-and-demagogues/)).
- He also calls student loan forgiveness a regressive transfer and describes anarcho-capitalism, with competing police and courts, as his ideal long-term system ([Caplan](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2022/10/bryan-caplan-feminists-billionaires-and-demagogues/)).
