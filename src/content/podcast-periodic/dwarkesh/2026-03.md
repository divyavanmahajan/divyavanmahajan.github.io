## Overview

March on the Dwarkesh Podcast paired a hard look at the limits of AI compute with two conversations about how discovery really happens. Dylan Patel walked through the chip supply chain, Terence Tao weighed what AI can and cannot do in mathematics, and historian Ada Palmer showed how slowly new technologies such as print found their markets.

## Main themes

### Chips, not power, cap AI compute

- Dylan Patel argues the binding constraint is moving back to chips: “the longest lead time supply chains are not power or data centers” but semiconductors ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- He says clean rooms are the biggest fab bottleneck this year and next, and that by 2028-2029 the limit falls to ASML's EUV tools. ASML makes about 70 a year, rising to just over 100 by 2030 ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- Patel's maths: about 3.5 EUV tools per gigawatt, so roughly 700 tools would support about 200 GW of AI chips a year by the end of the decade. He expects China to have working EUV tools by 2030 but not mass production ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- Power, by contrast, is solvable. Turbines, engines, fuel cells and batteries that unlock about 20% of the US grid could add hundreds of gigawatts, and he expects about half of new capacity to be behind the meter ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).

### Compute economics and Anthropic

- Patel says Anthropic added $4 billion of revenue in January and $6 billion in February, and that its caution on compute left it behind OpenAI ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- He argues H100s have gained value: some labs pay $2.40 an hour for two to three years against a $1.40 build cost, because a GPU's value is set by the best model it can serve ([Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).

### AI in mathematics

- Terence Tao says AI has helped solve about 50 of roughly 1,100 Erdős problems, almost all with little prior literature, and that pure one-shot AI solutions have since become rare ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/)).
- Tao calls AI tools “like jumping machines” that reach walls humans cannot, but says they are poor at building partial progress or spotting intermediate stages ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/)).
- He argues generating ideas is now nearly free, so verification and judgement are the bottleneck. His papers carry more code and literature review, but core reasoning still happens on paper ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/)).

### How discovery and new technology really spread

- Dwarkesh Patel suggests Kepler was a “high-temperature LLM” trying random relationships against Tycho Brahe's data. Tao adds that Brahe's precise measurements deserve equal credit ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/)).
- Ada Palmer says Gutenberg borrowed about $1.5 million for paper, printed around 300 Bibles and went bankrupt. Printers took about 40 years to make money ([Ada Palmer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/why-leonardo-was-a-saboteur-gutenberg-went-broke-and-florence-was-weir/)).
- Palmer describes Florence's commoner republic, ruled by nine guild members drawn by lottery, and argues censors in every era worried about the wrong texts ([Ada Palmer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/why-leonardo-was-a-saboteur-gutenberg-went-broke-and-florence-was-weir/)).
