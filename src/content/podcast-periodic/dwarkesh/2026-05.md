## Overview

May on the Dwarkesh Podcast was built around two chalkboard lectures. Reiner Pope built an AI chip from logic gates upwards and Eric Jang rebuilt AlphaGo with LLM coding tools. A third conversation, with geneticist David Reich, looked at what ancient DNA says about recent human evolution.

## Main themes

### Chip design is a fight against data movement

- Reiner Pope, CEO of MatX, argues the arithmetic itself is cheap and most of a chip's cost lies in selecting, moving and synchronising data. Dwarkesh Patel discloses he is an angel investor in MatX ([Chip Design](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/reiner-pope-chip-design-from-the-bottom-up/)).
- Pope notes that “A multiply-accumulate happens at every single step of a matrix multiply”, and that its area grows with the square of bit width, which is why low precision works so well ([Chip Design](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/reiner-pope-chip-design-from-the-bottom-up/)).
- In a classic CUDA core he estimates seven-eighths of the cost goes on register-file access. Systolic arrays in Tensor Cores and TPUs keep weights local, and MatX has discussed a splittable systolic array as a middle path ([Chip Design](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/reiner-pope-chip-design-from-the-bottom-up/)).

### AlphaGo, search and LLM reinforcement learning

- Eric Jang says “AlphaGo’s core conceptual breakthrough was using neural nets to make this search problem tractable.” He rebuilt it for a few thousand dollars and open-sourced the result ([AlphaGo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/eric-jang-building-alphago-from-scratch/)).
- Jang explains that Monte Carlo tree search proposes a strictly better action at every move, turning RL into stable supervised learning. Current LLM RL instead treats a whole sequence as one action ([AlphaGo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/eric-jang-building-alphago-from-scratch/)).
- Patel argues policy-gradient RL learns very little per sample at low pass rates, and that longer agent trajectories reduce samples per FLOP. Jang doubts PUCT-style search carries over to LLMs, since the same child is rarely sampled twice ([AlphaGo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/eric-jang-building-alphago-from-scratch/)).

### Automated AI research

- Jang calls automated scientific research one of the most exciting skills frontier labs are developing. He used a Claude skill called Experiment to run experiments and compile plots ([AlphaGo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/eric-jang-building-alphago-from-scratch/)).
- He found Opus 4.6 and 4.7 strong at tuning and running experiments but weak at choosing the next experiment. He suggests Go could serve as a hard-to-cheat outer loop for training automated scientists ([AlphaGo](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/eric-jang-building-alphago-from-scratch/)).

### Ancient DNA and recent human evolution

- David Reich describes a large ancient-DNA study led by his colleague Ali Akbari, which argues that natural selection in Europe and the Middle East has been widespread and intensified in the Bronze Age ([David Reich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/david-reich-why-the-bronze-age-was-an-inflection-point-in-human-evolut/)).
- Reich stresses the caveats: the team spent years trying to make the result go away, and he offers a speculative new model of Neanderthal origins while saying it is probably wrong ([David Reich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/05/david-reich-why-the-bronze-age-was-an-inflection-point-in-human-evolut/)).
