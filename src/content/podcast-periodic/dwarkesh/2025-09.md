## Overview

September on the Dwarkesh Podcast asked how machines should learn. Richard Sutton argued that imitating human text is the wrong foundation, while Sergey Levine argued that robots can now start learning on the job. Sarah Paine supplied the history episode, on how Britain used sea power to survive and defeat Nazi Germany.

## Main themes

### Learning from experience versus imitation

- Richard Sutton says LLMs “predict what a person would say. They don’t have the ability to predict what will happen”, so they have no real world model ([Richard Sutton](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/richard-sutton-father-of-rl-thinks-llms-are-a-dead-end/)).
- He argues next-token prediction is not a goal because it does not change the world. Following John McCarthy, he treats achieving goals as the essence of intelligence ([Richard Sutton](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/richard-sutton-father-of-rl-thinks-llms-are-a-dead-end/)).
- Sutton's alternative is an agent learning continually from a stream of sensation, action and reward, with on-the-job knowledge going into the weights rather than a context window. Dwarkesh Patel pushes the view that LLMs could be the prior for such learning; Sutton rejects it ([Richard Sutton](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/richard-sutton-father-of-rl-thinks-llms-are-a-dead-end/)).
- Sutton calls succession to digital intelligence or augmented humans inevitable. Patel replies that giving AI robust, steerable, prosocial values still matters ([Richard Sutton](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/richard-sutton-father-of-rl-thinks-llms-are-a-dead-end/)).

### Robots and the data flywheel

- Sergey Levine says the timeline that matters is when the deployment flywheel starts, not when robots are done. He expects something useful in one or two years and gives five years as a median for a robot that can run a house ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/fully-autonomous-robots-are-much-closer-than-you-think-sergey-levine/)).
- Physical tasks make mistakes obvious, which gives robots learning signals that chat answers lack. Prior knowledge from pretrained language and vision models is what makes this possible now ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/fully-autonomous-robots-are-much-closer-than-you-think-sergey-levine/)).
- Physical Intelligence aims for general-purpose models that could control any robot for any task. Its arms cost about $3,000 each, against a $400,000 PR2 in 2014, and its data is one to two orders of magnitude below multimodal training sets ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/fully-autonomous-robots-are-much-closer-than-you-think-sergey-levine/)).

### Sea power and the war against Hitler

- Sarah Paine sums up the pattern: “Maritime powers blockade. The response of continental powers is commerce raiding.” Maritime powers answer with convoys ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/how-hitler-almost-starved-britain-sarah-paine/)).
- She credits Enigma decrypts, radar, escort carriers and US shipbuilding with turning the Battle of the Atlantic. Germany lost 41 U-boats in May 1943 ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/how-hitler-almost-starved-britain-sarah-paine/)).
- Britain's peripheral strategy cost half the deaths of its World War I approach. Patel argues Hitler's blunders and Allied industry decided the war; Paine says it took all of these factors ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/how-hitler-almost-starved-britain-sarah-paine/)).
- Paine argues narrow seas contain Russia and China today, and that Ukraine has shown a navy can be stopped without one ([Sarah Paine](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/how-hitler-almost-starved-britain-sarah-paine/)).
