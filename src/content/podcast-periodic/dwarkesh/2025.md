## Overview

In 2025 the Dwarkesh Podcast staged a long argument about how fast AI arrives, from the AI 2027 scenario to guests who put AGI a decade or more away. Later in the year the question shifted to what models still lack: continual learning and generalisation. China and Sarah Paine's history lectures ran alongside.

## Main themes

### How soon, and how fast

- Scott Alexander and Daniel Kokotajlo defend AI 2027, saying “coding is what starts the intelligence explosion”, with a US-China race deciding whether alignment gets fixed ([AI 2027](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/ai-2027-month-by-month-model-of-intelligence-explosion-scott-alexander/)).
- Ege Erdil and Tamay Besiroglu see no software-only explosion and put a full remote worker around 2045 ([AGI is still 30 years away](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/agi-is-still-30-years-away-ege-erdil-tamay-besiroglu/)).
- Mark Zuckerberg expects AI to write most of Meta's AI code within 12-18 months ([Mark Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/04/mark-zuckerberg-ai-will-write-most-meta-code-in-18-months/)).
- Sholto Douglas and Trenton Bricken expect white-collar automation within five years ([Is RL + LLMs enough for AGI?](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/05/is-rl-llms-enough-for-agi-sholto-douglas-trenton-bricken/)).

### Continual learning and the end of easy scaling

- Jeff Dean pictures many teams training modular pieces that plug into one larger model ([Jeff Dean & Noam Shazeer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/jeff-dean-noam-shazeer-25-years-at-google-from-pagerank-to-agi/)).
- Richard Sutton calls LLMs a dead end that imitates people instead of learning from experience ([Richard Sutton](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/09/richard-sutton-father-of-rl-thinks-llms-are-a-dead-end/)).
- Andrej Karpathy says agents need about a decade because models lack continual learning and reliability ([Andrej Karpathy](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/andrej-karpathy-agi-is-still-a-decade-away/)).
- Ilya Sutskever says pre-training is running out of steam and poor generalisation is the open problem ([Ilya Sutskever](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/11/ilya-sutskever-we-re-moving-from-the-age-of-scaling-to-the-age-of-rese/)); Adam Marblestone looks to the brain's reward functions ([Adam Marblestone](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/12/adam-marblestone-ai-is-missing-something-fundamental-about-the-brain/)).

### AI and economic growth

- Tyler Cowen says “It's very hard to get explosive economic growth for any reason, AI or not” ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/tyler-cowen-the-1-bottleneck-to-ai-progress-is-humans/)).
- Satya Nadella dismisses AGI milestones: “The real benchmark is: the world growing at 10%.” ([Satya Nadella, February](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/02/satya-nadella-microsoft-s-agi-plan-quantum-breakthrough/)).
- In November Nadella explains multi-model infrastructure and seven more years of access to OpenAI models ([Satya Nadella, November](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/11/satya-nadella-how-microsoft-is-preparing-for-agi/)).

### China: party, economy and energy

- Victor Shih argues the CCP puts preserving its power above technical merit, including on AI ([Victor Shih](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/05/xi-jinping-s-paranoid-approach-to-agi-debt-crisis-politburo-politics-v/)).
- Arthur Kroeber describes the state as a giant VC fund backing fierce domestic competition ([Arthur Kroeber](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/06/why-china-s-manufacturing-economy-is-dominating-arthur-kroeber/)).
- Casey Handmer argues off-grid solar plus batteries will power most new AI data centres ([Casey Handmer](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/08/china-is-killing-the-us-on-energy-does-that-mean-they-ll-win-agi-casey/)).

### History of great-power strategy

- Sarah Paine's lectures cover Japan's missing grand strategy, Mao, sea power and Russia's role in holding China back ([Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/), [How Russia sabotaged China's rise](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/10/sarah-paine-how-russia-sabotaged-china-s-rise/)).
- Stephen Kotkin argues holding power made Stalin, and that Leninist regimes cannot buy legitimacy with growth ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/07/stephen-kotkin-how-stalin-became-the-most-powerful-dictator-in-history/)).
