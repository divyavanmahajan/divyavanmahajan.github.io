## Overview

April on the Dwarkesh Podcast moved between the business of AI chips, the arithmetic of running models and the history of science. Jensen Huang defended Nvidia's position and argued against export controls, Reiner Pope explained serving costs from first principles, and Michael Nielsen questioned whether AI can simply automate discovery.

## Main themes

### Nvidia, China and export controls

- Jensen Huang argues that conceding China, which he puts at about 40% of the world's tech industry, “is a disservice to our national security” as well as to US technology leadership ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).
- Dwarkesh Patel counters that any marginal compute helps China train better models, citing Anthropic's Mythos and its reported vulnerability findings. Huang replies that Mythos was trained on fairly mundane capacity that China has in abundance ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).
- Huang calls the CUDA ecosystem Nvidia's great treasure, built over 20 years of mostly loss-making investment. He calls Anthropic the unique case behind TPU and Trainium growth ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).

### Supply chain bottlenecks clear

- Huang says CoWoS is no longer a bottleneck because the industry doubled capacity several times over two years, and that EUV and logic can scale within two or three years given a demand signal ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).
- He sees energy policy and trades such as plumbers and electricians as harder constraints, and says GPUs are allocated first in, first out rather than to the highest bidder ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).

### The maths of training and serving LLMs

- Reiner Pope of MatX shows that batching drives cost: the break-even batch is roughly 300 times a model's sparsity ratio, about 2,000 to 3,000 sequences for DeepSeek-style models ([Reiner Pope](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/reiner-pope-the-math-behind-how-llms-are-trained-and-served/)).
- He describes serving as a train: “A new train departs every 20 milliseconds”, a cycle set by HBM capacity divided by bandwidth ([Reiner Pope](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/reiner-pope-the-math-behind-how-llms-are-trained-and-served/)).
- Pope says KV caches can be neither amortised across the batch nor sharded across pipeline stages. He reads API prices to infer cache sizes and storage tiers, and estimates frontier models are trained on about 100 times more tokens than Chinchilla-optimal ([Reiner Pope](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/reiner-pope-the-math-behind-how-llms-are-trained-and-served/)).

### Why science resists automation

- Michael Nielsen says the Michelson-Morley experiment ruled out some ether theories but not all, and that Einstein later was not sure he had known of it ([Michael Nielsen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/michael-nielsen-how-science-actually-progresses/)).
- Nielsen argues there is no fixed method, so many research programmes must be kept alive: the reasoning that found Neptune failed for Mercury ([Michael Nielsen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/michael-nielsen-how-science-actually-progresses/)).
- He credits most of AlphaFold's success to the Protein Data Bank rather than the AI, and suggests such models may be a new type of object. Patel doubts raw gradient descent would make a shift like Ptolemy to Copernicus ([Michael Nielsen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/michael-nielsen-how-science-actually-progresses/)).
