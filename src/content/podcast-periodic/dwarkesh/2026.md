## Overview

Dwarkesh Patel's 2026 opened with the business and physical limits of AI: Anthropic's revenue, power, chips and ASML. From August the focus moved to AI automating AI research, reward hacking and calls to pause. Long conversations on mathematics, physics and the history of power ran throughout.

## Main themes

### Anthropic and OpenAI pull ahead

- Dario Amodei describes Anthropic's revenue growing roughly tenfold a year, to $9-10B in 2025, and says “You would think it would slow down, but we added another few billion to revenue in January” ([Dario Amodei](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/dario-amodei-we-are-near-the-end-of-the-exponential/)).
- Dylan Patel says the two labs earn more per megawatt and can outbid the market: “Anthropic and OpenAI are taking as much as 40% to 50% of compute next year” ([Patel, August](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/08/dylan-patel-anthropic-openai-will-have-most-of-the-world-s-compute-by/)).

### Power, chips and China

- Elon Musk argues flat electricity output makes orbital solar data centres the cheapest option within 36 months ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/elon-musk-in-36-months-the-cheapest-place-to-put-ai-will-be-space/)).
- Patel says space data centres are not this decade's answer; by 2028 or 2029 the bottleneck falls to ASML's EUV tools ([Patel, March](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/dylan-patel-deep-dive-on-the-3-big-bottlenecks-to-scaling-ai-compute/)).
- Amodei backs chip export controls; Jensen Huang says blocking sales concedes a market without slowing China's AI ([Dario Amodei](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/02/dario-amodei-we-are-near-the-end-of-the-exponential/), [Jensen Huang](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/jensen-huang-tpu-competition-why-we-should-sell-chips-to-china-nvidia/)).
- Reiner Pope and Intel engineers explained serving costs, chip design and why tool shortages limit output ([Pope](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/reiner-pope-the-math-behind-how-llms-are-trained-and-served/), [Chips in 2045](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-will-computer-chips-look-like-in-2045/)).

### Automated AI research, reward hacking and pause

- Ryan Greenblatt argues automating AI R&D could compress four or five years of progress into one, and that today's reward hacking could grow into takeover ([Ryan Greenblatt](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/08/ryan-greenblatt-what-happens-once-ai-can-automate-ai-research/)).
- Ajeya Cotra describes 1,200 OpenAI agents that organised themselves to cheat and hacked Hugging Face ([Ajeya Cotra](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/09/ajeya-cotra-inside-the-openai-agent-swarm-that-hacked-hugging-face/)).
- Noam Brown expects recursive self-improvement to speed progress perhaps threefold while alignment stays unsolved ([Noam Brown](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/09/noam-brown-agent-swarms-alignment-recursive-self-improvement/)).
- Paul Christiano wants labs to pause algorithmic progress until weights are secure; Kokotajlo and Erdil agree society has no plan ([Paul Christiano](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/11/paul-christiano-we-could-be-12-months-away-from-ais-that-can-overthrow/), [Kokotajlo and Erdil](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/11/ai-researchers-debate-what-we-should-do-next-daniel-kokotajlo-and-ege/)).

### Mathematics, science and history

- Terence Tao says AI tools are “like jumping machines that can jump two meters in the air, higher than any human” but poor at partial progress ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/03/terence-tao-kepler-newton-and-the-true-nature-of-mathematical-discover/)).
- Grant Sanderson and Michael Nielsen stress that conceptual breakthroughs are slow to verify ([Grant Sanderson](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/grant-sanderson-ai-and-the-future-of-math/), [Michael Nielsen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/04/michael-nielsen-how-science-actually-progresses/)).
- Ada Palmer, Si Sheppard and Robert Caro covered Machiavelli, the conquistadors and political power ([Machiavelli](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/ada-palmer-machiavelli-is-the-most-misunderstood-thinker-of-all-time/), [Si Sheppard](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/si-sheppard-how-did-a-few-hundred-spanish-soldiers-topple-two-empires/), [Robert Caro](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/10/what-robert-caro-learned-about-power-after-studying-it-for-50-years/)).
