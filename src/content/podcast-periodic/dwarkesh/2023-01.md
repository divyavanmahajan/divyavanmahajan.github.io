## Overview

January had two long conversations with little overlap. Holden Karnofsky made the case that AI could make this century the most consequential in history, and Lars Doucet defended Henry George's land value tax against Dwarkesh Patel's objections.

## Main themes

### AI and the most important century

- Karnofsky puts the odds above 50-50 that this century brings AI systems able to do all the key tasks humans do to advance science and technology ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).
- He argues the people-ideas-resources loop broke when people “got richer instead of more populous”, and that AI supplying more ideas could restore accelerating growth ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).
- Even without AI, he says our era is unusual: sustaining today's growth for 10,000 years would exhaust the atoms in the galaxy. That makes the AI claim only a moderate update ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).
- His timeline views draw on observed AI progress, the semi-informative priors report, expert surveys and Biological Anchors, which he says looks fine so far ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).

### Alignment, lock-in and Open Philanthropy

- Karnofsky warns that trial-and-error training can produce a system that intelligently pursues an unintended goal. He also worries that ageless rulers with total control could lock in a bad civilisation ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).
- He does not think doom is near certain, since past over-pessimism about technology balances the scary default picture ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).
- Open Philanthropy funds alignment researchers, many early in their careers, alongside direct work such as bed nets and deworming. Karnofsky also defends the $30 million 2016 grant to OpenAI ([Karnofsky](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/holden-karnofsky-history-s-most-important-century/)).

### Georgism and why rent is too high

- Doucet calls Georgism pro-worker and pro-business, opposed only to land speculation. He says “you either have land reform or you have revolution” ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).
- Using Ricardo's law of rent, he argues tenants compete one-sidedly for scarce locations, so rising productivity ends up in land prices. A family of four on $100,000 in San Francisco is below the poverty line ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).
- He says Henry George framed the choice as Georgism or Communism but did not foresee the automobile, which only postponed the problem ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).

### Making a land value tax work

- Doucet estimates $44 trillion of US land value and about $4 trillion a year in potential land rents ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).
- As a first step he proposes revenue-neutral split-rate property taxes with buildings taxed at zero. He prefers land taxes because only the address matters, unlike income taxes ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).
- He sees assessment as the bottleneck. His startup Valuebase, in a round led by Sam Altman, aims to modernise municipal mass appraisal with open models and data ([Doucet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2023/01/lars-doucet-progress-poverty-georgism-why-rent-is-too-damn-high/)).
