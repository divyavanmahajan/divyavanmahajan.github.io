## Overview

June on the Dwarkesh Podcast had three long conversations with little overlap. Two economists debated what human work and income look like after AGI, and Grant Sanderson looked at where AI stands in mathematics. In between, historian Ada Palmer reread Machiavelli as a patriot writing about how to keep power.

## Main themes

### Work and wages after AGI

- Dwarkesh Patel notes that for centuries about 60% of the economy has been paid to humans in wages. Alex Imas calls this stability a Kaldor fact, though some suspect it is an accounting artefact ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).
- Imas argues that relational services, where a human in the loop is part of the value, could stay scarce. His experiments show a human-made art print loses value when 500 copies exist while an AI print does not, and he says the doctor giving a diagnosis, not the ballerina, is the right reference class ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).
- Phil Trammell expects new varieties of machine-made goods to keep the human share small. He argues that an agent that never satiates in capital will save more and end up holding most wealth ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).

### Sharing the gains

- Both guests see a messy middle, with mass automation but little new wealth, as a narrow window. Imas cites phone operators, automated over 20 years from 1920 to 1940, as a slow-drip example ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).
- Imas worries UBI makes people dependent on whoever holds power. Of the alternative he says: “That’s the problem of universal basic capital: targeting.” Trammell suggests a broad tax used to buy and hand out shares of AI companies ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).
- For poorer countries such as Nigeria, the guests favour indexing AI gains, helped by open models and IPOs, over retraining alone ([What remains scarce after AGI](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/alex-imas-and-phil-trammell-what-remains-scarce-after-agi/)).

### AI and the future of mathematics

- Dwarkesh says “AI has been making the fastest progress in mathematics out of any other field”. Sanderson adds that progress is spiky even within math: olympiad geometry fell early, combinatorics still resists ([AI and the future of math](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/grant-sanderson-ai-and-the-future-of-math/)).
- Sanderson finds it odd that models with superhuman breadth rarely connect fields, and expects filling in those connections to be most of the useful progress over the next five years ([AI and the future of math](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/grant-sanderson-ai-and-the-future-of-math/)).
- New concepts are harder to reward. Using group theory, Sanderson and Dwarkesh describe a verification loop of about a hundred years, and Sanderson suggests rewarding small, compressed concepts, perhaps via Kolmogorov complexity ([AI and the future of math](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/grant-sanderson-ai-and-the-future-of-math/)).

### Machiavelli and keeping power

- Ada Palmer argues The Prince is “a manual not of how to gain power, but of how to keep power”, written privately for Florence's rulers ([Machiavelli](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/ada-palmer-machiavelli-is-the-most-misunderstood-thinker-of-all-time/)).
- She says Machiavelli thought Cesare Borgia did everything right and lost only to fortune, and that Borgia's neutral justice made his rule popular with ordinary people ([Machiavelli](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/ada-palmer-machiavelli-is-the-most-misunderstood-thinker-of-all-time/)).
- Patronage held Renaissance society together. Romans rioted for more nepotism, and most capital convictions ended in fines because patrons intervened ([Machiavelli](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2026/06/ada-palmer-machiavelli-is-the-most-misunderstood-thinker-of-all-time/)).
