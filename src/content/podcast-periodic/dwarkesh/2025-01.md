## Overview

January belonged to Naval War College historian Sarah Paine, who gave three lectures with Q&A on the Cold War in South Asia, Japan in the Second World War and Mao's conquest of China. Her common thread is that strategy fails when a country ignores the other side and confuses battlefield wins with strategic success. The month opened with Tyler Cowen arguing that AI will lift growth only modestly because people and institutions are the binding constraint.

## Main themes

### Know the other side

- Paine argues that before intervening you must map who is whose primary adversary. Russia and India shared a China problem, Pakistan and China shared an India problem, and the US shared neither, so its aid to one side alienated the other ([The war for India](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-the-war-for-india-lecture-interview/)).
- She warns against analysing wars only through one's own actions, which she calls half-court tennis, and against mirror imaging another culture ([Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/)).
- Limited versus unlimited objectives came up in both of these lectures. Paine says China gained territory in 1962 but made India a permanent enemy, and that India now sees China as its main adversary, which may align it with the US ([The war for India](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-the-war-for-india-lecture-interview/)).

### Japan without a grand strategy

- Paine reads the Bushido literature as being about deportment and honourable death rather than winning, and says its stress on willpower “denigrates strategy” ([Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/)).
- She calls Pearl Harbor an excellent military operation and a strategic disaster. Japan never produced more than 1/13th of US steel and coal output and ran about one support soldier per rifleman ([Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/)).
- Army-navy rivalry ran deep: separate, secret war plans from 1906 and regular liaison meetings only in 1944. Dwarkesh Patel asked whether the oil embargo made war avoidable, and Paine was sceptical ([Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/)).

### Mao, Chiang and the Chinese Civil War

- Paine calls Mao a military genius who reunified China and “the psychopath incapable of running an economy in peacetime” ([How Mao conquered China](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-how-mao-conquered-china-lecture-interview/)).
- She credits propaganda, survey-based land reform and people's war, and says Chiang Kai-shek lost because the peasantry hated him while the Communists handed out land ([How Mao conquered China](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-how-mao-conquered-china-lecture-interview/)).
- The Great Leap Forward caused China's only nationwide famine, with 40 million deaths, driven by policy rather than weather. Patel argued the US underfunded Chiang; Paine said nation-building in China was not feasible ([How Mao conquered China](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-how-mao-conquered-china-lecture-interview/)).

### Humans as the bottleneck to AI growth

- Tyler Cowen expects AI to add about half a percentage point to annual growth, not 20%-plus, because regulation, energy and slow-moving sectors bind once intelligence is abundant ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/tyler-cowen-the-1-bottleneck-to-ai-progress-is-humans/)).
- He notes that government, healthcare, education and nonprofits make up about half the US economy, and that market data such as real interest rates look strikingly normal ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/tyler-cowen-the-1-bottleneck-to-ai-progress-is-humans/)).
- Cowen says he now writes mostly for AI readers, and names war as his biggest worry about new technology ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/tyler-cowen-the-1-bottleneck-to-ai-progress-is-humans/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Sarah Paine — Why Japan lost WWII](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-why-japan-lost-wwii-lecture-interview/): the clearest case study of culture shaping strategy, with hard production and logistics figures.
- [Sarah Paine — How Mao conquered China](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-how-mao-conquered-china-lecture-interview/): a balanced reading of Mao as both strategist and catastrophe, plus a lively debate on US policy.
- [Sarah Paine — The war for India](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/sarah-paine-the-war-for-india-lecture-interview/): a framework for judging alliances by shared primary adversaries, with lessons for a second Cold War.
- [Tyler Cowen — The #1 bottleneck to AI progress is humans](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/01/tyler-cowen-the-1-bottleneck-to-ai-progress-is-humans/): a sceptical economist's case against explosive AI growth, with Patel pushing back.
