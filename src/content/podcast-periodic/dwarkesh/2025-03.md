## Overview

March had one long interview and one AMA. Harvard evolutionary biologist Joseph Henrich argued that humans succeed through cumulative culture and large, connected populations rather than individual brainpower. For the launch of his book The Scaling Era, Dwarkesh Patel took questions from Anthropic researchers Sholto Douglas and Trenton Bricken on AI research, careers and how he runs the show.

## Main themes

### Collective brains, not individual intelligence

- Henrich notes that Neanderthals had larger brains yet lost, and that “human brain size has been declining for the last 10,000 years” as knowledge spread across specialised people ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).
- He explains the collective brain: innovation depends on a population's size and interconnectedness, and collective brains can shrink when groups lose contact, as with the Dorset ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).
- Henrich uses cassava processing to show practices that work without anyone knowing why, and argues people underestimate copying errors as a source of novelty ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).

### The Church, kinship and the Industrial Revolution

- Henrich's WEIRD thesis: the Church banned polygyny and cousin marriage out to sixth cousins and freed inheritance, breaking up Europe's intensive kinship into nuclear families ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).
- He links this to guilds, cities, universities and trust in strangers, and to the consolidation of Europe's collective brain that led to the Industrial Revolution. He says he found little evidence the Church intended to destroy kinship ties ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).
- Henrich warns the impersonal world of WEIRD institutions is fragile, citing declining moral universalism in rural US areas since 2008 ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).

### What this means for AI

- Patel suggests AIs could copy themselves and share knowledge at scale. Henrich worries about too much homogeneity, and both agree networks of minds matter more than a single superintelligence ([Joseph Henrich](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/joseph-henrich-humans-defeated-smarter-species-with-cultural-evolution/)).
- In the AMA, Patel says he knows of no LLM that has connected facts across fields the way humans have. Douglas replies that the field has not yet trained models for discovery in a meaningful, scaled way, and Bricken points to primitive memory scaffolding ([AMA](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/ama-career-advice-given-agi-how-i-research-ft-sholto-trenton/)).

### Careers and running the show

- Douglas expects each person's leverage to grow until they manage teams of AIs, so “deep technical knowledge in fields will still matter in four years” ([AMA](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/ama-career-advice-given-agi-how-i-research-ft-sholto-trenton/)).
- Patel is sceptical of career advice in general and argues models still lack the long-term coherence to run a company. Under fast timelines he expects a six-month window of very important decisions ([AMA](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/ama-career-advice-given-agi-how-i-research-ft-sholto-trenton/)).
- Patel says YouTube Shorts drove at least half the podcast's growth, and he picks guests he wants to spend one to two weeks researching ([AMA](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2025/03/ama-career-advice-given-agi-how-i-research-ft-sholto-trenton/)).
