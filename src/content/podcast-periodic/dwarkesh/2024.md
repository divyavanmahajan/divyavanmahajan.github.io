## Overview

In 2024 the Dwarkesh Podcast kept asking when AGI arrives. A second strand followed the physical side of scaling: power, data centres and chips. Safety ran through the year, from evaluations to takeover risk. Between the AI episodes came history, energy and institutions.

## Main themes

### AGI timelines and what scaling can do

- Hassabis says large models are not enough on their own and wants AlphaZero-style planning on top ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- Schulman expects post-training and long-horizon RL to drive the next gains, and admits there is no firm plan if AGI comes in two or three years ([John Schulman](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/05/john-schulman-openai-cofounder-reasoning-rlhf-plan-for-2027-agi/)).
- Chollet argues LLMs scale skill through memorisation rather than intelligence, and launches the ARC Prize with Mike Knoop ([Francois Chollet](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/06/francois-chollet-why-the-biggest-ai-models-can-t-solve-simple-puzzles/)).
- Gwern describes intelligence as search powered by compute, which is why he backed scaling early ([Gwern](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/11/gwern-anonymous-writer-who-predicted-ai-trajectory-on-12k-year-salary/)).

### Power, data centres and chips

- Zuckerberg says energy will bind before capital, and that a gigawatt cluster “would be the size of a meaningful nuclear power plant only going towards training a model” ([Mark Zuckerberg](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/04/mark-zuckerberg-llama-3-10b-models-caesar-augustus-1-gw-datacenters/)).
- Dylan Patel projects the biggest cluster growing from 100K GPUs to 300-500K the next year, and sees under-investing as the worse risk for big tech ([Asianometry & Dylan Patel](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/10/asianometry-dylan-patel-how-the-semiconductor-industry-actually-works/)).

### Superintelligence and national power

- Aschenbrenner predicts AGI around 2027 and says “people vastly underrate the chances of this looking like a government project” ([Leopold Aschenbrenner](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/06/leopold-aschenbrenner-2027-agi-china-us-super-intelligence-race-the-re/)).
- He calls lab secrets extremely easy to steal and arms control unstable in a close race with China (same episode).
- Tony Blair calls AI the central governing task and doubts leaders would know where to begin in a crisis ([Tony Blair](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/06/tony-blair-why-political-leaders-keep-failing-at-major-change/)).

### Alignment, interpretability and takeover risk

- Hassabis says “we don’t have good enough evaluations and benchmarks for things like if the system can deceive you”, and proposes hardened sandboxes ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/demis-hassabis-scaling-superhuman-ais-alphazero-atop-llms-alphafold/)).
- Trenton Bricken hopes dictionary learning can find a deception circuit before a future model is approved ([Sholto Douglas & Trenton Bricken](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/03/sholto-douglas-trenton-bricken-how-llms-actually-think/)).
- Joe Carlsmith locates takeover risk in untestable motivations and concentrated power, and argues for balance of power ([Joe Carlsmith](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/08/joe-carlsmith-preventing-an-ai-takeover/)).

### History, energy and institutions

- Tyler Cowen expects AI to speed growth but rules out sustained 10% growth because of bottlenecks ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/01/tyler-cowen-hayek-keynes-smith-on-ai-animal-spirits-anarchy-growth/)).
- Daniel Yergin says energy security is ignored until it hits, and expects a slow, messy transition ([Daniel Yergin](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/09/daniel-yergin-oil-destroyed-hitler-fracking-destroyed-putin/)).
- Patrick Collison and Patrick McKenzie both trace progress and failure to culture and incentives in institutions ([Patrick Collison](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/02/patrick-collison-why-silicon-valley-s-most-talented-should-leave/), [Patrick McKenzie](/podcasts/dwarkesh/2024/07/patrick-mckenzie-money-laundering-big-tech-censorship-sbf-japan/)).
