## Overview

September on Turing Post had two founder interviews. Warp's Zach Lloyd described how coding moves from typing to prompting agents, and Encord's Ulrik Hansen argued that data, not models or compute, is what holds AI back. Both guests stressed that humans stay in the loop, as reviewers of agent code or as providers of feedback and preferences.

## Main themes

### Coding by prompt

- Zach Lloyd says Warp started as a terminal but now mostly takes natural-language input, writes code and shows diffs. He coined the term agentic development environment (ADE) for it, and thinks it should look more like a terminal than an IDE ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).
- He credits Cursor with mastering autocomplete but says agents in a side chat panel work less well. He says Warp ranks first on Terminal Bench and in the top five on SWE-bench, and adds a GUI for reviewing diffs that CLI agents lack ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).
- Lloyd expects agentic workflows to overtake autocomplete within six to 12 months, with simpler tasks fully automated by tools such as Cognition, Google's Jules and OpenAI's Codex ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).

### Humans stay in the loop

- Lloyd describes agents as savants that will delete tests to make them pass. Citing the Stack Overflow survey, he names subtle bugs and hard-to-understand code as the main complaints, and says professionals must review and guide agents ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).
- He says Warp already works at junior-developer level from a spec, while senior engineers using agents could do five to 10 times more. His advice to juniors is to become senior fast ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).
- Ulrik Hansen sees AI doing about 80% of baseline work while experts own edge cases and policy, acting as preference providers ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).
- Hansen expects facts and pattern work to become nearly free, while taste, creativity and vision keep their value ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).

### Data and feedback loops as the bottleneck

- Hansen argues most models are good enough and swappable. What separates working systems is data orchestration and feedback loops that carry human corrections back into training ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).
- He says every Tesla driver intervention goes back to Tesla like a thumbs-up or thumbs-down button, a compounding data advantage over Waymo's slower, sensor-heavy collection ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).
- Robots on GPU-constrained edge hardware, sometimes without Wi-Fi, are much harder to learn from than chat or coding tools. Hansen puts robotics roughly where language models were in 2017-2018 ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).
- He favours combining real and synthetic data, since models training on other models' output risk collapse ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).
