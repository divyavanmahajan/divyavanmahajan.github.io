## Overview

Turing Post's 2025 interviews were mostly one founder or executive answering a single when-will-we question. The spring and summer episodes dealt with hallucinations, memory, coding agents and the trust enterprises need before agents can act. By December the show moved to physical AI, AI browsers and AI for science, with NVIDIA, OpenAI and Axiom Math as guests. One idea ran through the whole year: models are good enough, and data, context, infrastructure and control now limit progress.

## Main themes

### Physical AI, world models and visual memory

- Spencer Huang says robots need NVIDIA's three computers, conventional simulation and world models together. He warns that in world models “the physics is never grounded, and so it's never consistent” ([NVIDIA's physical AI](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/spencer-huang-nvidia-s-big-plan-for-physical-ai-simulation-world-model/)).
- Memories.ai's Shawn Shen argues embodied AI needs a separate on-device visual memory that indexes and retrieves what it sees ([Memories](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/are-memories-the-missing-half-of-ai-brain/)).
- Encord's Ulrik Hansen had already argued that data orchestration and human feedback, not models, limit real-world AI ([Encord](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-really-blocks-ai-progress-ulrik-hansen-from-encord-thinks-it-s/)).

### Trusting and controlling agents in the enterprise

- Microsoft's Eric Boyd expects agents to earn trust one task at a time through guardrails, identity and governance ([Eric Boyd](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/06/when-will-we-fully-trust-ai-to-lead-a-conversation-with-eric-boyd-cvp/)). Toloka's Olga Megorskaya says humans stay essential as experts and as the benchmark ([Toloka](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/06/why-ai-still-needs-us-a-conversation-with-olga-megorskaya-ceo-of-tolok/)).
- Rubrik's Anneka Gupta warns that agents are “programmed to execute an outcome without regard to the changes they'll actually make to achieve that outcome.” She calls for visibility, guardrails and quick undo ([Rubrik](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/can-we-control-ai-that-controls-itself-anneka-gupta-from-rubrik-on/)).
- VAST Data's Renen Hallak argues agents need a new operating system that ties data, compute and policy together ([AI OS](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/why-do-we-need-a-special-operating-system-for-ai/)).

### Agents on the web, and the protocols behind them

- Webflow's Linda Tong says bots are becoming a main audience for websites, so sites need agent-ready structure and AEO alongside SEO ([Webflow](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/08/ai-changed-the-web-here-s-how-to-build-for-it-a-conversation-with-lind/)).
- Block's Alex Hancock argues models are smart enough already, and that protocols like MCP and good context let agents act reliably ([Goose and MCP](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/08/how-to-make-ai-actually-do-things-alex-hancock-block-goose-mcp-steerin/)).
- Ben Goodger calls ChatGPT Atlas “like a Netscape 1.0 moment for this new era of the web”, with agent mode using either site DOM or computer-use vision ([Atlas](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/ben-goodger-on-chatgpt-atlas-why-openai-built-an-ai-browser-for-agi/)).

### Coding by prompt

- Replit's Amjad Masad argues agents are the next abstraction above code, and that tiny AI-augmented teams will build valuable companies ([Replit](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-stop-coding-a-conversation-with-amjad-masad-ceo-and-co-fo/)).
- Warp's Zach Lloyd sees development moving from hand to prompt, but wants professional developers in tight review loops rather than vibe coding ([Warp](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/09/what-is-the-future-of-coding-warp-s-vision/)).

### Accuracy, memory and inference cost

- Lamini's Sharon Zhou treats hallucinations as solvable for well-defined enterprise tasks. Vectara's Amr Awadallah says they won't disappear, so RAG with access control and guardian agents are still needed ([Lamini](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/04/when-will-we-solve-ai-hallucinations-a-conversation-with-sharon-zhou-c/), [Vectara](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/08/beyond-the-hype-what-silicon-valley-gets-wrong-about-rag-amr-awadallah/)).
- Pinecone's Edo Liberty frames AI memory as a storage problem. Predibase's Devvret Rishi expects specialised open models that keep learning in production ([Pinecone](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-give-ai-true-memory-a-conversation-with-edo-liberty-ceo-a/), [Predibase](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/05/when-will-we-train-once-and-learn-forever-insights-from-dev-rishi-ceo/)).
- Fireworks AI's Lin Qiao says GPU inference costs keep most GenAI apps from becoming viable businesses ([Fireworks](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/08/when-will-inference-feel-like-electricity-lin-qiao-co-founder-ceo-of-f/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Spencer Huang: NVIDIA's Big Plan for Physical AI](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/spencer-huang-nvidia-s-big-plan-for-physical-ai-simulation-world-model/): the year's clearest case for simulation plus world models as a data flywheel for robots.
- [Ben Goodger on ChatGPT Atlas](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/ben-goodger-on-chatgpt-atlas-why-openai-built-an-ai-browser-for-agi/): the head of engineering on why OpenAI sees the browser as the first body for an AI brain.
- [Can We Control AI That Controls Itself?](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/can-we-control-ai-that-controls-itself-anneka-gupta-from-rubrik-on/): practical advice on logging, governance and undoing agent mistakes.
- [What AI Is Missing for Real Reasoning?](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/what-ai-is-missing-for-real-reasoning-axiom-math-s-carina-hong-on-how/): Carina Hong's design for an AI mathematician combining Lean proofs, a knowledge base and a conjecture model.
- [The Future of Cancer Diagnosis](/podcasts/turing-post/2025/12/the-future-of-cancer-diagnosis-digital-pathology-and-ai/): Akash Parvatikar on digitising pathology first and building explainable AI assistants after.
