## Overview

April was a quiet month on Turing Post, with two unrelated conversations. NVIDIA's Sanja Fidler covered world models, simulation and physical AI, recorded at GTC. CK-12 co-founder Neeru Khosla explained how AI lets educators see where students go wrong.

## Main themes

### World models and learned simulation

- Fidler says transformers and world models are not rivals: the transformer is an architecture, while world modelling is a task, a simulator that predicts what happens next ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).
- She traces self-driving simulation from artist-built scenes that took two months per intersection to NeRF and Gaussian-splat reconstructions. NVIDIA runs two million of those a day, but they cannot change how other road users behave ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).
- Alpa Dreams, announced at GTC, learns simulation from general video and then driving data, and runs in real time. It still depends on separate map-and-cuboid simulation that can fall out of sync ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).

### Physical AI as the frontier

- Fidler says “I think for us, the physical AI is the frontier, that AGI is definitely not there yet.” Data is the upper bound, and touch data for robots is scarce ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).
- She expects autonomous driving at mass scale in probably under five years, while general robotics looks like driving did in 2017-18 ([World models](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/transformers-are-not-the-end-game-world-models-physical-ai-and-ai-s-ne/)).

### AI that shows how students think

- Khosla says earlier technology gave richer formats but never revealed what was happening in students' minds. With AI, deep knowledge tracing can show exactly where a child holds a misconception ([Education](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/ai-could-change-education-forever-neeru-khosla-explains-why/)).
- CK-12's tutor Flexi has more than 50 million student users. Its tagged questions surface misconceptions, such as how the sun burns without oxygen in space ([Education](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/ai-could-change-education-forever-neeru-khosla-explains-why/)).
- Khosla argues “prompting by itself is a great learning tool” and that language skills now matter for using AI well ([Education](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/ai-could-change-education-forever-neeru-khosla-explains-why/)).
- She says schools resisted CK-12 partly because free looked like junk, and expects change to take time: “I think within five years we’ll start to see deeper systemic change. But we have to change the system.” ([Education](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/04/ai-could-change-education-forever-neeru-khosla-explains-why/)).
