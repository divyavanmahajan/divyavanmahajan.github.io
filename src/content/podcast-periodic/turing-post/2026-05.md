## Overview

May on Turing Post had three long interviews. Two were about who gets to build with AI now that coding agents work well: Clem Delangue of Hugging Face and Ammaar Reshi of Google AI Studio. The third, with Eric Ries, turned to how companies keep their mission once success attracts people who want to extract value.

## Main themes

### Agents become users and builders

- Delangue says the biggest change during his leave was the adoption of coding agents. He would not be surprised if Hugging Face soon had more agent users than humans: “I wouldn’t be surprised if by the end of this year we had more agent users than human users of Hugging Face.” ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).
- He says platforms must become token-efficient for agents, so the team is prioritising CLIs, APIs and agents.md files. Hugging Face's ML Intern fine-tunes small models and passed the team's researcher interview test in half an hour ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).
- Delangue expects AI builders to grow from low millions to perhaps 100 million. He argues that if more people built AI, there would be less video slop and more work on biology, medicine and climate ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).

### Open source and local models

- Delangue says 99% of workloads today are calls to large proprietary APIs. He expects something closer to 5% frontier API use, with the rest on specialised open or local models ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).
- He argues open repositories are patched faster than proprietary systems, and that companies citing safety for staying closed often have business reasons. Mistral and Cohere, he says, benefited from releasing work openly ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).
- He opposes lobbying in Washington against open source. Without open models, datasets and libraries, he warns, only OpenAI, Anthropic and big tech could do AI, and the US would lose its lead ([Delangue](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/will-everyone-become-an-ai-builder-clem-delangue-on-hugging-face-agent/)).

### Building apps by prompting

- Reshi says models are now good enough at code and speed is the bottleneck, especially on a phone. Flash is AI Studio's default model, and compute capacity limits free use ([AI Studio](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/inside-google-ai-studio-ammaar-reshi-on-vibe-coding-agent-swarms-and-t/)).
- He wants AI Studio to hide plumbing: an app that stores movies should get Firebase without the user hearing about Firebase or OAuth. A tap-by-tap feature helps users build rich prompts ([AI Studio](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/inside-google-ai-studio-ammaar-reshi-on-vibe-coding-agent-swarms-and-t/)).
- Reshi expects single chat threads to become something like Slack, with stand-ups and agents taking Kanban tickets. He says agent interfaces will “revolve around transparency, around verification of the task, and around just direction and coordination.” ([AI Studio](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/inside-google-ai-studio-ammaar-reshi-on-vibe-coding-agent-swarms-and-t/)).

### Keeping companies incorruptible

- Ries calls companies making money without creating value corruption. He says a force he calls financial gravity pulls organisations toward mediocrity, while successful ones can become gravitational actors themselves ([Ries](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/eric-ries-on-building-incorruptible-companies-ai-disruption-and-the-fu/)).
- The book's core principle, he says, is that mission protection is “always too early until it's too late.” Advisers tell founders to wait until they have lost the leverage ([Ries](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/eric-ries-on-building-incorruptible-companies-ai-disruption-and-the-fu/)).
- On AI, Ries criticises executives rewarded for AI-driven layoffs and argues for augmenting people. He sees a possible AI mission guardian that checks a company's systems against its stated mission ([Ries](/podcasts/turing-post/2026/05/eric-ries-on-building-incorruptible-companies-ai-disruption-and-the-fu/)).
