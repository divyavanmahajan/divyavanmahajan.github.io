## Overview

March had two very different guests. Leslie Kaelbling gave an engineer's view of how robots should plan and learn, while Eric Weinstein ranged across AI risk, physics, academia and the economy, arguing that institutions have lost touch with reality.

## Main themes

### Robots: what to build in and what to learn

- Kaelbling says the question that interests her most is “what to build in and what to learn”. She treats it as an engineering question rather than a matter of dogma ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- She argues end-to-end learning is too sample-hungry, and that sample complexity falls only by narrowing the hypothesis space. She wants more priors like convolution, such as the notion that objects exist ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- She says expert systems failed because experts cannot articulate how they decide and give post-hoc stories instead ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).

### Planning with abstraction and uncertainty

- Kaelbling says you cannot plan a PhD at the level of images and torques, so spatial and temporal abstraction must shrink the state space and horizon. You plan the flight and trust you can manage the walk through the airport later ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- In belief space a robot's task becomes controlling its own beliefs, which lets it gather information deliberately, like a driver glancing over a shoulder. Optimal planning for POMDPs can be undecidable, so she accepts approximation ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).
- She also describes founding the open-access Journal of Machine Learning Research, and worries that short research horizons discourage hard multi-year problems ([Kaelbling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/leslie-kaelbling-reinforcement-learning-planning-and-robotics/)).

### Artificial outtelligence and powerful toys

- Weinstein argues the near-term danger is software that evolves under selective pressure and exploits people without understanding anything. In his words, “artificial general intelligence is not needed to parasitize us.” ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
- He calls the stability since 1945 unusual, and says a generation that never felt existential danger has become complacent about nuclear risk. He has argued for resuming above-ground nuclear testing ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).

### Stalled physics, academia and work

- Weinstein calls theoretical physics the most profound intellectual community ever built, yet says string theory has not delivered and little theory since the mid-1970s has won a Nobel through prediction ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
- He argues academics must show loyalty to the consensus, and that funders should back the people they fear using judgement rather than rules ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
- He says repetitive jobs are exposed to automation and that capitalism which abandons the median person is “flirting with revolution”. He suggests pairing hyper capitalism with hyper socialism ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/03/eric-weinstein-revolutionary-ideas-in-science-math-and-society/)).
