## Overview

August 2020 arrived soon after GPT-3, and four guests weighed in on it, mostly sceptically, with Fridman often arguing that scale might still surprise. Brain-inspired AI, neuroplasticity and how to measure intelligence formed the core of the month. Robotics, exoplanets, mortality and maths teaching filled out the rest.

## Main themes

### GPT-3 and the limits of scaling

- Dileep George says the 175-billion-parameter GPT-3 models the text world, not the world, and that scaling alone will not deliver AGI ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/)).
- François Chollet thinks GPT-3 pattern-matches new tasks to ones seen in training, and expects data rather than compute or model size to be the bottleneck ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- David Eagleman calls GPT-3 a good impersonator whose best outputs are picked by humans; Grant Sanderson expects it to struggle with maths, which needs hypothesis testing ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/), [Grant Sanderson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/grant-sanderson-math-manim-neural-networks-teaching-with-3blue1brown/)).

### What intelligence is and how to measure it

- Chollet defines intelligence as the efficiency of acquiring new skills, not skill itself. His ARC benchmark rests only on core knowledge priors; after a Kaggle competition the best score was about 20 percent ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- Chollet calls the Turing test a cop-out and thinks L5 driving is probably out of reach for pure end-to-end learning; Fridman disagrees on both points ([François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- Eagleman argues AI lacks a sense of relevance and a drive to survive, contrasting a wolf that adapts to losing a leg with the stuck Mars rover Curiosity ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/)).

### Brains as models for AI

- George says you cannot build a brain without a theory of it. His Recursive Cortical Network broke text CAPTCHAs and learned MNIST from tens to hundreds of examples ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/)).
- Eagleman describes the brain as livewired, constantly rewiring around its data. His company Neosensory sells a wristband that lets deaf users hear through their skin ([David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/)).
- On Neuralink, George calls the intermediate steps reasonable, Eagleman doubts healthy people will accept surgery, and Chollet does not see bandwidth as the bottleneck ([Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/), [David Eagleman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/david-eagleman-neuroplasticity-and-the-livewired-brain/), [François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).

### Robots that use physics and touch

- Russ Tedrake argues robots should work with their natural dynamics and stop avoiding contact, which is central to manipulation ([Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).
- From the DARPA Robotics Challenge he learned that robustness comes from simulation, testing and rigour. He worries deep learning makes rigorous thinking easy to skip ([Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).

### Life, death and meaning

- Sara Seager expects life beyond Earth to show up first as atmospheric gases, possibly within one or two decades if telescopes like her Starshade concept are built ([Sara Seager](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/sara-seager-search-for-planets-and-life-outside-our-solar-system/)).
- Sheldon Solomon argues awareness of death drives most behaviour. Fridman suggests a sense of mortality may be needed for AI that humans can love ([Sheldon Solomon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/sheldon-solomon-death-and-meaning/)).
