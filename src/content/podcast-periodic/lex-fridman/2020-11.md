## Overview

November was a month of big questions. Dan Carlin took on evil and the collapse of civilisations, Alex Filippenko the fate of the universe and the loneliness of intelligent life, and Manolis Kellis the meaning of life itself. Yaron Brook made the case for Objectivism, while Andrew Huberman and Lisa Feldman Barrett explained how the brain shapes performance, love and behaviour.

## Main themes

### The meaning of life

- Kellis argues that “searching for meaning is actually the point”. Humans stand out because they can perceive every layer of reality, from atoms to ecosystems ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).
- Kellis says the human brain has barely changed in about 70,000 years, so recent progress comes from shared cultural software such as language and printing ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).
- Filippenko says each person has to find their own meaning, often tied to goals, and calls the origin of the elements in stars perhaps the greatest story ever told ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).

### Evil, history and existential risk

- Carlin argues that motives matter when judging evil, which he calls “a situational eye of the beholder kind of question”. He contrasts Alexander with Hitler ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/)).
- Carlin thinks a French military response to the Rhineland remilitarisation would have stopped Hitler. He flatly rejects the hopeful idea that evil people are less capable of destruction ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/)).
- Barrett does not see Hitler as an anomaly. Most people can do bad things given enough push from their environment, and kindness is just as widespread ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/lisa-feldman-barrett-love-evolution-and-the-human-brain/)).
- Carlin trusts individuals but not collective behaviour, which makes slow threats harder to solve. Filippenko ranks human-made risks above asteroids or solar flares ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/), [Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).

### An accelerating universe and a lonely galaxy

- Filippenko, on both teams behind the 1998 discovery, says expansion slowed for about nine billion years and has sped up for the last five billion ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).
- He thinks dark energy is probably vacuum energy but warns it could prove a modern version of Ptolemy's epicycles ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).
- Citing the Fermi paradox, Filippenko leans towards humans being the only intelligent civilisation in the Milky Way, and calls Elon Musk's Mars timeline far too optimistic ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).

### Objectivism and capitalism

- Yaron Brook says reason is humanity's only means of knowledge, and from it Rand derives rational self-interest, individual rights and capitalism ([Yaron Brook](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/yaron-brook-ayn-rand-and-the-philosophy-of-objectivism/)).
- Brook defines capitalism as full private ownership with government limited to protecting rights, calls anarchism infantile, and argues billionaires get rich through win-win transactions ([Yaron Brook](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/yaron-brook-ayn-rand-and-the-philosophy-of-objectivism/)).

### The brain, performance and Neuralink

- Andrew Huberman argues peak performance comes from matching internal arousal to the task. Deliberately doing what you hate trains a top-down control circuit that carries over to the rest of life ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/andrew-huberman-neuroscience-of-optimal-performance/)).
- Huberman praises Neuralink for picking a concrete goal but suggests deeper brain targets may offer more value. Kellis doubts brain-to-brain transfer beats conversation ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/andrew-huberman-neuroscience-of-optimal-performance/), [Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).
- Barrett calls the layered lizard-brain story a myth and describes the brain as a prediction machine running a budget for the body ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/lisa-feldman-barrett-love-evolution-and-the-human-brain/)).
