## Overview

November 2020 had almost no engineering and a lot of big-picture thinking. Dan Carlin and Yaron Brook covered history, power and political philosophy, and Alex Filippenko explained the accelerating universe. Two neuroscientists and Manolis Kellis then turned to how the brain works and what gives life meaning.

## Main themes

### History, power and collective failure

- Dan Carlin trusts people as individuals but doubts them collectively, which he says makes slow threats such as environmental damage harder than sudden ones. Lex Fridman argues the more hopeful case ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/)).
- Carlin says motive matters when judging evil, and that force cannot be abolished because civilization needs a counterforce ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/)).
- From his talk-radio years, Carlin says short listening windows reward knee-jerk positions. He weighs podcast monetization deals such as Joe Rogan's with Spotify, provided there is no content interference ([Dan Carlin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/)).

### Reason, rights and capitalism

- Yaron Brook presents Ayn Rand's Objectivism: reason as the only means of knowledge, rational self-interest as ethics, and government limited to protecting individual rights ([Yaron Brook](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/yaron-brook-ayn-rand-and-the-philosophy-of-objectivism/)).
- Brook argues billionaires get rich through mutually beneficial trades and that competing private firms could replace the FDA's role in testing drugs. Fridman tests him on inequality ([Yaron Brook](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/yaron-brook-ayn-rand-and-the-philosophy-of-objectivism/)).
- Brook calls anarchism infantile, saying rival private police forces would lead to civil war ([Yaron Brook](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/yaron-brook-ayn-rand-and-the-philosophy-of-objectivism/)).

### The universe and our place in it

- Alex Filippenko, who was on both teams behind the 1998 discovery, explains how calibrated Type Ia supernovae revealed an accelerating universe. He thinks dark energy is most likely vacuum energy but says that is unproven ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).
- He expects self-repairing machines, not humans, to travel between stars, and thinks humans are probably the only intelligent species in the Milky Way ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).
- Filippenko argues the Nobel three-person limit does not fit team science ([Alex Filippenko](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/)).

### The brain as a predictive, trainable system

- Andrew Huberman says peak performance comes from matching internal arousal to the task. He treats fear, focus and willpower as circuits that can be trained ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/andrew-huberman-neuroscience-of-optimal-performance/)).
- Huberman argues that doing the thing you hate most trains top-down control that carries over to other areas of life. He wants neuroscience and Neuralink to start with tractable problems before consciousness ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/andrew-huberman-neuroscience-of-optimal-performance/)).
- Lisa Feldman Barrett calls the layered lizard-brain story a myth and says no human ability is unique; what is distinctive is having them all interwoven ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/lisa-feldman-barrett-love-evolution-and-the-human-brain/)).
- She doubts love at first sight because the brain fills gaps with predictions, and argues most people can do bad things given enough environmental push ([Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/lisa-feldman-barrett-love-evolution-and-the-human-brain/)).

### Meaning and shared culture

- Manolis Kellis argues that searching for meaning is the meaning, and that humans are unusual in perceiving every layer of reality ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).
- Kellis says recent progress comes from cultural software shared horizontally, running on brain hardware that has barely changed in 70,000 years. He compares human scale-up from great apes to GPT-3 versus GPT-2 ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Dan Carlin: Hardcore History](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/dan-carlin-hardcore-history/): history as human nature placed into changing systems, with a sober view of collective action.
- [Alex Filippenko: Supernovae, Dark Energy, Aliens](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/alex-filippenko-supernovae-dark-energy-aliens-the-expanding-universe/): a first-hand account of how the accelerating universe was found.
- [Andrew Huberman: Neuroscience of Optimal Performance](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/andrew-huberman-neuroscience-of-optimal-performance/): practical framing of arousal, focus and willpower.
- [Manolis Kellis: Meaning of Life](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/11/manolis-kellis-meaning-of-life-the-universe-and-everything/): culture as software, with a link to AI scaling.
