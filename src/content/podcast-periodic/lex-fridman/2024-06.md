## Overview

June on the Lex Fridman Podcast leaned towards AI from three directions: a safety researcher who thinks superintelligence cannot be controlled, a search CEO building on today's models, and a physicist who sees language models as a cultural technology rather than a new species. The other two episodes dealt with public figures under scrutiny, Kevin Spacey on the allegations against him and Andrew Huberman on handling online critics.

## Main themes

### Can superintelligence be controlled

- Roman Yampolskiy argues that controlling a general superintelligence is like a perpetual safety machine that must work first time and forever, so the only winning move is not to build it. Fridman pushes back that progress will be incremental enough to learn from ([Yampolskiy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/roman-yampolskiy-dangers-of-superintelligent-ai/)).
- Yampolskiy separates extinction risk, mass-suffering risk and the loss of meaning when machines can do every job. He argues against open-sourcing agents, while Fridman says open research lets people study failures while stakes are low ([Yampolskiy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/roman-yampolskiy-dangers-of-superintelligent-ai/)).
- He says ten times more compute buys better models, but ten times more safety funding does not buy ten times more safety. He backs narrow AI and a pause tied to safety capabilities rather than a date ([Yampolskiy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/roman-yampolskiy-dangers-of-superintelligent-ai/)).
- Sara Walker takes the opposite view: the real risks are human ones such as deepfakes, weapons control and fake kidnapping calls, and doomerism is a kind of social immune response ([Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/sara-walker-physics-of-life-time-complexity-and-aliens/)).

### Building an answer engine against Google

- Aravind Srinivas describes Perplexity as an answer engine where every sentence must be backed by a retrieved source, an idea taken from academic citation ([Perplexity](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/aravind-srinivas-perplexity-ceo-on-future-of-ai-search-the-internet/)).
- He argues Google's link-based ad business cannot afford to replace links with answers, much as it left low-margin cloud to Amazon. Perplexity leans on subscriptions ([Perplexity](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/aravind-srinivas-perplexity-ceo-on-future-of-ai-search-the-internet/)).
- Smarter models alone will not solve search, he says. BM25 still beats most embeddings, so ranking mixes term retrieval, domain authority and recency, and users can pick GPT-4o, Claude 3 or Perplexity's own Sonar ([Perplexity](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/aravind-srinivas-perplexity-ceo-on-future-of-ai-search-the-internet/)).

### Life, time and computation

- Walker argues life is the process by which information structures matter over time, so living things are small in space but large in time. She counts language, memes and mathematics as forms of life ([Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/sara-walker-physics-of-life-time-complexity-and-aliens/)).
- She describes large language models as a dynamic archive of human language at training time, a societal technology rather than a new species ([Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/sara-walker-physics-of-life-time-complexity-and-aliens/)).
- Walker disputes Stephen Wolfram's ruliad and says computation is a language our biosphere built, not the base layer of reality ([Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/sara-walker-physics-of-life-time-complexity-and-aliens/)).

### Public figures under scrutiny

- Kevin Spacey describes acting as serving the writing and never judging the character. Fridman frames the second half around due process; Spacey admits crossing boundaries but denies the most serious claims ([Spacey](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/kevin-spacey-power-controversy-betrayal-truth-love-in-film-and-life/)).
- Andrew Huberman argues science educators should invite their critics on, correct mistakes in public and stay in the reasonable centre. He describes an AI-generated ad that falsely quoted him ([Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/andrew-huberman-focus-controversy-politics-and-relationships/)).
- Fridman says rude scientists online erode trust in science, and wants Trump and Biden to sit for two- to three-hour conversations instead of short TV interviews ([Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/06/andrew-huberman-focus-controversy-politics-and-relationships/)).
