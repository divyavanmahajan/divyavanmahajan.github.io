## Overview

The podcast started in late August 2018 as a series of long interviews with AI researchers and scientists. The early episodes asked whether machines can think or feel, and whether advanced AI is a risk. Later guests turned to what deep learning still lacks and how robots and game-playing systems learn. A few episodes left AI to cover Python, Stack Overflow and Google.

## Main themes

### Intelligence, consciousness and machines

- Max Tegmark treats intelligence and consciousness as patterns of information processing, so he sees AGI as possible ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/08/max-tegmark-life-3-0/)).
- Christof Koch disagrees on experience: digital machines may match human intelligence but, without brain-like causal power, will not feel anything ([Christof Koch](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/09/christof-koch-consciousness/)).
- Juergen Schmidhuber thinks intelligence comes down to a simple algorithm built on compression, curiosity and self-improvement ([Juergen Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).

### AI risk and how to control it

- Tegmark frames the main task as aligning AGI's goals with ours ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/08/max-tegmark-life-3-0/)).
- Steven Pinker argues doom scenarios confuse intelligence with a will to power, and that safety-minded engineering deserves more weight ([Steven Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- Stuart Russell wants AI built around uncertainty about human objectives, so machines defer to people instead of chasing a fixed goal ([Stuart Russell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/stuart-russell-long-term-future-of-ai/)).

### What deep learning still lacks

- Yoshua Bengio calls for new training objectives, causal and disentangled representations and agents that learn by acting ([Yoshua Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).
- Vladimir Vapnik says the unsolved part is how a teacher picks good predicates and invariants, which he says can cut training data a hundredfold ([Vladimir Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/vladimir-vapnik-statistical-learning/)).
- Schmidhuber expects the next wave to be active machines that learn by acting ([Juergen Schmidhuber](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/juergen-schmidhuber-godel-machines-meta-learning-and-lstms/)).

### Learning by acting: robots and games

- Pieter Abbeel names self-play, demonstrations and simulator ensembles as ways to give robots more learning signal ([Pieter Abbeel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/pieter-abbeel-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Tuomas Sandholm explains how Libratus beat top poker pros with data-free game theory, and sees game solving moving into business and military strategy ([Tuomas Sandholm](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/tuomas-sandholm-poker-and-game-theory/)).

### Building software, communities and companies

- Guido van Rossum explains Python's focus on programmer productivity and why he stepped down as BDFL after the PEP 572 fight ([Guido van Rossum](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/guido-van-rossum-python/)).
- Jeff Atwood argues online communities need strict, purpose-built rules, and that mature programmers win through people skills ([Jeff Atwood](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/11/jeff-atwood-stack-overflow-and-coding-horror/)).
- Eric Schmidt stresses building for scale and planning five years ahead, with healthcare and education as AI's near-term priorities ([Eric Schmidt](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/12/eric-schmidt-google/)).
