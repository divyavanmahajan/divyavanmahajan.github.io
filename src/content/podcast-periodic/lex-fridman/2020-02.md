## Overview

February was the most technical month of early 2020: seven researchers and engineers, most of them senior figures in machine learning. Three offered competing accounts of what intelligence is, two argued for practical, engineering-minded AI, and two covered the hardware future of chips and quantum computers.

## Main themes

### Competing theories of intelligence

- Vladimir Vapnik says intelligence means finding a few good predicates that shrink the space of admissible functions, so learning needs far less data. He challenges researchers to match the best digit recognisers with about 100 times fewer examples ([Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/vladimir-vapnik-predicates-invariants-and-the-essence-of-intelligence/)).
- Marcus Hutter defines intelligence as performing well across many environments. His AIXI model, Solomonoff induction plus long-term planning, is provably optimal but uncomputable, a gold standard for AGI work ([Hutter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/marcus-hutter-universal-artificial-intelligence-aixi-and-agi/)).
- John Hopfield argues brains use feedback, synchronised firing and collective dynamics that artificial networks ignore, and doubts the brain is as deep as the deepest networks ([Hopfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/john-hopfield-physics-view-of-the-mind-and-neurobiology/)).
- Vapnik calls convolution a single hand-built invariance, while Hutter sees no difference between compression, understanding and prediction ([Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/vladimir-vapnik-predicates-invariants-and-the-essence-of-intelligence/), [Hutter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/marcus-hutter-universal-artificial-intelligence-aixi-and-agi/)).

### Machine learning as engineering, not minds

- Michael I. Jordan says today's AI is a new engineering field for decisions under uncertainty and for markets, not human-like intelligence. He argues consequential decisions need error bars, causal questions and awareness of other people's choices ([Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).
- Jordan blames ad and click-through business models for many problems and wants users to control what is known about them ([Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).
- Andrew Ng compares AGI worries to overpopulation on Mars and names bias, wealth concentration, job displacement and deepfakes as more urgent ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/)).

### Deploying AI in the real world

- Ng sees the next opportunity in manufacturing, agriculture and healthcare, where small, messy data matters and the model is perhaps five percent of the code ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/)).
- He recalls that early Google Brain overinvested in unsupervised learning but got scale right, persuaded by a chart showing bigger models performing better ([Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/)).
- Jim Keller says Tesla's Autopilot hardware had to be cheap enough for every car and calls driving largely a ballistics problem; Fridman pushes back ([Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).

### Chips and quantum computers

- Keller describes Moore's law as thousands of overlapping innovations and expects 10 to 20 more years of shrinking. He argues designs should be rewritten from scratch about every five years ([Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/)).
- Scott Aaronson explains quantum computing as interference of amplitudes, not trying every answer in parallel. He calls Google's supremacy result real but far from breaking cryptography ([Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/scott-aaronson-quantum-computing/)).
- Aaronson warns that many quantum machine learning claims have been dequantized, and expects chemistry and materials simulation to come first ([Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/scott-aaronson-quantum-computing/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Michael I. Jordan: Machine Learning, Recommender Systems, and the Future of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/): a sober reframing of AI around decisions and markets.
- [Andrew Ng: Deep Learning, Education, and Real-World AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/): practical advice on deployment, learning habits and careers.
- [Scott Aaronson: Quantum Computing](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/scott-aaronson-quantum-computing/): a careful separation of quantum milestones from quantum hype.
- [Jim Keller: Moore's Law, Microprocessors, Abstractions, and First Principles](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/): a chip architect's case that Moore's law is far from dead.
- [Marcus Hutter: Universal Artificial Intelligence, AIXI, and AGI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/marcus-hutter-universal-artificial-intelligence-aixi-and-agi/): the most formal definition of intelligence on offer this month.
