## Overview

September had two history episodes and no AI. Paleontologist Dave Hone used T. rex to show how much can and cannot be inferred from fossils. Author Norman Ohler argued that drugs are a missing layer in the history of the Second World War.

## Main themes

### Dinosaurs as ordinary animals

- Dave Hone argues dinosaurs followed ordinary ecological rules, so modern ecology is the best guide to their behaviour. He describes T. rex as about 12 metres long and roughly seven tons, a power walker rather than a runner ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- Birds are living dinosaurs, around 11,000 species, whose nearest relative is something close to Velociraptor. Hone notes they lived alongside other dinosaurs for 100 million years ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- He attributes the end-Cretaceous extinction to an asteroid off the Yucatan about 66 million years ago. Dinosaurs fared badly because “they were mostly big and they were mostly on land” ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).

### Jurassic Park and weak evidence

- Hone says evidence for pack hunting “in any dinosaur at all is almost nonexistent”. The supposed group tyrannosaur trackway has only four or five footprints ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- The idea that T. rex cannot see you if you stay still appears to be Crichton's invention, though the films get its gait right ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- He argues the field needs population data more than new species, and points to Protoceratops, with over 100 good skeletons from embryos to adults ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).

### Drugs in the Third Reich

- Norman Ohler, author of Blitzed, says the Ardennes plan of May 1940 depended on reaching Sedan within three days and nights, which methamphetamine (sold as Pervitin) made possible ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- He sums up: “So Hitler was really an opioid guy, while the army was really meth-ed up.” He traces Hitler's opioid use under his physician Theodor Morell from August 1941 ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- Fridman presses him on historian Richard Evans's criticism. Ohler rejects the charge that the book excuses Nazi crimes and says the genocidal policies had nothing to do with drugs ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).

### Resistance, LSD and MKUltra

- Ohler describes Harro Schulze-Boysen and Libertas, whose Berlin resistance network of over 100 people recruited at parties every second Thursday ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- From the Sandoz archive he traces Nazi truth-drug research into the CIA's MKUltra, which he says continued the Nazi human experiments ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- He also argues more broadly that psychoactive substances shaped early civilisations, from Sumerian temple beer to the opium trade of Minoan Crete ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
