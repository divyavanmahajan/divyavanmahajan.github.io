## Overview

September had two history episodes far apart in time. Paleontologist Dave Hone reasoned from bones and bite marks about how T. rex lived. Author Norman Ohler argued that drug use is a neglected thread in the story of the Second World War. Both conversations turned on how much weight limited evidence can carry.

## Main themes

### Dinosaurs as ordinary animals

- Dave Hone argues dinosaurs lived in real ecosystems, so modern ecology is the best guide to their behaviour ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- He describes T. rex as about 12 metres long and roughly seven tons, a power walker rather than a runner. Healed bite marks suggest it hunted juveniles, while other marks show it also scavenged ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- Hone attributes the end-Cretaceous extinction to an asteroid off the Yucatan about 66 million years ago, with large land animals hit hardest ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).

### Reasoning carefully from sparse evidence

- Hone says the idea that T. rex cannot see you if you stand still appears to be Crichton's invention, and evidence for pack hunting in any dinosaur is very weak ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- He notes 1,500 to 1,600 valid species but argues population data matters more, pointing to Protoceratops with over 100 good skeletons ([Dave Hone](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/dave-hone-t-rex-dinosaurs-extinction-evolution-and-jurassic-park/)).
- Ohler accepts his mentor Hans Mommsen's warning against single-cause explanations. Lex Fridman presses him on criticism from historian Richard Evans and on how much weight drugs should carry ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).

### Drugs and the Second World War

- Ohler argues, from archive documents, that the Wehrmacht's early speed relied on methamphetamine supplied for the 1940 Ardennes attack, while opioids eroded Hitler's later leadership ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- He rejects the charge that this excuses Nazi crimes. Planned crimes are not mitigated by intoxication, he says, and the genocidal policies had nothing to do with drugs ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
- He also describes the Berlin resistance network around Harro Schulze-Boysen and Libertas, and traces Nazi truth-drug research into the CIA's MKUltra programme ([Norman Ohler](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/09/norman-ohler-hitler-nazis-drugs-ww2-blitzkrieg-lsd-mkultra-cia/)).
