## Overview

July opened with episode 300 and bracketed the month with two DeepMind guests on the road to general AI. In between, Lex Fridman talked with a former KGB illegal, an intelligence researcher, a heterodox economist, a Catholic bishop, a cosmologist and the CEO of Coinbase. The LaMDA sentience story came up in both AI conversations.

## Main themes

### DeepMind's path to general AI

- Demis Hassabis traces a line from AlphaGo to AlphaZero and MuZero that steadily removed human knowledge, and presents AlphaFold as the first big proof that AI can speed up science, with fusion control and quantum chemistry next ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).
- Oriol Vinyals describes Gato as a roughly one-billion-parameter transformer that handles text, images and actions in one sequence model, and calls it the beginning of general agents rather than the end ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Vinyals sees reuse of trained weights, as Flamingo did with Chinchilla, and teaching models through interaction as the next steps. He names memory and lifelong learning as open problems ([Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Both guests say current models are nowhere near sentient. Hassabis calls the Google engineer's LaMDA claim a projection and wants better interpretability before large-scale deployment ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/), [Oriol Vinyals](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/)).

### Spies, secrecy and trust

- Jack Barsky describes his recruitment in East Germany, two years of KGB training in Moscow and life as an illegal in the US. He says his handlers understood American life poorly, and argues the CIA understood Russia just as badly ([Jack Barsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/jack-barsky-kgb-spy/)).
- Barsky says love for his daughter made him quit in 1988, and that he chose to cooperate fully once the FBI found him ([Jack Barsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/jack-barsky-kgb-spy/)).
- Joe Rogan and Fridman discuss intelligence agencies, with Rogan assuming a platform his size would be a target. Rogan now suspects government UFO talk is cover for advanced drones ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/joe-rogan-comedy-controversy-aliens-ufos-putin-cia-and-freedom/)).

### Money, markets and regulation

- Steve Keen argues mainstream economics wrongly assumes equilibrium and ignores bank-created money, private debt and energy. He says this blinds it to financial crises and climate risk ([Steve Keen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/steve-keen-marxism-capitalism-and-economics/)).
- Keen draws on Janos Kornai to explain why socialist economies stagnated, while Fridman raises censorship and concentrated power when Keen speaks well of China's mixed model ([Steve Keen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/steve-keen-marxism-capitalism-and-economics/)).
- Brian Armstrong frames crypto as economic freedom for anyone with a smartphone. He proposes a simple legal test that sorts assets into commodities, securities, currencies and others, plus a sandbox for small startups ([Brian Armstrong](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/brian-armstrong-coinbase-cryptocurrency-and-government-regulation/)).

### Minds, faith and the cosmos

- Richard Haier argues the g factor is real, stable and strongly genetic, and that education interventions have not raised it. Fridman repeatedly raises the risk of misuse; both agree intelligence says nothing about a person's worth ([Richard Haier](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/richard-haier-iq-tests-human-intelligence-and-group-differences/)).
- Bishop Robert Barron argues God is the ground of being rather than a being in the world, and calls the problem of evil the strongest argument against God ([Robert Barron](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/robert-barron-bishop-robert-barron-christianity-and-the-catholic-churc/)).
- Martin Rees thinks human brains may need AI to grasp the deepest laws of physics, and that electronic successors may follow humans. He counts technological misuse among the biggest risks of this century ([Martin Rees](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/martin-rees-black-holes-alien-life-dark-matter-and-the-big-bang/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Demis Hassabis: DeepMind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/demis-hassabis-deepmind/): DeepMind's mission and the case for AI as a tool for science, told by its CEO.
- [Oriol Vinyals: Deep Learning and AGI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/oriol-vinyals-deep-learning-and-artificial-general-intelligence/): a clear account of how Gato works and what generalist models still lack.
- [Jack Barsky: KGB Spy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/jack-barsky-kgb-spy/): a first-hand story of Cold War tradecraft and why one spy walked away.
- [Brian Armstrong: Coinbase](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/07/brian-armstrong-coinbase-cryptocurrency-and-government-regulation/): a concrete regulatory proposal from the head of the largest US exchange.
