## Overview

August was the busiest month so far, with seven guests. Several argued that AI should be spread beyond a few big labs, while George Hotz and Keoki Jackson looked at autonomy on the road and in defence. Yann LeCun and Pamela McCorduck covered where deep learning came from and where it goes next, and Paola Arlotta discussed brain organoids as a research platform and the ethics around them.

## Main themes

### AI for more people

- Kevin Scott argues AI should be a platform, not something decided by “a handful of companies” in a few cities, and compares it to the steam engine becoming a common component ([Scott](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/kevin-scott-microsoft-cto/)).
- Scott describes Microsoft's data dignity work on ways people might earn income from their data, noting that individual data is only valuable in aggregate ([Scott](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/kevin-scott-microsoft-cto/)).
- Jeremy Howard says deep learning works with little data and one GPU, so domain experts should use it. He says “the key differentiator between people that succeed and people that fail is tenacity” ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).
- Howard argues that where doctors are scarce, AI should make them more productive rather than replace them ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/)).

### Autonomy on the road and in defence

- George Hotz says comma.ai is proud to be Level 2 and will not call openpilot 1.0 until it has driver monitoring that cannot be cheated. He calls Musk's view that monitoring is unnecessary stupid ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Hotz argues that “if your perception system output can be written in a spec document it is incomplete”, and backs end-to-end learning ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- He reports burn of about $200k a month against $100k revenue, with car insurance as the long-term plan, and gives Waymo a roughly three-year technical lead ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/george-hotz-comma-ai-openpilot-and-autonomous-vehicles/)).
- Keoki Jackson says AI-enabled autonomy will be built into everything Lockheed Martin fields, with humans kept in control through verification, a safety culture and policy ([Jackson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/keoki-jackson-lockheed-martin/)).

### Beyond labels: self-supervised and active learning

- LeCun says the next step is self-supervised learning of predictive world models that handle uncertainty, not more labels or model-free reinforcement learning ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
- He finds it surprising that huge networks trained on modest data work at all, and rejects the term AGI because humans are not general ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
- Howard says practical topics such as transfer learning and active learning are understudied; his ULMFiT paper brought transfer learning to NLP ([Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/jeremy-howard-fast-ai-deep-learning-courses-and-research/), [LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).

### AI's history and its winters

- Pamela McCorduck traces AI to an old wish, found in myth and Homer, to build intelligence outside the human skull ([McCorduck](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/pamela-mccorduck-machines-who-think-and-the-early-days-of-ai/)).
- She calls AI winters a commercial event while the science continues. Fridman counters that over-promising, such as autonomous-vehicle timelines, still cuts funding ([McCorduck](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/pamela-mccorduck-machines-who-think-and-the-early-days-of-ai/)).
- LeCun recalls neural nets falling out of favour around 1995 and his convolutional net patent expiring in 2007 ([LeCun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/08/yann-lecun-deep-learning-convolutional-neural-networks-and-self-superv/)).
