## Overview

May had two deep technical conversations and no AI-lab episode. Jean-Baptiste Kempf and Kieran Kunhya explained the open source software behind most online video, and Fermilab physicist Don Lincoln walked through the biggest open questions in physics.

## Main themes

### FFmpeg and VLC as hidden infrastructure

- Fridman cites an estimate that over 90% of video processing workflows involve FFmpeg. Kempf describes it as the low-level codec, muxer and filter libraries inside everything from VLC to Chrome ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).
- Both guests say this rests on perhaps 5 VLC and 10-15 FFmpeg core maintainers who refuse to compromise on quality ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).
- Each codec generation gives about 30% better compression for one to two orders of magnitude more CPU, and Kunhya calls FFmpeg “probably one of the biggest CPU users in the world” ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).

### Handwritten assembly and performance

- Kempf says the dav1d AV1 decoder has about 240,000 lines of handwritten assembly against 30,000 of C, and runs on probably 3 billion devices ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).
- Kunhya reports 10x to 50x speedups over C. Kempf says compiler auto-vectorization is multiple times slower and that hardware optimisation is what cannot be vibe coded ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).

### Big tech and open source maintainers

- Kunhya criticises Google for flooding volunteers with AI-generated security reports under a 90-day deadline, and Microsoft for offering a few thousand dollars for a high-priority bug ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).
- Kempf says he refused offers worth tens of millions to bundle ads into VLC, and refused two intelligence agencies that asked for a backdoor ([FFmpeg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/ffmpeg-the-incredible-technology-behind-video-on-the-internet/)).

### Antimatter and the matter imbalance

- Lincoln explains that for every billion antimatter particles there were a billion and one matter particles, and the mechanism behind that asymmetry is not understood ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).
- Fermilab is studying leptogenesis by comparing neutrino and antineutrino oscillations, though Lincoln would bet the rates turn out the same ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).
- At Fermilab's peak rate it would take a billion years to make a gram of antimatter. Lincoln calls antimatter propulsion an engineering and containment problem ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).

### Dark matter, dark energy and unification

- Lincoln traces physics as a series of unifications up to electroweak theory and the Higgs boson. He stresses that 2012 found “a particle consistent with the existence of the Higgs boson” ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).
- He moved from modified gravity to dark matter because of the Bullet Cluster and the galaxies DF2 and DF4 ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).
- He estimates a theory of everything is hundreds of years of accelerator progress away, and calls string theory untested after about 50 years ([Don Lincoln](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/05/don-lincoln-biggest-mysteries-in-physics-antimatter-dark-energy-toe-do/)).
