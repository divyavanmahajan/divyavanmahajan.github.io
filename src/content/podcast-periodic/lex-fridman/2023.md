## Overview

In 2023 the Lex Fridman Podcast turned towards AI after ChatGPT and GPT-4, with a spring run of episodes on alignment, existential risk and a proposed pause on training. By the summer the debate had moved to open-source models and whether regulation would entrench incumbents. Politics ran alongside all year, from Trump and free speech to Gaza after October 7.

## Main themes

### ChatGPT and GPT-4 arrive

- Days after GPT-4's launch, Sam Altman defends shipping early and says “I think if I had to pick some moment from what we've seen so far, I'd sort of pick ChatGPT.” ([Sam Altman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-altman-openai-ceo-on-gpt-4-chatgpt-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Stephen Wolfram says language models need computational language underneath for reliable facts ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/stephen-wolfram-chatgpt-and-the-nature-of-truth-reality-computation/)).
- Jimmy Wales sees language models as useful but unreliable for Wikipedia ([Jimmy Wales](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/jimmy-wales-wikipedia/)), and Joscha Bach calls them brute-force golems ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/08/joscha-bach-life-intelligence-consciousness-ai-the-future-of-humans/)).

### Alignment, existential risk and the pause

- Eliezer Yudkowsky argues capabilities are outpacing alignment, admitting “I think that GPT-4 is passed where I thought this paradigm was going to take us” ([Eliezer Yudkowsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/eliezer-yudkowsky-dangers-of-ai-and-the-end-of-human-civilization/)).
- Max Tegmark makes the case for a six-month pause on training beyond GPT-4 to escape a race to the bottom ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Sam Harris says nobody knows how to permanently tie a self-improving AI to human values ([Sam Harris](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-harris-trump-pandemic-twitter-elon-bret-idw-kanye-ai-ufos/)). Manolis Kellis would rather treat AI as a partner and expects jobs to become vocations ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).

### Open source against regulation

- Mark Zuckerberg says “I feel quite confident in the assessment that open sourcing models now is net positive” ([Mark Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Marc Andreessen warns fear-driven rules would entrench incumbents and says existing laws cover most bad uses ([Marc Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)). George Hotz sees centralised power as the real risk ([George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).
- In December Guillaume Verdon argues safety-led regulation would concentrate power, while Elon Musk wants AI grounded in physics and sees power, not chips, as the coming limit ([Guillaume Verdon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/12/guillaume-verdon-beff-jezos-e-acc-movement-physics-computation-agi/), [Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/elon-musk-war-ai-aliens-politics-physics-video-games-and-humanity/)).

### Trump, speech and tribal politics

- Jeremi Suri argues the Civil War never fully ended and weighs Trump's strengths and flaws ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/jeremi-suri-american-civil-war/)); Tim Urban says how people think matters more than left or right ([Tim Urban](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/02/tim-urban-tribalism-marxism-liberalism-social-justice-and-politics/)).
- David Pakman argues online partisanship blurs facts and opinions ([David Pakman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/david-pakman-politics-of-trump-biden-bernie-aoc-socialism-wokeism/)).
- Harvey Silverglate and Greg Lukianoff argue for near-absolute free speech against campus censorship and cancel culture ([Harvey Silverglate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/05/harvey-silverglate-freedom-of-speech/), [Greg Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).

### Israel, Gaza and great-power war

- Benjamin Netanyahu defends his judicial reform and puts peace with Arab states first ([Benjamin Netanyahu](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/07/benjamin-netanyahu-israel-palestine-power-corruption-hate-and-peace/)); Mohammed el-Kurd frames the conflict as one about land ([Mohammed El-Kurd](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/07/mohammed-el-kurd-palestine/)).
- After October 7, Jared Kushner links the Hamas attack to easing pressure on Iran ([Jared Kushner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/10/jared-kushner-israel-palestine-hamas-gaza-iran-and-the-middle-east/)). John Mearsheimer applies offensive realism to Ukraine, Gaza and China ([John Mearsheimer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/11/john-mearsheimer-israel-palestine-russia-ukraine-china-nato-and-ww3/)).
- Robert F. Kennedy Jr calls Putin's invasion illegal but provoked by the US ([Robert F. Kennedy Jr](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/07/robert-f-kennedy-jr-cia-power-corruption-war-freedom-and-meaning/)).
