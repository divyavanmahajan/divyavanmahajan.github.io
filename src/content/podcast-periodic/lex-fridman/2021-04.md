## Overview

April continued the show's Bitcoin run with two more guests, while Fridman kept pressing the community on its certainty and toxicity. Tyler Cowen and Jeremi Suri covered growth and American power, and Yannis Pappas brought history through comedy. Risto Miikkulainen supplied the month's AI episode on evolutionary computation, and Georges St-Pierre described fighting as a science.

## Main themes

### Bitcoin as values and money

- Nic Carter argues Bitcoin encodes values: fixed monetary policy, property rights, censorship resistance and leaderless governance. What matters is that nobody can change the issuance schedule ([Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/)).
- Carter says the small-block side won the blocksize wars rightly, because Bitcoin should be a slow settlement layer with payment layers on top. Robert Breedlove also expects scaling to come from layers such as Lightning ([Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/), [Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/)).
- Breedlove calls central-bank inflation a hidden tax that distorts prices and erodes trust, and argues Bitcoin's fixed supply restores a check on government ([Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/)).
- Fridman, who says he holds no cryptocurrency, questions maximalist certainty and asks for kindness. Carter explains the defensiveness as a response to past co-option attempts ([Robert Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/), [Nic Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/)).

### Growth, conformity and business

- Tyler Cowen says he has turned optimistic about growth, pointing to green energy, batteries and other breakthroughs rooted in cheaper computation ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).
- Cowen argues big business mostly innovates better than government, and that the worst enforced mediocrity is at universities just below the top dozen ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).
- He sees Bitcoin taking over some of gold's role but expects regulation to erode DeFi's advantages, and calls WallStreetBets a new kind of esport ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).

### Power and history

- Jeremi Suri argues the presidency now concentrates more power in one person than is healthy, and that great leaders persuade through listening, storytelling and empathy ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/jeremi-suri-history-of-american-power/)).
- Suri says Washington gained authority by giving up command, and contrasts him with Putin, who has stayed too long ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/jeremi-suri-history-of-american-power/)).
- Yannis Pappas says power reveals rather than corrupts people, and that charm is the con artist's main tool, citing Bernie Madoff ([Yannis Pappas](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/yannis-pappas-history-and-comedy/)).
- Cowen expects China to muddle through rather than overtake the US, and says Russia depends too much on fossil fuels ([Tyler Cowen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/tyler-cowen-economic-growth-and-the-fight-against-conformity-and-medio/)).

### Evolution as a creative search

- Risto Miikkulainen argues evolutionary algorithms are creative because they lack human assumptions. In a tic-tac-toe tournament an evolved network won by making rival programs run out of memory ([Risto Miikkulainen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/risto-miikkulainen-neuroevolution-and-evolutionary-computation/)).
- He says population methods suit problems where failures are affordable, and that evolution can tune deep learning architectures and loss functions ([Risto Miikkulainen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/risto-miikkulainen-neuroevolution-and-evolutionary-computation/)).
- He sees intelligence as rooted in social cooperation. In his simulations, hyenas learned to team up to steal kills from lions ([Risto Miikkulainen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/risto-miikkulainen-neuroevolution-and-evolutionary-computation/)).

### Fighting as a science

- Georges St-Pierre says he never liked fighting, only its science: studying opponents and building plans A, B and C ([Georges St-Pierre](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/georges-st-pierre-the-science-of-fighting/)).
- Frame-by-frame analysis showed BJ Penn had the fastest reaction time but a slow reset, so St-Pierre used constant feints to tire him ([Georges St-Pierre](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/georges-st-pierre-the-science-of-fighting/)).
