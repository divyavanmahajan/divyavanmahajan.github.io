## Overview

Lex Fridman published 84 conversations in 2020, still centred on AI but widening steadily into physics, the brain, history and personal meaning. The AI guests split between those who expect deep learning to keep scaling and those who think it is missing something basic. Robots and self-driving cars were a steady second thread, ending with Waymo's driverless service. From the spring the pandemic, institutions and free speech came up more often.

## Main themes

### What intelligence is, and how to build it

- Daniel Kahneman likens deep learning to fast, automatic System 1 thinking that lacks reasoning and causality ([Daniel Kahneman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/daniel-kahneman-thinking-fast-and-slow-deep-learning-and-ai/)). Vladimir Vapnik and Marcus Hutter offer mathematical definitions instead ([Vapnik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/vladimir-vapnik-predicates-invariants-and-the-essence-of-intelligence/), [Hutter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/marcus-hutter-universal-artificial-intelligence-aixi-and-agi/)).
- Ilya Sutskever says the field keeps underestimating deep learning and that AGI may need only a few more ideas ([Ilya Sutskever](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/ilya-sutskever-deep-learning/)). David Silver frames intelligence as the reinforcement learning problem ([David Silver](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/04/david-silver-alphago-alphazero-and-deep-reinforcement-learning/)).
- Sceptics push back: Ben Goertzel bets on hybrid symbolic-neural systems, Dileep George on brain-inspired models, and François Chollet defines intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/), [Dileep George](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/dileep-george-brain-inspired-ai/), [François Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/francois-chollet-2-fran-ois-chollet-measures-of-intelligence/)).
- Andrew Ng and Michael I. Jordan treat AI as engineering for real deployments and markets, not a step towards human-like minds ([Andrew Ng](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/andrew-ng-deep-learning-education-and-real-world-ai/), [Michael I. Jordan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/michael-i-jordan-machine-learning-recommender-systems-and-the-future-o/)).

### Consciousness and the predictive brain

- David Chalmers holds that subjective experience resists physical explanation, while Roger Penrose argues understanding is not a computation ([David Chalmers](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/david-chalmers-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness/), [Roger Penrose](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/roger-penrose-physics-of-consciousness-and-the-infinite-universe/)).
- Joscha Bach calls minds software running on brains ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)). Karl Friston and Lisa Feldman Barrett describe the brain as a prediction machine ([Karl Friston](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/karl-friston-neuroscience-and-the-free-energy-principle/), [Lisa Feldman Barrett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/lisa-feldman-barrett-counterintuitive-ideas-about-how-the-brain-works/)).
- Matt Botvinick sees psychology, neuroscience and AI as one enterprise ([Matt Botvinick](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/matt-botvinick-neuroscience-psychology-and-ai-at-deepmind/)).

### Robots and cars that learn from the world

- Ayanna Howard and Anca Dragan argue robots must adapt to and model the people around them ([Ayanna Howard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/ayanna-howard-human-robot-interaction-and-ethics-of-safety-critical-sy/), [Anca Dragan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/03/anca-dragan-human-robot-interaction-and-reward-engineering/)).
- Sergey Levine places the robot gap in the mind, not the body; Russ Tedrake wants robots to stop fearing contact ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/), [Russ Tedrake](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/08/russ-tedrake-underactuated-robotics-control-dynamics-and-touch/)).
- On driving, Sertac Karaman expects geofenced deployments first, George Hotz backs end-to-end networks, and Dmitri Dolgov describes Waymo's driverless service in Phoenix ([Sertac Karaman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/05/sertac-karaman-robots-that-fly-and-robots-that-drive/), [George Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/george-hotz-hacking-the-simulation-learning-to-drive-with-neural-nets/), [Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).

### Computing from chips to languages

- Jim Keller says Moore's law is far from dead; David Patterson sees domain-specific accelerators as the way forward ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/02/jim-keller-moore-s-law-microprocessors-abstractions-and-first-principl/), [David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- Brian Kernighan, James Gosling and Chris Lattner trace Unix, C, Java and compilers to design trade-offs and productivity ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/), [James Gosling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/james-gosling-java-jvm-emacs-and-the-early-days-of-computing/), [Chris Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/chris-lattner-the-future-of-computing-and-programming-languages/)).
- Scott Aaronson and Richard Karp both bet that P does not equal NP ([Scott Aaronson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/scott-aaronson-computational-complexity-and-consciousness/), [Richard Karp](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/richard-karp-algorithms-and-computational-complexity/)).

### History, power and institutions

- Stephen Kotkin argues Russia needs durable institutions rather than strong individuals ([Stephen Kotkin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/01/stephen-kotkin-stalin-putin-and-the-nature-of-power/)).
- Eric Weinstein says US institutions have stalled, while Lex holds that good people and new platforms can still win ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/10/eric-weinstein-on-the-nature-of-good-and-evil-genius-and-madness/)).
- Michael Malice argues for decentralisation and against cynicism ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-malice-the-white-pill-freedom-hope-and-happiness-amidst-chaos/)). Michael Mina says regulation held back cheap rapid COVID-19 tests ([Michael Mina](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-mina-rapid-testing-viruses-and-the-engineering-mindset/)).
