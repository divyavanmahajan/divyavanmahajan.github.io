## Overview

July 2021 was a ten-episode month with a strong political thread: a North Korean defector and an anarchist author both used Animal Farm to explain how ordinary people sustain tyranny. Power and its limits also ran through conversations on military leadership and the war on drugs. The science episodes covered the origin of life, Planet Nine and string theory.

## Main themes

### Totalitarianism and the state

- Yeonmi Park says reading Animal Farm in South Korea showed her that “everybody was responsible to create this dystopia in my country” ([Yeonmi Park](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/yeonmi-park-north-korea/)).
- Park describes a country with no internet, a caste system and a regime that kept Pyongyang fed while starving the countryside. Fridman pushes back on casting China as an enemy ([Yeonmi Park](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/yeonmi-park-north-korea/)).
- Michael Malice argues that petty officials enjoying small amounts of power are a more common form of evil than overt killers, and that “anarchism is not a location it's a relationship” ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).
- For his 200th episode, Fridman defends government as an imperfect but improvable collective, while Malice says markets would provide every government service better ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).

### Leadership, war and autonomous weapons

- Jocko Willink says harsh feedback, as from Elon Musk, works only when the team knows it comes from care for the mission. He advises Musk to train subordinate leaders so he can look up and out ([Jocko Willink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/jocko-willink-war-leadership-and-discipline/)).
- Fridman worries about an AI weapons race with China. Willink says he would support more surgical machines and that you can put “a kill switch on a system”, while admitting he does not know AI's shortfalls ([Jocko Willink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/jocko-willink-war-leadership-and-discipline/)).

### Drug policy, from smuggling to therapy

- Fridman opens the Roger Reaves episode by citing the cost of the war on drugs. Reaves recounts flying loads for the Medellin Cartel, refusing to testify and spending about 33 years in prison, and says it was not worth the money ([Roger Reaves](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/roger-reaves-smuggling-drugs-for-pablo-escobar-and-the-medellin-cartel/)).
- Reaves argues drugs should be treated as a health issue, and Malice and Fridman also criticise the war on drugs ([Roger Reaves](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/roger-reaves-smuggling-drugs-for-pablo-escobar-and-the-medellin-cartel/), [Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).
- Rick Doblin describes MAPS's strategy of using medicine and science as the opening wedge for drug policy reform, funded through donations and a public benefit corporation rather than a conventional pharma model ([Rick Doblin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/rick-doblin-psychedelics/)).

### Life, planets and strings

- Sara Walker argues life is a matter of information and causal history, not a single molecule, and her assembly theory offers a way to detect it on other worlds ([Sara Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/sara-walker-the-origin-of-life-on-earth-and-alien-worlds/)).
- Konstantin Batygin says clustered orbits in the distant Kuiper belt point to an unseen planet of about five Earth masses. Fridman mentions Edward Witten's proposal to find it with a fleet of probes ([Konstantin Batygin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/konstantin-batygin-planet-9-and-the-edge-of-our-solar-system/)).
- Cumrun Vafa concedes string theory has no experimental evidence yet, but credits his advisor Witten with teaching him that connections between ideas can replace mathematical rigour ([Cumrun Vafa](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/cumrun-vafa-string-theory/)).

### Endurance and the land

- Zach Bitter builds ultramarathon performance on a large aerobic base and a clear reason to keep going, and treats his low-carb diet as personal rather than universal ([Zach Bitter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/zach-bitter-ultramarathon-running/)).
- Anya Fernald argues regenerative grazing can make meat part of the climate solution, and wants AI and better data to help it scale ([Anya Fernald](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/anya-fernald-regenerative-farming-and-the-art-of-cooking-meat/)).

## Notable episodes

- [#196 – Yeonmi Park: North Korea](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/yeonmi-park-north-korea/): a first-hand account of life under the Kim regime and an argument that systems, not people, explain it.
- [#200 – Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/): the 200th episode, a sustained debate between anarchism and Fridman's defence of government.
- [#197 – Jocko Willink](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/jocko-willink-war-leadership-and-discipline/): military leadership lessons tested against tech CEOs and AI weapons.
