## Overview

September split between the 2024 election and deep history. Donald Trump and Vivek Ramaswamy set out their positions on the 2020 election, the federal government, Ukraine and China, with Lex Fridman pressing both on election fraud claims. Three historians then covered Rome, twentieth-century totalitarianism and the ancient Americas, and the war in Ukraine ran through three of the five episodes.

## Main themes

### Trump, Ramaswamy and the 2024 election

- Trump presents himself as a dealmaker. He says he is driven by both loving winning and hating losing, and that he chose not to prosecute Hillary Clinton to bring the country together ([Trump](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/donald-trump-interview/)).
- Pressed by Fridman on 2020, Trump calls the election a fraud and proposes paper ballots, voter ID, same-day voting and proof of citizenship. Ramaswamy proposes a similar single-day, paper-ballot vote, while Fridman objects to the fake electors scheme ([Trump](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/donald-trump-interview/), [Ramaswamy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vivek-ramaswamy-trump-conservatism-nationalism-immigration-and-war/)).
- Ramaswamy wants conservatives to say what they stand for. His central proposal is a 75% headcount cut across federal agencies, which he says recent Supreme Court rulings make more feasible ([Ramaswamy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vivek-ramaswamy-trump-conservatism-nationalism-immigration-and-war/)).

### Ukraine, Russia and China

- Trump claims he would have a Ukraine deal guaranteed as president-elect but gives no details, warning “you have to get Ukraine done. That could end up in a third world war” ([Trump](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/donald-trump-interview/)).
- Ramaswamy would offer Russia reopened economic ties and NATO assurances in exchange for leaving its military alliance with China. He argues less dependence on China and clear red lines on Taiwan lower the risk of war ([Ramaswamy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vivek-ramaswamy-trump-conservatism-nationalism-immigration-and-war/)).
- Liulevicius is pessimistic about peace. He argues that without a Nuremberg-style reckoning Putin draws selectively on Tsarist and Stalinist memory ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).

### How Rome rose and fell

- Gregory Aldrete argues Rome's strength was integration: it demanded troops rather than taxes from conquered cities, giving it manpower that outlasted defeats such as Cannae ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).
- He calls Augustus a poor general but a master of propaganda, who took the powers of key offices without holding them. “He rules for like 50 years, and by the time he dies, there’s no one literally almost left alive who can remember the Republic” ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).
- Aldrete contrasts Rome with Alexander, whose empire fragmented because it rested on personal loyalty. He sees no single fall, and judges that by around 500 the West was no longer Roman ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).

### Totalitarian ideologies

- Vejas Liulevicius argues Marxism's claimed scientific certainty let Lenin, Stalin and Mao justify almost any means. He notes that “communism comes to power in most of these instances as a result of war” ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).
- He describes Soviet collectivisation as a failure built on falsified statistics, ending with a country importing grain despite rich farmland ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).
- Responding to Darryl Cooper, he argues Nazi aims in the East were never limited, citing Mein Kampf and plans for permanent war on the Eastern Front ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).

### The ancient Americas

- Ed Barnhart argues the Americas produced independent civilisations comparable to Egypt and Mesopotamia, with pyramids on the Peruvian coast older than Egypt's ([Barnhart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/ed-barnhart-maya-aztec-inca-and-lost-civilizations-of-south-america/)).
- He contrasts the Aztecs, who ruled through fear and sacrifice, with the Inca's roads, labour system and accounting, and says much knowledge was lost after European contact ([Barnhart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/ed-barnhart-maya-aztec-inca-and-lost-civilizations-of-south-america/)).
