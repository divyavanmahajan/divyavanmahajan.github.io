## Overview

September split between the 2024 US election and long-form history. Donald Trump and Vivek Ramaswamy set out conservative positions while Fridman pressed both on the 2020 election. Three historians covered Rome, twentieth-century totalitarianism and the ancient Americas.

## Main themes

### The 2024 election and the right

- Donald Trump presents himself as a dealmaker who would end the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, but declines to give his plans for Russia or China ([Trump](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/donald-trump-interview/)).
- Fridman repeatedly presses Trump on 2020 fraud claims and division. Trump proposes paper ballots and voter ID, and says Project 2025 has nothing to do with him ([Trump](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/donald-trump-interview/)).
- Vivek Ramaswamy proposes a 75% headcount cut across federal agencies and says he and Elon Musk discussed the Department of Government Efficiency idea ([Ramaswamy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vivek-ramaswamy-trump-conservatism-nationalism-immigration-and-war/)).
- Fridman objects to the fake electors scheme. Ramaswamy points to the Hunter Biden laptop story and would trade Western ties for Russia leaving its alliance with China ([Ramaswamy](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vivek-ramaswamy-trump-conservatism-nationalism-immigration-and-war/)).

### How Rome rose and fell

- Gregory Aldrete argues Rome's strength was integration: it demanded troops, not taxes, from conquered cities and kept raising armies after defeats such as Cannae ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).
- The Republic fell, he says, because veterans, aristocrats, allies and slaves felt cheated of the spoils while each strongman broke another precedent ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).
- Aldrete calls Augustus a poor general but a master of propaganda, and says there was no single fall: plague, climate, lost grain sources and slow communication all played a part ([Rome](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/gregory-aldrete-the-roman-empire-rise-and-fall-of-ancient-rome/)).

### Ideology and totalitarianism

- Vejas Liulevicius argues Marxism combined a supposedly scientific law of history with room for heroic revolutionaries, which let Lenin, Stalin and Mao justify almost any means ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).
- He calls the Soviet famine deliberate and rejects Darryl Cooper's claims about Churchill and the death camps, citing Mein Kampf and Generalplan Ost ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).
- Without a Nuremberg-style reckoning, he says, Putin draws selectively on Tsarist and Stalinist memory, and he is pessimistic about peace in Ukraine ([Liulevicius](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/vejas-liulevicius-communism-marxism-nazism-stalin-mao-and-hitler/)).

### The ancient Americas

- Ed Barnhart argues people reached the Americas far earlier than once believed and that Peru built pyramids before Egypt, with thousands against Egypt's roughly 140 ([Barnhart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/ed-barnhart-maya-aztec-inca-and-lost-civilizations-of-south-america/)).
- He rejects Graham Hancock's single lost mother civilization in favour of independent cultures, and says the 2012 Maya end date was a misreading ([Barnhart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/ed-barnhart-maya-aztec-inca-and-lost-civilizations-of-south-america/)).
- Barnhart says much knowledge was lost when disease killed about 90% of the Americas' population within 50 years of European contact ([Barnhart](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/09/ed-barnhart-maya-aztec-inca-and-lost-civilizations-of-south-america/)).
