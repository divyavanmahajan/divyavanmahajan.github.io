## Overview

August was shaped by Lex Fridman's return from four weeks in Ukraine, which he brought into conversations with a CIA veteran, a comedian, Jordan Peterson and Noam Chomsky. Alongside the war, the month covered UFOs seen by Navy pilots, John Carmack's five-hour engineering marathon, and two champions of games of skill.

## Main themes

### The war in Ukraine and great-power risk

- Andrew Bustamante assessed in August 2022 that Russia was winning and treats war as an economic game. Fridman, just back from the front, countered that Ukrainian morale is high and that world support matters ([Andrew Bustamante](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/andrew-bustamante-cia-spy/)).
- Noam Chomsky calls the invasion a supreme crime but argues Western policy focuses on weapons and neglects diplomacy. He warns that pursuing Russia's total defeat gambles with nuclear war, and that US-China cooperation is a precondition for tackling climate and nuclear risk ([Noam Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/noam-chomsky-putin-ukraine-china-and-nuclear-war/)).
- Fridman tells Duncan Trussell and Jordan Peterson about the hatred the war is creating; with Trussell he argues hate should not be applied to a whole nation ([Duncan Trussell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/duncan-trussell-comedy-sentient-robots-suffering-love-burning-man/), [Jordan Peterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/jordan-peterson-life-death-power-fame-and-meaning/)).
- Magatte Wade reads African abstentions over Ukraine as resistance to Western pressure, and argues that economic freedom, not aid, is the route out of poverty ([Magatte Wade](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/magatte-wade-africa-capitalism-communism-and-the-future-of-humanity/)).

### Secrets, surveillance and the unexplained

- Ryan Graves says his F/A-18 squadron tracked unidentified objects off Virginia Beach for months after a radar upgrade, and nearly collided with one. He treats it as a flight-safety problem that needs open, data-driven study ([Ryan Graves](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/ryan-graves-ufos-fighter-jets-and-aliens/)).
- Bustamante describes intelligence as a business of predictable human behaviour, ranks the world's agencies, and defends post-9/11 metadata collection. Fridman sees Edward Snowden as holding the powerful to account ([Andrew Bustamante](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/andrew-bustamante-cia-spy/)).

### Technology and AI

- John Carmack argues his biggest wins came from understanding the whole stack and dropping what users did not need, from id Software's engines to VR at Oculus and Meta ([John Carmack](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/john-carmack-doom-quake-vr-agi-programming-video-games-and-rockets/)).
- Carmack gives 55-60% odds of AGI showing signs of life by 2030, expects it to rest on a few simple ideas, and rejects fast-takeoff fears ([John Carmack](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/john-carmack-doom-quake-vr-agi-programming-video-games-and-rockets/)).
- Graves wants machine learning to make engagements look random to an enemy, under human command, while Trussell worries a superintelligence built inside a corporation or state could escape control ([Ryan Graves](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/ryan-graves-ufos-fighter-jets-and-aliens/), [Duncan Trussell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/duncan-trussell-comedy-sentient-robots-suffering-love-burning-man/)).

### Games, probability and competition

- Liv Boeree says poker taught her to think in probabilities. She argues that Moloch, a trap where everyone trades values for short-term advantage, sits behind harms from social media to AI race dynamics ([Liv Boeree](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/liv-boeree-poker-game-theory-ai-simulation-aliens-existential-risk/)).
- Magnus Carlsen says his edge is evaluating positions, not deep calculation, and explains why he will not defend his World Championship title ([Magnus Carlsen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/magnus-carlsen-greatest-chess-player-of-all-time/)).

### Meaning and suffering

- Jordan Peterson argues growth comes from voluntarily facing suffering, and that leaders who panic and use compulsion should not be trusted. Fridman asks whether Peterson's own fear of Marxism drives him the same way ([Jordan Peterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/jordan-peterson-life-death-power-fame-and-meaning/)).
- Trussell draws on Buddhism, his cancer and depression to argue that facing mortality honestly opens the way to love and presence ([Duncan Trussell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/08/duncan-trussell-comedy-sentient-robots-suffering-love-burning-man/)).
