## Overview

June 2020 had five conversations with little overlap between them. Two looked at minds and AGI from outside the deep learning mainstream (Joscha Bach and Ben Goertzel), one at the hardware underneath AI (David Patterson). The other two were about how lasting work gets made, in medicine (Robert Langer) and in writing (Steven Pressfield).

## Main themes

### AGI beyond deep learning

- Ben Goertzel argues that current deep networks will not reach AGI on their own. He wants hybrid systems like OpenCog, where logic, neural and evolutionary learning share one knowledge representation ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- He says AGI went from a maverick pursuit to a term national leaders use: “Putin knows what AGI is and he's like Russia needs to become the leader in AGI” ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Goertzel wants decentralised mechanisms for AI, health and politics. Through SingularityNET he argues that whoever controls the platform for AI cooperation will shape early AGI ([Ben Goertzel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/ben-goertzel-artificial-general-intelligence/)).
- Joscha Bach sees most of today's AI as advanced information processing and says it lacks unified learning, where everything sensed goes into one model ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).

### Minds as models

- Bach defines intelligence as the ability to make models, and the mind as software that models both the world and a self living in it ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).
- He calls consciousness a model of the contents of attention, and argues that only a simulation, not a physical system, can be conscious ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).
- Bach is pessimistic about industrial civilisation: “I think that our present civilization is not sustainable”. Fridman counters that technology and the spread of ideas could win the race ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/joscha-bach-artificial-consciousness-and-the-nature-of-reality/)).

### Hardware after Moore's law

- David Patterson says general-purpose processors now improve only a few percent a year, so the way forward is accelerators built for particular applications ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- He calls machine learning's reliance on matrix multiply a godsend for designers, but says ML hardware startups fail when they neglect the software stack ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).
- Patterson explains why RISC won on measured performance and predicts the open RISC-V instruction set may become the most popular, starting with embedded devices ([David Patterson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/david-patterson-computer-architecture-and-data-storage/)).

### Turning science into companies

- Robert Langer has helped launch about 40 companies. He looks for a platform discovery, a top-journal paper, animal data and broad patents ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
- He says the companies that did well had great business people, which he ranks above the science ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
- Langer argues patents are what make the more than two billion dollars needed to bring a drug to market fundable, while admitting they can limit access ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).

### Discipline and creative work

- Steven Pressfield frames creative work as ego against the greater self, with Resistance as the ego's fearful voice ([Steven Pressfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/steven-pressfield-the-war-of-art/)).
- He says there is no hack: learning to tune in took him about thirty years, and he keeps a strict daily routine of gym first, then about three hours of writing ([Steven Pressfield](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/steven-pressfield-the-war-of-art/)).
- Langer offers a parallel from science: early rejections by Nature and Science, then decades of work before results reached patients ([Robert Langer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/06/robert-langer-edison-of-medicine/)).
