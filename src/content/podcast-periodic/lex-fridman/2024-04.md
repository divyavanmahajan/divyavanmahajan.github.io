## Overview

April split between politics and science. Tulsi Gabbard, Bassem Youssef and Andrew Callaghan each described how power, money and the media shape what Americans hear about war and each other. Edward Gibson and Sean Carroll brought the science, both with a view on what LLMs do and do not understand, and judo champion Neil Adams talked about defeat.

## Main themes

### War, power and dissent

- Tulsi Gabbard argues a bipartisan military industrial complex makes it easier to start wars than to question them. She points to the revolving door between the Pentagon, Congress and defence contractors ([Tulsi Gabbard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/tulsi-gabbard-war-politics-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).
- Gabbard calls for a negotiated peace in Ukraine and says smears are used to avoid debate. She and Fridman both worry about normalised talk of tactical nuclear weapons ([Tulsi Gabbard](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/tulsi-gabbard-war-politics-and-the-military-industrial-complex/)).
- Bassem Youssef says the Israel-Palestine conflict is defined by an imbalance of military, political and media power. Fridman pushes back on Hamas and on propaganda from both sides ([Bassem Youssef](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/bassem-youssef-israel-palestine-gaza-hamas-middle-east-satire-fame/)).

### Media, satire and a divided public

- Youssef recounts how his satire show drew 30 to 40 million viewers per episode in Egypt, led to questioning under the Muslim Brotherhood and ended in exile in 2014 ([Bassem Youssef](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/bassem-youssef-israel-palestine-gaza-hamas-middle-east-satire-fame/)).
- Andrew Callaghan argues corporate media profits from division and that QAnon steers people away from real class grievances. His answer is to listen to people at the margins ([Andrew Callaghan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/andrew-callaghan-channel-5-gonzo-qanon-o-block-politics-alex-jones/)).
- Callaghan describes a copyright takedown over a 10-second clip that he is taking to court for smaller creators, and gives his first public account of the 2022 allegations against him ([Andrew Callaghan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/andrew-callaghan-channel-5-gonzo-qanon-o-block-politics-alex-jones/)).

### Language, physics and what LLMs understand

- Edward Gibson argues languages are communication tools shaped to keep dependent words close. He cites brain-imaging and patient evidence that language is separate from thought ([Edward Gibson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/edward-gibson-human-language-psycholinguistics-syntax-grammar-llms/)).
- Gibson calls LLMs arguably the best current theory of language form, but says they are weak on meaning: a tweaked Monty Hall problem still fools them ([Edward Gibson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/edward-gibson-human-language-psycholinguistics-syntax-grammar-llms/)).
- Sean Carroll argues physics advances by trusting tested equations past intuition, which gives black holes, many worlds and dark energy. On LLMs, he cites work showing much of a model's apparent Othello board representation came from the probe itself ([Sean Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/sean-carroll-general-relativity-quantum-mechanics-black-holes-aliens/)).

### Winning and losing

- Neil Adams says champions show themselves in defeat. His two Olympic final losses haunted him and fed a period of heavy drinking before he became a coach and commentator ([Neil Adams](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/neil-adams-judo-olympics-winning-losing-and-the-champion-mindset/)).
- Adams argues correct technique and a lot of randori matter more than drills, noting Japanese players do 50-60 randori a week ([Neil Adams](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/04/neil-adams-judo-olympics-winning-losing-and-the-champion-mindset/)).
