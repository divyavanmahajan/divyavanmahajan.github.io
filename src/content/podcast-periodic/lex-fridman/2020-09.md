## Overview

September was a personal month for the show. Lex Fridman recorded a solo tribute to his grandmother, and conversations with Ryan Hall and Joe Rogan returned again and again to love, struggle, grace and how to live. Alongside that came Stephen Wolfram's hypergraph theory of physics, Replika's AI companion, the origins of Java and a Navy pilot's account of the 2004 Tic Tac encounter.

## Main themes

### Grace, power and cancel culture

- Ryan Hall calls cancel culture “poisonous on its face” because punishing every past mistake discourages people from changing for the better. He says most people want mercy rather than justice ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)).
- Asked whether he would talk to Alex Jones, Hall says yes and argues that silencing people is worse than the problem it targets. Fridman worries about conspiracy theories spreading through anonymous trolls ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)).
- Joe Rogan says being cancelled or publicly shamed is devastating, and expects anger whichever candidate wins the US election ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).

### Love, loss and the meaning of life

- Fridman frames his grandmother's influence as five lessons: mental toughness, physical strength, thinking before speaking, belief in himself and love. He says she taught him to “love people love life love the world no matter what the world does to you” ([In Memory of My Grandmother](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/lex-solo-grandmother-lex-solo-3-in-memory-of-my-grandmother/)).
- Rogan says there is no single meaning of life. Love, family and friends come first, then genuine passions, and he warns against a life of just doing a job ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).
- Manolis Kellis tells his mother he aims to be fulfilled rather than happy, and prefers a view of life focused on the journey and its struggles ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/manolis-kellis-origin-of-life-humans-ideas-suffering-and-happiness/)).

### Struggle, martial arts and self-discipline

- Hall describes jiu jitsu as a philosophy expressed physically, with techniques as vocabulary and principles as grammar ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/ryan-hall-martial-arts-and-the-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-grace/)).
- Rogan calls himself his harshest critic, says hard training drains excess energy, and rates grappling as the best base for self-defence ([Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).
- David Goggins came up in three episodes as a model of mental toughness, including Fridman's link between Goggins and his grandmother's mindset ([In Memory of My Grandmother](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/lex-solo-grandmother-lex-solo-3-in-memory-of-my-grandmother/), [Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/), [Joe Rogan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/joe-rogan-conversations-ideas-love-freedom-the-joe-rogan-experience/)).

### AI companions and loneliness

- Eugenia Kuyda says Replika asks users after each conversation whether they feel better, and about 80% of conversations make people feel better. It also tracks loneliness over time with questionnaires ([Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/)).
- She concluded the project was less about technology and “more about human vulnerabilities”. GPT-3, fine-tuned with OpenAI, now supplies about one in five responses ([Eugenia Kuyda](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/eugenia-kuyda-friendship-with-an-ai-companion/)).

### Computation, physics and engineering

- Stephen Wolfram argues that simple rules rewriting a hypergraph can generate space and time. Causal invariance gives special relativity, and quantum mechanics becomes motion in branchial space ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/stephen-wolfram-fundamental-theory-of-physics-life-and-the-universe/)).
- Wolfram says computational irreducibility is a basic limit on science, and that causal invariance is essentially the idea behind eventual consistency in distributed databases ([Stephen Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/stephen-wolfram-fundamental-theory-of-physics-life-and-the-universe/)).
- James Gosling traces Java to pointer bugs and CPU lock-in, and the JVM to a grad-school bytecode hack ([James Gosling](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/james-gosling-java-jvm-emacs-and-the-early-days-of-computing/)).
- David Fravor says the 2004 Tic Tac was a physical object that outperformed anything he knows, and wants it studied without stigma ([David Fravor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/09/david-fravor-ufos-aliens-fighter-jets-and-aerospace-engineering/)).
