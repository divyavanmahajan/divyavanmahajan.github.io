## Overview

November ranged from AI governance to the foundations of computing and physics. Elon Musk and Michael Kearns discussed how AI should be regulated and constrained, while Noam Chomsky and Gilbert Strang gave very different views of what deep learning is. Sean Carroll and Dava Newman covered quantum mechanics and the road to Mars.

## Main themes

### Governing AI: regulation, fairness and privacy

- Elon Musk calls for a government agency to oversee AI, comparable to the FAA, and warns that regulation usually follows disaster ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).
- Michael Kearns says fairness can be a measurable constraint, with a Pareto curve of error against unfairness that policymakers pick a point on. He warns that protecting groups separately still allows fairness gerrymandering ([Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).
- Kearns sees privacy as more settled: anonymisation breaks when datasets are combined, while differential privacy adds calibrated noise ([Kearns](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/michael-kearns-algorithmic-fairness-bias-privacy-and-ethics-in-machine/)).
- Bjarne Stroustrup says fuzzy machine-learning accuracy suits pre-screening but not life-critical controls, and the human-handover model in cars worries him ([Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/)).

### Minds, brains and machines

- Musk describes Neuralink's near-term goal as treating brain and spinal injuries, with a longer-term aim of a digital layer above the cortex. He lists durable electrodes, low-power chips and robotic implantation as the hard problems ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).
- Noam Chomsky argues language is an innate internal system, and that deep learning is useful engineering but tells us nothing about language. Asked about Neuralink, he says a machine could expand cognition only as a book does ([Chomsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/noam-chomsky-language-cognition-and-deep-learning/)).
- Musk says highway Autopilot rarely needs intervention, while complex traffic lights and winding roads remain unsolved and perception is harder than planning ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).

### Foundations: linear algebra and C++

- Gilbert Strang argues linear algebra deserves more weight than calculus because data arrives as matrices. He frames deep learning as linear algebra plus a simple fold, repeated many times ([Strang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/gilbert-strang-linear-algebra-deep-learning-teaching-and-mit-opencours/)).
- Strang says the singular value decomposition has become fundamental because data matrices are rectangular ([Strang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/gilbert-strang-linear-algebra-deep-learning-teaching-and-mit-opencours/)).
- Stroustrup argues well-designed abstractions are his main tool for performance, under a zero-overhead principle. He describes concepts arriving in C++20 and the committee's move to a three-year cycle ([Stroustrup](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/bjarne-stroustrup-c/)).

### Physics and space

- Sean Carroll argues many-worlds is the simplest version of quantum mechanics and the best starting point for explaining how spacetime emerges ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/sean-carroll-quantum-mechanics-and-the-many-worlds-interpretation/)).
- Dava Newman wants a decade on the Moon as a proving ground for Mars in the 2030s, plus skin-tight mobility suits. With 20-minute delays she says Mars systems must be fully autonomous ([Newman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/dava-newman-space-exploration-space-suits-and-life-on-mars/)).
- Newman rejects Mars as a backup planet, while Musk disputes Carl Sagan's line that there is nowhere to migrate ([Newman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/dava-newman-space-exploration-space-suits-and-life-on-mars/), [Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/11/elon-musk-neuralink-ai-autopilot-and-the-pale-blue-dot/)).
