## Overview

December left current affairs aside for two deep dives into old ideas. British Museum curator Irving Finkel talked about cuneiform, flood myths and ancient games. Set theorist Joel David Hamkins walked through infinity, Gödel's incompleteness theorems and his view that mathematics has many universes rather than one.

## Main themes

### Writing and the record of the past

- Finkel dates the oldest evidence of writing to around 3500 BC, between the Euphrates and Tigris. He says the crucial step was realising that pictures could stand for sounds ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- He argues that Sumerian pictographs came at the end of a long earlier tradition, and admits “Many Assyriologists would leave the room.” ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- Finkel puts cuneiform's 3,000-year life down to early standardisation through lexicography, inertia and scribes guarding their power ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- The British Museum holds about 130,000 cuneiform tablets, and Finkel says millions more must still be in the ground. He defends keeping most of the collection off display as a store for future study ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).

### Flood myths and ancient games

- A museum visitor brought in the Ark Tablet, from about 1700 BC. Its 60 lines read like a blueprint for a giant round coracle ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- Finkel argues the Genesis flood shows literary dependence on the Babylonian story, adapted during the exile with sin replacing noise as the cause. He thinks the original memory is of a real local flood, not a global one ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).
- He reconstructed the rules of the Royal Game of Ur from a second-century BC tablet. The game lasted nearly 3,000 years, from Egypt to Crete, and is played again today ([Irving Finkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/irving-finkel-deciphering-secrets-of-ancient-civilizations-flood-myths/)).

### Infinity and the diagonal argument

- Hamkins says almost all mathematicians before Cantor thought actual infinity incoherent. Cantor showed the real numbers are uncountable, and that any set's power set is strictly larger ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- He argues one idea links Cantor, Russell's paradox and the halting problem: “almost every major result in mathematical logic is using in an abstract way the idea of diagonalization” ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- For Hamkins, asking whether infinity exists is no different from asking whether the number five exists ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).

### Incompleteness and a multiverse of mathematics

- Hamkins compares a theory proving its own consistency to a used car salesman vouching for himself. He finds incompleteness eye-opening rather than traumatic ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- The continuum hypothesis stayed open until Gödel's 1938 result, and Cohen's forcing later showed it independent of ZFC. Hamkins says no large cardinal axiom settles it ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
- He reads this independence as evidence of many set-theoretic universes, against his former supervisor Hugh Woodin. He also says current LLMs have given him basically zero help with his mathematics ([Joel David Hamkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/12/joel-david-hamkins-infinity-paradoxes-that-broke-mathematics-g-del-inc/)).
