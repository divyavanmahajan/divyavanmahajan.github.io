## Overview

March mixed one large AI conversation with two about creative work. Jensen Huang laid out NVIDIA's view of computers as token factories. Game director Jeff Kaplan and music educator Rick Beato talked about craft, small teams and where AI fits in games and music.

## Main themes

### NVIDIA and the AI factory

- Huang argues computing has moved from retrieval to generation, turning data centres into factories that produce tokens. He says the share of GDP spent on computation will be 100 times higher than in the past ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).
- Extreme co-design means optimising chips, memory, networking, power and cooling together, because the problem no longer fits in one GPU. Huang runs the company the same way: “That’s why I don’t do one-on-ones.” ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/))
- Putting CUDA on GeForce raised costs by about 50% at a 35% gross-margin company, and the market cap fell to around $1.5 billion. Huang now names CUDA's install base as NVIDIA's main advantage ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).

### Power for data centres

- Huang says the grid is built for worst-case peaks and runs at about 60% of peak most of the time ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).
- He wants data centres that degrade gracefully and utilities that sell power with weaker guarantees, instead of six-nines uptime contracts ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).

### Agents and jobs

- Huang argues capable agents will use existing tools rather than replace software. Agents spawning sub-agents form large teams, which he sees driving an agentic scaling law ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).
- He separates a job's purpose from its tasks, noting that the number of radiologists grew even though AI drives their tools. People whose job is just the task will be highly disrupted, and he would hire the candidate who is expert in AI ([Jensen Huang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jensen-huang-nvidia-the-4-trillion-company-the-ai-revolution/)).

### Building games at Blizzard and after

- Kaplan joined Blizzard in 2002, when it had fewer than 200 people, after playing about 272 days of EverQuest with guild-mates who worked there. He credits the company's early strength to three gamer-programmers who shielded developers ([Jeff Kaplan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jeff-kaplan-world-of-warcraft-overwatch-blizzard-and-future-of-gaming/)).
- He blames leadership, himself included, for Titan's failure: hiring many artists before there was a vision. Overwatch came out of a six-week pitch by the remaining team ([Jeff Kaplan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jeff-kaplan-world-of-warcraft-overwatch-blizzard-and-future-of-gaming/)).
- Kaplan says executives working 40-hour weeks should not tell anyone to stay late. He also says social media rewards maximum hyperbole, and creators now face a fire hose of criticism ([Jeff Kaplan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jeff-kaplan-world-of-warcraft-overwatch-blizzard-and-future-of-gaming/)).
- He calls AI in game development mostly a hot mess: useful for chores but often wrong, and unethical when it uses creators' work without permission ([Jeff Kaplan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/jeff-kaplan-world-of-warcraft-overwatch-blizzard-and-future-of-gaming/)).

### Music craft and AI-generated songs

- Beato argues musical ability is learned like a language, through early exposure, daily practice and working things out by ear ([Rick Beato](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/rick-beato-greatest-guitarists-of-all-time-history-future-of-music/)).
- He generated about 130 song ideas with Suno and found three good ones. He sees AI as an idea generator that benefits skilled songwriters most, since they can tell good output from bad ([Rick Beato](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/rick-beato-greatest-guitarists-of-all-time-history-future-of-music/)).
- His kids spotted AI songs from artefacts in the vocal reverb, which he links to training on low bit-rate MP3s. Fridman says people want authenticity ([Rick Beato](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/03/rick-beato-greatest-guitarists-of-all-time-history-future-of-music/)).
