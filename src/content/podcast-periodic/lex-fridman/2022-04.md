## Overview

April was a wide month for the show, with nine episodes. The war in Ukraine shaped two conversations about conflict and its human cost, while Qualcomm's CEO and Michael Saylor covered chips and money. Rick Rubin's ideas on creativity echoed through the Huberman and Grimes episodes, and Lee Cronin returned to debate aliens with Sara Walker.

## Main themes

### War, violence and its cost

- Economist Chris Blattman argues war is rare because it is so costly, and happens when five forces, above all unaccountable power, remove the room for a deal ([Chris Blattman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/chris-blattman-war-and-violence/)).
- Blattman reads Ukraine as a refusal to give up sovereignty, and explains how Medellín's mafia board keeps gang violence in check. Fridman, half Ukrainian and half Russian, defends people who stand up to invaders ([Chris Blattman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/chris-blattman-war-and-violence/)).
- Filmmaker Skye Fitzgerald argues that much of today's hunger is deliberate, with starvation used as a weapon in Yemen, Ethiopia and Ukraine, and that targeted sanctions on leaders carry weight ([Skye Fitzgerald](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/skye-fitzgerald-hunger-war-and-human-suffering/)).

### Chips, connectivity and money

- Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon frames 5G as built for a fully connected society and wants Qualcomm known for on-device AI at the edge: PCs, cars, robots and industry ([Cristiano Amon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/cristiano-amon-qualcomm-ceo/)).
- Amon calls the pandemic an aggravation of the chip shortage rather than its cause, pointing to new car models carrying about 10x the chips, and welcomes the US and European chips acts ([Cristiano Amon](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/cristiano-amon-qualcomm-ceo/)).
- MicroStrategy's Michael Saylor argues inflation is badly mismeasured and presents Bitcoin as digital property no one controls. Fridman pushes back that securities law is not the same as ethics ([Michael Saylor](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/michael-saylor-bitcoin-inflation-and-the-future-of-money/)).

### Creativity and the creative process

- Rick Rubin describes arriving at every project blank, demonstrating every idea and doing a ruthless edit down to the songs you cannot live without ([Rick Rubin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/rick-rubin-legendary-music-producer/)).
- Andrew Huberman credits Rubin with convincing him to build a fixed routine before recording, and shares tools for focus, stress and relationships ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/andrew-huberman-focus-stress-relationships-and-friendship/)).
- Grimes places herself in the post-Rubin school of producers, argues for optimistic, protopian art, and floats sunset clauses on laws. Fridman criticises politicians who show no curiosity about AI and recommender systems ([Grimes](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/grimes-music-ai-and-the-future-of-humanity/)).

### Minds, life and aliens

- Sara Walker and Lee Cronin agree that assembly theory can measure life as accumulated memory, but disagree on how common alien life is. Cronin says GPT-3 imitates without creating novelty; Fridman replies that being fooled may be enough ([Alien Debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/alien-debate-sara-walker-and-lee-cronin/)).
- Stanford psychiatrist Karl Deisseroth argues psychiatry still lacks objective tests and that causal research tools may link individual symptoms to specific brain circuits ([Karl Deisseroth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2022/04/karl-deisseroth-depression-schizophrenia-and-psychiatry/)).
