## Overview

March had little AI and a lot of crypto, speech and power. A cryptographer and a Bitcoin investor gave two very different views of blockchains, while a law professor and a student founder argued for open debate on campus. Cal Newport, Saagar Enjeti and two martial artists filled out the month with work habits, politics and fighting.

## Main themes

### Cryptocurrency: design and belief

- Silvio Micali calls money a social construct and a blockchain a shared ledger that creates common knowledge. The hard problem is deciding who writes the next page ([Silvio Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/)).
- Micali says Bitcoin is too slow and its mining has concentrated in a few pools. Algorand instead uses private cryptographic lotteries to pick about 1,000 voters per block ([Silvio Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/)).
- Anthony Pompliano also calls money a belief system and argues Bitcoin optimises for security, with faster payment layers on top. He predicts $100,000 in 2021 and $1 million by the end of 2026 ([Anthony Pompliano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/anthony-pompliano-bitcoin/)).
- Micali expects several interoperable chains rather than one winner, while Pompliano calls the tribalism between camps childish ([Silvio Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/), [Anthony Pompliano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/anthony-pompliano-bitcoin/)).

### Speech and justice on campus

- Ronald Sullivan argues that everyone deserves a competent defence, because the treatment of unpopular clients sets the rights of all. He says Harvard was wrong not to renew his faculty dean post after he joined Harvey Weinstein's team ([Ronald Sullivan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ronald-sullivan-the-ideal-of-justice-in-the-face-of-controversy-and-ev/)).
- Sullivan says hard topics belong at a university, with the line drawn at uncivil discourse rather than controversial ideas ([Ronald Sullivan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ronald-sullivan-the-ideal-of-justice-in-the-face-of-controversy-and-ev/)).
- Ryan Schiller built Librex, an anonymous feed for verified students, citing a Gallup figure that 61% of students fear speaking their minds. He says Librex refuses to sell user data ([Ryan Schiller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-schiller-librex-and-the-free-exchange-of-ideas-on-college-campuse/)).

### Attention, work and media

- Cal Newport says context switching destroys knowledge work. He blames unscheduled back-and-forth messaging, not email itself, and wants teams to replace it with explicit processes ([Cal Newport](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/cal-newport-deep-work-focus-productivity-email-and-social-media/)).
- Newport predicts the big social platforms will fragment into small, paid, niche communities ([Cal Newport](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/cal-newport-deep-work-focus-productivity-email-and-social-media/)).
- Saagar Enjeti expects journalism to resemble the partisan press of the 1800s, with many people famous to small audiences ([Saagar Enjeti](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/saagar-enjeti-politics-history-and-power/)).

### Politics and power

- Enjeti argues that power runs through systems and personnel, and that a president changes government only with a focused agenda and the right people ([Saagar Enjeti](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/saagar-enjeti-politics-history-and-power/)).
- He says voters now split mainly by whether they hold a four-year degree, and that clean, popular policies could break the deadlock ([Saagar Enjeti](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/saagar-enjeti-politics-history-and-power/)).
- Josh Barnett argues that a state always emerges as groups grow beyond small numbers, against Fridman's hope that abundance lets cooperation win ([Josh Barnett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/josh-barnett-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-the-martial-arts/)).

### Fighting from first principles

- Barnett says violence is part of every person and that the mat is one of the most honest places to be ([Josh Barnett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/josh-barnett-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-the-martial-arts/)).
- Ryan Hall argues most practitioners are end users of systems they do not understand. He sets traps where every choice loses, which Fridman links to mechanism design ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-hall-solving-martial-arts-from-first-principles/)).
- On AI, Hall worries about extreme power differentials, while Fridman points to value-alignment research ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-hall-solving-martial-arts-from-first-principles/)).
