## Overview

March 2021 had two cryptocurrency episodes that took opposite views of Bitcoin, and two that defended open debate in universities. Cal Newport made the case against email-driven work. Two martial artists and a political correspondent covered violence, strategy and power.

## Main themes

### Bitcoin and the blockchain trilemma

- Silvio Micali says “the first thing that bitcoin got right is to understand that there was the need of a cryptocurrency”, but calls it too slow to transact and says two or three mining pools can control the chain ([Silvio Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/)).
- Micali describes a blockchain as a ledger everyone can write to and read. Algorand picks about 1,000 of 10 billion tokens by cryptographic lottery to agree each block, and upgrades need about 90 percent of the stake ([Silvio Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/)).
- Anthony Pompliano frames Bitcoin as digital sound money that optimises for security, with faster layers on top. He says his maximalism is about an automated world on open decentralised protocols and calls the tribalism between camps childish ([Anthony Pompliano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/anthony-pompliano-bitcoin/)).

### Justice, academic freedom and campus speech

- Ronald Sullivan argues that unpopular clients deserve a competent defence because their treatment sets everyone's rights. He says his wrongful-conviction work has freed over 6,000 people ([Ronald Sullivan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ronald-sullivan-the-ideal-of-justice-in-the-face-of-controversy-and-ev/)).
- Sullivan calls Harvard's decision not to renew his faculty dean post, after he joined Harvey Weinstein's defence, “a complete and utter repudiation of academic freedom” ([Ronald Sullivan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ronald-sullivan-the-ideal-of-justice-in-the-face-of-controversy-and-ev/)).
- Ryan Schiller built Librex, an anonymous feed for verified students. He says anonymity is a tool, not the purpose, and that leaving out usernames protects people from doxxing in small communities ([Ryan Schiller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-schiller-librex-and-the-free-exchange-of-ideas-on-college-campuse/)).

### Focus and the knowledge-work day

- Cal Newport says context switching poisons knowledge work. His target is the hyperactive hive mind workflow of unscheduled messaging, not email as a tool ([Cal Newport](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/cal-newport-deep-work-focus-productivity-email-and-social-media/)).
- He recommends deep-work blocks of at least an hour, a hard stop at five, and team processes such as shared documents and office hours. He predicts big social platforms will fragment into small paid communities ([Cal Newport](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/cal-newport-deep-work-focus-productivity-email-and-social-media/)).

### Fighting, strategy and free will

- Josh Barnett argues violence is part of every person and that the mat is an honest place. On politics he says a state will always emerge at scale and that “for every one unit of freedom you need two units of accountability” ([Josh Barnett](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/josh-barnett-philosophy-of-violence-power-and-the-martial-arts/)).
- Ryan Hall says most practitioners are end users of systems they do not understand. He sets traps where every choice loses, which Fridman links to mechanism design ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-hall-solving-martial-arts-from-first-principles/)).
- Hall argues belief in free will is more productive. Fridman says that, illusion or not, belief in it protects the individual from the group ([Ryan Hall](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-hall-solving-martial-arts-from-first-principles/)).

### Power in US politics

- Saagar Enjeti argues that personnel is policy: the bureaucracy runs on autopilot unless a president is hyper-intentional ([Saagar Enjeti](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/saagar-enjeti-politics-history-and-power/)).
- He says voters now split mainly by education and culture, and that clean, popular measures could break the deadlock. He expects media to resemble the partisan press of the 1800s ([Saagar Enjeti](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/saagar-enjeti-politics-history-and-power/)).
