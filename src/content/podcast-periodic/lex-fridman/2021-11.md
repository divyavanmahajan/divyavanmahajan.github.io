## Overview

November paired two views of self-driving trucks, one from a sociologist and one from Waymo, with conversations on money, history and how products get built. Niall Ferguson and Robert Crews brought history and geopolitics, while Kevin Systrom and Boris Sofman talked about what made their companies work or fail. Two wrestling episodes closed out the month.

## Main themes

### Autonomous trucks and the people who drive

- Steve Viscelli argues that trucking went from a well-paid Teamsters job to one where many drivers barely earn minimum wage, and that the issue is job quality rather than a driver shortage ([Viscelli](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/steve-viscelli-trucking-and-the-decline-of-the-american-dream/)).
- He estimates about 300,000 jobs directly at risk and says history shows “that new technology doesn't help workers or raise their wages” unless workers have power ([Viscelli](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/steve-viscelli-trucking-and-the-decline-of-the-american-dream/)).
- Boris Sofman describes Waymo Via's start with L4 trucks moving between transfer hubs, roughly twice a car's sensors, and about 1,000 simulated miles for every real one ([Sofman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/boris-sofman-waymo-cozmo-self-driving-cars-and-the-future-of-robotics/)).

### Money, Bitcoin and trust

- Niall Ferguson traces money from Mesopotamian debt tablets to medieval bills of exchange, which he sees as an early peer-to-peer payment system ([Ferguson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/niall-ferguson-history-of-money-power-war-and-truth/)).
- He calls Bitcoin “an option on digital gold”, rates Ethereum and smart contracts as the bigger innovation, and opposes central bank digital currencies on surveillance grounds ([Ferguson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/niall-ferguson-history-of-money-power-war-and-truth/)).
- Ben Askren backs Bitcoin for remittances and the unbanked, and Neal Stephenson finds cryptographically enforceable contracts more interesting than digital money ([Askren](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/ben-askren-wrestling-and-mma/), [Stephenson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/neal-stephenson-sci-fi-space-aliens-ai-vr-the-future-of-humanity/)).

### Building products: Instagram and Anki

- Kevin Systrom says Instagram began as the check-in app Burbn and survived by cutting everything except photo sharing. A simple stack of Python, Django, Postgres and Redis lasted to about 50 million users ([Systrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/kevin-systrom-instagram/)).
- Systrom sees reinforcement learning as the frontier for social networks but asks “how do you design a scalar reward for someone's happiness over time” ([Systrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/kevin-systrom-instagram/)).
- He argues Facebook needs to produce a hit and has not stayed true to the job its main product does ([Systrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/kevin-systrom-instagram/)).
- Sofman says Cozmo won people over through character, not hardware, but Anki failed when about 85% of volume landed in Q4 and a financing round collapsed in 2019 ([Sofman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/boris-sofman-waymo-cozmo-self-driving-cars-and-the-future-of-robotics/)).

### History, war and institutions

- Robert Crews calls the 2001 invasion of Afghanistan a panicked mistake that treated a global al-Qaeda network as if it had a home address. He warns that frozen banks could lead to deadly hunger ([Crews](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/robert-crews-afghanistan-taliban-bin-laden-and-war-in-the-middle-east/)).
- Ferguson names Britain's August 1914 decision as history's great turning point and makes the case for the University of Austin as a defence of academic freedom ([Ferguson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/niall-ferguson-history-of-money-power-war-and-truth/)).
- Outgoing NIH director Francis Collins defends NIH oversight and its public-private research partnerships, while Fridman presses him on how leaders communicated during the pandemic ([Collins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/francis-collins-national-institutes-of-health-nih/)).

### The wrestler's mindset

- Askren says champions pair a hatred of losing with seeking out the hardest opponents, and argues against heavy drilling in favour of graded sparring ([Askren](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/ben-askren-wrestling-and-mma/)).
- Tom Brands credits visualisation, self-talk and self-chosen repetition for his 1996 Olympic gold, and runs Iowa with a staff that stays in lockstep ([Brands](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/tom-brands-iowa-wrestling/)).
