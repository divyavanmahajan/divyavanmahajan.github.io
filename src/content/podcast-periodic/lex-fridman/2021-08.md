## Overview

August 2021 split between the mind and the practice of science. Jeff Hawkins and Joscha Bach offered competing models of intelligence and consciousness, Wojciech Zaremba explained OpenAI's Codex, and three guests covered how science actually gets done, from Newton's prism to LIGO to the economics of journals. The rest of the month went to sleep, strength training and the history of ritual.

## Main themes

### Models of the mind

- Jeff Hawkins argues the neocortex is about 150,000 cortical columns, each a complete model of the world, that reach a single perception by voting ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jeff-hawkins-the-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Hawkins says machines built this way would be like a map, with no drives of their own, and that self-replication is the real danger. Fridman counters with recommender systems as harm that appears only at scale ([Jeff Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jeff-hawkins-the-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Joscha Bach describes experience as a brain-generated dream world, with the self and free will as models the system makes of itself and consciousness as an attention mechanism. Fridman presses him on the hard problem ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/joscha-bach-nature-of-reality-dreams-and-consciousness/)).

### Language models and Codex

- Wojciech Zaremba calls compute, algorithms and data three multiplicative levers, and says OpenAI's internal studies found more gains came from compute ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He presents Codex, the model behind GitHub Copilot, as the next step after assembly, C and Python, helped by the fact that code can be run and unit-tested ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Bach calls GPT-3 amnesiac but thinks Codex could matter a great deal because most industry programming is not creative ([Joscha Bach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/joscha-bach-nature-of-reality-dreams-and-consciousness/)).
- Zaremba backs iterative deployment and broad collective control of AGI; Fridman raises national AI races and autonomous weapons ([Wojciech Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)).

### How science moves forward

- Jed Buchwald, a former assistant to Thomas Kuhn, argues new theories win because they let researchers build new devices and findings, using the wave theory of light and Newton's prism work as examples ([Jed Buchwald](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jed-buchwald-isaac-newton-and-the-philosophy-of-science/)).
- Barry Barish explains how LIGO measured a change of one part in 10^21, and insists the hard part was engineering. Fridman, a roboticist, is less convinced ([Barry Barish](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/barry-barish-gravitational-waves-and-the-most-precise-device-ever-buil/)).
- Barish describes almost 10 years of iteration without a detection before the 2015 signal, and wants a detector 10 times more sensitive ([Barry Barish](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/barry-barish-gravitational-waves-and-the-most-precise-device-ever-buil/)).

### Papers and publishing

- Luís and João Batalha say papers are not written for clarity, and built Fermat's Library and its annotation tools to help ([Fermat's Library](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/luis-and-joao-batalha-lu-s-and-jo-o-batalha-fermat-s-library-and-the-a/)).
- Luís criticises impact factors, unpaid peer review and paywalls, and argues for preprints. Fridman calls for all papers to be free and for crowdsourced review ([Fermat's Library](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/luis-and-joao-batalha-lu-s-and-jo-o-batalha-fermat-s-library-and-the-a/)).

### Body, sleep and ritual

- Matt Walker argues that no system in body or mind escapes damage from lost sleep; Fridman defends trading sleep for passion as an informed choice ([Matt Walker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/matt-walker-sleep/)).
- Chris Duffin treats strength as an engineering problem and describes leaving a corporate career to build Kabuki Strength ([Chris Duffin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/chris-duffin-the-mad-scientist-of-strength/)).
- Brian Muraresku argues that ancient Greek mystery rites and early Christianity may have used spiked drinks, and that archaeochemistry can test the idea ([Brian Muraresku](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/brian-muraresku-the-secret-history-of-psychedelics/)).
