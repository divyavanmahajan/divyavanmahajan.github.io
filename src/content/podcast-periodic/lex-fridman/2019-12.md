## Overview

December 2019 was the busiest month of the year so far, with nine episodes. Several guests questioned how far deep learning can go and proposed what it lacks: causal models, analogy and common sense. Others covered applied AI at Amazon and in self-driving, the economic effects of automation, and fears about AI, alongside physics and Donald Knuth on computing.

## Main themes

### What deep learning is missing

- Melanie Mitchell says of supervised deep learning, “I think there's some fundamental limits to how far they're gonna get”. She leans towards innate knowledge and sees concept formation through analogy as AI's central open problem ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Judea Pearl argues deep networks only estimate conditional probabilities. Questions about interventions and counterfactuals need explicit causal models and the do-calculus ([Pearl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/)).

### Conversational AI at Amazon

- Rohit Prasad says Alexa launched with about 13 domains and now has 90,000-plus skills, and auto-corrects millions of misrecognised requests without human supervision ([Prasad](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/)).
- An early bet on deep learning for far-field speech cut error rates by a factor of five before the Echo launch ([Prasad](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/)).
- He estimates the Alexa Prize goal of a coherent 20-minute conversation is still five to ten years away, and sees reasoning and memory as the hard part ([Prasad](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/)).

### Self-driving, automation and jobs

- Sebastian Thrun calls machine learning the biggest innovation in building intelligent systems. He says handling 90% of driving is easy but each further fraction is hard ([Thrun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/)).
- Mitchell expects self-driving to stay limited to mapped areas for a long time because of the long tail of edge cases ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Ray Dalio says automation widens wealth and opportunity gaps and should be treated as a national emergency. His rule: “if the future can be different from the past and you don't have deep understanding you should not rely on AI” ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/ray-dalio-principles-the-economic-machine-artificial-intelligence-the/)).
- Thrun argues education should be a basic human right and cites Udacity's large self-driving and scholarship cohorts ([Thrun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/)).

### AI fears, robots and consciousness

- Michael Stevens is not very worried about AI because enough people already worry, and notes Plato worried about writing. Fridman counters with Elon Musk's view ([Stevens](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/michael-stevens-vsauce/)).
- Mitchell argues against paperclip-style superintelligence scenarios because she does not think intelligence separates from values ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Whitney Cummings calls fear of robots largely a privileged concern and sees robots offering care and judgment-free companionship ([Cummings](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/whitney-cummings-comedy-robotics-neurology-and-love/)).
- Pearl defines consciousness as having a blueprint of your own software; Stevens thinks it can be explained within physics ([Pearl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/), [Stevens](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/michael-stevens-vsauce/)).

### Physics and the craft of computing

- Jim Gates calls string theory productive mathematics awaiting observational proof, as general relativity waited for the 1919 eclipse. He found error-correcting codes inside his Adinkra graphs ([Gates](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/jim-gates-supersymmetry-string-theory-and-proving-einstein-right/)).
- Knuth describes The Art of Computer Programming as still growing, with over 300 pages on SAT solvers, and suspects P may equal NP without yielding a usable algorithm ([Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/donald-knuth-algorithms-tex-life-and-the-art-of-computer-programming/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Judea Pearl: Causal Reasoning, Counterfactuals, Bayesian Networks, and the Path to AGI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/): the clearest statement of why correlation-fitting cannot answer causal questions.
- [Melanie Mitchell: Concepts, Analogies, Common Sense & Future of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/): a grounded critique of deep learning with concrete failure cases.
- [Rohit Prasad: Amazon Alexa and Conversational AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/): inside history of building a mass-market voice assistant.
- [Sebastian Thrun: Flying Cars, Autonomous Vehicles, and Education](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/): lessons from the DARPA challenge, self-driving and online education.
- [Donald Knuth: Algorithms, TeX, Life, and The Art of Computer Programming](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/donald-knuth-algorithms-tex-life-and-the-art-of-computer-programming/): a reflective close to the year from computing's best-known author.
