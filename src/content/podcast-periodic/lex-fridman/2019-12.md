## Overview

December brought nine guests. The clearest thread was scepticism about correlation-based deep learning: Judea Pearl, Melanie Mitchell and Donald Knuth each pointed to what it misses. Alongside that came practical AI products, robots as companions, self-driving cars and a few guests from well outside AI.

## Main themes

### What deep learning misses

- Judea Pearl argues deep networks are conditional probability estimators that cannot answer questions about interventions or counterfactuals. Humans should supply a qualitative causal model and let the machine apply the do-calculus ([Pearl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/judea-pearl-causal-reasoning-counterfactuals-bayesian-networks-and-the/)).
- Melanie Mitchell calls flexible concept formation through analogy AI's central open problem. She cites the Atari Breakout agent that failed when the paddle moved a few pixels, and places human-level AI more than a century away ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Mitchell says most common sense is written down nowhere, which is why projects like Cyc fall short. Knuth worries about the gap between understanding and the illusion of understanding ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/), [Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/donald-knuth-algorithms-tex-life-and-the-art-of-computer-programming/)).
- Ray Dalio is wary of opaque machine-learned models where the future may differ from the past ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/ray-dalio-principles-the-economic-machine-artificial-intelligence-the/)).

### AI that talks and keeps company

- Rohit Prasad calls open-ended conversation a harder test of intelligence than games. He traces Alexa from far-field speech recognition to anticipating users' goals, and says trust through transparency and control comes first ([Alexa](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/)).
- Prasad says long-term memory and reasoning are at a very early stage, and the Alexa Prize bar of a coherent 20-minute chat is still years away ([Alexa](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/rohit-prasad-amazon-alexa-and-conversational-ai/)).
- Whitney Cummings argues fear of robots is largely a privileged concern, and that robots could tutor, protect and offer judgment-free companionship ([Cummings](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/whitney-cummings-comedy-robotics-neurology-and-love/)).

### Self-driving and education

- Sebastian Thrun says reaching 90% of driving situations is easy but each further fraction is hard. He uses Tesla Autopilot daily and argues cameras are sufficient ([Thrun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/)).
- Mitchell sees the long tail as the core problem and expects self-driving to stay in mapped areas for a long time ([Mitchell](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/melanie-mitchell-concepts-analogies-common-sense-future-of-ai/)).
- Thrun credits DARPA for funding outcomes rather than effort, describes Udacity's scale, and claims Kitty Hawk's quiet electric aircraft could cut yearly commuting from 300 hours to 30 ([Thrun](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/sebastian-thrun-flying-cars-autonomous-vehicles-and-education/)).

### Economics, automation and principles

- Dalio argues for an idea meritocracy of people who disagree, and says Bridgewater has encoded its principles in algorithms for about 25 years ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/ray-dalio-principles-the-economic-machine-artificial-intelligence-the/)).
- He calls automation a two-edged sword that widens income gaps and should be treated as a national emergency, prioritising equal opportunity through education ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/ray-dalio-principles-the-economic-machine-artificial-intelligence-the/)).
- Michael Stevens says the YouTube algorithm mostly mirrors what people choose to watch, and is not very worried about AI risk ([Vsauce](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/michael-stevens-vsauce/)).

### Physics and programming

- Jim Gates calls string theory a consistent body of mathematics still waiting for observational proof, as general relativity waited for the 1919 eclipse ([Gates](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/jim-gates-supersymmetry-string-theory-and-proving-einstein-right/)).
- Knuth describes how BDDs and SAT solvers reshaped The Art of Computer Programming, and suspects P may equal NP without yielding a usable algorithm ([Knuth](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/12/donald-knuth-algorithms-tex-life-and-the-art-of-computer-programming/)).
