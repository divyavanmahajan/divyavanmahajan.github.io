## Overview

January ranged from a creator business to religion, history and hard science. MrBeast explained how he builds viral video and a company around it, Omar Suleiman and Jeremi Suri took on faith and American politics, and Dennis Whyte and David Kipping covered fusion power and the search for life beyond Earth.

## Main themes

### Viral video as a craft and a business

- MrBeast argues virality is a trainable skill: simple thumbnails, titles under 50 characters, and close attention to retention and YouTube's satisfaction surveys ([MrBeast](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram/)).
- He scales his company by hiring obsessed, coachable people and training them in his taste; his CEO James lived with him for two years before running the business alone ([MrBeast](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram/)).
- He says short vertical video can now go viral on every platform at once, and that keeping Walmart shelves stocked with Feastables was his biggest surprise ([MrBeast](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram/)).

### Faith, identity and politics

- Omar Suleiman describes Islam as trust in an all-wise God, lived through prayer, fasting and service, and calls Ramadan a spiritual bootcamp ([Omar Suleiman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/omar-suleiman-islam/)).
- He argues that most conflicts framed as religious are political at root, and that groups like ISIS grew out of political chaos in Iraq ([Omar Suleiman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/omar-suleiman-islam/)).
- On Israel and Palestine he gives his own view that Israeli policy meets the definition of apartheid; Fridman says leaders on all sides are also worth hearing ([Omar Suleiman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/omar-suleiman-islam/)).

### America's unfinished Civil War

- Jeremi Suri argues the Senate, the Electoral College and the Supreme Court gave slaveholders outsized power, and that flaws in these institutions kept the war's conflicts alive after 1865 ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/jeremi-suri-american-civil-war/)).
- He links the disputed 1876 election and unpunished Confederate leaders to today's fights over elections. Fridman pushes back on his reading of January 6th ([Jeremi Suri](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/jeremi-suri-american-civil-war/)).

### Fusion power and life beyond Earth

- Dennis Whyte says high-temperature superconducting magnets and better computing let small teams and private firms such as Commonwealth Fusion Systems move faster than ITER, with grid power aimed at the early 2030s ([Dennis Whyte](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/dennis-whyte-nuclear-fusion-and-the-future-of-energy/)).
- Whyte puts the December 2022 ignition result at a gain of about 1.5 and says a laser power plant would need a gain near 100. He sees economics, not physics, as the main remaining problem ([Dennis Whyte](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/dennis-whyte-nuclear-fusion-and-the-future-of-energy/)).
- David Kipping stays deliberately agnostic about alien life and explains why habitable moons could outnumber planets ([David Kipping](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/david-kipping-alien-civilizations-and-habitable-worlds/)).
- After trying ChatGPT, Kipping suggests civilisations may pass briefly from biological to artificial intelligence, which would bear on the Fermi paradox ([David Kipping](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/01/david-kipping-alien-civilizations-and-habitable-worlds/)).
