## Overview

October 2018 paired a psychologist and a deep learning pioneer on the limits and risks of AI. Steven Pinker took apart the standard existential-risk stories, while Yoshua Bengio argued that today's neural networks understand their data only shallowly and need new training objectives, not just more scale. Both guests put more weight on concrete near-term harms than on machine takeover.

## Main themes

### What current AI does not understand

- Pinker says deep learning extracts high-order statistical regularities but lacks a semantic level of understanding. He sees no barrier in principle to silicon matching a brain, but doubts a full replica is worth building ([Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- Bengio argues the gap is neither data nor architecture but the objectives systems learn under. He wants causal structure, disentangled variables and the rules relating them, and language grounded in world models ([Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).
- Bengio notes that recurrent nets handle sequences of hundreds of steps, while people revise judgments made a year earlier. He links long-span credit assignment to selective memory and consciousness ([Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).

### Existential risk as a weak argument

- Pinker argues that intelligence does not bring a drive for dominance; humans bundle the two because of natural selection, while an AI's goals are whatever we set ([Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- He calls the paperclip scenario self-defeating, since it imagines a system clever enough to cure cancer but too stupid to see that this does not mean killing everyone ([Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- Bengio considers existential risk very unlikely but worth study ([Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).

### The risks worth attention

- Bengio wants public debate on face-recognition surveillance, autonomous weapons, jobs, concentration of power and discrimination. He says debiasing methods such as adversarial training are mature enough for governments to require, for example of insurers ([Bengio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/yoshua-bengio-deep-learning/)).
- Pinker warns that fatalism about imagined threats crowds out likely ones such as pandemics, nuclear war and climate change. He and Fridman agree autonomous vehicles could cut the roughly 40,000 US road deaths a year ([Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
- On jobs, Pinker expects income from automated work such as crop picking and coal mining can be redistributed ([Pinker](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2018/10/steven-pinker-ai-in-the-age-of-reason/)).
