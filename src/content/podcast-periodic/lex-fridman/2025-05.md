## Overview

May had three long conversations with no shared thread: a cosmologist on black holes, a country singer on working-class America, and a historian on World War II. After two months dominated by current politics, Trump dropped out of the conversation entirely.

## Main themes

### Black holes as a test bed for physics

- Janna Levin argues that black holes are “more general and more fundamental than just the death state of a star”. At the event horizon, she says, there is only empty spacetime, more a place than a thing ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).
- On the information paradox she rejects information loss, since quantum mechanics treats preserving information as a sacred principle. She favours ER = EPR, which suggests gravity may emerge from quantum entanglement, while noting it is still a conjecture ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).
- She recounts LIGO's roughly 50-year path to the first gravitational-wave signal in September 2015, and hopes dark matter, dark energy and extra dimensions turn out to be linked ([Janna Levin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/janna-levin-black-holes-wormholes-aliens-paradoxes-extra-dimensions/)).

### Blue-collar America and the cost of corporate culture

- Oliver Anthony argues that the people who build and maintain society have the least representation, and that trades from farming to plumbing are dying because they are no longer popular in culture ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
- He says “corporate HR has been implemented into our whole thought process of everything”, and that fear of losing his job is why his songs carry a pseudonym. Fridman adds that polite bureaucracy can hide atrocities, citing Chernobyl ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
- After Rich Men North of Richmond went viral he received over 50,000 messages and turned down $8 million offers, saying signing would betray his listeners. He now plans non-profit venues outside the Ticketmaster and Live Nation system ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).
- He speaks openly about depression and drinking, and credits quitting alcohol with letting him be honest with himself ([Oliver Anthony](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/oliver-anthony-country-music-blue-collar-america-fame-money-and-pain/)).

### World War II as a war of logistics

- James Holland argues the war was decided by supply chains, factories and training. He says Hitler was no military genius: for the 1940 plan against France, “The concept is from Manstein’s, and the execution is Guderian’s” ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
- He says Germany invaded the Soviet Union with 2,000 vehicle types, and that the drive for Caucasus oil made no sense without refineries, pipelines or tankers ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
- Holland calls Chamberlain's 1938 decision understandable rather than right, and says France's risk aversion was itself the greater risk. He adds that the Battle of Britain story usually leaves out nightly RAF raids on German airfields ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
- He calls D-Day the zenith of coalition warfare, with 6,939 vessels and 155,000 men landed in 24 hours, two-thirds of them British and Dominion troops ([James Holland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/05/james-holland-world-war-ii-hitler-churchill-stalin-biggest-battles/)).
