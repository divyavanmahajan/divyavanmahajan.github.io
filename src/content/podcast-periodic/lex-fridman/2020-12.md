## Overview

December brought the show back to AI, with Michael Littman appearing twice and Waymo's CTO Dmitri Dolgov explaining how a public driverless service came to run in Phoenix. Around that sat conversations on education, drug and testing policy, the religious side of UFO belief, and Michael Malice's year-end case for hope.

## Main themes

### Reinforcement learning and AI risk

- Michael Littman says he is “not particularly moved” by the idea of accidentally creating a superintelligence. Like a 747, AGI will not assemble itself, and building it will teach us how to control it ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Littman says AlphaGo developed its own strategies, and credits the skilled people behind such results. He expects self-play gains and Bitter Lesson exponentials to flatten into sigmoids ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He argues GPT-3-style models cannot master language without people pushing back in conversation. Fridman is more open to exponential progress surprising us ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).

### Self-driving as a social problem

- Teaching his son to drive convinced Littman that driving is “a social interaction activity”, and that a gradual rollout in closed communities would have been a better plan ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Dmitri Dolgov traces Waymo from a dozen people at Google in 2009 to fully driverless public rides in Phoenix on 8 October 2020 ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).
- Dolgov argues cameras, lidar and radar complement each other, rejecting Elon Musk's view of lidar as a crutch. Waymo's stack mixes learned and rule-based models, and he frames scaling around technology, evaluation and operations ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).

### Machine learning and education

- Charles Isbell says machine learning is about data and the questions it answers more than algorithms. Students in his course may copy algorithm code but must analyse data sets ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).
- Isbell argues students pay for the campus experience, not just classes, and that COVID compressed a decade of change in higher education into a few years. Georgia Tech's online master's reaches about 11,000 students ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).
- Isbell calls Bell Labs a high-water mark of US basic research. Littman traces Siri back to the DARPA program started by their former boss ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).

### Drug and testing policy

- Matthew Johnson uses behavioural economics to explain addiction, favours regulating drugs rather than banning them, and warns that a nicotine ban without alternatives could create black markets ([Matthew Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/matthew-johnson-psychedelics/)).
- Michael Mina argues cheap at-home rapid tests could curb COVID spread, but regulators judge tests only as medical devices, so nobody owns the public-health use ([Michael Mina](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-mina-rapid-testing-viruses-and-the-engineering-mindset/)).

### Belief, hope and excellence

- Diana Walsh Pasulka argues belief in UFOs and non-human intelligence works like a new religion, closely tied to how people imagine and build technology ([Diana Walsh Pasulka](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/diana-walsh-pasulka-aliens-technology-religion-and-the-nature-of-belie/)).
- Michael Malice's white pill holds that history shows good people can defeat evil systems. He argues every country is already in anarchy relative to others ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-malice-the-white-pill-freedom-hope-and-happiness-amidst-chaos/)).
- John Clarke, Fridman's jiu-jitsu coach, says excellence means removing every excuse and accepting sacrifice. Fridman asks for more patience with beginners' dreams ([John Clarke](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/john-clarke-the-art-of-fighting-and-the-pursuit-of-excellence/)).
