## Overview

December 2020 returned to AI, with Michael Littman on reinforcement learning, Dmitri Dolgov on Waymo and a joint episode on machine learning and education. Two conversations dealt with health policy during the pandemic. The month closed with episodes on belief, freedom and the pursuit of excellence.

## Main themes

### Reinforcement learning and the limits of AI

- Michael Littman is not persuaded by the runaway-superintelligence story. He compares AGI to a 747 that will not assemble itself, so building it will teach us how to control it. Lex Fridman is more open to surprise ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Littman credits skilled people such as Gerry Tesauro and David Silver behind RL breakthroughs, and expects self-play gains and other exponentials to flatten into sigmoids ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He argues GPT-3-style models cannot master language without people pushing back in conversation, and that driving needs a theory of other drivers' minds ([Michael Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Charles Isbell says the AI risk to worry about is data-driven tools that make bad decisions more efficiently ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).

### Driverless cars reach the public

- Dmitri Dolgov says Waymo opened fully driverless rides to the public in Phoenix on 8 October 2020, after a project that began at Google in 2009 ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).
- The fifth-generation system has 29 cameras, five lidars and six radars, built for large-scale manufacturing. Dolgov rejects the view of lidar as a crutch and describes a hybrid of learned and rule-based models ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).
- He frames scaling around core technology, repeatable evaluation and deployment, and product and operations ([Dmitri Dolgov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/)).

### Teaching machine learning

- Isbell argues machine learning is about the data and the questions it answers more than the algorithm, and grades students on their analysis of data sets ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).
- Littman separates hopeful struggle from hopeless struggle. Isbell says Georgia Tech's online master's reaches about 11,000 students at a fraction of the campus price ([Isbell and Littman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/)).

### Health policy in a pandemic year

- Michael Mina frames the pandemic as an engineering problem and argues cheap at-home rapid tests could cut spread, but regulators judge them only as medical devices and nobody owns the public-health use case ([Michael Mina](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-mina-rapid-testing-viruses-and-the-engineering-mindset/)).
- Matthew Johnson discusses psychedelics research at Johns Hopkins and favours regulating rather than banning drugs, while warning against overclaiming ([Matthew Johnson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/matthew-johnson-psychedelics/)).

### Belief, freedom and excellence

- Diana Walsh Pasulka argues belief in UFOs works like a new religion and is closely tied to how people imagine and build technology ([Diana Walsh Pasulka](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/diana-walsh-pasulka-aliens-technology-religion-and-the-nature-of-belie/)).
- Michael Malice argues against cynicism, citing the end of the Cold War as proof that the good guys can win. Fridman pushes for more care where Malice favours provocation ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-malice-the-white-pill-freedom-hope-and-happiness-amidst-chaos/)).
- Fridman's coach John Clarke says excellence means removing every excuse and accepting sacrifice. Fridman asks for more patience with beginners ([John Clarke](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/john-clarke-the-art-of-fighting-and-the-pursuit-of-excellence/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Michael Littman: Reinforcement Learning and the Future of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-littman-reinforcement-learning-and-the-future-of-ai/): an RL veteran's measured take on scaling, self-play and AI risk.
- [Dmitri Dolgov: Waymo and the Future of Self-Driving Cars](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/dmitri-dolgov-waymo-and-the-future-of-self-driving-cars/): the path from the DARPA challenge to a public driverless service.
- [Charles Isbell and Michael Littman: Machine Learning and Education](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/charles-isbell-and-michael-littman-machine-learning-and-education/): data-first teaching and the economics of online degrees.
- [Michael Mina: Rapid Testing](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/12/michael-mina-rapid-testing-viruses-and-the-engineering-mindset/): a case study in how regulation shapes what gets deployed.
