## Overview

June was the most AI-heavy month of the year so far. Mark Zuckerberg, Marc Andreessen and George Hotz each argued that open models are safer than AI concentrated in a few hands, and Chris Lattner and Hotz described new software stacks for AI hardware. Two conversations with Bert Kreischer and Matthew McConaughey were about how to live well.

## Main themes

### Open source versus concentrated AI

- Zuckerberg says open models like Llama, still far from superintelligence, become more secure and efficient through community work. He wants governance to focus on how much autonomy systems get, not on intelligence ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Andreessen contrasts a few God models protected by regulatory capture with a billion open LLMs. He casts AI regulation as a Baptists-and-bootleggers story and names China winning AI dominance as the risk he takes most seriously ([Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)).
- Hotz blames human misuse, not rebellious machines, and argues open-sourcing models guards against a single controlled AI. Fridman pushes back on lowering the barrier to harm ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).
- Lattner worries that $100 million training runs will concentrate AI in a few hands ([Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/chris-lattner-future-of-programming-and-ai/)).

### Software for AI hardware

- Lattner says AI is held back by complexity: thousands of operators and chips, and a split between Python research and C++ deployment. Mojo, a Python superset, is his answer, launched early at version 0.1 ([Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/chris-lattner-future-of-programming-and-ai/)).
- Hotz says AI chip startups fail on software. His tinygrad uses about 25 primitive operations, and the planned $15,000 tinybox would run 65-billion-parameter LLaMA from a home wall outlet ([Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).
- Both see code-generating LLMs as helpers. Lattner expects them to take over rote work; Hotz calls Copilot junior-engineer level and wants an AI that finds bugs ([Lattner](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/chris-lattner-future-of-programming-and-ai/), [Hotz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/george-hotz-tiny-corp-twitter-ai-safety-self-driving-gpt-agi-god/)).

### Platforms, business models and trust

- Zuckerberg expects many specialised AIs for creators and small businesses on WhatsApp, not one central assistant. He also explains Meta's flatter management and Quest 3 against Apple's Vision Pro ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/mark-zuckerberg-future-of-ai-at-meta-facebook-instagram-and-whatsapp/)).
- Jimmy Wales says Wikipedia stays trustworthy because it has no ads or engagement algorithm, and argues business models shape outcomes more than intentions. ChatGPT-4 invented athlete quotes for him, but he sees promise in tools grounded in citations ([Wales](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/jimmy-wales-wikipedia/)).
- Andreessen expects search to give way to direct answers, and warns models lose training data if people stop making web pages ([Andreessen](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/marc-andreessen-future-of-the-internet-technology-and-ai/)).

### Joy, luck and hardship

- Kreischer credits his career to chasing fun without ego, trusting friends like Tom Segura and Joe Rogan, and not being afraid to look foolish in promotion ([Kreischer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/bert-kreischer-comedy-drinking-rogan-segura-churchill-kim-jong-un/)).
- McConaughey found that less than half of his successes were engineered; the rest came from trusting what he could not plan. He calls AI a prompt that reflects the questions we ask it ([McConaughey](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/06/matthew-mcconaughey-freedom-truth-family-hardship-and-love/)).
