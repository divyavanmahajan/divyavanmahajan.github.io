## Overview

April carried on March's AI debate, with Max Tegmark defending the six-month pause letter and Manolis Kellis arguing against it. Robotics came from two directions, Boston Dynamics' move into products and Simone Giertz's maker business. Edward Frenkel and Paul Rosolie added mathematics and the Amazon.

## Main themes

### To pause or not to pause

- Max Tegmark argues labs are stuck in a race where no one can slow down alone, and that the open letter gives them cover to coordinate. He says six months was chosen to blunt the China objection ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Tegmark lists risky steps already taken: teaching AI to code, connecting it to the internet and opening APIs for agents. He proposes running only AI code that can pass a simple proof checker ([Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).
- Manolis Kellis asks what was done in the previous six months, wants Google and Meta models opened up, and prefers rules on uses rather than on training ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).
- Kellis treats AI as the next layer of evolution and argues alignment should run both ways, with AI given rights. Fridman pushes back on human-like bots and phishing ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).

### Robots, from labs to products

- Robert Playter says Boston Dynamics is moving from research lab to product business, and that robots must be useful in industry before consumer robots make sense ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- He puts about 1,100 Spots in the field, mainly for industrial inspection, and says the Stretch box-moving robot began shipping to DHL and Maersk in January ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- Playter welcomes Tesla's humanoid effort as validation and describes a pledge, co-signed by other robot makers, not to arm general-purpose robots ([Robert Playter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/robert-playter-boston-dynamics-ceo-on-humanoid-and-legged-robotics/)).
- Simone Giertz moved from deliberately failing robots to product design, and says manufacturing is a separate discipline from building one-offs ([Simone Giertz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/simone-giertz-queen-of-sh-tty-robots-innovative-engineering-and-design/)).

### Limits of computation

- Edward Frenkel says he no longer believes everything reduces to computation. He cites Gödel's incompleteness theorem and questions whether language models could make imaginative leaps in mathematics ([Edward Frenkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/edward-frenkel-reality-is-a-paradox-mathematics-physics-truth-love/)).
- Frenkel doubts any theory of everything exists. Tegmark, by contrast, compares language models to the Wright brothers' plane: simple, yet close to human ability ([Edward Frenkel](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/edward-frenkel-reality-is-a-paradox-mathematics-physics-truth-love/), [Max Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/max-tegmark-the-case-for-halting-ai-development/)).

### Making a living from what you build or protect

- Paul Rosolie says Junglekeepers pays loggers more to protect the Amazon than to cut it, and that funding from a viral 2019 video now helps protect 50,000 acres ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/paul-rosolie-amazon-jungle-uncontacted-tribes-anacondas-and-ayahuasca/)).
- Giertz says a serious illness showed her how fragile an ad-based YouTube business is. She now runs a self-funded store and uses her channel for R&D ([Simone Giertz](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/simone-giertz-queen-of-sh-tty-robots-innovative-engineering-and-design/)).
- Kellis expects AI to take over routine work so that people move from jobs to vocations ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/04/manolis-kellis-evolution-of-human-civilization-and-superintelligent-ai/)).
