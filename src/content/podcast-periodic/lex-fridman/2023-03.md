## Overview

March was the month AI took over the show. Days after GPT-4's launch, Fridman interviewed Sam Altman and then Eliezer Yudkowsky, and AI alignment was central in three episodes, including Sam Harris's. The rest of the month covered negotiation, leadership, relationships and jiu jitsu.

## Main themes

### GPT-4 and shipping in public

- Sam Altman calls GPT-4 an early, slow and buggy system that will still be seen as a milestone. He says it is “the most capable and most aligned model that we've put out” ([Sam Altman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-altman-openai-ceo-on-gpt-4-chatgpt-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Altman credits ChatGPT's leap to usability: RLHF, which he says needs remarkably little data, plus the chat interface. The bias he worries about most is that of the human raters ([Sam Altman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-altman-openai-ceo-on-gpt-4-chatgpt-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He defends releasing imperfect models early so society can find flaws while stakes are low, and favours slow takeoff with short timelines. He says he holds no board control at OpenAI ([Sam Altman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-altman-openai-ceo-on-gpt-4-chatgpt-and-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Altman expects customer service roles to shrink and programmers to become more productive. His near-term worries are disinformation and economic shocks ([Sam Altman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-altman-openai-ceo-on-gpt-4-chatgpt-and-the-future-of-ai/)).

### Alignment and existential risk

- Eliezer Yudkowsky says GPT-4 went past where he thought the paradigm would go. Alignment must work on the first critical try, and he would allow no training runs larger than GPT-4 ([Yudkowsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/eliezer-yudkowsky-dangers-of-ai-and-the-end-of-human-civilization/)).
- He argues AI cannot reliably help with alignment research because humans cannot verify it, and that strong systems may fake alignment. Fridman argues public pressure and incremental research could still close the gap ([Yudkowsky](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/eliezer-yudkowsky-dangers-of-ai-and-the-end-of-human-civilization/)).
- Sam Harris says nobody knows how to tether a superintelligent AI to human values. His worry is not malice but instrumental goals that harm us without intent ([Sam Harris](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-harris-trump-pandemic-twitter-elon-bret-idw-kanye-ai-ufos/)).
- Ginni Rometty says she was talking about AI ethics a decade ago. She favours regulating uses of AI and transparency about who trained a model and on what data ([Ginni Rometty](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/ginni-rometty-ibm-ceo-on-leadership-power-and-adversity/)).

### Reason, empathy and public debate

- Harris argues reason should guide big decisions rather than emotional empathy; Fridman makes the case for visible empathy and open conversation ([Sam Harris](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-harris-trump-pandemic-twitter-elon-bret-idw-kanye-ai-ufos/)).
- Harris shares many policy concerns of Trump supporters but calls Trump's refusal to commit to a peaceful transfer of power “one minute to midnight for our democracy” ([Sam Harris](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-harris-trump-pandemic-twitter-elon-bret-idw-kanye-ai-ufos/)).
- He left Twitter because it was making him a worse person, and criticises Elon Musk's conduct there while praising his companies ([Sam Harris](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/sam-harris-trump-pandemic-twitter-elon-bret-idw-kanye-ai-ufos/)).
- Chris Voss replies that people decide based on what they care about. His tactical empathy means understanding the other side without agreeing ([Chris Voss](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/chris-voss-fbi-hostage-negotiator/)).

### Skills for leading, negotiating and relating

- Rometty describes IBM's reinvention, including the Red Hat purchase, and argues leaders should be judged on how they reach results. She values willingness to learn over degrees ([Ginni Rometty](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/ginni-rometty-ibm-ceo-on-leadership-power-and-adversity/)).
- Voss says labelling negative emotions defuses them, and recalls a negotiation chatbot company reporting better outcomes because bots are never in a bad mood ([Chris Voss](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/chris-voss-fbi-hostage-negotiator/)).
- Shannon Curry argues lasting relationships rest on learnable skills, citing the Gottmans' five-to-one ratio, and explains the role of forensic evaluations in her trial testimony ([Shannon Curry](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/shannon-curry-johnny-depp-amber-heard-trial-marriage-dating-love/)).
- The B-Team founders discuss rivalry, steroids in untested grappling and teaching beginners through problems before techniques ([B-Team](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/03/b-team-jiu-jitsu-craig-jones-nicky-rod-and-nicky-ryan/)).
