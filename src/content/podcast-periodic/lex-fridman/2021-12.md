## Overview

December ended the year with Elon Musk's third appearance and Ray Dalio's account of how empires rise and fall. Peter Wang and Peter Woit brought software and physics, while two pandemic episodes and a New Year's Eve talk with Michael Malice turned on trust in institutions and power.

## Main themes

### Musk: physics first, then manufacturing

- Elon Musk says the hard part of his projects is volume manufacturing, not ideas. For Starship, the bottleneck is making Raptor engines at scale ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- He reasons from first principles and in the limit, saying “I have never met anyone who could break physics.” ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- Musk puts the cost to the Martian surface at about a billion dollars per ton and says it must fall at least a thousandfold. For Mars he proposes short laws that are easier to repeal than to pass ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- On Tesla, he says the hard part of self-driving is turning camera images into an accurate vector space, and describes the Tesla Bot as reusing the Autopilot computer and training ([Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).

### Empires, money and crypto

- Ray Dalio describes a big cycle of debt, internal order and external order that he tracks with 18 measures, and says the main US war is with itself ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/)).
- He says China has become the largest trading country and that “they put out about eight times as many computer engineers” as the US ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/)).
- Dalio prefers gold to Bitcoin, while Musk argues Bitcoin's volume and latency limit it as a currency and that Dogecoin's fixed issuance suits spending better ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/), [Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).

### Open source and the end of classical software

- Peter Wang credits the SciPy stack's success to a small group of domain scientists and says Anaconda pays a share of revenue back to the open-source community ([Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).
- He argues we are moving to cybernetic systems where software closes the decision loop without humans, and that “the biggest cybernetic system in the world is the financial markets” ([Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).
- Wang explains that Conda exists because numerical libraries must be built per chip, OS and GPU setup. Fridman pushes back on his pessimism about social media ([Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).

### Physics beyond string theory

- Peter Woit argues string theory failed because its many ways of compacting extra dimensions mean it predicts nothing testable ([Woit](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-woit-theories-of-everything-and-why-string-theory-is-not-even-wr/)).
- He now works on Penrose's twistor theory in four dimensions, and says overselling failed ideas erodes trust in science ([Woit](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-woit-theories-of-everything-and-why-string-theory-is-not-even-wr/)).

### Pandemic, trust and power

- Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla says the vaccine came fast because Pfizer committed about $2.3 billion at once and set stretch goals. Fridman presses him on FDA transparency, industry reputation and mandates ([Bourla](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/albert-bourla-pfizer-ceo/)).
- Jamie Metzl argues the circumstantial evidence favours a lab incident in Wuhan and that the Chinese government's cover-up has blocked a real investigation ([Metzl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/jamie-metzl-lab-leak-theory/)).
- Michael Malice, an anarchist, argues only opposing power restrains elites, while Fridman puts more faith in reforming institutions from within. Malice says he would have pressed Bourla harder on incentives ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/michael-malice-new-year-s-special/)).
