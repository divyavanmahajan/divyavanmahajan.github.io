## Overview

December closed 2021 with a heavy line-up: Elon Musk's third appearance, Ray Dalio on the rise and fall of empires, and Anaconda's Peter Wang on Python and open source. A recurring thread was trust, in science, in pandemic institutions and in the powerful, which ran from a string theory critic through two COVID-related conversations to the New Year's Eve special.

## Main themes

### Engineering at scale: rockets, robots and self-driving

- Elon Musk says the hard part of his projects is volume manufacturing, not ideas. Starship's biggest problem is making Raptor engines at scale ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- Musk says the cost per ton to the Martian surface must fall at least a thousandfold, and suggests Mars laws with sunset clauses and an easier path to repeal than to pass ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- On Autopilot, Musk says the hard part is turning camera images into an accurate vector space. He describes the Tesla Bot as a general-purpose robot reusing the Autopilot computer and training ([Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).

### Empires, money and power

- Ray Dalio describes a repeating big cycle of debt, internal order and external order, tracked with 18 measures, and says the main US war is with itself ([Ray Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/)).
- Dalio says China has become the largest trading country and is improving faster in education and technology, and sees Taiwan as the only likely trigger for military war ([Ray Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/)).
- Dalio prefers gold to Bitcoin, while Musk calls money a database and argues Bitcoin's volume and latency limit it as a currency ([Ray Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/), [Elon Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- Michael Malice argues that unchecked power lets elites act without consequence, while Fridman puts more faith in reforming institutions from within ([Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/michael-malice-new-year-s-special/)).

### Open source and the end of classical software

- Peter Wang argues we are moving from classical software to data-driven cybernetic systems, where input data and performance targets become part of correctness ([Peter Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).
- Wang says the SciPy stack, built by about a dozen people, drives billions of dollars of value a day, and uses it to question scarcity-era ideas of property ([Peter Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).
- He explains that Conda exists because compiled numerical libraries must be built per chip, OS and GPU setup ([Peter Wang](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-wang-python-and-the-source-code-of-humans-computers-and-reality/)).

### Trust in science and institutions

- Peter Woit argues string theory failed because it can accommodate almost anything, and that overselling it misleads students and erodes trust in science ([Peter Woit](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-woit-theories-of-everything-and-why-string-theory-is-not-even-wr/)).
- Jamie Metzl argues that a cover-up by the Chinese government has blocked any real investigation into COVID-19's origins, and describes the WHO process as heavily constrained ([Jamie Metzl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/jamie-metzl-lab-leak-theory/)).
- Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla says the vaccine came fast because Pfizer committed about $2.3 billion at once and set stretch goals. Fridman presses him on regulator transparency and mandates, and later reflects with Malice on criticism that the interview was too soft ([Albert Bourla](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/albert-bourla-pfizer-ceo/), [Michael Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/michael-malice-new-year-s-special/)).
