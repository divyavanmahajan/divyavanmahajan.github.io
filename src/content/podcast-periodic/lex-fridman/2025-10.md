## Overview

October brought three founders and thinkers with strong views on freedom, human nature and creativity. Pavel Durov explained how Telegram resists governments, Julia Shaw argued that evil is a label rather than a trait, and Dan Houser looked back on Rockstar's open worlds. Two of the three also weighed in on what AI can and cannot do.

## Main themes

### Privacy and independence under state pressure

- Pavel Durov says fear and greed are the main enemies of freedom. Owning 100% of Telegram lets him refuse requests to hand over private messages or censor peaceful political speech ([Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- He describes his detention in France and says French intelligence later asked him to block channels before the Romanian election and in Moldova. He refused and made the requests public ([Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- When Russia banned Telegram in 2018, the company rotated millions of IP addresses through proxies. He estimates about 50 million users in Iran despite the ban there ([Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).

### Small teams and business models

- Durov says a core engineering team of about 40 people runs almost 100,000 servers, and that small teams force automation. Encryption keys are split across jurisdictions ([Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).
- Telegram Premium has over 15 million subscribers and will bring in more than half a billion dollars this year. Ads are contextual rather than based on personal data ([Durov](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/pavel-durov-telegram-freedom-censorship-money-power-human-nature/)).

### Crime, memory and the people behind harm

- Julia Shaw argues dark traits sit on a continuum and everyone can do terrible things in the right circumstances. Most murders, she says, are fights over trivial things, and recidivism is low ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- In her PhD study, three suggestive interviews led 70% of participants to remember committing a crime that never happened. Her advice to witnesses is to write things down at once ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- Her book on green crime explains cases such as Volkswagen's Dieselgate through conformity, rationalisation and social pressure ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).

### What AI can and cannot do for people

- Shaw warns that chatbots tell people what they want to hear and that leading prompts distort both human memory and model output. Her company Spot uses a chatbot to run a scripted cognitive interview for workplace reports ([Julia Shaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/julia-shaw-criminal-psychology-of-murder-serial-killers-memory-sex/)).
- Dan Houser argues LLMs have done the first 90-95% of sounding human, and the last 5% will be most of the work. He expects AI to help with cheap, low-level work but not original ideas ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).

### Story in open-world games

- Houser says great open worlds combine a living, systemic world with strong story and rounded characters. He thinks Red Dead Redemption 2 reconciled the two best ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).
- He recalls proposing that John Marston must die, breaking Rockstar's rule about player freedom, and describes his new company Absurd Ventures, which stays focused on single-player open worlds ([Dan Houser](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/10/dan-houser-gta-red-dead-redemption-rockstar-absurd-future-of-gaming/)).
