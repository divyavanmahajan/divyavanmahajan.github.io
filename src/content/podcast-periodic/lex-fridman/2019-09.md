## Overview

September 2019 mixed AI sceptics and builders. François Chollet and Peter Norvig questioned hype and asked what trustworthy AI needs, while Vijay Kumar and Colin Angle described robots that work in the air and in the home. Regina Barzilay and Leonard Susskind brought in medicine and physics.

## Main themes

### What deep learning can and cannot do

- François Chollet argues intelligence comes from a brain, a body and an environment together, so improving one part only exposes a new bottleneck. He calls the singularity story closer to a belief system than a scientific argument ([Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)).
- Chollet sees deep learning as interpolation that needs dense data, and expects progress from combining it with program synthesis. He does not expect a full AI winter but warns of backlash, saying overselling AI is “like the equivalent of taking on credit card debt yeah but for for trust” ([Chollet](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/francois-chollet-fran-ois-chollet-keras-deep-learning-and-the-progress/)).
- Leonard Susskind, who advises machine-learning physicists at Google X, says nobody really understands why machine learning generalises so well ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).

### Robots in the air and in the home

- Vijay Kumar defines true autonomy as flying with no GPS, no link to a base station, no pilot and no map. He says agility means you “see the unexpected and you're able to maneuver in a safe way” ([Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).
- Kumar sees battery energy density and data-hungry learning as the limits ahead. He finds search and rescue a more interesting human-robot problem than driving, and worries that swarms are easy to weaponise ([Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/)).
- Colin Angle says a home robot must solve a frequent, disliked chore and deliver clearly more value than it costs. iRobot has sold 25 million robots and bet on cheap cameras rather than lasers ([iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/)).
- Both Kumar and Angle discussed autonomous vehicles; Angle would still consider a cheap lidar as a backup sensor for cars ([Kumar](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/), [iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/)).

### Trust, fairness and the standard textbook

- Peter Norvig says the 1995 edition of his book with Stuart Russell bet on probability and machine learning but missed big data. The fourth edition adds a deep learning chapter by Ian Goodfellow ([Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)).
- Using recidivism tools as an example, Norvig explains that equal calibration and equal error rates cannot both be met, so humans must choose the trade-off ([Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)).
- He says he prefers “to talk about trust and uh validation and verification rather than just about explainability”, backed by testing and dialogue ([Norvig](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/)).

### AI adoption in medicine

- Regina Barzilay argues the models are already good enough for earlier detection, and that the barriers are data access, hospital incentives and regulators catching up ([Barzilay](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/regina-barzilay-deep-learning-for-cancer-diagnosis-and-treatment/)).
- She says it took two years to get data access and proposes an organ-donor-style opt-in for medical records. She expects consumers of healthcare to push change ([Barzilay](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/regina-barzilay-deep-learning-for-cancer-diagnosis-and-treatment/)).

### Physics and quantum computing

- Susskind expects quantum computers to matter most for simulating chemistry, materials and quantum gravity, with factoring-style speedups being rare ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).
- He sees string theory's main contribution as showing gravity and quantum mechanics can coexist, and treats the arrow of time as a thermodynamic effect ([Susskind](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/leonard-susskind-quantum-mechanics-string-theory-and-black-holes/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Peter Norvig: Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/peter-norvig-artificial-intelligence-a-modern-approach/): a clear account of fairness trade-offs and how the field's textbook changed.
- [Colin Angle: iRobot](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/colin-angle-irobot/): rare business detail on why robotics companies fail and what makes a home robot sell.
- [Vijay Kumar: Flying Robots](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/09/vijay-kumar-flying-robots/): what true autonomy means for small aerial robots and swarms.
