## Overview

July ranged widely: a neuroscientist, a physicist, a venture investor, a self-driving car CEO and a product chief. The only thread across episodes was Tesla and Elon Musk, raised by both Kai-Fu Lee and Chris Urmson in the context of autonomous driving. Elsewhere the month covered China's tech ecosystem, how the brain might guide AI, and how Spotify uses machine learning.

## Main themes

### Self-driving cars: deployment and safety

- Chris Urmson draws lessons from CMU's DARPA teams, crediting multi-beam lidar for the Urban Challenge, and says it matters to society “that somebody gets there” ([Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).
- Responding to Elon Musk, Urmson says any useful sensor should be used given about 37,000 US road deaths a year. Kai-Fu Lee also questions Tesla's decision to skip lidar ([Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/), [Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- Urmson calls disengagement counts a poor metric and proposes per-task comparisons with human failure rates plus an aviation-style event pyramid. He warns that people over-trust Level 2 driver assistance ([Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).
- He expects tens of thousands of driverless vehicles within ten years, starting in moderate-speed urban areas rather than freeway trucking ([Urmson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/chris-urmson-self-driving-cars-at-aurora-google-cmu-and-darpa/)).

### China, Silicon Valley and AI jobs

- Lee contrasts China's data-heavy, execution-driven teams with American teams that look for new algorithms. He says Chinese firms moved from copying to originals such as TikTok, Pinduoduo and Mobike ([Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- He describes the state as an infrastructure builder: 8,000 incubators, smart highways and guiding funds that act as passive LPs ([Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).
- Lee argues routine white-collar work goes first, because replacing it needs only software. He favours retraining over a plain basic income cheque ([Lee](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/kai-fu-lee-ai-superpowers-china-and-silicon-valley/)).

### Learning from the brain

- Jeff Hawkins argues the neocortex runs one common algorithm built on reference frames, with thousands of columns modelling objects and voting on a shared answer ([Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- He says real neurons are prediction engines with thousands of synapses, and that back-propagation of an error signal “really can't happen in brains” ([Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Hawkins reports that enforced sparsity made networks harder to fool with adversarial examples, and rejects the view that scale alone is enough ([Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/jeff-hawkins-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).

### The universe, aliens and consciousness

- Sean Carroll describes the universe as a computation rather than a computer, and argues against the simulation hypothesis ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/)).
- He puts the likely number of other intelligent species at zero or billions, and leans to zero because billions would have been noticed ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/)).
- Carroll says artificial consciousness is possible but not close, and expects a gradual approach rather than a single breakthrough ([Carroll](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/sean-carroll-the-nature-of-the-universe-life-and-intelligence/)).

### Spotify, piracy and creator tools

- Gustav Söderström frames early Spotify as legal and fast piracy, launched in Sweden with fast broadband and no music revenue left to lose ([Söderström](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/gustav-soderstrom-spotify/)).
- He explains that user-made playlists made collaborative filtering work, and that recommendations succeed when the product sets the right expectation ([Söderström](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/gustav-soderstrom-spotify/)).
- He wants creators to get software-style feedback loops and A/B tests. Fridman warns they can become a slave to the algorithm ([Söderström](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2019/07/gustav-soderstrom-spotify/)).
