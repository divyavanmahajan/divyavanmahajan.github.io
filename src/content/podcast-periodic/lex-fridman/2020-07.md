## Overview

July 2020 was a busy month of eight episodes, many with Berkeley and MIT professors. Two Berkeley researchers, Sergey Levine and Jitendra Malik, argued that robots and vision systems must learn more like children, and both used Tesla Autopilot as a reference point. Classic computer science came from Brian Kernighan and Richard Karp, while Peter Singer and Ian Hutchinson brought ethics, energy and religion.

## Main themes

### Robots and vision that learn from the world

- Sergey Levine says robot hardware is close to good enough and the gap is intelligence. He argues common sense emerges from having to act in our world, while an image captioner “lives in its own world that consists of pixels and English sentences” ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/)).
- Levine sees learning from large logged datasets (offline RL) as the key open problem, with the hard part being knowing when a model's predictions can be trusted ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/)).
- Jitendra Malik says current vision systems need far more data than humans, and that teenagers learning to drive already understand the visual world and mostly learn control ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/)).

### Autopilot, edge cases and near-term risk

- Fridman notes Tesla Autopilot is approaching a million vehicles and runs on a single multi-task network ([Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/), [Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/)).
- Malik is pessimistic on full autonomy soon: each extra nine of reliability takes far longer, and his own Tesla misjudged a skateboarder ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/)).
- Malik calls fears of AGI within 10-20 years unwarranted and wants attention on present harms such as bias. Levine worries most about people with bad intent and poorly chosen objectives ([Jitendra Malik](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/jitendra-malik-computer-vision/), [Sergey Levine](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/sergey-levine-robotics-and-machine-learning/)).

### Brains and machines inform each other

- Matt Botvinick of DeepMind describes a circle where AI ideas such as meta-learning suggest how the brain works, and brain evidence validates AI methods ([Matt Botvinick](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/matt-botvinick-neuroscience-psychology-and-ai-at-deepmind/)).
- On his distributional dopamine work, Botvinick says the team “enumerated a set of predictions all of which ended up being fairly clearly confirmed”, with replication pending ([Matt Botvinick](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/matt-botvinick-neuroscience-psychology-and-ai-at-deepmind/)).
- Manolis Kellis argues engineers and AI designers can learn robustness from the fault-tolerant way evolution builds living systems ([Manolis Kellis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/manolis-kellis-human-genome-and-evolutionary-dynamics/)).

### Foundations of computer science

- Richard Karp calls P versus NP the central problem of theoretical computer science and would bet P is not equal to NP. He says SAT solvers still handle millions of variables in practice ([Richard Karp](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/richard-karp-algorithms-and-computational-complexity/)).
- Brian Kernighan traces Unix and C to a small, open Bell Labs community. He credits C's survival to expressiveness, efficiency and Unix portability ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/)).
- Kernighan says AWK gives the most value for the effort of any language to learn, and worries that pip and npm leave programmers unsure what they installed ([Brian Kernighan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/brian-kernighan-unix-c-awk-ampl-and-go-programming/)).
- Karp doubts the Turing test and notes deep learning works without theoretical understanding ([Richard Karp](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/richard-karp-algorithms-and-computational-complexity/)).

### Suffering, faith and energy

- Peter Singer argues suffering matters regardless of species and that robots deserve rights only if they become conscious. Fridman pushes back on whether displayed suffering might be enough ([Peter Singer](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/peter-singer-suffering-in-humans-animals-and-ai/)).
- Ian Hutchinson wants more fusion funding, calls fission underrated, and says energy and population problems are social rather than technological ([Ian Hutchinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/ian-hutchinson-nuclear-fusion-plasma-physics-and-religion/)).
- Hutchinson argues against scientism, explains his Christian faith, and doubts that minds can be uploaded ([Ian Hutchinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2020/07/ian-hutchinson-nuclear-fusion-plasma-physics-and-religion/)).
