## Overview

February was the show's most AI-heavy month. Nathan Lambert and Sebastian Raschka surveyed the state of AI in 2026, from post-training and scaling to China and open weights. Peter Steinberger told the story of OpenClaw and argued that personal agents will absorb most apps.

## Main themes

### Anthropic, OpenAI and AI coding

- Lambert says Anthropic is “known for betting very hard on code”, and credits Claude Opus 4.5 and Claude Code for its lead in coding. He adds that Claude Code is built with Claude Code ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Fridman uses Cursor and Claude Code about half and half, but cautions that the coding hype on X may not reflect actual use, where ChatGPT and Gemini dominate ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert still sees OpenAI as the most likely source of a new paradigm, citing o1, Deep Research and Sora ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Steinberger first built OpenClaw on the Claude Code CLI, renamed it after Anthropic asked him to, and now prefers OpenAI's Codex for coding while calling Opus the best general model ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).

### Scaling, open weights and AGI timelines

- Both guests argue no lab keeps a lasting technical lead. The big gains of 2025 came from reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards and inference-time scaling, not new architectures ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert says DeepSeek is losing its lead among Chinese open models to GLM, MiniMax and Kimi K2 Thinking. His ATOM Project argues about $100 million could produce competitive American open models ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
- Lambert expects big tech to spend $100 billion long before an automated AI researcher arrives. Raschka expects amplification rather than a paradigm change ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).

### Personal agents as the operating system

- Steinberger's first prototype took an hour. On a trip, the agent handled a voice message it was never built for by converting the file and sending it to OpenAI for transcription ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- He says agents could make 80% of apps obsolete, because the agent already has the context. In his view every app becomes a slow API that agents reach through the browser ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- On security, he admits prompt injection is unsolved and warns against cheap models such as Haiku or local models. Many early reports came from users exposing the debug interface to the internet ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).

### What happens to programmers

- Steinberger prefers the term agentic engineering and builds codebases that are easy for agents to navigate. He expects hand-coding to become like knitting ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/)).
- He thinks high developer salaries will go away but demand for builders will remain: “You are, you are still a builder.” ([OpenClaw](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/peter-steinberger-openclaw-the-viral-ai-agent-that-broke-the-internet/))
- Lambert expects software engineering to shift towards system design and outcomes. Raschka asks how people will become experts if they never do the work themselves ([State of AI](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2026/02/ai-sota-2026-state-of-ai-in-2026-llms-coding-scaling-laws-china-agents/)).
