## Overview

In 2021 the Lex Fridman Podcast published about two episodes a week and ranged far beyond AI. Crypto was the strongest single thread in the spring, with a run of Bitcoin, Ethereum and Cardano guests. AI veterans argued over which path leads to general intelligence, physicists debated string theory, and many guests took on free speech, institutions and pandemic policy. Fighters and coaches returned all year to talk about how champions are made.

## Main themes

### Crypto money and blockchain design

- Anthony Pompliano, Nic Carter and Robert Breedlove each argue that Bitcoin's fixed supply makes it a store of value and a check on central-bank inflation ([Pompliano](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/anthony-pompliano-bitcoin/), [Carter](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/nic-carter-bitcoin-core-values-layered-scaling-and-blocksize-debates/), [Breedlove](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/04/robert-breedlove-philosophy-of-bitcoin-from-first-principles/)).
- Silvio Micali, Vitalik Buterin and Charles Hoskinson present competing designs for scaling, from cryptographic lotteries to proof of stake and rollups ([Micali](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/silvio-micali-cryptocurrency-blockchain-algorand-bitcoin-and-ethereum/), [Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-2-0/), [Hoskinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/charles-hoskinson-cardano/)).
- Alex Gladstein makes the human rights case for Bitcoin, while Fridman questions the community's certainty ([Gladstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/alex-gladstein-bitcoin-authoritarianism-and-human-rights/)).

### Rival paths to AI

- Douglas Lenat argues AI needs millions of hand-built common-sense rules, while Jay McClelland defends thought emerging from networks of simple units ([Lenat](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/douglas-lenat-cyc-and-the-quest-to-solve-common-sense-reasoning-in-ai/), [McClelland](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/jay-mcclelland-neural-networks-and-the-emergence-of-cognition/)).
- Wojciech Zaremba sees scaled next-word prediction and Codex as the practical path to more general AI ([Zaremba](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/wojciech-zaremba-openai-codex-gpt-3-robotics-and-the-future-of-ai/)); Jeff Hawkins builds on cortical columns instead ([Hawkins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/08/jeff-hawkins-the-thousand-brains-theory-of-intelligence/)).
- Rodney Brooks says real-world robots are far harder than demos suggest, while Elon Musk bets on camera-only self-driving and a humanoid robot ([Brooks](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/rodney-brooks-robotics/), [Musk](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/elon-musk-spacex-mars-tesla-autopilot-self-driving-robotics-and-ai/)).
- Max Tegmark wants AI to be intelligible, not just powerful ([Tegmark](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/max-tegmark-ai-and-physics/)).

### Physics and the edge of the solar system

- Cumrun Vafa defends string theory through its theoretical connections; Peter Woit says it can accommodate almost anything ([Vafa](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/cumrun-vafa-string-theory/), [Woit](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/peter-woit-theories-of-everything-and-why-string-theory-is-not-even-wr/)).
- Konstantin Batygin makes the case for Planet 9, and Avi Loeb argues 'Oumuamua may be alien technology ([Batygin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/konstantin-batygin-planet-9-and-the-edge-of-our-solar-system/), [Loeb](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/01/avi-loeb-aliens-black-holes-and-the-mystery-of-the-oumuamua/)).
- Stephen Wolfram explains how simple rules generate complexity ([Wolfram](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/stephen-wolfram-complexity-and-the-fabric-of-reality/)).

### Speech, institutions and the cycles of power

- Eric Weinstein, Ronald Sullivan and Ryan Schiller argue that institutions and campuses suppress dissent ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/eric-weinstein-difficult-conversations-freedom-of-speech-and-physics/), [Sullivan](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ronald-sullivan-the-ideal-of-justice-in-the-face-of-controversy-and-ev/), [Schiller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/03/ryan-schiller-librex-and-the-free-exchange-of-ideas-on-college-campuse/)).
- Ray Dalio describes a big cycle of debt and internal order, and Niall Ferguson calls history a garden of forking paths ([Dalio](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/ray-dalio-money-power-and-the-collapse-of-empires/), [Ferguson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/niall-ferguson-history-of-money-power-war-and-truth/)).
- Michael Malice calls the state illegitimate force, while Fridman defends government as an imperfect collective ([Malice](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/07/michael-malice-totalitarianism-and-anarchy/)).

### Pandemic policy and public trust

- Vincent Racaniello says underinvestment and poor communication left the world unprepared; Michael Mina says regulation held back cheap home tests ([Racaniello](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/09/vincent-racaniello-viruses-and-vaccines/), [Mina](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/10/michael-mina-rapid-covid-testing/)).
- Fridman presses Pfizer's Albert Bourla and NIH's Francis Collins on trust, transparency and oversight ([Bourla](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/albert-bourla-pfizer-ceo/), [Collins](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/11/francis-collins-national-institutes-of-health-nih/)).
- Bret Weinstein and Jamie Metzl argue that institutions distorted or blocked open inquiry into COVID-19 ([Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/bret-weinstein-truth-science-and-censorship-in-the-time-of-a-pandemic/), [Metzl](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/12/jamie-metzl-lab-leak-theory/)).
