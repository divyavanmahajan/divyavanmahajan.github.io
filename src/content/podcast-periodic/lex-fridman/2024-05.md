## Overview

May had only two episodes, both about the natural world rather than politics. Paul Rosolie spoke from deep in the Peruvian Amazon about conservation, and neuroscientist Charan Ranganath explained why memory exists to serve the future, with a closing comparison to AI.

## Main themes

### Conservation in the Amazon

- Recorded at night after a hard expedition, Paul Rosolie argues the jungle's dangerous animals are mostly defensive and that the real threats are logging, gold mining and road building ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
- Rosolie credits listeners of his first appearance with a wave of donations to Jungle Keepers, whose protected land has grown to 66,000 acres. He says $30 million over two years would create a corridor linking uncontacted-tribe reserves with Manu National Park and Tambopata ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).
- He stresses people as well as trees, from helping Puerto Nuevo win land title to ranger careers. Fridman argues that thinking about aliens teaches humility, while Rosolie puts Earth's known life first ([Paul Rosolie](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/paul-rosolie-jungle-apex-predators-aliens-uncontacted-tribes-and-god/)).

### Memory, and what AI is missing

- Charan Ranganath argues memory is a selective, reconstructive tool for the present and future, not an archive. Forgetting is by design, and the goal is remembering better, not more ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- He says memories form at moments of surprise or transition, and that errors drive learning: testing yourself works because exposing mistakes resembles backprop ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
- The same reconstruction explains deja vu and false memories. Asked about OpenAI adding memory, Ranganath says he will not accept that AI replicates human intelligence until it makes the same characteristic mistakes people make ([Charan Ranganath](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2024/05/charan-ranganath-human-memory-imagination-deja-vu-and-false-memories/)).
