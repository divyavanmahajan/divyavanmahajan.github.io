## Overview

July had two long conversations that came at AI from opposite ends. David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH) talked about programming as a craft, small companies and owning your own servers, while Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind talked about AGI, scientific discovery and risk. The one thread linking them was what AI means for programmers.

## Main themes

### AI and the work of programmers

- DHH uses AI every day as a tutor and pair programmer but types his own code. He says that when AI drives, “I can literally feel competence draining out of my fingers” ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- He doubts the claim that 90% of code will be AI-written by year end. Lex Fridman counters that vibe coding with careful correction could become a real skill ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- Hassabis says coding and maths are easier for AI because synthetic data can be generated and checked. He expects great programmers who embrace AI tools to become about 10x more productive ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- More broadly, Hassabis expects AI to have about ten times the impact of the Industrial Revolution, arriving ten times faster ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).

### Small software and owning the stack

- DHH says Basecamp and HEY are each just over 100,000 lines of code, the first Basecamp took 400 billed hours, and the default 37signals team is one programmer and one designer ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- 37signals' AWS spend peaked at about $3.2-3.4 million a year. It moved seven applications onto Dell servers in just over six months and projects about $10 million in savings over five years. DHH notes that AWS runs at almost 40% margin ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- He says 37signals tried engineering managers and concluded it should not have them, and that hiring turns on a cover letter and a realistic programming test ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).
- On Apple, DHH recounts the HEY fight over the 30% App Store cut and says Apple is terribly behind on AI, with faltering software quality ([DHH](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/dhh-david-heinemeier-hansson-dhh-future-of-programming-ai-ruby-on-rail/)).

### Testing for AGI and learning nature

- Hassabis puts AGI by 2030 at about a coin flip. He says a real AGI cannot be a jagged intelligence that is strong in some areas and flawed in others ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- He would test it with tens of thousands of cognitive tasks and months of probing by top experts, and looks for Move 37 moments, such as deriving relativity from knowledge cut off in 1900 ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- His wider argument is that systems shaped by selection have learnable structure. He cites AlphaFold, AlphaGo and Veo 3, and describes AlphaEvolve as LLMs proposing solutions with evolutionary search on top and a human in the loop ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).

### Risk and games

- Hassabis will not give a p(doom) figure but says “it’s definitely non-zero and it’s probably non-negligible”. He wants about ten times more safety research and says restricting bad actors while giving good actors access is unsolved ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
- On games, he expects AI within five to ten years to generate stories around the player, calling it “the ultimate choose your own adventure sort of game” ([Demis Hassabis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/07/demis-hassabis-future-of-ai-simulating-reality-physics-and-video-games/)).
