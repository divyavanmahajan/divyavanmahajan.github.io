## Overview

February followed the GameStop saga, with two guests explaining WallStreetBets and Robinhood from inside finance and venture capital. Builders of JavaScript, Brave, AI chips and satellite thrusters described how they make things work, and the two Weinsteins argued about speech, institutions and stagnation. The month closed with Andrew Huberman on sleep and effort.

## Main themes

### GameStop, Robinhood and crowds

- Richard Craib says redditors bought GameStop because it was shorted beyond its float, which made a squeeze a positive-sum trade. He separates concentrated, press-driven shorting from quant shorting like Numerai's ([Richard Craib](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/richard-craib-wallstreetbets-numerai-and-the-future-of-stock-trading/)).
- Craib's Numerai gives away obfuscated data, takes only predictions and uses NMR token staking to weight a crowdsourced ensemble ([Richard Craib](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/richard-craib-wallstreetbets-numerai-and-the-future-of-stock-trading/)).
- Jason Calacanis, an early Robinhood investor, says the trading pause came from a liquidity crunch and collateral demands, not hedge fund pressure, though it was poorly communicated. Craib also points to collateral demands from prime brokers ([Jason Calacanis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jason-calacanis-startups-angel-investing-capitalism-and-friendship/), [Richard Craib](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/richard-craib-wallstreetbets-numerai-and-the-future-of-stock-trading/)).
- Calacanis explains venture maths: seven of ten startups go to zero, so one or two must return the fund. He blames engagement-ranked feeds for outrage culture ([Jason Calacanis](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jason-calacanis-startups-angel-investing-capitalism-and-friendship/)).

### Building computers, browsers and engines

- Jim Keller argues that great systems come from craftsmanship and clean abstraction layers, and that scale beats efficiency. He says GPUs emulate graphs, so Tenstorrent builds hardware that runs them directly ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jim-keller-the-future-of-computing-ai-life-and-consciousness/)).
- Brendan Eich recounts writing JavaScript in about 10 days and says the web rewards shipping first and improving steadily ([Brendan Eich](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/brendan-eich-javascript-firefox-mozilla-and-brave/)).
- Eich explains that third-party cookies made cross-site tracking possible by accident. Brave blocks them and matches ads locally, giving users 70% of gross ad revenue ([Brendan Eich](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/brendan-eich-javascript-firefox-mozilla-and-brave/)).
- Natalya Bailey of Accion Systems says ion engines once cost 20-25 million dollars while customers now want them for around 10,000. Chip-scale manufacturing of colloid thrusters is her answer ([Natalya Bailey](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/natalya-bailey-rocket-engines-and-electric-spacecraft-propulsion/)).

### Speech, institutions and stagnation

- Eric Weinstein argues that speech once came bundled with locality and friction, so the internet needs a new legal abstraction. Fridman says Amazon removing Parler from AWS troubled him more than platform bans ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/eric-weinstein-difficult-conversations-freedom-of-speech-and-physics/)).
- Weinstein says institutions have lost coherence and starve unconventional science. He calls growth a societal immune system and plans to release Geometric Unity on April 1st ([Eric Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/eric-weinstein-difficult-conversations-freedom-of-speech-and-physics/)).
- His son Zev Weinstein argues that deep thinking becomes more dangerous when growth stalls, yet first-principles thinking is the only way out ([Zev Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/zev-weinstein-the-next-generation-of-big-ideas-and-brave-minds/)).

### Mind, sleep and new science media

- Andrew Huberman explains that sleep has two drivers, adenosine build-up and a 24-hour temperature cycle, and says the culture has created too much anxiety about sleep ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/andrew-huberman-sleep-dreams-creativity-the-limits-of-the-human-mind/)).
- Huberman and Fridman see value in machine learning and cameras for reading a person's state, and argue science communication should not be left to institutions ([Andrew Huberman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/andrew-huberman-sleep-dreams-creativity-the-limits-of-the-human-mind/)).
- Keller turns from chips to leadership, describing organisations drifting from chaos toward stifling order unless a leader pushes back ([Jim Keller](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/02/jim-keller-the-future-of-computing-ai-life-and-consciousness/)).
