## Overview

April split between geopolitics and creative work. Dave Smith made a libertarian anti-war case and historian Jeffrey Wasserstrom explained Xi Jinping's China in the middle of a tariff war. Robert Rodriguez and Tim Sweeney talked about building films and games with small teams and the right tools.

## Main themes

### War, blowback and responsibility

- Dave Smith argues that killing innocent people is never justified and that campaigns from Yemen to Gaza create more of the enemy they target, citing General McChrystal's insurgent math ([Dave Smith](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/dave-smith-israel-ukraine-epstein-mossad-conspiracies-antisemitism/)).
- Smith says NATO expansion provoked the Ukraine invasion while agreeing Putin bears responsibility for launching it. Fridman pushes back on Russia's responsibility and on whether a stated aim to spare civilians carries ethical weight ([Dave Smith](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/dave-smith-israel-ukraine-epstein-mossad-conspiracies-antisemitism/)).
- Smith, who is Jewish, says he sees more Jew hatred on X than ever, and that calling legitimate criticism antisemitic empties the word ([Dave Smith](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/dave-smith-israel-ukraine-epstein-mossad-conspiracies-antisemitism/)).

### China under Xi

- Wasserstrom says Xi shares Mao's personality cult but not his love of chaos, borrowing from Confucius, Mao and even Chiang Kai-shek to support single-party rule ([Wasserstrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/jeffrey-wasserstrom-china-xi-jinping-trade-war-taiwan-hong-kong-mao/)).
- He describes a post-1989 bargain of consumer choice in exchange for no political choice, with room for change widening until the 2008 Olympics and narrowing since, as Hong Kong shows ([Wasserstrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/jeffrey-wasserstrom-china-xi-jinping-trade-war-taiwan-hong-kong-mao/)).
- With US tariffs at 145% and Chinese at 125%, Wasserstrom argues relations hinge on third parties, and that a move on Taiwan is more likely if Xi feels desperate than confident ([Wasserstrom](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/jeffrey-wasserstrom-china-xi-jinping-trade-war-taiwan-hong-kong-mao/)).

### Games, engines and platform power

- Tim Sweeney says engine licences kept Epic alive while Unreal was built, and that Epic has always shipped its tools with its games ([Tim Sweeney](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/tim-sweeney-fortnite-unreal-engine-and-the-future-of-gaming/)).
- He sees large multiplayer social games like Fortnite as the real metaverse. Verse, a new functional logic language, aims to lift Unreal's single-threaded limit of about 100 players per match ([Tim Sweeney](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/tim-sweeney-fortnite-unreal-engine-and-the-future-of-gaming/)).
- Sweeney calls Apple's 30% cut a junk fee and puts the cost of running Google Play at about 6% of revenue. He expects AI to multiply what artists can do rather than replace them ([Tim Sweeney](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/tim-sweeney-fortnite-unreal-engine-and-the-future-of-gaming/)).

### Small teams beating big budgets

- Robert Rodriguez argues that limits on money, time and equipment improve the work. He made El Mariachi for $7,000 as a practice film and it won Sundance ([Robert Rodriguez](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/robert-rodriguez-sin-city-desperado-el-mariachi-alita-and-filmmaking/)).
- He says a director who can edit in his head moves faster than a crew of 150 to 200 where nobody edits, which is how he shot Bruce Willis's Sin City scenes in nine days ([Robert Rodriguez](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/robert-rodriguez-sin-city-desperado-el-mariachi-alita-and-filmmaking/)).
- Sweeney tells a parallel story: about 30 people built Fortnite Battle Royale in four weeks ([Tim Sweeney](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/04/tim-sweeney-fortnite-unreal-engine-and-the-future-of-gaming/)).
