## Overview

September ranged widely: a metaverse interview with Mark Zuckerberg recorded as photorealistic avatars, a biographer's view of Elon Musk, and long conversations on free speech, divorce and design. Meta and Albert Einstein were the only items that came up in more than one episode. AI appeared mainly through Meta's plans for many specialised assistants and through Musk's real-world AI work.

## Main themes

### Meta's mixed reality and many AIs

- Zuckerberg explains that Codec Avatars send an encoded model of a face, which uses less bandwidth than video. Today's scans take hours; the goal is a two-to-three-minute phone scan, rolled out over the next few years ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).
- He calls the $500 Quest 3 the first mainstream mixed reality device and predicts rooms holding as many holograms as physical objects by about the end of the decade ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).
- Meta wants many AIs rather than one superintelligence: a plain assistant, business AIs and character AIs. Zuckerberg says creator AIs are “a much harder problem, I think, than starting with new characters” ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).
- He says the value of open-sourcing Llama 2 is significantly greater than the risks, and hopes to open-source the next model after similar red-teaming ([Zuckerberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/mark-zuckerberg-first-interview-in-the-metaverse/)).

### Musk, Einstein and the biographer's craft

- Walter Isaacson argues Musk harnesses the demons of a difficult childhood, and that his faults and achievements cannot be separated ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)).
- He lists Musk's three missions: multi-planetary humans, sustainable energy, and making sure AI “is safe and is aligned with human values” ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)).
- Isaacson links Einstein, Leonardo, Jobs and Musk as visual thinkers, and predicts Musk will be remembered for real-world AI such as FSD 12 and Optimus ([Isaacson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/walter-isaacson-elon-musk-steve-jobs-einstein-da-vinci-ben-franklin/)). Einstein also came up with Neri Oxman, through his letter to his daughter about love ([Oxman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/neri-oxman-biology-art-and-science-of-design-engineering-with-nature/)).

### Cancel culture and campus speech

- Greg Lukianoff counts well over 1,000 attempts to punish professors since 2014, about two-thirds successful and around 190 firings, and says about a third of successful attempts come from the right ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).
- At the bottom five schools in FIRE's ranking, 81% of deplatforming attempts succeeded. He argues administrators often help shout-downs happen ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).
- He compares social media to the printing press, a period of epistemic anarchy that top-down bans cannot fix ([Lukianoff](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/greg-lukianoff-cancel-culture-deplatforming-censorship-free-speech/)).

### Marriage, divorce and design with nature

- James Sexton argues marriages end through slow disconnection rather than one event. He cites 56% of marriages ending in divorce but 84% remarrying within five years ([Sexton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/james-sexton-divorce-lawyer-on-marriage-relationships-sex-lies-love/)).
- Sexton says an agreed divorce costs about $3,500 to $5,000, while contested cases can run to millions. He calls prenups an excellent idea and warns against weaponising children ([Sexton](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/james-sexton-divorce-lawyer-on-marriage-relationships-sex-lies-love/)).
- Oxman argues design should grow products with nature rather than assemble them against it, using what she calls computational templating, from the Silk Pavilion to her company OXMAN ([Oxman](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2023/09/neri-oxman-biology-art-and-science-of-design-engineering-with-nature/)).
