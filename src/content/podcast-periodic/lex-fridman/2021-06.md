## Overview

June 2021 was led by two blockchain founders, Vitalik Buterin and Charles Hoskinson, who both made the case for proof of stake from rival camps. A second cluster dealt with risk and trust: how civilisation coordinates against catastrophe, and how institutions handled the pandemic. Mathematics and the search for life on Venus rounded out the month.

## Main themes

### Proof of stake and rival blockchains

- Buterin says the team has been de-emphasising the Eth 2.0 brand in favour of step-by-step upgrades. Proof of stake would cut new ether issuance from about 4.7 million a year to between 500,000 and one million ([Vitalik Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-2-0/)).
- Buterin estimates about 100x from rollups times about 100x from sharding, and argues that misbehaving validators can be identified and slashed ([Vitalik Buterin](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-2-0/)).
- Hoskinson says proof of work concentrates into “a ruling class of a mining oligarchy”, while Cardano's design pushes stake towards more than 3,000 pools ([Charles Hoskinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/charles-hoskinson-cardano/)).
- Hoskinson calls Cardano a financial operating system built on 105 papers and Haskell, and is openly critical of Bitcoin's refusal to evolve. Fridman defends fun and risk-taking as sources of innovation ([Charles Hoskinson](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/charles-hoskinson-cardano/)).

### Civilisation and catastrophic risk

- Daniel Schmachtenberger credits the Bretton Woods order with about 75 years without superpower war, but says “the bretonwood solution is basically over or almost over” ([Daniel Schmachtenberger](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/daniel-schmachtenberger-steering-civilization-away-from-self-destructi/)).
- He argues deterrence works with two players but not with many actors holding cheap weapons such as drones, and that single metrics on platforms become paperclip maximisers ([Daniel Schmachtenberger](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/daniel-schmachtenberger-steering-civilization-away-from-self-destructi/)).
- Fridman is more optimistic, pointing to love, markets and Wikipedia as forces for emergent order ([Daniel Schmachtenberger](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/daniel-schmachtenberger-steering-civilization-away-from-self-destructi/)).

### Pandemic policy, trust and health economics

- Rob Reid argues for an international agreement against risky pathogen research and for affordable detection systems, and criticises public-health messaging for not stating probabilities ([Rob Reid](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/rob-reid-the-existential-threat-of-engineered-viruses-and-lab-leaks/)).
- Bret Weinstein argues scientific institutions were captured and that platforms censored dissent. Fridman frames the episode as a defence of open discussion and says he disagrees with Weinstein on some points ([Bret Weinstein](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/bret-weinstein-truth-science-and-censorship-in-the-time-of-a-pandemic/)).
- David Sinclair expects cheap sensors, home tests and AI to reshape healthcare, with hospitals and insurers driven by cost. He calls health data security his top concern ([David Sinclair](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/david-sinclair-extending-the-human-lifespan-beyond-100-years/)).

### Mathematics, shapes and machines

- Jordan Ellenberg says much of modern mathematics asks when two things should count as the same, through symmetry, topology and unusual notions of distance ([Jordan Ellenberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/jordan-ellenberg-mathematics-of-high-dimensional-shapes-and-geometries/)).
- He notes that tasks once worth a PhD thesis now take a few lines of code, while AI attempts to prove interesting theorems have so far not done well ([Jordan Ellenberg](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/jordan-ellenberg-mathematics-of-high-dimensional-shapes-and-geometries/)).

### Looking for life on Venus

- Clara Sousa-Silva says the Venus phosphine signal is exciting but still unconfirmed, and that only about 4% of the molecules of interest have understood spectral fingerprints ([Clara Sousa-Silva](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/clara-sousa-silva-searching-for-signs-of-life-on-venus-and-other-plane/)).
- She expects simple life to be common and intelligent life rare, and finds alien visits less likely than a hollow Earth. Fridman takes the more open-minded view ([Clara Sousa-Silva](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2021/06/clara-sousa-silva-searching-for-signs-of-life-on-venus-and-other-plane/)).
