## Overview

June returned to AI. Sundar Pichai explained how Google recovered its footing in the AI race, and Terence Tao described what AI and proof assistants can and cannot yet do for mathematics. The month closed with a heated debate on the US strikes on Iran's nuclear sites.

## Main themes

### Google's AI bets, from Pichai's chair

- Sundar Pichai repeats that “AI is the most profound technology humanity will ever work on”, because it accelerates creation itself. Fridman notes analysts had called for him to step down when Google seemed to be losing the AI race ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- Pichai names merging Brain and DeepMind as the key decision, alongside TPUs and Waymo. Monthly Gemini tokens grew 50x in a year to 480 trillion, and Google skips Ultra models because each Pro reaches most of Ultra's capability more cheaply ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- AI Mode fans out multiple searches per query as a separate tab, and Pichai says sending users to the human-created web stays a core design principle ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- He measures a 10% gain in engineering velocity from AI, with about 30% of code using AI suggestions, yet plans to hire more engineers and add in-person interviews to check fundamentals ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).

### AI timelines and the limits of today's models

- Pichai uses Karpathy's term artificial jagged intelligence for today's uneven models. He expects dramatic progress by 2030 but says “I don’t think we’ll quite get there by 2030”, placing AGI slightly after ([Sundar Pichai](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/sundar-pichai-ceo-of-google-and-alphabet/)).
- Terence Tao estimates current AI tools replicate 30 to 40% of the skills mathematics needs. He warns that AI-generated proofs can look superficially flawless while hiding basic errors, and that guiding a model through a known proof was more exhausting than working alone ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).
- Tao notes AlphaProof's silver-medal result needed about three days of Google server time per problem, and still predicts research-level human-AI collaborations by 2026 ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).

### Mathematics at scale with Lean

- Tao's Equational Theories Project used Lean to settle about 22 million implications among some 4,000 algebraic laws with about 50 contributors; all but two were settled ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).
- He explains why Navier-Stokes and the twin prime conjecture resist proof: a conspiracy that only special cases trigger cannot be ruled out. He recalls the Green-Tao theorem as negotiating a property one collaborator could prove and the other could use ([Terence Tao](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/terence-tao-hardest-problems-in-mathematics-physics-the-future-of-ai/)).

### The Iran strikes debate

- Mark Dubowitz defends the US strikes as deterrence. He says Trump told Iran to dismantle its programme under US supervision or have it destroyed, and that Khamenei erred in rejecting the Oman offer ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
- Scott Horton argues Iran sought only a latent deterrent, that zero enrichment is a poison pill, and that attacking Iran makes a bomb more likely. Both agree Trump should not have left the JCPOA without negotiating a better deal ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
- Dubowitz expects fewer nuclear states if the strikes work; Horton fears the NPT could fall apart. Fridman intervened when Horton questioned Dubowitz's loyalty as an immigrant ([Iran debate](/podcasts/lex-fridman/2025/06/iran-war-debate-nuclear-weapons-trump-peace-power-the-middle-east/)).
