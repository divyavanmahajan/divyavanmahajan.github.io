## Overview

In 2026 The Moonshot Podcast walked through X's projects one domain at a time: food, health, energy, water, construction, chips, security and supply chains. The common thread was how X decides to stop: kill criteria, cost models and a culture that treats failure as learning. The year so far closed with Astro Teller, Adam Savage and Sergey Brin on the moonshot mindset.

## Main themes

### Kill criteria and cost models

- Kathryn Zealand says X can only narrow hundreds of projects to a handful by getting very good at killing them ([Supercharging Human Health](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/03/supercharging-human-health/)).
- Makani's wind kites were shut down when cost models showed no radical advantage, while Malta spun out with a deadline set against future lithium battery costs ([Chasing the Wind, Storing the Sun](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/)).
- H2E and Foghorn both worked technically but missed their cost-based kill criteria; X shut them down and published the results ([Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/)).

### Energy, water and climate

- Raj Apte argues storage cost is about the quantity of energy stored, and that “you can't really go past 20% or 30% of your grid without putting in big scale storage” ([Chasing the Wind, Storing the Sun](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/)).
- Kathy Hannun pitched Foghorn's drop-in fuel as a way to “Reinvent the fuel instead of reinvent every car, every plane, every boat” ([Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/)).
- Hannun later founded Dandelion, which cuts the upfront cost of home geothermal and now installs hundreds of systems at a time with home builders ([Underground Potential](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/05/underground-potential/)).

### Making hidden data visible

- Emily Ma says Chorus was meant to give food, and later everything, a voice so it could report where it is and whether it is near temperature limits ([Emily Ma on Solving Food Waste](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/01/moonshot-podcast-deep-dive-emily-ma-on-solving-food-waste/)).
- Chronicle turned Google's security log processing into a service that correlates data over years rather than days ([The Digital Trail](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/05/the-digital-trail/)).
- Rey Banatao argues recycling fails for lack of molecular data on materials, which Materra's AI sorting aims to supply ([Rey Banatao on Turning Trash into Treasure](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/03/the-moonshot-podcast-deep-dive-rey-banatao-on-turning-trash-into-treas/)).

### Chips, AI and engineering with models

- Tammo Spalink says training chips optimise for throughput while inference chips should optimise for response time; Positron and Bodger bet on specialised chips and automated chip design ([Silicon Horizons](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/silicon-horizons/)).
- Sergey Brin calls Google Brain, which started at X, probably X's most important project ([The Moonshot Mindset](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/06/the-moonshot-mindset-with-adam-savage-and-sergey-brin/)).
- X teams apply models to plant breeding and biomanufacturing ([Coding The Natural World](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/coding-the-natural-world/)), and Anori applies AI to building design and permitting ([Breaking New Ground](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/breaking-new-ground/)).

### Failure as the method

- Astro Teller says “Radical innovation is mostly about failure and learning” and that X has started and killed about 2000 ideas in 16 years ([The Moonshot Mindset](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/06/the-moonshot-mindset-with-adam-savage-and-sergey-brin/)).
- Teller argues moonshots are speeding up; Brin says society will have to adjust to adjusting as AI improves ([The Moonshot Mindset](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/06/the-moonshot-mindset-with-adam-savage-and-sergey-brin/)).
