## Overview

July brought two deep-dive interviews with Astro Teller about machines that move among people. André Prager explained how Wing made drone delivery work through radical simplicity, and Catie Cuan argued that choreography and sound decide whether robots feel safe and welcome.

## Main themes

### Drone delivery is a systems problem

- Prager says flying the aircraft was largely solved when he joined Wing, with about 30 people and Adam Woodworth's split hover and cruise architecture already in place. The hard part was loading, charging, traffic and where drones sleep ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).
- A single nest in Brisbane did more than a thousand deliveries a day after COVID. Prager compares that to a grain of sand dropped into an aquarium every 30 seconds, and says outbound paths are randomised ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).
- Teller recalls the 2015-2016 reset of shuttling drones between two pads a thousand times a week, which Prager calls the turning point ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).

### Simple hardware, smarter software

- Prager's rule is that parts which do not exist need no development: “Everything that's not there doesn't break.” Intelligence is added later through software updates ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).
- The passive hook took two years and about 90 prototypes. The winch motor senses force and position, so software can detect pulls, touchdown and snags ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).
- His patented autoloader, inspired by a restaurant umbrella, has no power, electronics or moving parts, so operators can keep spares. A contact charging pad replaced heavy inductive coils ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).

### Finding the real source of noise

- Halving hover noise with lower-RPM propellers and unequal blade pairs did not change complaint rates. Data showed complaints came from people overflown in cruise, who heard a sound like a Formula 1 car ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).
- Four cruise propellers, a longer wingspan and flying about 4 m/s slower largely ended the complaints ([Wing](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-andre-prager-on-prototyping-at-wing/)).

### Choreography and sound in robot design

- Cuan argues robots differ from computers because they move, so choreographers belong in robot design. She predicts tens of thousands of robot choreographers in future ([Robots](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-catie-cuan-on-dancing-and-living-with-robots/)).
- Unpredictable motion reads as creepy, such as grabbing a cup without looking at it first. In settings like nursing homes, she says, the perception of safety matters as much as safety itself ([Robots](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-catie-cuan-on-dancing-and-living-with-robots/)).
- Everyday Robots' music mode mapped each joint to an instrument, bells for the gripper and cello for the torso. A study found people rated these robots as more animate, likable, intelligent and safe ([Robots](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-catie-cuan-on-dancing-and-living-with-robots/)).

### Living with billions of robots

- Cuan expects billions of robots in our lifetimes and notes the outside field once saw machine learning in robotics as a dead end; now “you cannot be a robotics company today without having an AI story.” ([Robots](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-catie-cuan-on-dancing-and-living-with-robots/)).
- On jobs, she cites a Wall Street Journal figure that 57% of today's jobs did not exist in the 1960s, and points to roles such as robot veterinarians ([Robots](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/07/deep-dive-catie-cuan-on-dancing-and-living-with-robots/)).
