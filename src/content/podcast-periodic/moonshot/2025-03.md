## Overview

March was the show's opening run, with Astro Teller using X's fifteenth anniversary to tell the origin stories of its best-known projects. Episodes covered self-driving cars and delivery drones, the birth of Google Brain, internet by balloon and laser, and data-driven farming on land and at sea. A common thread was X's habit of building rough prototypes fast and attacking the hardest part first.

## Main themes

### Waymo, Wing and the hard part after the demo

- Sebastian Thrun says Larry Page pushed him to build a self-driving car when he thought it was insanely hard, and that a friend's death in a traffic accident drove his interest in car safety ([Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/)).
- Andrew Chatham recalls a freeway driver-assist trial cancelled early after testers fell asleep, which led Waymo to aim for full level-four autonomy. He says deep learning from around 2014 made the service possible and that Waymo now leans on simulation and synthetic data ([Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/)).
- For Wing, André Prager says “the flying part isn't so much the moonshot. The interesting part is everything else”, meaning payload handling, charging and traffic management. Margaret Nagel describes the path from per-flight approvals to over 450,000 commercial deliveries ([Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/)).

### Google Brain and the bet on scale

- Andrew Ng says senior researchers dismissed the idea that bigger learning systems perform better. A sushi dinner with Larry Page, suggested by Thrun, launched what became Google Brain ([Machines That Think](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/machines-that-think-the-ai-moonshot/)).
- Jeff Dean explains why large networks need huge datasets and a lot of compute, and recalls the unlabelled YouTube cat detector that made the New York Times front page ([Machines That Think](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/machines-that-think-the-ai-moonshot/)).
- Benoît Schillings argues software is still written much as it was in 1982. Project Ada trained models to evolve and write code before moving to Google, and Ng describes building an app for his daughter in under a day ([Machines That Think](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/machines-that-think-the-ai-moonshot/)).
- Scott Huffman expects non-coders to build whole apps soon, while Ng argues AI can make intelligence cheap ([Machines That Think](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/machines-that-think-the-ai-moonshot/)).

### Connectivity by balloon and laser

- Loon steered balloons in the stratosphere by changing altitude, and an AI model taught itself to tack like a sailboat. Nick Kohli says it ended because the economics did not work for telecom partners ([Essential Connections](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/essential-connections/)).
- Mahesh Krishnaswamy explains that light can carry far more data than radio. Loon's balloon-to-balloon laser links became Taara, whose terminals beam fibre-speed internet between rooftops ([Essential Connections](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/essential-connections/)).
- Strive Masiyiwa says Taara linked Kinshasa and Brazzaville across the Congo river in under half a day, cutting Brazzaville's data costs ([Essential Connections](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/essential-connections/)).

### Data for food production

- Elliott Grant warns that there is “not a lot of slack in the global system for food” and argues AI lacked good field data, so Mineral built rovers to photograph crops ([Feeding The World](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/feeding-the-world/)).
- David Guerena says breeders had never seen the continuous flower and pod data the rovers produced, and hopes to cut a 10-year bean breeding cycle to 2-3 years ([Feeding The World](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/feeding-the-world/)).
- Evan Rapoport notes that more than half of the world's seafood is farmed. Tidal estimates salmon weight from stereo images and now runs in hundreds of pens across four countries ([Feeding The World](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/feeding-the-world/)).

### Prototyping culture

- Teller's rule is to “Start with the riskiest part of the problem first, teaching the monkey to recite Shakespeare” ([Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/)).
- Joe Sargent's design kitchen built Wing's early drones and Mineral's crude rigs, from wheelchair motors to a golf cart on stilts ([Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/), [Feeding The World](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/feeding-the-world/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Driving The Future](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/driving-the-future/): the founding stories of Waymo and Wing, and how X organises small, secret teams.
- [Machines That Think – The AI Moonshot](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/machines-that-think-the-ai-moonshot/): Ng and Dean on the scaling bet behind Google Brain, plus early AI coding work.
- [Essential Connections](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/essential-connections/): a candid account of why Loon ended and how its laser links lived on as Taara.
- [Feeding The World](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/03/feeding-the-world/): two projects that treated field data as the missing ingredient for AI in farming.
