## Overview

April on The Moonshot Podcast was about how X decides what to keep building. Astro Teller revisited energy and water projects that worked technically but failed on cost, and two chip projects that graduated to Google. Episodes on building design and on biology as an engineering discipline rounded out the month.

## Main themes

### Cost models and kill criteria

- Fort Felker says Makani's tethered wind kite worked, but cost models showed only a marginal saving as conventional turbines got much cheaper. He advises starting hard cost modelling early ([Chasing the Wind](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/)).
- Raj Apte compared Malta's molten-salt storage with where lithium battery prices would be in future. His model implied a sell-by date around 2040, so the team had to move at once; Malta is now a separate company preparing a site in Spain ([Chasing the Wind](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/)).
- H2E aimed for water from air at $0.01 per liter and reached line of sight to about $0.10. Neil Treat thinks a new material could close the gap ([Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/)).
- Kathy Hannun killed Foghorn's seawater-to-fuel work when no path to $5 a gallon appeared, saying “Opportunity cost is so high at X because there's so much opportunity at X” ([Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/)).

### Prototypes, failure and what comes after

- Teller asked Felker to crash at least five aircraft early, and Felker ran two tether designs in parallel; the solid-rod one broke on its first flight ([Chasing the Wind](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/)).
- Both water projects built working prototypes, then published their results. Foghorn's technology later went into Ebb Carbon, and Hannun went on to start Dandelion ([Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/)).

### Chips for the AI era

- Tammo Spalink says training chips optimise for throughput while inference chips should optimise for response time. Positron's early bet on small 8-bit and 4-bit formats is now standard in inference chips ([Silicon Horizons](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/silicon-horizons/)).
- Apte puts chip design at $10-20 million for a small chip and $50-100 million for a medium one. He wants a hardware compiler that does the job in an hour for $1,000 ([Silicon Horizons](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/silicon-horizons/)).
- Bodger uses inverse design and computer search. Apte says “We don't really care if the chip is explainable. It just needs to be right.” The team is now in-house at Google ([Silicon Horizons](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/silicon-horizons/)).

### AI for designing and permitting buildings

- Michelle Kaufmann says Genie and its spin-out Flux delivered cost-aware building designs in seconds, but were ahead of the technology and the industry ([Breaking New Ground](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/breaking-new-ground/)).
- Adrian Walker cites retiring senior builders, 20-25% contingency per building and a global shortage of about 250 million homes ([Breaking New Ground](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/breaking-new-ground/)).
- Julia Ling says Anori is “not generating images. We're generating specific geometry, specific graphs” checked against regulations. Walker plans the first AI-assisted pilot builds within a year ([Breaking New Ground](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/breaking-new-ground/)).

### Biology as an engineering discipline

- Teller argues the century will be remembered for biology becoming engineering as much as for AI. Brad Zamft describes Heritable Agriculture, an X spin-out that uses data and models to improve crops for a $3 trillion industry ([Coding the Natural World](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/coding-the-natural-world/)).
- Relly Brandman describes A-Life as X's effort to make biomanufacturing predictable with AI models, recommending the most valuable experiments to customers' lab teams ([Coding the Natural World](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/coding-the-natural-world/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Ocean Fuel, Desert Rain](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/ocean-fuel-desert-rain/): the clearest account of X writing kill criteria early and acting on them.
- [Silicon Horizons](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/silicon-horizons/): two chip bets, inference hardware and automated design, that both graduated to Google.
- [Chasing the Wind, Storing the Sun](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/chasing-the-wind-storing-the-sun/): one energy project closed on cost and one spun out against a lithium deadline.
- [Breaking New Ground](/podcasts/moonshot/2026/04/breaking-new-ground/): a second attempt at software for building design, with lessons from the first.
