## Overview

May on The Moonshot Podcast was about prototyping as the engine of learning at X. One episode traced Google Glass from early prototypes to the iyO audio wearable; the other, a live SXSW panel, set out how X treats failure, risk and stopping projects.

## Main themes

### Prototypes exist to be broken

- Astro Teller shows early Glass prototypes that are falling apart after 14 years and says that is the point: “You break them so you can learn” ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- Isabelle Olsson describes shipping a new internal Glass prototype every month, and Joe Sargent says the Design Kitchen built thousands of them, starting from safety glasses with a compute backpack ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- Teller explains why some ideas resist this approach: a space elevator cannot be built a foot higher at a time and would need material far stronger than steel for its weight ([SXSW](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/prototyping-the-future-live-from-sxsw-2025/)).

### What Glass taught X

- The Explorer programme gave Glass to about 10,000 people, including hairdressers, firefighters and nurses. Teller calls it one of the most interesting things X has done ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- Teller admits marketing made Glass look like a finished product, which led to hype, privacy concerns and backlash; sales paused in early 2015. His main lesson is timing: Glass was too early for consumers ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- Ivo Stivoric says a small team took Glass V2 from chip to production in 11 months for industrial and medical customers, leading to Glass Enterprise Edition in 2019 ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).

### iyO and machine-mediated sound

- Jason Rugolo describes outward-facing microphones feeding an onboard computer that decides what reaches your ears. An early demo separated two people reading aloud at once, in real time at about 30 milliseconds ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- Rugolo chose a visible, custom-fit device over a hidden one, saying the aim is to “Create so much value that that we don't have to hide it.” ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).
- iyO plans to launch with Babel, a translation app built on isolating the target speaker. A Wi-Fi version was targeted for August or September, with LTE later ([Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/)).

### Failure, learning and kill criteria

- Teller argues nobody learns when they are right, so avoiding failure means avoiding learning. He wants to destigmatise failure to tighten learning loops ([SXSW](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/prototyping-the-future-live-from-sxsw-2025/)).
- Catie Cuan adds that “to categorize something as a failure, you have to have a notion of what success is” ([SXSW](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/prototyping-the-future-live-from-sxsw-2025/)).
- Teller says false negatives are free because problems and ideas are plentiful, and uses the monkey-and-pedestal joke to argue for tackling the riskiest part first ([SXSW](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/prototyping-the-future-live-from-sxsw-2025/)).
- He describes X moving from a fire-and-forget attitude towards teams to more coaching and shared time across projects ([SXSW](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/prototyping-the-future-live-from-sxsw-2025/)).

## Notable episodes

- [Supersenses](/podcasts/moonshot/2025/05/supersenses/): a candid look back at Google Glass and how its lessons on timing and wearability shaped iyO.
